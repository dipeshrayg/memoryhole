Today's top stories
New information reveals that, over the weekend, the U.S. removed all long-range bombers from the British air base near where five men were arrested last week in a suspected terror plot. A U.S. official who is not authorized to speak publicly told NPR that the decision to abruptly send home a dozen B-1 bombers from Royal Air Force base Fairford was due to a "credible threat" from Iran, which potentially involved a drone attack.
- 🎧 The war with Iran has shown that U.S. military bases, both at home and abroad, are now seen as more vulnerable, NPR's Greg Myre tells Up First. Iranian drones and missiles have targeted U.S. bases across the Middle East since the start of the conflict. For the past three decades, the U.S. has established these bases to protect its allies, but the rise of drone threats has changed this perspective. Now, the U.S. must defend a network of military bases - a task that is complicated and costly, Myre says.
High school students in France are blocking schools today, after hundreds of thousands of them marched across the country yesterday. They are joined by teachers, parents and labor union members. The movement, which began in a particularly run-down high school facing teacher absenteeism, aims to improve learning conditions and address inequalities in the French education system.
- 🎧 The French school system plays a crucial role in shaping the national identity, promoting the idea that anyone can achieve their goals if they apply themselves in l'école de la Republique, or the school of the republic, NPR's Eleanor Beardsley says. She adds that this concept is at the heart of France's democracy and vision of itself as a meritocracy and egalitarian society. Even elite public schools in Paris are joining the movement. French news reports show clashes between youth and police and cars, trash cans and schools burning. Initially, the far-right condemned delinquency, but many also saw police aggression. Critics argue that the government responded too late and then framed the issue as urban violence instead of addressing the students' demands.
A former head of Germany's foreign intelligence agency will appear before a federal judge today to face charges of espionage and attempted treason. Federal prosecutors accuse August Hanning of using classified information to brief a foreign intelligence agency. He served as the head of Germany's CIA equivalent for seven years from 1998 to 2005. Prosecutors allege he shared sensitive political information for 12 years after leaving office. They have not yet disclosed who Hanning's alleged clients were.
- 🎧 NPR's Esme Nicholson says that categorizing Hanning's alleged actions as a major setback to Germany's security apparatus is putting it mildly. Prosecutors allege that a former aide to Hanning leaked documents intended for high-level briefings, raising concerns among Germany's western allies about the credibility of its intelligence agencies and whether their own agents have been compromised. This arrest coincides with a warning from Martin Jaeger, Germany's new foreign intelligence chief, that Russia is waging a "shadow war" in Germany through sabotage, cyber attacks, and disinformation campaigns. Nicholson says the consequences of this conflict are not only humiliating but also potentially dangerous.
As Israel marks three years since Hamas' surprise Oct. 7 attacks, new reports in Israeli and U.S. media say regional allies warned Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu that Hamas was planning to attack. Four people in the Middle East with direct knowledge of the warnings told NPR that Netanyahu was personally informed about the potential attacks and his security agencies were notified multiple times in the weeks and days leading up to Oct. 7, 2023. Netanyahu has denied this, labeling the reports "fake news" and recently filing a defamation lawsuit against the news outlets that first published the information. He and his defense minister now warn that Hamas might attempt to attack again on this Oct. 7 memorial day and leading up to Israel's national elections later this month.
Living better
Living Better is a special series about what it takes to stay healthy in America.
Parental monitoring plays a key role in shielding kids from the dangers of social media and excessive screen time. Parents can empower their children to navigate the digital world confidently and safely by establishing clear boundaries early and having open conversations about online habits. Here are some ways guardians can help their kids use their devices more safely:
- 📱 Put the phone away during meals and bedtime. This will help protect sleep and reduce the risk of eating disorders.
- 📱 Apply rules around screen time to the whole family, including parents and guardians.
- 📱 Ask children open-minded questions about what they are doing online. Open communication helps kids feel comfortable sharing what they experience online.
- 📱 Establish early communication with children about cyberbullying, online scams, Instagram filters and privacy issues for their awareness.
