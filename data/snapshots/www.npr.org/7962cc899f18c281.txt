Today's top stories
The U.S. government continues to pay for ads promoting President Trump, even after he announced that his MAGA Inc. super PAC would take over funding. A federal judge ordered the ads to be taken down, yet they remained on air. An NPR analysis of data from AdImpact finds that nearly $22 million in taxpayer funds have been spent since the campaign began in late September. Almost $10 million of that was spent after Trump pledged on an Oct. 5 Truth Social post to fund the campaign privately. The White House has called the ads public service announcements, but a lawsuit by Common Cause and others argued they were funded without congressional approval, violating restrictions on taxpayer-funded publicity and propaganda.
Trump has ramped up his campaign travel ahead of the midterm elections. He rallied supporters in Youngstown, Ohio, last night and is heading to Alaska today. More events are planned for later this week. He has been trying to encourage his supporters to vote by telling them to pretend he is on the ballot.
- 🎧 Trump's events resemble his presidential campaign rallies: He emphasizes his accomplishments while calling out superfans and insulting Democrats, NPR's Danielle Kurtzleben tells Up First. Kurtzleben adds that Trump's campaign trail emphasizes that the GOP is still his party, despite his declining approval. Candidates like Ohio GOP gubernatorial candidate Vivek Ramaswamy and GOP Sen. Jon Husted saw large, appreciative, but Kurtzleben says the benefits of appearing alongside Trump remain unclear.

Federal agents searched Rep. Ilhan Omar's home in Washington, D.C., yesterday morning. Omar, D-Minn., says the search was politically motivated by the Trump administration. The Department of Justice told NPR in a statement that Homeland Security Investigations agents "executed a judicially authorized search warrant," approved by the DOJ, as part of an ongoing federal investigation. Current and former federal officials with knowledge of the search who were not authorized to speak publicly confirmed the details, revealing that this multi-year probe includes whistleblower complaints and focuses on allegations of immigration fraud.
- 🎧 One of Omar's neighbors, 79-year-old Sharon Jackson, tells NPR's Luke Garrett she was awoken yesterday at around 4:30 a.m. by loud knocking and that vans and agents wearing gloves were walking to and from the congresswoman's house. Vice President JD Vance previously accused Omar of immigration fraud, which she denies. Omar is an American citizen who left Somalia as a refugee. She stated on social media that the president has spent a decade targeting her with smear campaigns and vowed not to be silenced. Trump says that he is not involved in the investigation into Omar.

The U.S. is experiencing its worst measles resurgence in decades. More than 3,600 confirmed cases and two deaths have been reported as of late September. Outbreaks in several states and exposures in public places like bars, sports stadiums and public transit have raised concerns about travel safety. Almost all U.S. measles cases involve unvaccinated individuals, and as the outbreaks grow in some states, the risk of exposure is getting higher. Here's what to know before traveling by plane or train.
- ✈️ People who've had two doses of the MMR vaccine are considered protected against measles.
- ✈️ The disease is highly contagious. It spreads through the air when an infected person coughs, breathes or sneezes. That airspace can remain contaminated for up to two hours.
- ✈️ Check with your healthcare provider about your vaccination status. They may recommend a booster based on your medical history and health.
- ✈️ Immunocompromised individuals may be unable to receive vaccination and often rely on herd immunity for protection. For measles, the vaccination threshold for herd immunity is 95%.
- ✈️ Measles can be hard to track because symptoms - such as fever, cough, and rash - typically appear seven to 14 days after exposure and can sometimes take long as 21 days to surface.

The Metropolitan Museum of Art's new blockbuster exhibition showcases the works of two 20th-century artists. Jackson Pollock is well-known for his drip paintings. His wife, Lee Krasner, has often been overshadowed by her husband. This show finally gives Krasner the recognition she deserves. With more than 120 pieces on display - 74 from Krasner and 48 from Pollock - the show highlights Krasner as a titan of Abstract Expressionism, too. Check out some of the photos from the exhibit.
