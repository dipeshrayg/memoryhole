Welcome to Edition 9.20 of the Rocket Report! Last weekend was a picture of the US launch business in miniature, and emblematic of the opposing trajectories of two of the nation's most experienced rocket makers. First, on Saturday, SpaceX completed a perfect midair catch of its Starship upper stage at its launch pad in Texas after spending nearly a day in orbit. The Super Heavy booster was lost at sea, but SpaceX proved it can recover that piece of the rocket on previous flights. While SpaceX showed it is well on the way to fielding a fully reusable rocket, United Launch Alliance suffered its first-ever total launch failure with the Vulcan rocket. That is terrible news for not only ULA, but also the US Space Force and Amazon, which have booked some 70 launches on Vulcan over the next few years. Europe's leading satellite startup eyes Italian rocket maker. Finnish satellite developer ICEYE announced Tuesday that it is seeking to buy Italian rocket-maker Avio for more than $2.2 billion in a transaction aimed at creating Europe's first true vertically integrated end-to-end space company, Ars reports. Avio builds the Vega C rocket, a solid-fueled launcher designed to carry up to 3.3 metric tons (7,300 pounds) of payload to low-Earth orbit. ICEYE is, by far, the most successful space startup in Europe, with a valuation of more than $12 billion after the company's most recent fundraising round in June.
