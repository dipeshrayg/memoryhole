Federal Communications Commission Chairman Brendan Carr today warned broadcasters about airing "fake polls," amplifying one of the many complaints that President Trump has regularly made about the news media.
Carr's post on X said, "Many journalists and broadcasters have been amplifying or airing these Polls without regard to basic journalistic ethics and without informing the public that those Polls lack a scientific, accurate, or even real basis... Broadcasters are advised to avoid engaging in News Distortions or running Broadcast Hoaxes by airing Polls that would violate their regulatory obligations."
Carr urged broadcasters to "provide their viewers with additional context and information about Polls they have covered." In fact, one small polling outfit was exposed as a hoax this week, and a similar incident occurred in August. News outlets have reported on these hoaxes, providing the very "context" that Carr said is needed.
Carr's post did not mention any specific fake polls or any specific broadcasters that aired them. The vagueness of Carr's post may be useful for his and Trump's ongoing battle against the news media. In August, Trump demanded that the FCC punish or rebuke an NBC journalist for saying he has "mixed results" in election endorsements and wrote that the FCC must do something about "THE FAKE POLLS USED BY OUR CROOKED MEDIA."
Ex-FCC chairs: Carr wants "self-censorship"
Carr's latest warning to broadcasters came as no surprise, given that he has spent much of the past two years threatening broadcast licenses and opening investigations into Trump's least favorite news companies. Former Republican and Democratic FCC chairs and commissioners recently said Carr is seeking to censor and chill the speech of broadcasters through a proceeding that threatens the licenses of ABC-owned stations.
