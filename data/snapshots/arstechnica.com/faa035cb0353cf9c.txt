Plex, which made a name for itself by letting users share movies they own, is now helping sell movie theater tickets. The company is looking to monetize its content-discovery capabilities and expand its business beyond its media server software.
Plex partnered with Fandango, which has sold movie tickets since 2000, to drive ticket sales from people searching for movies to stream. Per the announcement:
Starting today, Plex users in the US will see a Fandango ticketing option on title pages for movies currently playing in theaters, allowing them to click through to Fandango to find showtimes and purchase tickets. The integration also enables direct attribution through Kochava, giving studios and advertisers greater visibility into how campaigns and audience engagement translate into ticket sales.
Fandango says it sells tickets for 31,000 movie theater screens in the US. The Plex deal allows it to leverage data from Plex's streaming service (Plex claims users of the service "make more than 100 million streaming decisions each month"), including "ratings and cross-platform viewing" to gain "insight into audience intent," the announcement said.
"Plex has a unique view into what movie fans are actively considering, from what they're searching for to what they're watching across services," Jerramy Hainline, executive vice president at Fandango, said in a statement. "By connecting those discovery signals with Fandango ticketing, we're making it easier for fans to turn that interest into a trip to the theater, while giving studios and advertisers a more direct, measurable path to ticket sales."
