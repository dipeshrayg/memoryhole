Rahul Gandhi says India's election chief 'will go to jail'
Indian opposition leader Rahul Gandhi said Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar would be removed from office and jailed, accusing him of facilitating electoral fraud.
Speaking at a rally organized by the opposition INDIA alliance in New Delhi, Gandhi alleged that Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Home Minister Amit Shah owed their positions to Kumar.
"Gyanesh Kumar will be removed from the post of Chief Election Commissioner. Gyanesh Kumar will go to jail," Gandhi told supporters.
The Congress leader also accused authorities of manipulating elections through electronic voting machines and changes to electoral rolls.
"For years, vote theft has been happening in this country," Gandhi said, citing several state elections and the 2024 parliamentary vote.
He further claimed that 15% of India's voters had been removed from electoral rolls following the 2024 election. The allegations have not been independently verified.
Gandhi also criticized economic inequality and the treatment of farmers, young people and workers.
"The farmer is not getting his rights. The youth are not getting employment. The laborer is not getting his rights, and the entire wealth has gone into the hands of two or three people," he said.
Home Minister Amit Shah denounces opposition rally
Union Home Minister Amit Shah took aim at opposition leader Rahul Gandhi from an event in Punjab state's Chandigarh on Sunday as the opposition alliance reached Ramlila Maidan for its rally.
He slammed the Congress for calling the opposition rally on the anniversary of the 1984 anti-Sikh riots, which Shah said were "incited" by the main opposition party,
"Our party held a press conference requesting that they not hold the event on November 1, 2, 3 - the very days the anti-Sikh riots took place - as it would deeply hurt the sentiments of the Sikh community, including mothers, sisters, and children. But they remained adamant," Shah said.
The senior minister of the Modi government highlighted that he was speaking from Punjab, which he went on to say was the "land of the (Sikh) Gurus."
"I strongly condemn this vile attempt. India and Punjab will never be able to forget this," he said, referring to the opposition rally.
Congress and the opposition have accused Shah and Modi of "vote theft" amid allegations of electoral irregularities.
On Saturday, Shah challenged Gandhi and Samajwadi Party chief Akhilesh Yadav to discuss the "vote theft" row in a special session of the parliament. He has accused the opposition of evading parliamentary debate and making baseless allegations against India's poll body.
INDIA bloc leaders arrive at Ramlila Maidan
India's opposition leaders, led by Congress' Rahul Gandhi, have reached Ramlila Maidan in New Delhi - the ground-zero of their rally against a sweeping review of voter lists and their demand for the resignation of the chief election commissioner.
The rally is November's first public display of dissent, following near-daily protests in October by opposition parties, India's 'Cockroach' movement, student organizations and civil society groups over the Special Intensive Revision of voter rolls in the world's largest democracy.
Gandhi, Prime Minister Narendra Modi's political arch-rival, rode through an almost 1.5 kilometer-long (0.9 mile-long) stretch in New Delhi in an open-top jeep, with leaders of India's opposition parties seated behind him.
Thousands of people, including supporters and cadres of the opposition from different states, began gathering at Ramlila Maidan from Sunday morning, waving the national flag and party flags as they awaited the arrival of the opposition convoy.
Visuals on Indian news channels showed the opposition leaders taking their seats on a stage set up at the historic square.
Gandhi is expected to address the rally shortly.
Opposition leaders set off for Ramlila Maidan
Congress leader Rahul Gandhi has shared visuals of the opposition INDIA alliance's rally headed toward Ramlila Maidan in New Delhi.
A split screen showed a large crowd of supporters gathered at the rally site as promient opposition lawmakers seated onboard an open-top vehicle made their way to the Ramlila Maidan through Delhi's roads.
"Come to Connaught Place, let's march together. Or meet directly at Ramlila Maidan - but you must come," Gandhi wrote on X earlier at noon local time as he called for the march to "Save the Vote, Save the Nation."
Delhi enforces winter pollution plan
With winter on the horizon in New Delhi, dread of the annual smog that season brings with it has also set in.
Starting today, the Indian capital is imposing strict and sweeping pollution curbs to tackle the toxic winter smog.
The restrictions include a ban on older vehicles registered outside the city, a suspension of dust-generating demolition activities and outdoor civil construction activities, doubling of parking fees to discourage the use of private vehicles and reduction of physical attendance in government and private-sector offices to 50%.
Agencies including the Delhi Pollution Control Committee, the Municipal Corporation, the city's transport department and traffic police have been tasked with ensuring a stringent enforcement of the winter pollution rules.
The measures will be implemeted for varying time frames, with some lasting until February 28.
"Delhi's Winter Action Plan 2026-27 comes into effect with stricter pollution controls, stronger enforcement and 24×7 monitoring. We humbly request all Delhiites to follow the guidelines and contribute towards making Delhi's air cleaner and healthier," Delhi Chief Minister Rekha Gupta's office said.
How has Modi's BJP reacted to the opposition stir?
The Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) of Prime Minister Narendra Modi is staging a rally of its own in New Delhi, setting the stage for a battle of marches.
The BJP march will take place in West Delhi in remembrance of the 1984 anti-Sikh riots, with the ruling party accusing the Congress of staging a political rally on the anniversary of the violence.
The deadly riots took place following the assassination of the then prime minister, Congress'Indira Gandhi- opposition leader Rahul Gandhi's paternal grandmother - by two Sikh bodyguards on October 31, 1984.
"The sobs of the 1984 riot victims cannot be drowned out by the noise of rallies!" the BJP wrote on X.
"The wounds of 1984 are still raw today. Congress cannot bury them under the din of its theatrics!" the party added.
The BJP also shared videos from different states in the country of rallies held over the weekend under its campaign named "Save Democracy, Remove Anarchy" to counter the Congress and the opposition.
The BJP deems the opposition protests over alleged voter roll manipulations as attempts to "create anarchy."
INDIA bloc holds big rally against electoral irregularities, election chief
India's political opposition is carrying out a major march in New Delhi to protest the nationwide revision of electoral rolls that they say benefits Prime Minister Narendra Modi's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).
They are also seeking the resignation of Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar.
The 'Chalo Dilli' march, heralded by the main opposition Congress party, will begin from Central Delhi's Connaught Place Minto Road Junction to reach Ramlila Maidan.
Top faces of the opposition alliance - called the INDIA bloc - are among those expected to participate. These include Congress' Rahul Gandhi and Mallikarjun Kharge, Trinamool Congress chief Mamata Banerjee, Samajwadi Party supremo Akhilesh Yadav and Rashtriya Janata Dal's Tejashwi Yadav.
India's youth-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), which carried out its own demonstrations against Kumar last month, has also backed the opposition rally.
What is India's electoral roll controversy?
Under a contentious nationwide voter-verification exercise, called the Special Intensive Revision (SIR), around 130 million names have been purged from electoral rolls.
The opposition and critics say that those struck off the voter lists include a large number of minority voters and accuse Modi's BJP of using the exercise as a tool to secure future electoral gains. BJP denied the allegations.
The outcry against election chief Kumar began in September after an investigative report by an Indian daily said that there were divisions in the country's poll body - the Election Commission of India (ECI) - over the revision of voter lists, which it oversees.
Concerns emerged over the ECI's independence.
The ECI has maintained the SIR removes deceased, relocated and duplicate voters and helps prevent the inclusion of "foreign illegal immigrants."
Opposition stages symbolic impeachment of election chief
On Saturday evening, the INDIA bloc staged a mock impeachment of the chief election commissioner. The symbolic proceedings saw opposition members reenacting a parliamentary session in a garden at Congress President Mallikarjun Kharge's residence.
"I don't think anything like this has ever happened in history. The entire opposition sitting in a garden to discuss the impeachment of the CEC, it is absurd," leader of opposition Rahul Gandhi said.
He added that the opposition lawmakers had to hold the mock exercise - which even featured furniture, seating arrangements and carpeting resembling the Lok Sabha (lower house of the Indian parliament) - because they "have nowhere else to go."
"We have no other option. Every single institution ... is closed for us," Gandhi said.
Meanwhile on Sunday, the opposition expects thousands of supporters to descend upon Ramlila Maidan.
It is the first major demonstration to be held at the famous ground, located between Old and New Delhi, since it was labeled as one of the three designated protest sites in the capital last month.
Early visuals showed supporters beginning to gather at Ramlila Maidan and posters of the 'Chalo Dilli' march propped up in the capital.
The Delhi Police said that around 200 to 250 CCTV cameras have been set up at the site as part of the security arrangements.
News agency ANI reported that around 4,000 security personnel have been deployed at Ramlila Maidan.
"The organizers have set up various enclosures, and we have police personnel stationed within them. There are also pathways between these enclosures, where our police personnel are deployed to ensure the area remains secure," deputy commissioner of police (Central District) Rohit Rajbir Singh told ANI.
The capital's traffic police announced curbs and diversions in Central Delhi ahead of the rally.
