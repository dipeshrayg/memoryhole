A European Union (EU) court told the bloc on Wednesday to draw a new timeline for plans to ban cages for farm animals.
Judges at the European General Court ruled that the EU has failed to follow through on a pledge to ban the practice.
In 2021, the European Commission set out the goal to pass a legislation by the end of 2023 to stop farmers from cooping hens, rabbits, sows and other animals in cages. The move came after 1,4 million people signed a petition.
But it failed to follow up on the pledge. This resulted in the campaigners requesting a new timeline and eventually led to a court case when that was also not happening.
The Luxembourg-based General Court, the European Union's second-highest, on Wednesday ruled in favor of the animal rights campaigners.
"The Commission is under an obligation - which is not limited in time - to set out a timeline for all the measures which it intends to take in response to such an initiative," the court said.
According to the "European citizens' initiative" system, the Commission has to receive the petitions that gather over one million signatures across at least seven bloc countries. However, it is not obligated to meet the demands.
The court noted that if the Commission decides to take action, it has to keep the public and the European Parliament posted on a timeline in accordance with the principle of "good administration."
About 143 million chickens are kept in cages in EU
The Commission said it was analyzing the ruling before making a decision. It can still appeal the ruling.
It has previously stated an intention to put forward a proposal for the phased elimination of cage systems for chickens by the end of this year, as well as a separate proposal to transition from farrowing crates to pen-based systems for pigs by 2027.
According to the Albert Schweitzer Foundation, no timetable has been set for calves, rabbits, ducks, geese and quail.
In the EU, about 143 million egg-laying chickens are kept in cages while the majority of female pigs spend at least part of their lives caged.
The use of cage systems in agricultural production accredited as "organic" is already prohibited in the EU.
