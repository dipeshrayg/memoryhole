Jammu and Kashmir assembly seeks restoration of statehood, BJP protests
Jammu and Kashmir Chief Minister Omar Abdullah on Friday moved a resolution in the legislative assembly seeking the immediate restoration of full statehood to the region leading to chaos in the assembly, media reports said.
"We have been waiting. In public and private meetings and every forum, we made the government of India remember that you had made a promise with the people of J&K but it is not being fulfilled," Abdullah said in the assembly of the Union Territory.
Describing it as Prime Minister Narendra Modi's promise, Abdullah said that it should now be fulfilled, adding that the central government had promised to restore it after delimitation and elections.
He also cited administrative difficulties arising from J&K's status as a Union Territory.
The resolution was supported by the ruling National Conference, as well as other parties, but triggered an uproar from Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP) legislators who objected to seeking greater autonomy for J&K.
Prime Minister Narendra Modi's BJP government in 2019 had revoked Kashmir's special status, known as Article 370, which had allowed Jammu and Kashmir to have its own constitution and a degree of internal autonomy.
Under locally approved laws, this meant that only local Kashmiris could vote, own land, and apply for government jobs.
A land ownership provision, called Article 35A, was also scrapped, leading to accusations of being a move by the BJP to engineer a demographic change in the Muslim-majority region.
IN PICTURES: Protests across New Delhi call for women's safety yet again
Women, particularly students, have taken to the streets across New Delhi in recent days to protest recent cases of sexual violence against women and girls.
The demonstrations were prompted by the alleged gang rape of a 17-year-old girl in a Delhi park, as well as reported cases of molestation involving minors in Jamui and Samastipur in the eastern state of Bihar.
India-Pakistan tensions flare at UNGA
India and Pakistan traded accusations at the United Nations General Assembly (UNGA) on Friday, with Islamabad criticizing India's stance on Kashmir and New Delhi accusing Pakistan of supporting cross-border terrorism.
Addressing the General Assembly, Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif called for "a just and fair resolution of the Kashmir dispute," alleging human rights violations in the region.
Sharif also brought up the military clashes last year between the uneasy neighbors, calling them "external aggression from India under a false pretext."
Pakistan considers Indian missile strikes on May 7 to be the start of the low-level conflict, which ran for less than a week, while India contends that the strikes were retaliation for an attack in Indian-administered Jammu and Kashmir in April last year.
He added that India's decision to hold the Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance had no legal basis and accused New Delhi of "weaponizing" water.
India rejected Sharif's remarks. First Secretary at India's UN mission Petal Gahlot accused Pakistan of "terrorism, deceit and duplicity" and said Islamabad's support for cross-border terrorism would have consequences.
Gahlot also criticized Pakistan's military establishment and its treatment of minorities, while reiterating India's position that Jammu and Kashmir is an integral part of the country.
