Audience boos AI-generate stage design at Richard Wagner festival
The audience at the annual Richard Wagner festival in Bayreuth booed after a performance of "Götterdämmerung" that was staged on a set that was designed with the help of artificial intelligence (AI).
The staging featured a collage of images which appeared to confuse members of the audience, who booed when curator Marcus Lobbes and his team took the stage at the end of the performance on Saturday evening.
The audience, however, warmly applauded the singers and musicians, as well as conductor Christian Thielemann.
Ahead of the performance, organizers had said AI would be used on stage for the first time as "an image-generating force."
The singers of Wagner's epic drama would be "the fixed point within a visually seething cosmos of light, texture, history, and association, amidst projections that erupt, constantly shift, and merge into one another," the festival's organizers said.
The AI-generated projections were drawn from images that Lobbes and his team had pre-selected, which included scenes from previous performances of Wagner's "Ring" cycle of four music dramas and from historical themes.
The collage, which included a commemorative stamp marking German reunification, images of former chancellor Helmut Kohl and the World Trade Center towers after the September 11 terror attacks, appeared to confuse the audience, according to German news agency dpa.
Chancellor Merz 'very stable and strong' - Söder
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz is in a "very, very stable and strong" position, according to Bavarian Premier Markus Söder, who said on Sunday that he is "totally convinced" that Merz will "remain chancellor."
Söder, of the Christian Social Union (CSU), the conservative Bavarian sister party of Merz's Christian Democratic Union (CDU), made the comments in an interview with public broadcaster ZDF amid falling poll ratings for the chancellor.
With some party members fearing a poor performance in upcoming state elections in autumn, speculation has grown that Merz could be replaced as head of the government.
Söder dismissed such suggestions and warned that such a scenario could lead to demands for an early federal election "which would only promote one thing: extremists gaining power."
Instead, Söder urged party members and supporters to "concentrate on the work which needs to be done" and called for "togetherness" and "unity."
Rhine water levels at record low
Water levels in the Rhine river reached historic lows in Cologne, Düsseldorf, Bonn, Duisburg and other cities this weekend due to continuing high temperatures and a lack of rain.
In Cologne, the Rhine fell to just 67 centimeters (26 inches), two centimeters lower than the previous record set in 2018. By Sunday, the water level (or Pegel in German) had risen back up to 69 centimeters.
In Düsseldorf, however, the water level dropped to just 21 centimeters on Sunday, also two centimeters lower than the previous low point.
Low water levels are driving up transport costs for the business sector and placing a particular burden on industries that rely on the transport of bulk goods. Inland vessels are forced to carry significantly less cargo or suspend operations entirely.
In addition to freight shipping, the exceptionally low water levels on the Rhine are also causing difficulties for tourism and passenger shipping.
According to one company, the Köln-Düsseldorfer Deutsche Rheinschiffahrt GmbH, numerous trips have had to be canceled, and certain vessels have been unable to dock at specific piers along the Rhine due to the receding waters.
"Freight traffic on the Rhine is already reacting to the falling water levels," warned Thilo Schaefer from the German Economy Institute (IW), telling the Rheinische Post newspaper that the reduced loads on river barges force transport costs upwards and also puts motorways and railways under more pressure.
Christoph Ploss, a conservative (CDU) politician who acts as the German government's maritime coordinator, warned that "low water levels must not become a ball and chain for the German economy."

Brass musicians wrap up a 72-hour performance in Bavaria
Around 1,850 brass musicians finished an unprecedented, 72-hour live performance with a church service In St. Jacob's church in Nürnberg, Bavaria on Sunday.
The event involved over 140 bands from across the German state, with them swapping roughly every 90 minutes to ensure there are no interruptions longer than 10 seconds.
The record is now to be confirmed by Germany's Record Institute.
Bavaria's Premier Markus Söder agreed to act as patron for the event.
In a statement, he conservative politician praised the "enthusiasm" and "passion" displayed by the musicians, and said that music creates "a strong sense of community - not only inside the circle of brass musicians, but also between them and their listeners."
Kölln cereal recalled due to 'foreign objects'
German cereal producer Kölln is expanding its contamination warning for its cereals with chocolate over fear that "foreign objects" might have gotten into the packages.
"We would like to emphasize that, to date, there has been only one known case in which a hard plastic part from our production process was found in a package. The foreign object was not baked into the product," they said in a statement online.
The warning now applies to cereal packages of 375 grams with batch numbers L6165, L6164 and L6235. The packages are also marked with "best before" dates of 17.04.2027, 16.04.2027 and 05.06.2027.
Kölln urged customers to return their product to supermarkets, promising a full refund regardless of having a receipt.
Deutsche Bahn chief Evelyn Palla blasts 'organized irresponsibility' in the company
The culture inside Germany's Deutsche Bahn (DB) is already shifting but the current crisis will take years to overcome, the head of the national railway company told Germany's Welt am Sonntag.
CEO Evelyn Palla criticized the old decision-making process in DB, saying that the central management often made big decisions on the operative level.
"The people in the headquarters, who made those decisions, don't have any responsibility for implementing them in the field," Palla said. "They have to be implemented by colleagues who are out there. But they say 'I didn't make this call, and it also doesn't work for me.' Then the headquarters says 'I have no operational responsibility, those people in the field did not implement [the decision] properly.' In the end it was something like organized irresponsibility."
She told the paper that much has been "reformed" and "slimmed down" since she took the reins of the state-owned firm last October, and that multiple administration levels inside DB had been cut.
"But all of that does not make for a better (Deutsche) Bahn," she added. "Things will only change when the management changes, when decentralized units take responsibility and live with it."
In the interview published on Sunday, she also said that the German railway network was "run into the ground by 25 years of saving money" and that tangible improvements will take time.
She pointed to DB's 200-year anniversary coming in 2035, saying that "this should be the year when the worst is behind us."

Klingbeil urges for solidarity with Spain over Ceuta border breach
German Vice Chancellor and Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil on Saturday called on Europeans to stand by Spain amid the debate over the surge of migrants into Ceuta.
Footage showing tens of thousands of migrants crossing into Spain's North African exclave of Ceuta raised concerns across the European Union.
"Migrants were used in an attempt to divide Europe," Klingbeil said.
He noted that Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez had restored control quickly and that most migrants who entered Ceuta had already returned to Morocco.
"Especially in situations like these, Europe must not allow itself to be divided," Klingbeil said.
Klingbeil's comments come after Italy officially suspended the Schengen agreement with Spain for one month in response to the mass crossing in Ceuta.
Popular German national park closed after storm leaves 1 dead
A popular park in eastern Germany was closed to visitors on Saturday after a storm caused damage and left one person dead overnight.
A 38-year-old man was killed on Friday evening near the Felsenbühne open-air theater in Rathen when he was struck by a falling tree.
Ten other people were injured, three of them seriously, while 40 people were evacuated.
Emergency crews resumed clean-up operations on Saturday.
Authorities closed the area between the Bastei rock formation, the villages of Rathen and Hohnstein, and the Polenztal valley, citing ongoing safety risks for visitors.
IN DEPTH: Germany weighs the future of minijobs
When Anahita Abdollahi starts her shift, she first checks her emails to make sure she's up to date on all the library requests.
The 26-year-old master's student from Iran describes her job as a "part-time librarian" - she helps people find books they need for their studies, answers phone calls, and waters the plants.
This type of employment is known in Germany as a "minijob," a system that allows millions of workers, including students, to avoid paying income tax or social security contributions.
