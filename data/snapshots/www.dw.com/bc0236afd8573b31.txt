Beijing said on Monday the leaders of the US, Russia and China will discuss "world peace" in their first trilateral summit in China this week.
Presidents Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin are due to meet in the southern Chinese city Shenzhen on Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.
China provided few details about the talks, but said they would involve "in-depth discussions on preserving global peace and promoting stability in relations."
"It will be of ground-breaking significance," foreign ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning told a news briefing on Monday, hours after Beijing confirmed the meeting.
Ukraine unlikely to be discussed
US President Donald Trump has previously said the meeting will be "interesting."
"We'll talk a lot of things, including denuclearization, but we'll be talking a lot of things. We're trying to end the war in Iran," he told reporters at a Florida airport on November 8.
In his brief remarks Trump avoided mentioning Russia's war in Ukraine.
According to a report published by the Wall Street Journal last month, Putin wanted the talks to broker a Ukraine peace agreement that would serve Moscow's interests.
US officials say Trump is not expected to raise the issue, AFP news agency reported.
Following his visit to China, German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier told public broadcaster ARD that Xi appeared irritated when asked whether the trilateral talks would cover Ukraine.
"He explicitly pointed out that this is a European conflict, in which the Europeans must also be at the table," Steinmeier told ARD earlier this month.
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz on Friday asked Trump to not make any "surprises" on Ukraine with Putin during the talks.
"Please, no surprises. Continue to play as part of 'Team Europe' and 'Team Ukraine'," he said.
Is an agreement on denuclearization possible?
Washington wants China included in future nuclear arms control deals, but Beijing insists the US and Russia must first reduce their arsenals.
China previously rejected Trump's proposal for broader disarmament talks following the expiration of a US-Russia nuclear treaty in February.
But according to the US State Department, in a call this week, top diplomats Wang Yi and Marco Rubio "agreed on the importance of meaningful leader-level conversations to advance goals such as peace and denuclearization."
United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said on Monday that if the three leaders "commit themselves to a meaningful denuclearization, it will be a great achievement for the world."
Where does the Iran war stand?
The Iran war, which began with US and Israeli strikes on February 28, is currently undergoing some de-escalation although major issues remain unresolved.
Both US and Israel are holding off major attacks as they held elections over the last weeks.
On the other hand, Tehran has continued seeking to control the Strait of Hormuz, a key transit point for global oil and natural gas supplies, while continuing to attack ships it considers unauthorized users of the waterway.
Before the midterm elections, US President Donald Trump said on October, "We may do another little Epic Fury," referring to US-Israeli strikes, without giving any timeframe.
Pakistan-mediated peace talks between the US and Iran on April failed to produce a durable settlement to the conflict.
As one of Iran's key economic partners, China has repeatedly sought to mediate in the conflict.
Beijing, says avoiding escalation is a "top priority."
It hosted leaders and diplomats from various Middle Eastern countries in recent weeks in an effort to persuade the parties to return to the negotiating table.
