Abdala Bucaram, Ecuador's former president, was found guilty of corruption and sentenced on Wednesday to nine years and four months in prison for being "collaborators" in organized crime.
Bucaram's son, Jacob Bucaram, was also sentenced for the illegal sale of at least21,000 COVID-19 tests and other medical supplies during the pandemic in 2020.
The trial, which lasted six years, continued following an appeal from the attorney general's office, after a judge dismissed the charges against Bucaram and his son in 2021.
What are the details of the case?
Bucaram heard the decision remotely from a guarded clinic in Guayaquil where he was receiving medical care.
At 74 years old, he may serve his sentence under house arrest due to his age, in accordance with Ecuadorean law.
He was arrested in 2020 following a raid on his home in Guayaquil, and was put under house arrest as he could not initially be imprisoned due to health problems.
The former president has undergone surgery for heart issues and was admitted to intensive care.
Bucaram posted a message on X stating that he had requested to be discharged from the clinic "despite being at risk of sudden death."
"I will be able to defend myself, and the country will learn horrifying things about this process," he added.
According to the investigation,COVID-19 tests were illegally transferred to Bucaram's residence, which was guarded by Quito traffic agents.
According to the public prosecutor's office, the tests and medical supplies were sold in various provinces of the country "without invoices and without paying taxes to the state."
Bucaram was removed from office in 1997 after being declared mentally unfit to rule, and had lived in exile in Panama for nearly two decades before returning to Ecuador in 2017.
