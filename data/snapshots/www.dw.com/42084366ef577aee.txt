Deutsche Bahn: Attacks on rail staff up 14%
Germany's state-run rail company Deutsche Bahn (DB) announced Monday that verbal and physical attacks on staff had jumped by 14% in the first half of 2026.
Regional operator DB Regio said that 989 incidents had been recorded since January 1. Of these, 781 involved pushing, grabbing and spitting, while a further 185 involved attempted physical assault.
DB says 22 employees suffered serious injuries in attacks and one, a conductor, was killed while checking tickets.
Transport Minister Steffen Bilger on Monday decried the situation, calling safety on trains "a fundamental prerequisite for functioning public transportation.
Bilger is pursuing tougher penalties for anyone who attacks train staff. Germany already has such laws on the books for attacks on first responders.
Moreover, DB is preparing to roll out a pilot program for the voluntary use of bodycams as a way to document, de-escalate, investigate and prosecute dangerous interactions.
Prosecutors suspect Satanic ritual behind dismembered body find
Public prosecutors in the southwestern German city of Darmstadt have asked that a 40-year-old woman be remanded into confinement over a murder that took place in an industrial park near Offenbach, Hesse, earlier this year.
The suspect was detained on an arrest warrant as she attempted to cross the German-Polish border on foot. Her detention came just two days after the dismembered limbs of a male victim were found in dumpsters in an industrial park on February 25.
Authorities say the suspect, who is described as a sex worker, suffers from mental illness and that the murder appears to have been part of a Satanic ritual.
Prosecutors added that she may not be legally responsible for her actions as a result of her illness - hence, prosecutors' request for confinement rather than going to criminal trial.
Darmstadt's district court will now determine whether the suspect should be held.
No further information regarding the suspect's mental health or her connections to Satanism were given.
Prosecutors say the woman selected her victim by chance - though she may have known him - and that he had no inkling of his fate.
"The man was not supposed to have any idea," read a statement. When the ritual was over, prosecutors say the suspect, "dismembered the body and discarded it in dumpsters."
Far-right hits 42% in Saxony-Anhalt poll
The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) has reached 42% in a new poll ahead of Saxony-Anhalt's September 6 state election, widening its lead over the center-right Christian Democrats (CDU).
The INSA survey for Germany's Bild newspaper put the CDU on 22%, followed by the Left Party on 13% and the center-left Social Democrats (SPD) on 6%.
The Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance (BSW) was on 5%, just enough to enter the state parliament. The environmentalist Greens and neoliberal Free Democrats (FDP) were both on 4% and would miss the threshold.
A BSW presence could prove crucial in deciding who becomes state premier. Party founder Sahra Wagenknecht has said CDU lead candidate Sven Schulze could not count on BSW votes.
BSW co-lead candidate Thomas Schulze said his party's entry into parliament could prevent another CDU-led government, while AfD candidate Ulrich Siegmund rejected the BSW's proposal for a nonpartisan premier backed by shifting majorities.
The AfD has said it is aiming for an outright majority in the election.
A separate Infratest dimap poll in late July put the AfD on 41% and the CDU on 24%.
Kane to receive European Golden Shoe in Munich
Bayern Munich striker Harry Kane is set to receive the European Golden Shoe on August 19 after finishing as the continent's leading league scorer last season.
Both the Bundesliga and Bayern say the England captain is to be presented with the European Sports Media award at an event in the FC Bayern Museum.
Kane scored 36 Bundesliga goals last season, finishing well ahead of Manchester City's Erling Haaland and Real Madrid's Kylian Mbappe in their respective leagues.
The 33-year-old will receive the trophy for the second time, having also scored 36 goals in his first season with Bayern in 2023/24.
The Golden Shoe has been awarded to Europe's top league goalscorer since 1968.
Former Bayern forwards Gerd Müller, in 1970 and 1972, and Robert Lewandowski, in 2021 and 2022, also won the award twice.

Economist urges Germany to rethink defense spending
Germany risks pouring hundreds of billions of euros into outdated military technology instead of preparing for future warfare, the head of the Kiel Institute for the World Economy (IfW) has warned.
IfW President Moritz Schularick criticized Defense Minister Boris Pistorius for continuing to spend heavily on tanks, frigates and other conventional systems rather than prioritizing artificial intelligence, robotics, drones and satellite technology.
"We have a lot of capital, a lot of industrial know-how and, in some areas, still highly advanced technologies," Schularick told the Süddeutsche Zeitung newspaper.
"What we don't have are hundreds of thousands of people who want to get into tanks and go to the front," he said.
Germany should therefore rely wherever possible on AI, robotics, drones and satellites rather than simply increasing troop numbers, manned vehicles and barracks, he argued.
Schularick said Germany's roughly €700 billion ($809 billion) program to modernize and strengthen the Bundeswehr risked failing to deliver unless procurement was fundamentally overhauled.
German rents keep rising amid housing shortage
Rents for new leases in Germany have continued to rise sharply as a persistent housing shortage keeps pressure on the market, a survey shows.
New rents for apartments in multi-family buildings were 3.2% higher in the second quarter than a year earlier, according to the Association of German Pfandbrief Banks (VDP).
The pace of growth eased somewhat compared with previous quarters, however.
Across Germany's seven largest cities, rents rose by an average of just 1.5%, but the picture varied sharply.
Düsseldorf recorded the strongest increase at 3.6%, while rents in Berlin were almost unchanged, rising just 0.6%.
Germany is estimated to be short of around 1 million homes, with supply particularly tight in major urban areas.
Home prices also continued to climb in the second quarter.
Apartments and houses were 1.9% more expensive than a year earlier and 0.3% higher than in the previous quarter, the VDP said.
Prices in the seven biggest cities rose 2.1% year-on-year, led by Hamburg at 3.8%.
Cologne followed with 2.5%, while Frankfurt and Düsseldorf both recorded increases of 2.4%.
German nurse murder probe expands to over 120 cases
German prosecutors are investigating scores more suspected cases involving a former nurse already convicted of murdering 10 patients.
Prosecutors in the western German city of Aachen say the probe extends to more than 120 additional cases.
They said 39 bodies had so far been exhumed as part of the widening investigation. Authorities in Cologne, who are conducting a separate probe, have exhumed another seven bodies.
The former nurse was sentenced to life imprisonment by Aachen Regional Court last November for 10 murders and 27 attempted murders. The court also found a particular severity of guilt, making release after 15 years unlikely.
According to the verdict, the man administered excessive doses of sedatives to seriously ill patients during night shifts at a hospital in the town of Würselen, sometimes combining them with painkillers or administering repeated doses.
The alleged motives were the man's own sense of personal unease and desire for order.
The nurse denied the allegations during his trial.
The offenses covered by his conviction took place between December 2023 and May 2024. The new investigation concerns possible killings before that period.

Share of young people in Germany remains at record low
Young people continue to make up a historically small share of Germany's population, according to figures from the Federal Statistical Office.
At the end of 2025, around 8.3 million people were aged between 15 and 24, representing 10% of the population.
That share has remained at a record low since the beginning of the 2020s.
The proportion of young people was highest in the first half of the 1980s, when the baby boomer generation was still in this age group.
In 1983, around one in six people in what is now Germany, or 16.7%, were aged 15 to 24.
Young people make up a larger share of the population among people with an immigration background.
The share of young people also varies by region.
Bremen had the highest proportion at 11.1%, followed by Hamburg at 10.5% and Baden-Württemberg at 10.4%. Brandenburg had the lowest share at 8.8%, while Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Saxony-Anhalt each stood at 9.1%.
Germany also remained slightly below the European Union average. Young people accounted for 10% of the population, compared with an EU average of 10.7%.
Minimum wage violations cost Germany billions, union says
Illegal practices used to evade Germany's minimum wage have cost workers, social security funds and the state as much as €64 billion over a decade, according to calculations by the German Trade Union Confederation (DGB).
The DGB estimates that an average of around two million workers a year have received less than the legal minimum since it was introduced in 2015.
The shortfall amounted to roughly €1.70 to €2.40 per hour, based on survey data on working hours and earnings.
For the period from 2015 to 2024, the union calculated that workers missed out on around €35 billion in pay.
A further €22 billion was lost to social security funds and about €7 billion in income tax revenue.
Germany's main employers' association said minimum wage violations were unacceptable and should be investigated and prosecuted, but urged caution over the DGB figures.
It said survey data could be imprecise, particularly when working hours were not recorded accurately.
Germany's statutory minimum wage currently stands at €13.90 an hour and applies to around six million workers, especially in sectors such as hospitality, retail and agriculture.
Germany to speed up drone defenses at airports
Germany has promised to accelerate upgrades to drone detection and defense systems at its airports following last week's explosive-drone incident at Leipzig/Halle.
"Reliable detection is absolutely crucial," Transport Minister Steffen Bilger told the news website Politico on Monday.
Bilger said Germany's air navigation service provider is responsible for safety in the airspace around airports and that early detection therefore plays a key role.
He said Frankfurt and Munich are already better equipped, but warned that other airports need to catch up quickly.
"We have no time to lose," Bilger said. "I want to accelerate further upgrades wherever possible."
The push follows the discovery last week of a drone carrying an explosive device in a secure cargo area at Leipzig/Halle Airport.
A federal police bomb disposal team defused the device.
Several Ukrainian cargo aircraft were nearby at the time. The airport is used as a hub for military aid shipments to Ukraine.
Man shot dead near Berlin's Zoo station
A 30-year-old man has been shot dead following a dispute involving several people near Berlin's busy Zoologischer Garten station.
Paramedics tried to resuscitate the victim, but he died at the scene, police said.
The suspected attackers fled and remain at large.
Police did not say how many shots were fired shortly before midnight, and the circumstances surrounding the dispute remain unclear.
A homicide unit has taken over the investigation.
The shooting took place at one of western Berlin's busiest transport hubs, close to the Kurfürstendamm shopping boulevard and the Kaiser Wilhelm Memorial Church.
The case comes amid a marked rise in firearms incidents in the German capital.
Berlin police recorded 1,119 cases involving firearms in 2025, up 68% from 2024. Shots were actually fired in more than 500 of those cases.
Police chief Barbara Slowik Meisel said earlier this year that disputes once settled with fists were now increasingly involving guns.
Other recent cases include shots fired at a restaurant window in Neukölln early on Sunday and the shooting of a 38-year-old man in the city's Schöneberg district the previous weekend.
Rhine could become impassable at key shipping point
The Rhine River could soon become effectively split in two for commercial shipping as water levels continue to fall amid persistent dry weather, Germany's inland shipping association has warned.
Jens Schwanen, managing director of the Federal Association of German Inland Navigation, said the gauge at Kaub, a crucial point for freight traffic, was forecast to fall into single digits for the first time this week.
"Commercial shipping will no longer be able to transport goods in the region at such low water levels," Schwanen told the Rheinische Post newspaper.
He said excursion boats and river cruises would also be affected.
"In principle, the Rhine would then no longer be continuously navigable and would be divided into two," Schwanen said.
Shipping would still be possible between the ports of Amsterdam, Rotterdam and Antwerp and parts of North Rhine-Westphalia, roughly as far as Cologne.
Further south, traffic could continue on tributaries and connected waterways including the Neckar, Moselle, Main and Main-Danube Canal.
Historic low-water levels were recorded at several points on the Rhine in North Rhine-Westphalia over the weekend after weeks of dry weather and too little rainfall across the river's catchment area.
The falling river is already forcing freight vessels to carry much lighter loads, pushing up transport costs for industries that depend on bulk cargo.
