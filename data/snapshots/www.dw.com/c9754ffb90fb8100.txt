Greens approve coalition agreement in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania
The Green Party in the northeastern state of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania has given the green light to a coalition agreement negotiated with the center-left Social Democrats (SPD)and socialist Left Party.
This comes after the Left Party in the state approved the agreement on Friday.
The SPD is expected to give its stamp of approval on Saturday afternoon to the deal, which was negotiated by the three parties following September 20 elections in the state.
The incumbent state premier, Manuela Schwesig of the SPD, intends to stand for reelection on Monday.
The designated coalition will hold a total of 39 seats in the 71-seat state parliament.
The far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD), which emerged as strongest single force from the election, won 32 seats and will be the only opposition party after both the conservative Christian Democrats (CDU) and pro-businessFree Democrats (FDP) fell beneath the 5% threshold to enter parliament.
The AfD made no attempt to form a coalition with the necessary majority, as all other parties refuse to cooperate with it.
Experts warn against single-provider dependency
Experts in Germany have warned companies, schools and government authorities against reliance on a sole provider for digital services after Microsoft services suffered a major worldwide outage.
"Anyone who makes themselves unilaterally dependent on a single source for central digital technologies risks production downtimes and competitive disadvantages in an emergency," said Ralf Wintergerst, president of the industry association Bitkom.
IT security expert Christopher Kunz from the Heise Online trade magazine said such dependency posed a risk to both German society and economy "because we are not only at the mercy of Microsoft's technical competence, but also the goodwill of the US government."
"Through sanctions, it can ensure in a flash that individual organizations or even entire nations lose access to the Microsoft cloud," he said.
Manuel Atug, the founder and spokesperson for AG Kritis, an alliance of independent experts for critical infrastructures, said there were enough alternative providers from Germany or Europe to allow a diversification.
On Thursday, central services of the Microsoft 365 suite, including Outlook, Teams and Copilot, began to be hit by outages worldwide, with disruptions continuing into Saturday European time.
Budget crisis looming if spending is not cut - experts
Leading economics experts have predicted a serious German budget crisis from next year if the issue of spending is not addressed.
Their remarks come as the budget committee of the Bundestag, the lower house of parliament, early on Friday approved a provisional 2027 federal budget after nearly 16 hours of negotiations.
The budget foresees new debt, including loans from special funds, totalling around €210 billion ($231.5 billion).
Spending in the core budget rises to a record €561 billion, while net borrowing climbs to almost €123 billion in the core budget alone.
"This budget policy cannot be sustained in the long term," Clemens Fuest, president of the Ifo Institute in Munich, told the Sunday edition of Die Welt newspaper, saying spending priorities had to be established
"All other spending should be frozen or, if possible, cut," Fuest said, adding that tax rises would be inevitable if this did not happen.
Jens Boysen-Hogrefe of the Kiel Institute for the World Economy (IfW Kiel) sees things similarly.
"Owing to economic fluctuations and special effects, there may be some breathing room in individual years, but the trend is clearly negative," he said, owing to the fact that the population is aging while interest payments are rising.
"Additional levies are likely even with strong spending discipline," he said.
Ansgar Heveling, the president of the Bundesrechnungshof, the federal audit court that monitors financial management by the government, called on the coalition government to do make more effort to consolidate the budget.
Noting that every third euro spent by the government is financed by borrowing, he said it was clear that it was living beyond its means, and warned that the share of interest spending in the federal budget would double by 2020.
Solar energy output set for record high in Germany in 2026
An above-average number of sunshine hours and the growing number of photovoltaic facilities has contributed to solar energy production that is approaching a record total for Germany, an industry association says.
By September 27, solar energy output had reached 90.2 terawatt-hours, which is already more than the 90 terawatt-hours generated for all of 2025, according to the German Solar Association (BSW -Solar) in Berlin.
That means that solar energy in net electricity generation in Germany is likely to reach a record level of more than 100 terawatt-hours in a single calendar year - almost 10 times the amount of electricity used by a major city like Hamburg in a year.
It is also almost 50 times as much as the 2.2 terawatt-hours generated byx solar power 20 years ago.
Carsten Körnig, the managing director of BSW-Solar, said the likely record was an important milestone as the world seeks to move away from climate-changing fossil fuels.
"Photovoltaics has developed from a niche technology into a cornerstone of our electricity supply," Körnig said.
"Society as a whole benefits from this: through cheaper electricity, fewer energy import and significantly lower climate-related damages," he said.
At 17.8% of total power generation, photovoltaics was the third most important energy source of domestically produced electricity behind wind power - the top energy source - followed by coal.
