Trump, Rutte press Russia to not 'expand war'
US President Donald Trump and NATO chief Mark Rutte called on Russia not to "expand the war" in Ukraine.
Rutte traveled to Florida for the meeting with Trump.
"We also discussed the importance of Russia avoiding any actions that intentionally, or unintentionally, expand the war, and the importance of obtaining a peaceful resolution to the war as rapidly as possible," they said in a joint statement sent by the White House.
Jared Kushner: Putin vowed not to use nuclear arms against Ukraine
US envoy Jared Kushner says Russian President Vladimir Putin has given Washington his word that he will not use nuclear weapons in the war in Ukraine, the dpa news agency reported, citing a podcast interview.
Kushner, who is US President Donald Trump's son-in-law, made the comments on the podcast "MD Meets," hosted by Axel Springer chief executive Mathias Döpfner.
"He's agreed not to use nuclear weapons, which obviously is a big thing, and he's reassured us that that's not his intention," Kushner said, adding that Putin had shown restraint in the conflict.
Western countries have repeatedly worried that Russia could turn to tactical nuclear weapons since launching the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
Meanwhile, Kushner denied that Washington is too close to Moscow, saying the US sees itself as a mediator.
"We have not put any pressure on President [Volodymyr] Zelenskyy or the Ukrainians," he said. He added that no country has given Kyiv more money than the US, and that information from US intelligence agencies still flows to Ukraine.
However, he blamed Ukraine for setting back peace efforts.
"There was the attack that the Ukrainians did on Putin's house at the end of December, which I think pushed him back," he said.
Russia claimed last December that Ukraine tried to hit Putin's Valdai residence with drones. The US Central Intelligence Agency says the residence was not the intended target.
Russia launches fresh ballistic missile attack on Kyiv
Russia launched more ballistic missiles at the Ukrainian capital, Kyiv, late on Wednesday.
Explosions were heard in the city, and a building in an industrial area was hit, Mayor Vitali Klitschko wrote on Telegram. He said residents should remain in shelters.
The Ukrainian air force confirmed the Russian attack. Telegram channels linked to the military reported that up to eight missiles had been fired from Russia's Bryansk region near the Ukrainian border. The missiles reportedly targeted districts of Kyiv east of the Dnipro River.
The full extent of the damage was not immediately clear.
Russia has stepped up attacks on infrastructure in Kyiv and other major Ukrainian cities in recent months, seeking to disrupt electricity, heating and water supplies.
Ukraine has struggled to defend itself against ballistic missiles amid shortages of interceptor missiles for its US-made Patriot air defense systems.
US envoy warns against escalation of war in Ukraine
In an interview with the German MD Meets Podcast, US envoy Jared Kushner warned against a downward spiral in the conflict in Ukraine, which he described as "a proxy war where Europe is funding and arming Ukraine against Russia."
Kushner, US President Donald Trump's son-in-law and chosen intermediary in negotiations with Russia's Vladimir Putin, also warned that "it won't take a lot of miscalculation for this thing to escalate massively."
After several failed rounds of talks involving his fellow envoy, Steve Witkoff, Kushner admitted that the issue of territoriy in eastern Ukraine remained a sticking point.
"What Steve and I have deciphered from having many, many hours with President Putin and from the Russian team, is that you know the territorial issue is right now a little bit of a deadlock," he said.
However, Kushner also appeared to accuse the Europeans of hypocrisy for supporting Ukraine while continuing to spend vast sums of money on Russian energy.
Berlin trial of woman suspected of spying for Russia set to start
A German-Ukrainian woman accused of spying for Russia goes on trial in Berlin on Thursday.
She is charged with providing an employee and secret agent at the Russian Embassy in Berlin with sensitive information.
The alleged spy is accused of tipping the man off about high intelligence-value events over a period of years and helping him to secure entry.
The dpa news agency reports that the Russian agent in question was the country's deputy military attache, who has since been expelled from Germany.
The woman had long been known as a pro-Russian networker in the Berlin area, having lived in the country for several decades.
The lawsuit against her from the federal prosecutor's office based in Karslruhe also alleges that she tried to "win over individuals from the Federal Defense Ministry and the weapons industry for her purposes."
She is accused of acting out of personal conviction, rather than, say, fear or blackmail. She was arrested in January this year.
The woman is accused of making contact with the Russian espionage asset as of October 2023 at the latest. Her most pressing task was to seek out information pertaining to Germany defense policy and the weapons industry, particularly in light of and where relevant to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
Ukraine says it attacked major Russian air base
The Ukrainian General Staff announced that Ukrainian forces had attacked a major Russian military air base, damaging five aircraft used to launch missiles and bombs at Ukraine.
According to the General Staff, three MiG-31 jets, which are used to fire hypersonic missiles, and two Su-34 fighter-bombers were knocked out of operation "for some time."
The report stated that the Savasleyka air base, located east of Moscow in the Nizhny Novgorod region, had been hit in the latest long-range attacks aimed at crippling Moscow's war effort.
Russia did not immediately comment on the announcement.
Russian sources say Europeans must take up Putin's offer for Moscow talks
Numerous sources inside Russian foreign policy circles on Wednesday underscored the Kremlin's desire for European diplomats to travel to Moscow and explain their intentions before they be allowed to participate in any Russia-Ukraine peace negotiations.
Russian President Vladimir Putin originally extended the invitation last month. On Wednesday, Foreign Policy Advisor Yury Ushakov said the invitation stands but that no Europeans have so far agreed to embark on the diplomatic sojourn.
Russia's Ambassador to Germany Sergei Nechayev on Wednesday told France's AFP news agency that reaction to Putin's invitation has been "restrained."
Nechayev claimed that Moscow remains, "ready to continue the dialogue and carry on with negotiations."
He also repeated the Kremlin claim that it is Europe that is the impediment to stopping the war, and that Russia - which launched and continues to execute its unprovoked years-long war of aggression in Ukraine - is the only party truly interested in peace.
Nechayev said the Kremlin doesn't see the same "honesty" from the Europeans that it does from US President Donald Trump's team - made up of his son-in-law Jared Kushner and his friend Steve Witkoff - and wants to "know precisely what their intentions are."
Russia has been accused of foot dragging as a way to prolong attacks on Ukraine. On Wednesday, Nechayev continued in that vein, saying: "Our request to Europe is this: Decide what it is you want, determine whom you will appoint as negotiators, and, most importantly, clarify what you aim to achieve."
Poland to create no-fly zone at Ukraine border, urges others to do same
Poland's Deputy Defense Minister Cezary Tomczyk told reporters that Warsaw will set up a no-fly zone inside its border with Ukraine and suggested other regional allies do the same in the wake of repeated Russian incursions into NATO airspace as part of Moscow's war of aggression.
"An ordinance is already being prepared by the government and should enter into force at the end of December or the beginning of January," said Tomczyk.
"We will also urge our allies - the entire NATO alliance - to establish such a principle along the eastern flank," he continued, "Poland was a pioneer in this regard, ‌and the Baltic states have since followed our lead."
Poland strictly limits air traffic at its border already.
On Wednesday, Tomczyk said the proposed no-fly zone will cover several dozen kilometers of territory from the border and provide added support for Poland's armed forces.
Merz defends Ukraine aid, says those opposed are lost or have other aims
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz defended his government's expenditures on Ukraine aid during a budget debate in the Bundestag on Wednesday.
Merz tackled criticism from opposition parties on the left and right head on, saying "Russia knows exactly what it wants. And Russia wants more than Ukraine."
"Russia is escalating in the Baltic region, in Poland, on the Black Sea. Russia is escalating ‌in space. And Russia is now escalating massively here, in our own country, too, even at the risk of loss of life," he added.
Merz said, "anyone who doesn't see that, doesn't understand the world around them, or they are pursuing entirely different aims."
The Left Party and Alternative for Germany (AfD) have both called for an end to Ukraine aid and better relations with Moscow.
Among other things, Germany blames Russia for an explosive drone incident at Leipzig airport this summer as well as cyberattacks, sabotage and airspace incursions across the EU and NATO.
Merz said on Wednesday that by supporting Ukraine, Germany is protecting itself.
"We are learning from Ukraine," said Merz, "technologically for civilian use but of course also in defense technology."
Moscow, Kyiv exchange overnight attacks on energy infrastructure
Kyiv and Moscow both assessed damage to energy infrastructure on Wednesday morning as both sides continued to inflict damage on one another amid Russia's over four-and-a-half-year attempted invasion of neighboring Ukraine.
Russia again focused its attacks on Ukraine's energy structure in an attempt to break the will of the country's civilian population as the bitter cold of winter sets in.
Moscow's troops attacked a thermal energy power plant, shutting it down; as well as targeting a gas production facility in Poltawa, where ensuing fires also shut down operations.
Ukraine's acting energy minister said that power outages were reported in five regions as a result of the Russian attacks.
In Russia's Samara region, Ukrainian drones hit at least two oil refineries, killing one person, sparking large fires and and causing energy blackouts.
France, Italy offer different takes on Ukraine's actions in Libya
Kyiv's attacks on Russian ships in and off the coast of Libya over the past couple of days have ruffled feathers among Ukraine's allies, with France and Italy, for instance, coming down on entirely different sides of the issue.
French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said Kyiv was justified in its actions because it has an "absolute right to self-defense." Libya on Wednesday announced that two of its soldiers had died from injuries sustained during a weekend drone attack that sank a Russian warship.
Speaking with French broadcaster TF1, Barrot said, "For nearly five years now, [Russian President] Vladimir Putin has not only been bombing Ukraine, but has also been constantly engaging in acts of aggression and harassing Europeans in an attempt to undermine our unity."
Barrot said Russia alone is to blame for the war and its expansion, and then asked, "What would we do if we were being bombarded day and night by an enemy - an invader?"
Italy's Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani on Wednesday criticized Kyiv's actions, saying, "It was a mistake to violate Libya's sovereignty" and warned against further similar activities in other regions.
Speaking with Italy's Corriere della Sera newspaper ‌on Wednesday, Tajani said, "Italy was absolutely unaware of Kyiv's intentions to strike in Libyan territorial waters, even inside a port."
Tajani said he had spoken with Ukraine Foreign Minister Andrii Sybihato and conveyed Italy's concern over the matter, adding that Rome would continue its staunch support for Ukraine and its sovereignty while at the same time seeking to keep the conflict from spreading.
