Two suspected IS members detained in NRW
Two Iraqi nationals have been arrested on suspicion of terrorism by German authorities. The arrests took place after evidence emerged that they had fought with the so-called Islamic State in Iraq a decade ago.
The arrests were made in the towns of Höxter and Bottrop in the western state of North Rhine-Westphalia. Cell phones and laptops were seized from the detainees' apartments.
The men, aged 29 and 46, have been accused of being members of a terrorist organization abroad, a spokeswoman for the Düsseldorf State Prosecutor's Office said. She added that the Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) received a tip and was then able to track down the suspects.
Authorities have not yet decided whether the men would be held in pretrial detention.
There are no indications that either of them was involved in any planned attacks in Germany, the spokeswoman added.
RWE settles with Trump administration to cancel offshore wind projects in US
German energy giant RWE announced it is abandoning its offshore wind power projects in the US, citing regulatory hurdles, and turning towards investments elsewhere.
The company said in a statement that it had reached a $1.22 billion (roughly €1.05 billion) settlement with the US government to relinquish its offshore wind leases in the New York Bight and off the coasts of Louisiana and California.
"After careful consideration, it was determined that there is no path forward to permit these projects in the US for the foreseeable future," RWE said.
The company said it now planned to reinvest the money in natural gas projects in the US "to meet rapidly growing energy demand" instead of focusing on wind turbines and renewable energy.
US President Donald Trump, who has become a vocal supporter of the fossil fuel industry, seems to reserve a particular distaste for what he typically calls "windmills."
Last week he returned to the issue in comments related to the UK, saying "any time you see windmills, you see a failing country," and claiming - falsely on both counts: "They're all made in China. They're not used by China, but they're made in China."
RWE said it was still investing in its US operations and aiming to almost double its power generation capacity by 2031. It also noted its continued heavy presence in offshore wind projects beyond Germany's borders.

Federal prosecutors take over Leipzig airport drone investigation
Federal prosecutors in Germany have confirmed that they will take over investigations of the drone incident at Leipzig/Halle Airport, given the grounds to suspect a dangerous attempt to attack air transport and cause an explosion.
"To this end, professional explosives and a detonator were attached to the drone," the federal prosecutor's office said. Beyond that, it noted there was evidence suggesting a mid-air collision between a plane and possibly another drone.
"This is a serious attack on transport and logistical infrastructure in Germany," the Office of the Federal Prosecutor said as it announced it was taking the lead. "As such, the act is capable of undermining the external and internal security of the Federal Republic of Germany."
Prosecutors said the federal investigative police force, the BKA, would take over the policing side of the case.
Prior to this announcement, the Dresden public prosecutor's office and the Saxony state chapter of the investigative police force had been conducting the investigation.
Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt had already said on Wednesday evening that the attempted attack seemed to be the work of highly trained professionals and that he did not rule out the possibility of "foreign powers" being involved in some way.
Chinese EV makers get most out of Germany subsidy
Chinese manufacturers benefited from the introduction of the German government's new electric car subsidy.
The €3 billion ($3.46 billion) subsidy scheme was introduced by Germany's conservative-led coalition government to incentivize consumers to purchase and lease electric cars, certain plug-in hybrids and EVs fitted with range extenders.
According to the Federal Motor Transport Authority (KBA), the Chinese manufacturer BYD was one of those who benefited. BYD registered sales of 5,240 new cars in Germany in July, more than four and a half times as many cars in July of the previous year.
"While the strong growth in electric cars is accelerating the ramp-up of electromobility in Germany, it has so far brought only limited benefits to the domestic automotive industry," said auto expert Constantin Gall of the consulting firm EY, adding that Chinese companies are the main beneficiaries.
The high demand for electric cars has been a boost for the entire new-car market in Germany.
Officials deny explosive drone was near plane carrying weapons for Ukraine, media report
The German Interior Ministry has denied reports that a drone carrying explosives was near a cargo aircraft packed with munitions destined for Ukraine, according to media reports.
In a meeting of the heads of the Bundestag's committees for Defense and Interior Affairs, it was made clear that no weapons or other munitions were on the aircraft, according to the dpa news agency, which reported information matching an earlier article by the Focus news magazine (see earlier entry).
Detlef Seif, the chair of the Internal Affairs Committee, also rejected the reports, telling Reuters that from all the conversations he was party to so far, "the aircraft was not loaded with ammunition or weapons."
Maret Montavon, head of communications and government relations at Mitteldeutsche Flughafen, which operates the airport in Leipzig, told the daily Leipziger Volkszeitung that there was no cargo on the aircraft.
"The Antonov aircraft in question was empty at the time of the incident and, therefore, carried no cargo. This assessment is consistent with the information we have received from Antonov Logistics SALIS," Montavon told the paper.
The ministry has not responded to an official request for comment.
Security sources have said a drone carrying explosives was located on Tuesday near a Ukrainian Antonov AN-124 Condor cargo aircraft. Federal Police defused the explosive device.
Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt is inviting several European counterparts to a meeting following the incident, his ministry confirmed on Thursday. The meeting will aim to coordinate a joint defense strategy to combat drones and to improve the protection of critical infrastructure.
Rare Focke-Wulf 190 aircraft back in sky after restoration in Turkey
Turkey's Sivrihisar Aviation Club Association has successfully restored a rare German World War II Focke-Wulf 190 (FW 190) fighter plane and returned it to the skies.
German pilot Klaus Plasa was at the controls for its flight earlier in the week.
The FW 190 went into service in 1941 and was a heavier plane than its better-known counterpart, the Messerschmitt Bf 109, and less comfortable at high altitudes. But this also made it a much more versatile aircraft, capable of flying longer distances and carrying more firepower and fuel.
The aircraft was downed in France in 1944. Its wreckage was taken to the US in 2000. Restoration work began there, but the aircraft never regained operational status.
The Turkish aviation club acquired the plane in 2023 and an 11-member team set to work restoring it over roughly 14 months.
In the early 20th century, Germany had helped modernize the military, including the air force, of what was then the Ottoman Empire. This collaboration continued in the early years of the Republic of Turkey.
By 1948, three years after Nazi Germany's defeat and with spare parts in short supply, Turkey - which by then was starting to eye an alliance with the US and membership in what would become NATO - retired its remaining FW 190 fleet.
The Sivrihisar Aviation Club Association said it wanted to preserve the countries' shared aviation heritage. It plans to display the rare aircraft at an air show in Eskisehir in September.
Estimates on the numbers vary, but no more than a few dozen FW 190s survive, most of them museum pieces that are not airworthy.

Two trams have collided in the western city of Gelsenkirchen.
Local newspaper the Westdeutsche Allgemeine Zeitung (WAZ) based in nearby Essen carried the first images of two trams that appeared to have collided end-to-end.
The crash took place on Kurt-Schumacher-Strasse near Bundesliga club Schalke's Veltins-Arena.

The police spokesperson also said one of the trains, which was conducting driver training, stopped and the other then crashed into it from behind. The cause of the incident was under investigation.
Low Rhine River level reveals WWII bomb in central Cologne
Authorities in Cologne were working to defuse or dispose of an unexploded World War II bomb, revealed as the Rhine River's water levels sink to record lows in the hot and dry summer.
The operation took place in the central Deutz district, just across the river from the main train station and Cologne's renowned cathedral.
The discovery of the US bomb led to efforts to clear a safe zone in a radius of roughly 400 meters from the scene. Around 360 people were affected, city authorities said.
One of central Cologne's green spaces, the Rheinpark on the right bank of the river, was cleared amid the operation.
By mid-afternoon, the City of Cologne published a list of areas that were cordoned off, asking people to stay clear.
It had said slightly earlier that it was not yet possible to say when the bomb would be defused or otherwise rendered safe.
Such finds are very common occurrences in Cologne, a strategically important city that was heavily bombed throughout much of World War II. Most of the bombs can be defused or disabled; a minority require a controlled explosion instead.
High temperatures and a lack of rain have often led to the summertime discovery of unexploded bombs in German rivers.

Germany's heat death toll rises to nearly 12,000
Nearly 12,000 people have died from the effects of extreme heat in Germany this year, according to new estimates from the Robert Koch Institute.
The German public health institute put the number of heat-related deaths between April 6 and July 26 at around 11,900, already well above any full-year total since 2016.
The previous annual high was around 9,000 deaths in 2018.
Around 9,600 of this year's deaths were linked to the exceptionally hot week from June 22 to 28.
Average temperatures across Germany reached 26.4 degrees Celsius (79.5 degrees Fahrenheit) that week, while daytime temperatures topped 40 degrees C (104 degrees F) in many places.
The institute described the latest total as a conservative estimate, meaning the true figure could be higher.
People aged 85 and above accounted for around 6,000 deaths, while almost 3,000 victims were aged between 75 and 84.
Around 1,800 deaths were recorded among people aged 65 to 74, while about 1,100 were among those younger than 65.
Heat rarely kills directly. More often, extreme temperatures worsen existing illnesses or put additional strain on the cardiovascular system.

Holger Lösch, deputy managing director of the Federation of German Industries, told the Rheinische Post newspaper that selective deepening would allow ships to carry more cargo during periods of low water and make supply chains more resilient.
"The low-water conference in Bonn must mark the starting point for accelerating targeted deepening of the Rhine," said Lösch.
Lösch also called for more investment in waterways, arguing that Germany's transport infrastructure had been underfunded for decades.
He said planning and approval procedures also needed to be sped up significantly.

Germany calls crisis talks over extreme low river levels
German Transport Minister Steffen Bilger is holding high-level talks in Bonn today as extreme low water levels disrupt shipping on the Rhine and other rivers.
Representatives from the shipping industry, ports and businesses are due to attend the meeting around lunchtime.
"We want to assess the current situation together and take short-term action where necessary," Bilger told the Kölner Stadt-Anzeiger newspaper ahead of the talks.
He said the government also wanted to draw longer-term lessons and make Germany's waterways more resilient to increasingly severe periods of low water.
Bilger is due to speak to reporters immediately after the meeting.
Low water levels are forcing inland cargo vessels to carry significantly lighter loads, pushing up transport costs and putting particular pressure on industries that rely on bulk freight.
