The European Commission confirmed on Thursday that it was targeted by a cyberattack through the use of AI tools.
The incident was detected on October 22 and there was no evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff as a whole, European Commission spokesperson Thomas Regnier told reporters.
"This was an AI-generated cybercampaign," he added.
Regnier did not elaborate on the attack or name a perpetrator. He said the Commission "has taken the necessary steps in response."
This is the first time the bloc reported an AI-assisted cyberattack.
Disruption reportedly lasted 10 days
Regnier's statement came after Politico reported earlier on Thursday that the Commission had been targeted. The report said the cyberattack disrupted several systems for 10 days and might have compromised large amounts of personal data. The European Commission has roughly 33,000 employees.
The Commission then convened its highest-level internal crisis management body for an urgent meeting about the cyber incidents, according to Politico.
"We temporarily took down several applications, and most of them have since then been restored," Regnier said. He also stated that they did not detect any evidence of a data breach affecting Commission staff.
Recent hybrid attacks on supermarkets, air traffic controllers, blood banks and broadcasters across Europe have raised worries among leaders that this may be part of a coordinated attempt at hybrid warfare.
France summoned the Iranian ambassador last week over cyberattacks that caused supermarket freezers to defrost, but Tehran has denied any involvement.
