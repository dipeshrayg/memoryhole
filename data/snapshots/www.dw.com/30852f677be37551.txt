Automotive workers protest nationwide as industry suffers crisis
Protesters from Germany's automotive industry have called for action from the government and company managements to save their jobs and factories as the country's car production sector faces a growing crisis.
Tens of thousands of people are expected to take part in rallies, which, according to the IG Metall union - Europe and Germany's largest - will take place at more than 280 sites.
Car industry being driven 'into a brick wall'
Speaking at carmaker Volkswagen's Wolfsburg factory, IG Metall's chairwoman, Christiane Benner, who is also a member of VW's supervisory board, said: "Things have really hit the fan."
"Companies like VW, BMW, Audi, Bosch, Mercedes and Porsche were synonymous with fantastic cars and high quality," she said.
"Now, management is driving the industry at full throttle into a brick wall."
Benner called on the government to bring down energy costs, give financial help to suppliers to help them adjust to new challenges and use "Made in EU" rules to bolster domestic production.
Massive job cuts underway
Many observers put the blame for the current crisis on companies having started too late with the shift to electric vehicles, while others say China's government support for its own producers has led to an unequal playing field in the sector.
US tariffs have also done their bit to damage what is one of Germany's key industries, while domestic demand has weakened.
VW itself plans to cut 100,000 jobs by the end of the decade in what would be the biggest ever restructuring in global automotive history, while Mercedes-Benz and BMW are also reducing their workforces.
German car exports down in first seven months of 2026 - Destatis
In 2026, German exports of passenger cars dropped by 4% up to the end of July in comparison with the same period last year, new figures by the Federal Statistical Office showed on Monday.
It exported altogether some 2 million cars at a worth of €73.5 billion ($84.4 billion), 8.9% less than a year ago, with almost a third going to the UK, the US and Italy.
Cars with electric and combustion engines both saw an export drop of 2.9%, while the export of those with hybrid motors fell by 8.2%.
Imports of new passenger cars rose by 16% to some 1.3 million vehicles.
China takes first place for car imports
Chinese vehicles climbed to first place among car imports to Germany at 175,000 vehicles.
That made up 13.8% of all car imports and represents a rise of 120.9% compared with the January-July period in 2025.
It thus pushed the former import leaders Czech Republic and Spain to second and third place respectively.
The rise in Chinese imports was largely driven by hybrid vehicles (+36%) and purely electric vehicles (+66%), while imports of Chinese cars with combustion engines went down 9%.
Elections latest: Merz defiant after state poll drubbings
Friedrich Merz's position as chancellor is looking increasingly fragile after his Christian Democrats (CDU) failed to make it into the state parliament of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania in weekend elections.
And that result came on the heels of the election in Saxony-Anhalt around two weeks ago, in which the CDU also put in a poor showing amid big gains by the far-right Alternative for Germany (AfD) party.
That, combined with his already very low approval ratings, means Merz is currently under huge pressure.

Germany brings in bumper cherry harvest
German orchard growers have harvested some 48,500 metric tons (53,462 US tons) of sweet and sour cherries this summer - 7.8% above the average for the past 10 years - new statistics show.
According to the German Federal Statistical Office (Destatis), some 80.2% of the harvest (38,900 tons) was sweet cherries, and 19.8% (9,600) was of the sour variety.
Pear growers also have reason to be content, with preliminary estimates putting the harvest of that fruit at 7.7% higher than the average over the past 10 years, though 3.3% down on last year.
The good fruit harvest comes despite Germany's grain production suffering greatly under the hot conditions during the summer.
Ex-Transport Minister Scheuer goes on trial for perjury
A perjury trial against former Transport Minister Andreas Scheuer kicked off at the Berlin District Court on Monday.
Scheuer and his co-defendant Gerhard Schulz, a former state secretary, are accused of having lied to a parliamentary committee investigating a failed scheme some seven years ago to levy a highway toll on foreign motorists.
Both of them deny the charges made against them.
What are the charges against Scheuer and Schulz?
The two are accused of lying to the committee about having signed a contract with a company to run the toll operations despite knowing that the European Court of Justice (ICJ) was likely to declare the scheme illegal.
Scheur's Christian Social Union (CSU), the Bavarian sister party of the Christian Democrats (CDU) currently led by Chancellor Friedrich Merz, had devised a plan to levy tolls on all drivers but then compensate German motorists by reducing their motor vehicle taxes.
The scheme was shot down in 2019 by the ICJ for breaching EU rules.
But by that time, the Transport Ministry under Scheuer had already signed binding contracts with the operators who were to be tasked with executing the plan.
The German government was then forced to pay €243 million ($283 million at current exchange rates) in damages to the companies involved.
Jail sentences possible
The trial is planned to take place over 12 days, with a verdict expected on November 25.
If found guilty, the accused could face jail sentences ranging from three months to five years.
