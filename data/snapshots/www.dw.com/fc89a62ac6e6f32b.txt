German weightlifters question Russia's 2030 Worlds award
Germany's weightlifting federation says it will critically examine the decision to award the 2030 world championships to Moscow, while stopping short of an outright condemnation.
The International Weightlifting Federation becomes the first Olympic sports federation to award Russia a global sporting event since its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
The German Weightlifting Federation said it would assess the decision's political, ethical and international implications.
"The complexity of the situation must not mean that fundamental questions and the necessary critical examination of this decision are neglected," it told the SID sports news agency.
The championships will also serve as a qualifying event for the 2032 Olympics, adding to the significance of the decision.
Track worker killed after ICE train hits two in Essen
A track worker has died and another has been seriously injured after an ICE high-speed train struck them in the western German city of Essen.
The two were among six workers on a section of Deutsche Bahn track late on Sunday evening, the fire service said.
Emergency crews pronounced one worker dead at the scene. The injured colleague was lifted from the tracks to street level using a fire engine's aerial ladder and taken to hospital.
Police are investigating the cause of the accident.
The line between Gelsenkirchen's main station and Essen-Altenessen remained closed in the morning as cleanup work continued. The train had been removed, a railway spokesperson said.
Long-distance services were being diverted between Gelsenkirchen and Duisburg. Regional passengers were advised to check alternative routes.
German cruise ship cancels voyage after port collision
German cruise operator Tui Cruises has canceled a 20-day voyage to the Canary Islands after its cruise ship "Mein Schiff 3" collided with two container vessels in the northern port of Bremerhaven.
Tugboats were helping the nearly fully booked cruise ship, carrying 2,428 passengers and 922 crew members, leave port on Sunday evening when a gust of wind pushed it toward two moored container ships, police said.
All three vessels sustained damage. One container ship suffered extensive damage to its port side, with several meters of its hull torn open above the waterline. The cruise ship also suffered significant damage, including to its lifeboats and bridge.
There were no injuries or water ingress on any of the vessels. All three ships have been temporarily prohibited from leaving port while investigations continue.
Tui Cruises said technical experts had inspected the vessel and determined that repairs at a shipyard were necessary, forcing the cancellation of the planned voyage.
The company described the cancellation as "the only responsible decision," stressing that passenger and crew safety remained its highest priority.
Merz and Steinmeier head to Asia on separate trips
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz and President Frank-Walter Steinmeier are departing on separate Asian tours to strengthen economic and diplomatic ties.
Merz will visit Singapore, Japan and South Korea on his longest overseas trip since taking office, accompanied by a high-level business delegation. He is seeking to broaden Germany's partnerships and reduce dependence on individual countries.
In Singapore, he will hold political talks and deliver a major foreign policy speech before traveling to Japan. In South Korea, he will attend an Asia-Pacific business conference and visit the demilitarized zone separating North and South Korea.
Steinmeier will make his second state visit to China, pressing for fairer trade relations and action to address economic imbalances, according to his office.
He is expected to speak at Peking University on Thursday and meet President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang on Friday.
Steinmeier also wants China to take greater international responsibility, including using its close ties with Moscow to help end Russia's war against Ukraine.
Union chief condemns Audi boss's Trump rally appearance
Germany's IG Metall union chief Christiane Benner has sharply criticized Audi CEO Gernot Döllner for appearing onstage at a campaign rally with US President Donald Trump.
Benner called the appearance "deeply embarrassing, politically unacceptable" and unworthy of Audi and its employees. She also accused Döllner of breaching the company's political neutrality rules.
Audi's code of conduct, published in April and signed by Döllner, bars public appearances with parties or politicians standing for election, and sponsorship of their events, during the three months before a vote.
Trump announced plans for an Audi factory in Georgia at Saturday's rally, with Döllner alongside him. The plant would produce around 200,000 vehicles annually, mainly large SUVs.
Benner stressed that the project still required approval, with the final decision "rightly and importantly" resting with Volkswagen's supervisory board, "with the full picture in mind."
She said employee representatives wanted safeguards for German sites alongside a clear North American strategy for Volkswagen and Audi. While acknowledging the US market's importance, she said IG Metall's priority was employment prospects in Germany.
