Bayern's Kane vows 'I'll be back' after ankle surgery
Harry Kane, Bayern Munich's star striker, has pledged to return to the field for the important matches at the end of the current campaign after undergoing surgery for a torn ankle ligament.
"I'll be back fresh and ready to go for the business end of the season," he wrote on social media above a photo of himself on his hospital bed, also thanking fans for their messages of support.
Kane, 33, was injured around 20 minutes into Bayern's 1-1 home draw with Cologne in their Bundesliga match on Friday.
Bayern issued a statement on Saturday saying that Kane had "successfully undergone surgery" on his left ankle but was expected to be out for three to four months.
England captain Kane, who finished the 2025-26 season with over 70 goals for club and country, was awarded the Ballon d'Or for his efforts.
Bavaria's late vice premier, Hubert Aiwanger, laid to rest
Hubert Aiwanger, the deputy premier and economy minister of the southern state of Bavaria, is being buried on Saturday in his native town of Inkofen.
Aiwanger, the federal leader of the Free Voters (FW) party, died suddenly two weeks ago of natural causes at the age of 55 in a hotel in Berlin.
The funeral service ahead of the burial was open to the public, with police saying they expected some 1,000 participants.
A memorial service was held in Munich a week ago to honor Aiwanger.
Germany drops funding for hub documenting right-wing violence
An online portal set up to collate records of right-wing violence in Germany since World War II is to lose its funding from the start of next year, a parliamentary inquiry has revealed.
In response to a question from Green politician Marlene Schönberger, the minister of state for culture, Wolfram Weimer, said that "[a]dditional funds for financing beyond the end of 2026 are not available" for the online hub, hosted on the "Archivportal-D" of the German Digital Library.
Schönberger, a member of the Bundestag's Committee on Internal Affairs, said she could not understand the decision by the government to cease funding for the portal.
"Not even eight months after the launch of the 'Right-Wing Violence' thematic portal, the federal government is now pulling the plug. That is completely incomprehensible," she said.
"The federal government must prevent right-wing violence, and where it fails to do so, it must at least acknowledge and document its continuity. It owes that to those affected," she said.
The hub was set up to bring together documents held in various archives in one online location.
US court approves Bayer Roundup cancer settlement
A judge in the US state of Missouri has approved a settlement under which Germany-based agrochemical company Bayer will pay up to $7.25 billion (€6.6 billion) over the next two decades to resolve lawsuits from cancer sufferers who say they should have been warned that its weed killer Roundup could cause lymphoma.
The settlement, which applies nationwide, means both Roundup users who are already ill with non-Hodgkin lymphoma and those who develop it in future could an average of between $10,000 and $165,000, with factors such as their use of the product, their age at the time of diagnosis and illness severity determining the exact sum.
The decision could be appealed.

IN DEPTH: The plight of Gen Z in Germany
Young people in Germany, as in many other parts of the world, have been taking to the streets to demonstrate for a fairer, safer world and a greater say in what policies are enacted by politicians.
But despite the fact that many of their complaints are fully justified, with jobs difficult to find despite a high level of study or training, housing that is increasingly unaffordable and a lack of government action against climate change to ensure the world remains habitable, the demonstrations have sometimes been criticized or even labeled as criminal.
DW looks at the problems facing Gen Z and its fears about its future in a world where young people have little say.
Electricity, gas bills to go up for millions
Many consumers who get their gas and electricity under the standard tariff offered by local utility providers will be paying considerably more for electricity and gas next year, according to comparison website Verivox.
Some 3.7 million households are expected to pay more for electricity, with a rise of some 2% expected.
The rise has been cushioned to some extent by coalition's recent agreement to lower the electricity tax rate for 2027 to the European minimum level to protect households and businesses.
Gas will become considerably more expensive for some 1.9 million households, with 361 regional utility providers announcing rises of some 14% for December, January and February.
"The wholesale prices for gas and electricity, which have risen sharply since the Iran war began in March 2026, are only now starting to have an effect on prices for consumers," Verivox energy expert Thorsten Storck said.
Almost half of people in Germany want Ukraine aid cut or dropped
Some 49% of people in Germany would like to see Berlin's financial, military and humanitarian aid to Ukraine amid the Russian invasion either reduced or cut completely, a survey has shown.
In all, 25% of respondents in a YouGov survey wanted aid to Ukraine to be dropped, while 24% were in favor of reducing.
Altogether 30% wanted to retain aid at the current level, and 9% felt it should be raised. Eleven percent were undecided on the issue.
The survey was conducted between November 13 and 16 on behalf of the newspaper Welt am Sonntag.
This week, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz hosted a meeting of Ukraine's European allies, known as the "Coalition of the Willing", in a bid to maintain a united front as peace talks in Ukraine continue.
