Teargas fired at small protest in Lyon - AFP journalist
Police have used teargas grenades to disperse a small protest in an area banned to all demonstrations in the southeastern city of Lyon, according to a journalist from the French news agency Agence France-Presse (AFP).
The group was protesting near a university faculty and the city's police station, according to AFP.
The agency said the police took action as the protesters tried to block the road.

IN DEPTH: Are French police the most violent in the EU?
With some 70,000 police officers to be deployed on Saturday, many people in France are fearing repeat violence not just from certain groups of protesters but also from the forces of law and order.
During recent student protests, a number of teenagers sustained serious injuries amid police actions, and multiple unconfirmed videos have shown incidents of brutality toward protesters at last week's wider demonstrations.
French police are among the most heavily armed in Europe and are subjected to less independent oversight than in many other countries.
Rights group Amnesty International has described the police response to the current protests as "disproportionate" and called upon French authorities to "act upon their human rights obligations."

Orleans protests expected to target Le Pen's party congress
A two-day congress of the far-right National Rally (RN) party in the city of Orleans, some 110 km (70 miles) south of Paris, is expected to draw its own protest by labor unions, left-wing parties, students and anti-racism groups.
The congress is to be hosted by Marine Le Pen, the party's lead candidate at French presidential elections in six months time.
Authorities fear clashes between counterprotesters and supporters of Le Pen, who has a nationalist, anti-immigration agenda and has received upwind from the recent protests by taking a strong law-and-order stance and citing "anarchic immigration" as one factor behind them.
The congress is likely to see the reelection of party president, Jordan Bardella.
France puts high security in place for fresh protests
French authorities say they will be deploying some 70,000 police on Saturday amid renewed protests as they look to prevent a repeat of the violence that marred mass nationwide demonstrations over social inequality a week ago.
Announcing the deployment, Interior Minister Laurent Nunez warned at a news conference in Paris that calls for violence and looting were circulating on social media.
Several restrictions on protests have been imposed across the country, including bans on a union-led march on one route in the capital, Paris, that organizers say they will disregard, citing safety concerns about a proposed alternative.
Leading museums in Paris, including the Louvre, will also remained closed.
The protests come a critical political moment for France, with elections due in six months to elect a successor to President Emmanual Macron.
Polls have shown far-right candidate Marine Le Pen as the frontrunner and indicated that the latest events might have shored up her popularity.

Macron calls shooting near Paris a 'racist terrorist attack'
French President Emmanuel Macron has condemned a shooting near Paris on Friday in which one person was killed and five others were injured, including a 12-year-old girl.
Macron called the attack "a turning point," saying it was "a terrorist act that was planned to kill our compatriots, our children, based on the color of their skin or their religion."
The victim killed was said by authorities to have been of "African origin," and most of the injured were also from foreign backgrounds.
The shooting is likely to further fuel tensions ahead of protests planned for Saturday, with some far-right politicians blaming immigration for the violence on the sidelines of protests in several French cities on October 17.

Welcome to our coverage
