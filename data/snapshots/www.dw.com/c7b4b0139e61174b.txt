A group of five EU member states, including Germany, was set to announce a new scheme on Monday to deport rejected asylum seekers to Rwanda.
A plan to set up so-called return hubs in a non-EU country had been announced by the group - which also includes Austria, the Netherlands, Greece and Denmark - had been announced back in September.
Monday's expected announcement comes after the EU introduced new regulations to speed up deportations and allow the establishment of hubs outside of the bloc.
What do we know about the EU deportation hub scheme?
Rwanda was not officially named as the chosen country, but people who had been briefed on the ongoing negotiations spoke to Reuters and AFP news agencies.
The central African country had also acknowledged being in "preliminary discussions" with the group of states.
Rwanda had also been the intended destination for a similar plan set up under the UK's former Conservative government that subsequently collapsed in UK courts.
However, the new EU scheme differs from the British one in that it is only intended for migrants who have exhausted all other legal avenues within the European Union.
Migration hubs have been seen by governments as a means to visibly show that they are taking action on migration amid surging anti-migrant sentiment and rising support for the far right.
What are the criticisms of the deportation hub plans?
While the leaders of the five countries said the scheme would comply with EU law, that has not protected them from criticism, especially over the plan to send migrants to a country with which they have no connection.
The Council of Europe, a pan-European human rights body, said in July that return hubs posed "considerable human rights risks."
Claims that the scheme is meant as a deterrence have been rejected by researchers who say there is no evidence that deterrence works.
"Enforcement changes how people travel, not whether they travel. Some pause or change route, and smugglers adapt and raise their prices," said Roberto Forin of the Mixed Migration Centre, a research institute that collects data along migration routes.
The choice to pick Rwanda has also been controversial, with the government President Paul Kagame in particular being accused of suppressing dissent and supporting the M23 rebel group in the neighboring Democratic Republic of Congo.
"Paying Kigali to host a deportation program would not only cause suffering and harm to those deported, it would finance and provide political leverage to a government responsible for serious human rights abuses at home and abroad," said Human Rights Watch's senior Africa researcher, Clementine de Montjoye.
