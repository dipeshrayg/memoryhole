Germany sees rise in IT sector unemployment
The economic slowdown in Germany has taken a toll on the job market for engineering and information technology (IT), according to a recent report by the Association of German Engineers (VDI) Engineering Monitor, in collaboration with the German Economic Institute (IW).
The latest report showed 58,392 unemployed individuals in engineering and IT professions. The figure represented the highest level of unemployment in the sector since the survey began in 2011.
It represents a 16.7% rise in unemployment compared to the previous year.
Meanwhile, the report highlighted that the number of job openings stood at 96,780 or 18.2% fewer than the previous year.
The study showed a wide discrepancy among each field, with a drop of job openings in civil enginnering of 3.8%, while the drop in computer science jobs fell by 33%.
"The decline in the number of job openings was particularly pronounced in Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, where the automotive manufacturing industry - which has been hit particularly hard by the economic downturn - plays a major role," the report stated.
But the VDI report cautioned that the findings do not indicate that Germany needs fewer workers in the field of IT and engineering.
"The modernization of our infrastructure, the transformation of the energy supply, digitalization, and new technologies will shape the demand for qualified engineers in the long term," VDI Director Adrian Willig said.
Southampton boss reveals threats after spying scandal
The German manager of English football club Southampton, Tonda Eckert, has spoken about receiving threats serious enough for police involvement after a spying scandal broke.
The affair emerged in May after a Southampton employee was seen watching Middlesbrough train ahead of the two clubs' Championship playoff semifinal.
When news of the illicit surveillance came to light, the English second-tier club was thrown out of the playoffs that could have given it a chance to return to the Premier League. It was also docked four points for the upcoming season, leaving some of the club's fans outraged.
"You are not prepared for something like that," the 33-year-old told German sports magazine Kicker.
Eckert said he was barely active on social media, which helped, but people still found ways to reach him.
"I don't want to go into details, but I was advised not to drive myself anymore and instead sit in the back of the car. That was very difficult," he said.
Eckert took responsibility for the incident and is now under investigation by England's Football Association. Eckert said he had not realized that what he authorized was prohibited but accepted that he should have known the rules.
"Above all, there was a constant, racing stream of thoughts. As head coach, I'm responsible for a decision that we deeply regret," he said.
The club has continued to back the Berlin-born coach.

Drought and disease hit German sugar beet harvest
Drought, extreme heat and disease are taking a heavy toll on sugar beet crops in Germany, with production in the south expected to fall by around a third from last year.
The Association of South German Sugar Beet Growers said such a yield would be the lowest since German reunification in 1990.
The poor harvest could also push up consumer prices, with crops suffering in France, Europe's largest sugar beet producer, as well.
Even fields in southern Germany that normally have reliable water supplies are showing clear signs of drought stress.
The association said weakened plants are more vulnerable to disease, causing some beets to develop a rubbery texture and reducing the amount of sugar that can be extracted.
The organization represents nearly 10,000 growers across seven regional associations. Its members cultivate around 100,000 hectares of sugar beet and supply seven factories.
Germany calls Rhine low-water conference
Germany's transport minister has called an expert conference in Bonn for Thursday as record-low water levels on the Rhine severely disrupt freight shipping.
A ministry spokesperson said Steffen Bilger had arranged the meeting at short notice, although the list of participants was still being drawn up.
The city of Cologne recorded its lowest Rhine level since measurements began on Saturday. Düsseldorf and Duisburg also reported historic lows over the weekend, sharply limiting how much cargo vessels can carry.
North Rhine-Westphalia Transport Minister Oliver Krischer has already called for a national task force bringing together the federal government, states, municipalities and the logistics industry.
Water levels have also fallen sharply along other major rivers, including the Elbe and Danube, with many monitoring stations recording new lows over the weekend.
Germany's Federal Institute of Hydrology said prolonged heat and dry weather were continuing to drain rivers across the country, while brief showers brought only temporary relief.
"Overall, the downward trend continues to dominate," the institute said.
DAX breaks soards to new high on hopes of US-Iran talks
Germany's DAX index has climbed above 26,000 points for the first time as hopes of easing tensions between the United States and Iran lifted markets.
The benchmark passed its previous record of 25,900.10 points shortly after trading began on Monday and hovered around the 26,000 mark by midday.
"Within just three weeks, the German benchmark has gained more than 1,300 points. The immediate impetus is coming from geopolitical easing," DZ Bank senior analyst Birgit Henseler said.
She added that second-quarter earnings in the United States and Europe had so far been stronger than expected.
The DAX has also benefited from its heavy weighting toward industrial and financial companies, leaving it less exposed than technology-focused indexes, which face concerns over a possible artificial intelligence bubble.
Oil prices fell sharply after US President Donald Trump announced fresh talks with Iran and called off a major military attack over the weekend.
Major Dark Web marketplace charged with drug trafficking
Public prosecutors in Frankfurt have charged the 31-year-old founder of the dark web marketplace Archetyp Market with drug trafficking following an investigation into the platform.
Germany's federal police service (BKA) found that the marketplace generated some €330 million ($380 million) in revenue since it was created in 2025. The profits came from the sale of prescription pills, cannabis products, crystal meth, cocaine, fetanyl, adn heroin, investigators said. Date rape drugs were also reportedly available.
The suspect, whose name was not given due to Germany's privacy laws, is accused of pocketing at least 5% of the total sales.
He had already been arrested last year at his apartment in Barcelona with the help of Spanish police, and has been in jail pending charges since.
The accused faces up to 15 years in prison if found guilty.

Germany's main consumer advocate group criticizes Deutsche Bahn's opaque price structure
As anyone who has tried to book a long-distance train journey in Germany will know, the price may vary wildly depending on how far in advance you book, how popular the service is, and what time of day you want to travel.
But even then, as the country's main consumer rights group Verbraucherzentrale (vzbv) notes, many of the vast differences in Deutsche Bahn (DB) prices remain completely opaque to passengers.
"Whoever tries to travel with Deutsche Bahn faces a complex price scale that makes planning a trip extremely difficult," said vzbv chief Ramona Pop on Monday.
Her organization looked at prices for travel between the major German cities of Berlin, Hamburg, Cologne, Frankfurt, and Munich from June 2024 through April 2026.
Vzbv criticized DB for consistently advertising only their "super savings" prices for select services.
They found that on over three-quarters of journeys scheduled between 7:00 am and 7:00 pm, prices were anywhere from 15 to 156% higher than the advertised "super savings" rate.
For its part, DB admits that there are "slight" variations in price depending on demand.
"Whoever finds this system unfair will end up deciding against train travel," Pop said, "which is something neither the government nor the rail service should want."
German retail sales rise in first half of 2026
German retail sales have increased in the first six months of 2026, with inflation-adjusted turnover up 0.7% compared with the same period last year.
Nominal sales, which are not adjusted for price changes, rose 2.2% in the first half, according to preliminary figures from the Federal Statistical Office.
The overall increase came despite a weaker June, when real retail sales fell 1.1% from May and slipped 0.2% compared with June 2025.
Food retail was among the weaker areas in June, with real sales down 1.4% from the previous month and 0.6% from a year earlier.
Online and mail-order trade performed more strongly, with real sales up 4.9% compared with June 2025 and nominal turnover rising 6.3%.
Petrol stations also showed an unusual pattern in June amid the Middle East war and Germany's temporary fuel discount. Real sales rose 2.1% from May, while nominal turnover fell 1.5%.
Bundestag may revisit pension reform
Pension reform was back in the spotlight over the weekend after three conservative state premiers criticized a raft of new laws recently agreed to by Germany's governing coalition of center-right Christian Democrats (CDU) and center-left Social Democrats (SPD).
At the heart of the debate is the possibility to retire as early as 63 in exchange for limitations on some benefits.
The three CDU premiers of Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt, and Thuringia were wary of their states shouldering a particularly heavy burden in financing the reform, as people there are the most likely to work a full 45 years before retiring.
SPD parliamentary co-leader Bärbel Bas said she was open to discussing the issue further, but warned that "it really is a comprehensive package that should remain intact as much as possible," and called on the three state leaders to discuss their concerns at the party level so that the CDU stance on the matter was unified.
Bavarian state premier Mark Söder, a member of the CDU's sister party the CSU, echoed Bas' stance that a such a comprehensive raft of measures should not be unraveled piece by piece.
While the ruling coalition has agreed to the proposals, they have not yet faced a final vote in the Bundestag.
Privacy advocates and regulators alike call for stricter rules on Meta Glasses
According to digital rights NGO HateAid, officials in German cities like Potsdam are already mulling rules to, for example, protect young women who fear being filmed by male strangers at public swimming pools using Meta Glasses.
Germany is known for being a highly privacy-conscious country, and photographing and filming strangers without their consent is against the law. However, as HateAid points out, the burden of proof often rests on the victim. Moreover, with Meta Glasses, it is much less obvious when one is being recorded than with a smartphone.
"This technology is fueling digital violence, as perpetrators now need virtually no technical know-how," HateAid said.
Thomas Fuchs, a privacy and digital regulation official for the city of Hamburg, told broadcaster ARD that he thinks an outright ban on smart glasses is possible.
Meta's main German corporate offices are located in the northern city-state.
His office tested the glasses, he said, and found that the LED light that is supposed to alert passersby that a user in filming is far too faint to be see in normal daylight.
He told ARD that he is speaking with colleagues in Ireland, where Meta has their European headquarters, on crafting policy at the EU-level.
Correction: An earlier version of this story incorrectly identified HateAid as calling for an all-out ban on Meta Glasses.
