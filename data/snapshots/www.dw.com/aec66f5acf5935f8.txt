UK right-wing populist leader Nigel Farage won Friday's by-election in Clacton, regaining the parliamentary seat he resigned a month earlier to trigger the vote.
He took about 63% of the vote, while his main challenger, comedian Jon Harvey running as trash-can-costumed "Count Binface," secured roughly 27% - his best result yet in eight election attempts. The turnout was 44%.
What triggered the Clacton by-election?
Reform UK's Farage called the special election to prove his voter support amid a parliamentary investigation into an undeclared £5 million ($6.75 million, €5.85 million) gift from Thailand-based crypto billionaire Christopher Harborne. Britain's major parties boycotted the race, calling it a self-serving stunt. This left Farage facing 33 mostly independent and novelty candidates.
Farage skipped the official vote count, saying police warned him of an "organized campaign to disrupt" the result. Essex police disputed reports of a credible threat but said a "proportionate and robust" security operation was in place. He also canceled a planned victory speech.
"I am damned if I am going to stand on a stage, having won a resounding victory... and be demeaned and humiliated by nobodies," he told supporters.
What happens next?
The win doesn't resolve Farage's political troubles. The parliamentary standards investigation, paused when he resigned, is expected to resume and could still force him from his seat. Farage says the money was a personal gift he wasn't required to declare.
Last month, a Survation poll put Farage's share of the vote at 73%, a figure he fell short of. It comes as Reform UK's national polling is also slipping behind a resurgent Labour Party under new Prime Minister Andy Burnham.
