A fat-burning molecule has an unexpected benefit for your muscles
- Date:
November 26, 2026
- Source:
University of Leeds
- Summary:
Researchers have discovered that a naturally occurring molecule released during exercise helps muscles become stronger, resist fatigue, and withstand damage. The molecule, already available as a dietary supplement, could open new avenues for treating muscle deterioration associated with type 2 diabetes.
- Share:
Researchers have identified a naturally occurring molecule that plays a crucial role in building muscle strength and improving physical performance. Released during exercise, the molecule could also provide the basis for future treatments to protect against muscle damage associated with type 2 diabetes.
The molecule, known as L-β-aminoisobutyric acid (L-BAIBA), is essential for muscles to adapt to physical activity. It helps muscles become stronger, improves their resistance to fatigue, and protects against damage and weakness.
The study, published in Nature Communications and funded by Diabetes UK and the Biotechnology and Biological Sciences Research Council (BBSRC), reveals that L-BAIBA is a central regulator of the biological changes that allow muscles to improve their strength, structure, and endurance in response to exercise training.
How Exercise Triggers a Natural Muscle Booster
Although the health benefits of exercise are well established, scientists still do not fully understand the biological processes that allow the body to adapt to physical demands. A research team led by Professor Lee Roberts at the University of Leeds investigated whether these processes could reveal new ways to treat chronic conditions such as type 2 diabetes.
L-BAIBA has attracted scientific interest for more than a decade. In research published in 2014, Roberts demonstrated that the molecule has beneficial effects on metabolism, helping to enhance the body's ability to burn fat in response to exercise. L-BAIBA is now commercially available as a dietary supplement.
The latest findings reveal another important function of the molecule. L-BAIBA acts as a chemical messenger that connects physical activity with beneficial changes in muscle structure and metabolism, helping muscles adapt to the demands placed on them.
Lee Roberts, Professor of Molecular Physiology and Metabolism at the University of Leeds, said: "Our research identifies L-BAIBA as an exercise-induced signal that helps muscles adapt, improve their metabolic capacity and ability to contract, and ultimately enhances physical performance.
"This gives us exciting new insight into how exercise benefits the body and highlights a promising target for future therapies aimed at preserving muscle function in chronic conditions such as diabetes."
Protecting Muscles Against Damage From Type 2 Diabetes
The researchers also discovered that L-BAIBA protects muscles against damage associated with type 2 diabetes. The condition can negatively affect muscle health, leading to a decline in muscle mass and strength, as well as disruptions in the way muscles process and use energy. These complications can significantly reduce a person's quality of life.
Because regular physical activity is an important part of managing type 2 diabetes, finding ways to improve muscle function and reduce fatigue could make a substantial difference for people who struggle to exercise.
Type 2 diabetes is a serious chronic condition that increases the risk of potentially life-threatening complications, including heart attacks and strokes. Its development is influenced by a complex combination of factors, including age, ethnicity, genetics, and living with overweight or obesity.
Although type 2 diabetes has traditionally been more common among older adults, diagnoses in people younger than 40 have been rising in recent years. Cases in this younger age group are now increasing more rapidly than among adults over 40.
A Promising Target for Future Diabetes Treatments
Anna Morris, Assistant Director of Research Strategy and Partnership at Diabetes UK, said: "Exercise plays an important role in managing type 2 diabetes, but for many people muscle weakness and fatigue can make staying active a real challenge. This research helps us better understand how muscles adapt to exercise and identifies a potential new target for improving muscle health. While these findings are still at an early stage, they could help shape future approaches that support people with type 2 diabetes to remain active and live healthier, longer lives."
Although the discovery opens promising avenues for future treatments, further research is needed to determine whether L-BAIBA supplementation can produce meaningful therapeutic benefits in people with type 2 diabetes.
