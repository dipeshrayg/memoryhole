These bizarre new beetles look so stretchy scientists named them Luffy

- Summary:
- Scientists have named a newly discovered beetle genus Luffy, inspired by the stretchy hero of the manga and anime One Piece. The insects have unusually long, slender mandibles, antennae, and mouthparts that resemble Luffyâs rubber-like ability to extend his body.
- Share:
The newly established beetle genus Luffy was not given its name simply as a playful reference. Researchers selected it because the insects have distinctive physical features that closely resemble the unusual abilities of Luffy, the main character in One Piece.
Compared with related beetles, members of this genus have exceptionally long and slender mandibles, antennae, and maxillary palps. Their stretched proportions reminded the scientists of Luffy's rubber body, which allows the fictional character to freely extend and enlarge his limbs.
Complete taxonomic descriptions of the genus and its species were published in the open-access, peer-reviewed journal ZooKeys.
Two New Luffy Beetle Species
So far, scientists have identified two species belonging to the new genus: Luffy schillhammeri and Luffy nika.
Luffy schillhammeri was discovered in broadleaf forests in Yunnan Province, China. Its species name honors Dr. Harald Schillhammer of the Natural History Museum Vienna for his extensive and outstanding contributions to rove beetle research.
The second species, Luffy nika, was found in Louang Namtha in northern Laos. The name "nika" refers to Luffy's legendary Devil Fruit awakening, "Hito Hito no Mi, Model: Nika" (also known as Gear 5).
This beetle has prominent white bands of hair on its elytra and across much of its body. That appearance reminded the researchers of Luffy's white, smoke-covered form during his Nika transformation.
A Missing Link in Rove Beetle Evolution
The discovery has significance beyond its memorable connection to manga. PhD student Fang-Shuo Hu and Alexey Solodovnikov of the Natural History Museum of Denmark conducted a broad review of every known genus in the Ocypus group.
Their analysis also examined several groups whose positions in the beetle family tree have remained uncertain or controversial (such as Acupronotes, Apostenolinus, and Staphylinus). By studying numerous specimens and carefully comparing small anatomical differences, the researchers reassessed how these groups are related.
The team identified the shared derived characteristics, or synapomorphies, that define the "Eucibdelus lineage" (such as the left mandible possessing dorsal ridge teeth and a completely sclerotized labrum).
Luffy beetles have the dorsal ridge teeth found in this lineage, but their labrum is not completely sclerotized. They also possess several distinctive features not seen in the other groups. Based on this combination of traits, the researchers concluded that Luffy is probably the sister group of the entire Eucibdelus lineage.
"This genus exhibits a unique combination of characters intermediate between the Eucibdelus lineage and other members of the Ocypus-group," the researchers note in their study, highlighting that our understanding of this insect lineage is continuously evolving with new discoveries.
By occupying this unusual position, the genus helps fill an important gap in the existing classification system. It also offers a fresh perspective on the evolutionary history of the subtribe Staphylinina.
Bringing Taxonomy to a Wider Audience
The journey from manga inspiration to publication in an international scientific journal shows that serious research can also be creative, memorable, and accessible.
The researchers hope the story of Luffy will help bring science closer to the public, increase awareness of the importance of biological taxonomy, and encourage more young people to explore biodiversity and classification research with the same adventurous spirit associated with Luffy.
