Lord Mandelson still held shares in his lobbying company six months after becoming US ambassador, despite government concens about conflicts of interest, newly released documents show.
The peer was sacked as ambassador last year by Sir Keir Starmer following revelations about the extent of his relationship with the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein.
This 85-page release is the third since MPs voted to publish documents aiming to reveal behind-the scenes-discussions about how he was appointed to the role.
The documents were previously withheld due to the Metropolitan Police investigation into allegations Lord Mandelson had passed market-sensitive government information to Epstein.
Sir Olly Robbins was sacked as head of the Foreign Office by Sir Keir Starmer during the scandal when it emerged Lord Mandelson had been granted clearance for the role despite concerns being raised by vetting officials.
The newly released documents highlight that Lord Mandelson was able to keep his shares in Global Counsel, the lobbying company he co-founded, while serving as ambassador to Washington.
They reveal civil servants' concerns about the risk of perceived conflict of interest.
But selling the shares was clearly not a condition of him taking up the role.
Part of that is because of the desire in Sir Keir's government at the time to have Lord Mandelson in Washington as quickly as possible.
That is exemplified in an email from Sir Olly, saying a process to police Mandelson's conflicts of interest could be "up and running in a light touch way".
Another factor is selling shares in a private company like Global Counsel requires finding a new buyer or selling them back to the company.
But the documents also reveal Lord Mandelson had not sold his shares back to Global Counsel because their offer was not the "fair value". Another attempt to sell to a different buyer fell through.
The result was Mandelson kept his shares, and while in office tried to set up a meeting between Global Counsel and a key Donald Trump advisor, as well as taking the firm's advice on a speech on Britain's role in the world.
Sir Olly then confirms the shares have not been divested as planned and, in an updated memo to Lammy he explains "the other party has not been able to execute this transaction" but Global Counsel now expects to "make an offer in September".
Lord Mandelson remained a shareholder until February 2026, the same month the company went into administration.
Emails about his dealings with his Washington counterparts show a close relationship, with one message from an unnamed White House official in April 2025 telling Lord Mandelson his label as the "Prince of Darkness" could not be "farther from the truth".
Ahead of a speech he was due to give on Britain's role "in the age of Trump", documents show him seeking advice from a Global Counsel employee in August 2025, during his time as ambassador.
The exchange contains feedback from an unnamed Global Counsel director, who suggested he should use "warmer language" about China and tone down "parts of your apologia of the Trump administration".
The director warned: "Scepticism of [the Trump administration] is now so deep â at least in the public policy-interested world outside government â that it would be counterproductive/unpersuasive if you came across as having drunk the Kool Aid."
Lord Mandelson responds to say he would water down his language, and in another email tries to arrange an "under the radar" lunch meeting to discuss China and trade between a GC employee and someone from the US President's Council of Economic Advisers.
On the domestic front, Lord Mandelson also sent texts to Sir Keir's then chief of staff Morgan McSweeney in September 2025 offering advice on a cabinet reshuffle.
He suggested moving Darren Jones to lead the science and technology department as a way to "solve your Darren issue", although McSweeney did not respond until after the reshuffle decisions had been made, with Jones given a newly-created role as chief secretary to the prime minister.
Lord Mandelson has yet to comment on the most recent release of papers relating to his appointment.
He has repeatedly let it be known he believes he has not acted criminally, did not act for personal gain and is cooperating with the police.
No charges have been made. A Met Police spokesperson told the BBC: "Our investigation into alleged misconduct in public office is ongoing."
Speaking to journalists in the Downing Street garden following the release of the documents, Prime Minister Andy Burnham said he would address culture change for Westminster as part of his 10 year plan, which he said will be published next month.
Security minister Dan Jarvis told MPs: "The government recognises that action needs to be taken in response to the issues raised by Mandelson's appointment."
He said action had already been taken to strengthen the vetting and appointments process, including strengthening the management of conflicts of interest.
Future appointments will not be made before security clearance has been granted, he said, adding that review by Sir Adrian Fulford of vetting procedures was nearing completion.
A separate review by Sir Anthony Finkelstein into use of personal messaging services including WhatsApp and Signal for business purposes is expected to be published in January next year.
