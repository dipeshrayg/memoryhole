Hard-hitting back row Greg Fisilau will make his first England start in one of five changes to their XV to face New Zealand.
The Plymouth-born 23-year-old, whose father Keni was a Tonga international, has impressed off the bench in each of England's previous three wins this autumn.
He brings an industrious carrying option to a back row that relies heavily on Ben Earl in that aspect of the game. Ben Curry's breakdown skills have been sacrificed to make space, with the Sale man relegated to the bench.
Exeter's Fisilau has carried more than any other player in the Prem this season, as well as sitting fourth in the top-flight's tackle standings.
Elsewhere Freddie Steward's good form is rewarded with an opportunity ahead of George Furbank at full-back, while Tommy Freeman returns on the wing for Saturday's rematch (16:40 GMT).
Freeman missed last weekend's 37-12 victory over the All Blacks with a minor thigh issue which forced his withdrawal in the hours before kick-off.
Ellis Genge comes into the starting front row after a string of barnstorming performances across the latter half of England's Nations Championship campaign.
He is joined in the front row by Luke Cowan-Dickie, who is preferred at hooker to Jamie George.
Northampton's 21-year-old scrum-half Archie McParland, who made his debut off the bench against Japan a fortnight ago, is among the replacements once again.
With less than a year until the Rugby World Cup, England head coach Steve Borthwick's auditions for fringe squad members are taking on more importance.
Victory would ensure an autumn clean sweep, a sixth win in succession and a third-place finish in the overall standings of the inaugural Nations Championship.
A win would also contribute to the northern hemisphere's points tally in a Ryder Cup-style competition which runs throughout the three days of action at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham.
The tournament is being held every other year between Rugby World Cups and British and Irish Lions tours in an attempt to bring more of a narrative to the Test calendar and draw in fans.
Six Nations holders France and world champions South Africa will contest Sunday's grand final (16:40 GMT).
England XV: Steward; Freeman, Slade, Atkinson, Joseph; F Smith, Van Poortvliet; Genge, Cowan-Dickie, Heyes, Itoje (c), Coles, Chessum, G Fisilau, Earl
Replacements: George, Baxter, Stuart, Martin, B Curry, Pollock, McParland, M Smith
Power, physicality and 2019 mistakes
When Fisilau first broke through at Exeter, a story emerged that his father banned him from playing touch rugby as a boy as he was concerned that it would teach him habits contrary to a traditional Pacific Islands physicality.
It turned out a clash with the family's church commitments was actually the motivation for Greg missing mini rugby.
Either way, Fisilau has certainly retained his explosive power, with several juddering hits, particularly against Japan, added to his highlight reel this autumn.
"It is clear the power and physicality he brings," said Borthwick.
"I think Greg adds in that area. We've seen him do it off the bench. And now I want to see him do it from the start of the game.
"He has grabbed his chance and taken it with both hands. I'm delighted for him, delighted for his family. His dad always said, until he plays for England, his dad's the best rugby player in the family. Well, Greg's now the best rugby player in the family, and I think he's very proud of it."
Borthwick added Fisilau would also be tasked with contesting the breakdown, where New Zealand, who won six turnovers to England's five last week, managed to get a toehold in Saturday's game.
"Greg has got an engine that is out of this world, and we're going to need that," said Borthwick. "We're going to need him in that breakdown presence, so that's why I preferred him at seven and keeping Ben Earl consistent at eight."
In bringing in Steward, who is a tall, rangy runner with great aerial ability rather than a playmaker in the mould of Furbank, Borthwick has made a stylistic change at full-back.
"They definitely have some different strengths," Borthwick said of his options at 15.
"I think Fred's carrying through the line is very significant, that little bit more power to create some more dent as well.
"He's really stepped up with his link play on edges, which has been very good. He has chased improvement. Everyone talks so much about his high-ball strength but there was always more to his game in that.
"He's also added the real effectiveness to his defence. Everyone knew he also had the long kicking game, but he's added that short kicking game.
"He deserves enormous praise for continuing to build his game."
Borthwick said that England's 2019 Rugby World Cup campaign, in which they lost to South Africa in the final after a superb 19-7 victory over New Zealand a week earlier, is informing their preparation for Saturday.
Borthwick was an assistant coach to predecessor Eddie Jones at the time. Jones subsequently said he regretted not making more changes to his line-up for the showpiece game - specifically not bringing in Joe Marler and Henry Slade to face the Springboks.
"It's certainly been a consideration, without question," said Borthwick of his 2019 experience.
"I was there then, and part of that review, and it was something we spoke about in the review. Trying to find the right balance, making the right number of changes and the right changes, is key.
"There's a freshness to the players, a number of them that want to come in, want to get their hands on the ball, and really take the game forward."
