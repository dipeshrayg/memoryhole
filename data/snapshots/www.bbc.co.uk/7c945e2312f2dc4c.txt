Great Britain's Davis Cup-winning captain Leon Smith says it is the "right time" for a change after announcing he will end his 17-year stint in the role.
Smith guided the British men's team to Davis Cup victory in 2015 - the nation's first win in the event since 1936.
The 50-year-old Scot said he had been mulling over his future for "most of this year" before deciding to step down after next week's Finals in Bologna.
Smith's successor has not yet been appointed, but the Lawn Tennis Association (LTA) has spoken to potential candidates and hopes to announce the new captain as soon as possible.
Former world number one Andy Murray, who was instrumental in Britain's 2015 triumph, is not believed to be interested in the role at this time.
"There comes a time whether it is personally you need a change, or it is good for the team to have something else," said Smith, who seemed emotional in a video call with reporters.
"I've done it for a very long time and I think there are others who will enjoy the role and can bring something different to the role."
"I love the role and haven't lost any passion for it. But it feels like the right time for me personally and the guys moving forwards."
With Smith picking the team and creating a tight bond between the group, Murray was the driving force on the court as he won all eight of his singles matches in the victorious campaign.
Murray was coached by Smith as a child and said winning the Davis Cup together was "one of the proudest moments" of his career.
"He did a great job of bringing everyone together and making sure we all felt we had a part to play. He got the best out of us that year," three-time Grand Slam champion Murray said.
"I've obviously known Leon a long time, I think the most fun we had together was when I was playing Davis Cup and Leon was on the bench. I'm really glad we got to share those moments.
"I wish Leon all the best as he steps down, and good luck to the new captain."
Smith, who will continue in his role as head of British men's tennis, took over in 2010 with the team in danger of being relegated to the lowest tier of the Davis Cup.
Within three years they were back in the top tier and just two years later Murray inspired them to the trophy.
Smith also guided the team to the semi-finals in 2016 and 2019.
While he can no longer rely on a superstar of Murray's ilk, Smith is hoping this year's crop can ensure his captaincy ends on another high.
Britain will rely on Wimbledon semi-finalist Arthur Fery and former world number eight Cameron Norrie in the singles, with world number one Henry Patten and three-time major champion Neal Skupski pairing up in the doubles.
Smith's side faces Germany - led by singles world number one Alexander Zverev - in the last eight on Thursday.
"I'm focused and really looking forward to captaining Great Britain for one more week in Bologna next week and hopefully creating a few more special memories together," Smith added.
Analysis: Smith will be a hard act to follow
Smith has won 70% of his ties since taking over as captain in the summer of 2010.
The venues and the crowds for many of the early ties were on the low side, until Britain moved through the divisions and Andy Murray returned to the team.
The travelling support surged, and the team celebrated their return to the World Group with a stunning win over the United States in a baseball stadium in San Diego in January 2014.
Less than two years later the Davis Cup was back in Britain as thrilling wins in Glasgow and the Queen's Club were followed by Murray's famous lob to seal victory over Belgium.
Great attention to detail has contributed to Britain's success; and far more often than not, Smith got the marginal selection calls right.
He will be an extremely hard act to follow, but will inevitably work closely with his successor given his ongoing role as the head of men's tennis.
