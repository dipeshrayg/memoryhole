Premier League clubs have voted to cover legal expenses for the organisation's five board directors if any of Manchester City's sponsors sue them.
The clubs met in London on Thursday for the first time since City were found guilty of a catalogue of charges relating to the breaching of financial rules by an independent commission.
City have appealed, with a sanction yet to be decided.
The club's sponsors Etihad and Etisalat have both said they are considering legal action against the Premier League.
At present, under the league's articles of association, a director is only financially covered should they win a legal case brought against them by a third party. But the league's rules will now be amended to extend legal indemnity for CEO Richard Masters, chair Alison Brittan and three other non-executive directors.
Sources have told BBC Sport that City and Nottingham Forest were the only two clubs that voted against the proposal to provide extra legal protection, with the other 18 clubs voting in favour.
Due to collective responsibility, it means that all clubs are now liable to cover the board's legal costs, raising the intriguing possibility of City being financially impacted by the legal action of its own sponsors.
It is understood City's CFO Ingo Bank asked Premier League chair Brittan during Thursday's meeting if the proposal was a direct result of threats of legal action from Etihad and Etisalat, but she said she could not comment.

The commission determined that the club arranged "sham" contracts with sponsors, which disguised funding from club owners the Abu Dhabi United Group.
Premier League CEO Masters said City "systematically broke Premier League rules for nearly a decade".
In October, City sponsor Etihad Airways said it was considering legal action against the Premier League.
In a statement, the United Arab Emirates-based airline said it "categorically rejects any finding, conclusion or implication that suggests the airline has ever been involved in improper commercial arrangements".
Etisalat signalled its intention to follow suit earlier this month and described the Premier League's verdict as "entirely false and defamatory".
