Veteran quarterback Aaron Rodgers may have played his last game in the NFL after being dropped by the Pittsburgh Steelers.
In a brief statement published late on Tuesday local time, the Steelers said Mason Rudolph will be their starting quarterback for Friday's home game against the Denver Broncos.
The Steelers added that Rodgers, the NFL's four-time Most Valuable Player, will be inactive for the game so will not serve as back-up to 31-year-old Rudolph.
Rodgers, who turns 43 next week, was not listed on the Steelers' injury list on Tuesday and ESPN reports, external leaving him out was a coaching decision.
Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy was in charge of the Green Bay Packers for most of Rodgers' 18-year spell in Wisconsin, including their Super Bowl win in 2011.
Rodgers, who joined Pittsburgh in 2025 after two years with the New York Jets, was "99% sure" he was going retire after leading the Steelers to last season's play-offs.
However, McCarthy's appointment by Pittsburgh persuaded him to play one more year.
Although the Steelers have a 6-4 record, they have suffered two straight defeats after losing 19-16 at the Philadelphia Eagles on Sunday.
When asked about a potential change on Monday, McCarthy said "Aaron Rodgers is our quarterback".
But a day later the Steelers announced that is no longer the case.
Rodgers handed 'chastening demotion' - analysis
Pittsburgh's 29-word statement,, external published at 23:10 local time, could be an unceremonious end to an illustrious career.
While Rodgers was unable to claim a second Super Bowl win, he was named the season MVP in 2011, 2014, 2020 and 2021. Only Peyton Manning (five) has won the award more times.
Rodgers insisted his 22nd year in the NFL would be his last, and he has been benched at a crucial stage in the season.
The Cincinnati Bengals (7-3) lead the AFC North division, with Pittsburgh third, but with seven games left the Steelers occupy the final play-off spot for the AFC Conference.
Although Pittsburgh are 6-4, they have had to grind out some of those wins as they have struggled offensively. They rank 22nd for both points and passing yards per game.
Rodgers is undoubtedly past his best, but his form has only dropped slightly from last season and the team's struggles are by no means entirely down to him.
The Steelers' depth among their wide receivers is thin. Michael Pittman is out injured and DK Metcalf has not delivered the production expected when he was brought in with Rodgers before the 2025 season.
Metcalf, 28, was even ejected during Sunday's loss at Philadelphia, meaning Rodgers had to lean on debutant Max Hurleman with the game on the line.
And it is not as if Pittsburgh had a hot-shot rookie quarterback pushing for an opportunity to impress.
Rudolph is in his ninth season and has started each as a back-up. After being drafted by the Steelers in 2018, he joined Tennessee for the 2024 season then returned to Pittsburgh, starting 19 of his 38 career games.
McCarthy is hoping Rudolph can spark his offence, but if he doesn't, would they revert to Rodgers or try the two young quarterbacks on their roster?
It would be some U-turn for the Steelers to ask the veteran to step back in after what has to be considered a chastening demotion, a move the team knew would grab headlines.
Teams seldom make such a sudden change at this stage in the season, especially those with a winning record and a quarterback of Rodgers' stature.
Rodgers has clashed with team management, team-mates and coaches throughout his career - including McCarthy - so would he be willing to come to his aid if asked?
Whatever happens over the coming weeks, this is certainly not the farewell one of the greatest quarterbacks of all-time was hoping for.
