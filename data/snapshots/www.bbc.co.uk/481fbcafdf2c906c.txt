Two men have been arrested on suspicion of burglary and trespass at the Prince and Princess of Wales's country home on the Sandringham estate, police say.
Norfolk Police said it received a call just before 18:00 GMT on Monday following a report of suspicious activity at Anmer Hall.
Police deployed dogs, drones and a helicopter to search the area and arrested two men on the estate, who remain in custody.
The property was unoccupied at the time, police added. Kensington Palace said it did not comment on security matters.
William and Catherine do not live at Anmer Hall full-time, but use the estate as a country retreat during the holidays.
Val Blackmur, an 83-year-old resident who lives on the road leading up to the house, told the BBC it was "amazing" if anybody had been "stupid" enough to try breaking in.
The country retreat, which has 10 bedrooms, was a gift to the couple from the late Queen Elizabeth II following their wedding in 2011.
The red brick Georgian mansion is on the private Sandringham estate in Norfolk and is about two miles (3km) east from Sandringham House, one of the King's private residences.
The Prince and Princess's main home is on the Windsor Castle estate in Berkshire.
The Sandringham estate spans approximately 31 sq miles (80 sq km).
Members of the public can visit it, although the house and its gardens are currently closed.
The Royal Parkland, which is the woodland to the west of the main house, is open to the public all year round.
Many other members of the Royal Family also live on the estate, including Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, who moved on to Marsh Farm in February.
Despite tight security around members of the Royal Family, there have been several intrusions on their property in recent decades.
One of the more recent incidents was in 2024, when Thames Valley Police said burglars had broken into a farm on the Windsor Castle estate and stolen two vehicles.
Blackmur said: "We never seem to have any trouble so when something out of the ordinary happens that sort of makes you prick your ears up and think, what was all that about?"
The 83-year-old said it was not unusual to hear police cars or helicopters in the area.
"We've lived here 50 years now... we've never heard nothing like that," she said.
"Up at Anmer Hall it's so secluded and out of the way, not in full view - I should think people would be shocked."
