Police have said they are trying to contact more than 100 gig-goers to help their investigation after an unknown substance was released at a concert at Manchester's Co-op Live arena.
Detectives believe a "small group of organised criminals targeted people in the crowd to commit a series of robberies on the night".
More than 12,000 people were evacuated from the arena during a performance by US rapper Don Toliver on Sunday.
One woman was taken to hospital and 88 others were treated at the scene after concertgoers reported a "horrible smell" and some people "coughing up blood".
No arrests have been made and the substance has not yet been identified.
Acting Chief Constable Tom Harding said: "We're working to identify those [robberies] suspects and the substance that we believe was released in the crowd as part of these incidents.
"All of this work is ongoing at pace.
He said there had been "similar incidents at two Don Toliver gigs in Europe that are also relevant to our enquiries".
Fans reported that a spray or gas was released into the crowd in Zurich and Paris.

Jess Kinyata, 20, from West London, who was one of 12,000 people evacuated from the arena, said before the "gas began" his 18 carat gold chain, worth about Â£1,500, was stolen from him during a set by the concert's opening artist, Victony.
GMP previously said there had been nine reports of jewellery being stolen during the concert.
Harding said officers had been "working through footage captured on mobile phones and CCTV".
He added: "There are over a hundred people at the gig that we are working to try and contact to potentially assist with our enquiries.
"We thank all those that have assisted us so far.
"We also know many more people were impacted on the night and afterwards, physically and psychologically."
North West Ambulance Service said it had deployed crews, including its Hazardous Area Response Team, who carried out decontamination "as a precaution".
Those who suffered symptoms had been taken to the Etihad stadium.
Some fans complained the process, which involved them changing into paper white suits, was "invasive" and "traumatic".
Many stayed for several hours before being able to leave in the early hours of Monday.
