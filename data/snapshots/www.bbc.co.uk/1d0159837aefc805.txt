'The theatre around it will grind people's gears'
Lis Saunders is the chair of the Northampton Saints Supporters Group.
"I have known Henry coming up through the academy and he is absolutely delightful," she told BBC Sport.
"The perception of his arrogance is wrong. He is just a young bouncy man, living his best life.
"Some people are getting a bit fed up with the whole theatre around [the contract stand-off], though.
"I am laissez-faire about it. If he stays it will be brilliant, but you can't keep someone who wants to go.
"The whole theatre around it is what will grind people's gears. You are starting to see that on some of the fan forums â people saying, 'Either sign or don't. Just don't mess around - we don't need this drama.'"
Saunders points out that, while Pollock can pull a crowd, Saints invariably fill Franklin's Gardens anyway. The turnstiles will carry on clicking at the same rate regardless of his presence.
It would be an emotional wrench rather than a financial loss if he moves elsewhere.
"As the players themselves say, he's an idiot, but he's our idiot," said Saunders.
"We've watched him grow up and he's very much ours. It would be really sad if he didn't stay - perhaps understandable, but really sad."

Where could he go?
Newcastle Red Bulls
The obvious destination were Pollock to move within the Prem.
Backed by Austrian energy drink dynasty, Pollock would be a statement signing that fits their youth-orientated brand. The club had plenty of headroom on the pay roll, spending barely half of the salary cap's upper limit in 2024-25 campaign.
However, a revamp of the playing squad since then will have given them less ability to manoeuvre, and Prem Rugby rules on domestic moves prevent Pollock from being the club's designated marquee player for his first two seasons elsewhere.
Pollock would also have to weigh up whether swapping the Prem champions for the league's bottom club, who are without Champions Cup rugby for 2026-27 at least, is desirable.

Japan
The money-making move of choice for many of the southern hemisphere's finest.
Richie Mo'unga, Pieter Steph du Toit and Malcolm Marx are among those who have enjoyed the lighter schedule and yen-heavy pay days in Japan.
While England internationals George Kruis and Alex Goode have made moves there in the past, there has been a shift lately to favour Japanese players over big-money imports.
Both Kruis and Goode were near the end of their careers. A switch to such relative obscurity would do little to build Pollock's brand in rugby's major markets.
'Other sports'
Perhaps the most intriguing comment made by Pollock this week was a desire to explore "other sports" during his career.
He has been a ringside regular since signing with Matchroom, most recently attending Katie Taylor's farewell fight at Dublin's Croke Park, and chatting with UFC fighter Ian Machado Garry at a recent charity fundraising event.
His high-energy style would suit a switch to rugby league, and it has been reported, external that a number of Super League clubs, buoyed by possible investment from the NRL, are monitoring Pollock's situation.
