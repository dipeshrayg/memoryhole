Two trains have collided in Spain just south of the capital Madrid on Wednesday morning.
Rail operator Adif says the incident occurred between a Renfe train and another of the Iryo company, which covers the Madrid to Seville route.
So far at least 30 people are confirmed to have died in the incident with casualty numbers expected to rise.
Here is what we know about the crash so far.
Where and when did the crash happen?
The two trains collided just after 09:00 (08:00 GMT) between municipalities Mora and Orgaz, and about 140km (100 miles) south of Madrid.
The accident happened when a high-speed train travelling from Madrid to the south of the country collided with another train that was apparently stationary. The crash caused at least two carriages to fall down an embankment.
Broadcaster RTVE, quoting the transport ministry, said a Renfe train rear-ended an Iryo train.
Renfe is Spain's national state-owned railway company while Iryo is a private high-speed railway operator.
All train traffic on this line has been stopped at stations along the route, Adif said.
Renfe said services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia remain suspended.
A passenger on board one of the trains told local media there was a heavy impact about 40 minutes into the journey.
What caused the crash?
The cause has not been confirmed, though reports in Spain suggest a high-speed train hit another train which had stopped on the tracks.
How many passengers were on board
More than 500 people were believed to be on board both trains.
Esther Padilla, spokesperson for the government of Castilla-La Mancha region, told TVE that one of the trains - managed by the Iryo company - was carrying 146 passengers plus one baby, while the Renfe-managed train had 397 passengers on board.
