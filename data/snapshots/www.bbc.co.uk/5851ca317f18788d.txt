Welsh rugby legend Sir Gerald Davies has questioned the Welsh Rugby Union's ability to fully capitalise on the nation's historic victory over New Zealand.
Wales' first win against the All Blacks in 73 years has provided a timely boost for everyone connected to rugby in the country during a period of significant uncertainty.
Steve Tandy's side delivered the memorable performance in front of a fervent but lowest Principality Stadium crowd of 55,152 for a Wales-New Zealand match.
Not too long ago Wales were mired in a world record 18-Test losing streak, while a shadow hangs over the future of regional rugby with the Welsh Rugby Union (WRU) intent on cutting a team in 2028.
Davies, WRU president from 2019 to 2023, believes the gutsy showing against New Zealand has lifted spirits but wants clear direction for Welsh rugby's future.
"New Zealand are the barometer around the world, but there's a big question about the future and where Wales goes from here," Davies told BBC Radio Cymru.
"Are the administrators within the union able to show leadership and the vision to improve the state of the game in Wales?"
The legendary British and Irish Lions wing said Wales needs a team to inspire the next generation to believe again after some tough years.
"It is great to celebrate and correct to feel proud of what we saw at the weekend, but the big question is what about tomorrow and what is the plan?" he added.
"So far, I'm afraid I don't think we've seen the leadership required [from the WRU].
"We have to give a big thanks to the players for Saturday, they have raised the spirit of the nation. It was a game full of successes for Wales.
"I hope that the leaders within the union, who until now have only created misery, show the vision to move the nation forward."

Davies - who sat on the Sir Tasker Watkins committee to reform Welsh rugby in the early 2000s - was not at the Principality Stadium, choosing instead to watch with his family at home.
Like many of his fellow former team-mates, the iconic winger watched on as the current generation achieved a feat they themselves could not manage in the red shirt of Wales.
Davies was, however, a key member of the Lions series win over the All Blacks in 1971, scoring twice in a 22-12 second-Test defeat in Christchurch before crossing again in a 13-3 third-Test victory in Wellington.
The 81-year-old, who won 46 caps for his country between 1966-78, was full of pride as he took in a display of attacking intent and defensive endeavour, even if there was a hint of jealousy.
"First and foremost, you have to congratulate the coaches and players for what they delivered on Saturday," said Davies.
"You have to admit, some of us are jealous of what they delivered on Saturday, but totally in support of what happened.
"Wales have broken the hoodoo at last after 73 years and what we saw on Saturday certainly shows the way forward for Wales.
"It showed that we can play quickly, that we can stand up physically to the All Blacks and that we had the confidence to deliver.
"It has lifted the nation's hearts. Not just us as individuals, but as a nation. We all feel pride towards the victory."
Wales have the chance to back up their memorable win when they host Australia on Saturday.
