Several universities in England have only been using below-average salaries in examples of student loan repayments on their websites, the BBC has found.
Treasury Committee chair Dame Meg Hillier, who led a recent inquiry into student loans, said some information about repayments was "misleading", including one instance where they were compared to the "cost of a daily coffee".
She also said the Department for Education (DfE) must include higher-salary examples on its own website, too.
The DfE said it would review the information it provides, while universities said their websites referred to government information and would be reviewed where necessary.
Student loan repayments are tied to earnings, so the more you earn, the more you repay every month - and graduates generally go on to earn more than non-graduates.

"I'm pretty shocked at what the BBC has uncovered here and I'm going to be raising it with the relevant MPs and urging them to write to the universities in their constituencies to make them step up and do a better job," Dame Meg said.
She said the DfE should be "taking the lead" by reflecting repayments for higher earners, but that universities also "have a responsibility to the young people that they're taking on board for the three or four years of their degree".
Dame Meg chaired an inquiry into student loans this year and said the DfE had been "mis-selling" Plan 2 loans, which were given between 2012 and 2023 in England and are still issued in Wales.
Her report said the DfE had not made clear how higher earnings would affect monthly repayments and criticised comparisons to Â£30-a-month phone contracts.
The DfE then pledged to "improve the current information that is provided to prospective borrowers" including "the impact of salary progression" on monthly repayments.
Lewis Wilson, from the National Union of Students, said the BBC's findings were "shocking", adding: "Students should be able to rely upon information from their universities and if this was any other financial product, it would have crossed the line into mis-selling."
The DfE said its guidance sets out "how repayments are calculated across the different repayment plans", adding that it was "vital" that university applicants have "clear and accurate information".
"We have committed to reviewing the information we provide following the Treasury Committee's recommendations, and we will. We are also working with universities to ensure the information they provide to students is clear and up to date," it added.
The University of Gloucestershire, the University of Birmingham and Harper Adams University said they had updated their websites and Birkbeck, University of London said it was reviewing the information.
