Second T20, Carrara
Australia 184-8 (20 overs): Chaudhary 45 (35); Curran 2-29
England 188-2 (13.1 overs): Donald 85 (32); Meredith 1-18
England won by eight wickets; series level 1-1
From Gorseinon to the Gold Coast to global recognition.
Welshman Aneurin Donald's demolition of Australia in the second T20 will make the rest of the world notice his talents and pave the way for numerous franchise deals, according to former England captain Michael Vaughan.
Donald's astonishing opening partnership of 151 in 10 overs with Phil Salt helped England hammer Australia and levelled the T20 series on the Gold Coast, as they chased 184 in just 13.1 overs.
Donald blitzed 85 from 32 balls, including nine sixes, while his compatriot Salt made 58 off 30 as the pair from small Welsh villages in Swansea and Denbighshire took the Australians apart.
Donald's 18-ball fifty was the joint third-fastest for England in the format as the pair raced to 79-0 in the powerplay, flogging Australia's second-string bowling attack to all corners of the ground in a remarkable display of power hitting.
"It was a night where we saw a player really step up to the big stage," Vaughan told BBC Sport.
"I'm sure we're going to see much more over the next few years from Donald, but I think tonight the rest of the world noticed and said 'oh, there's a player there'.
"He's got a few franchise gigs at the moment but to do it on the biggest stage against Australia, with the world watching, he's going to be talked about."
His opening partner Salt, who was dropped by Joel Davies on 10, was mostly able to watch from the non-striker's end before accelerating to a 24-ball half-century of his own, which came during a brutal over from spinner Davies that went for 31.
Donald looked gutted to miss out on a century after he holed out to long-on off Riley Meredith's bowling, before Salt was brilliantly caught and bowled by Ben Dwarshuis an over later.
However, England's phenomenal top order - which still awaits the return of Jos Buttler from injury - finished the job with ease as Tom Banton walked off unbeaten on 26 off eight balls, including a monstrous six for the winning runs.
Australia's 184-8 looked competitive on a surface that had assisted England's spinners and Sam Curran's slower balls - the hosts recovering from a stodgy middle over period after falling to 58-4 in the powerplay.
Davies added a handy cameo of 38 before Dwarshuis whacked Curran's last three balls for six to finish strongly but within just a few overs of the chase, Donald and Salt had silenced a lively Australian crowd in style.
The five-match series continues at the Gabba, Brisbane, on Friday.
Destructive Donald makes his mark
Donald made his T20 international debut as a 29-year-old earlier this summer on the back of consistent county and Hundred performances, but struggled to really cement his place with 56 runs in four innings.
Still, a player known for an incredible strike-rate in particular, he was backed by Harry Brook and Brendon McCullum to play freely and showcased that strength against an Australia attack that had no answers.
"He's the perfect T20 striker," Vaughan added.
"He has got a great base and a good eye and he doesn't over-complicate it. His mindset is to just try and hit everything for six, very much the modern T20 player.
"The game is moving forward and you need players like Donald who can hit the ball like that."
Brook himself said he was "speechless" at the end of the game, high praise from one of the world's best, as Donald and Salt made what could have been a tricky chase look like a stroll in the park.
Only three international T20s had been held at the Gold Coast's Carrara Oval previously so England were in relatively unknown territory.
Not that it mattered to Donald, who swatted Davies for four from his first ball to start the demolition.
His first couple of sixes off Jack Edwards sailed into the crowd despite looking like he had not timed them perfectly, giving Australia captain Mitch Marsh an insight into his new Perth Scorchers team-mate when the Big Bash League starts in December.
Though the pitch had looked difficult to face spin in Australia's innings, Donald and Salt both tucked into it with aplomb - before Salt's takedown of Davies, Donald clubbed Nikhil Chaudhary for 26 to bring up his fifty by the end of the fifth over.
Donald's showcase gives England a nice headache at the top of the order, with Buttler and Jacob Bethell still to come back into the fold - a frightening prospect for opposition bowlers.
England's mixed bowling still a cause for concern
Despite the batting brilliance, there were mixed fortunes for England's bowlers with a contrast between their spinners and seamers.
Australia's powerplay was bizarre. Their run-rate was healthy, just shy of 10 runs an over, but they threw away four wickets which meant they had to be more cautious throughout the middle.
Marsh was caught and bowled by Henry Crocombe, Jake Fraser-McGurk was out slogging to Curran before debutant James Coles produced a fine run out to get rid of Sam Harper.
Coles then took the catch in the deep to get rid of Tim David, and that allowed the masterful Adil Rashid and Liam Dawson to get to work, bowling seven overs on the bounce as Australia's middle order came to a standstill.
Rashid tied Lachlan Shaw and Joel Davies in knots early on as they struggled to pick up his variations before he removed the former for a stodgy 10 from 17 balls.
Davies dug in before accelerating at the death, with assistance from the big-hitting Edwards as Jamie Overton's struggles continued.
The 18th over conceded 16 as he only bowled two overs for 28, before Crocombe's penultimate over flew for 16 and Dwarshuis got to work on Curran, who was also taken apart in the final over in Australia's win on Saturday.
Unfortunately for Curran, he had previously been sensational at mixing up his pace deceptively and had figures of 2-9 off his first three, but completely lost control of his yorkers in the final three balls which the powerful Dwarshuis sent flying.
England will be grateful for the return of Jofra Archer to their ranks as their difficulties at closing out an innings are becoming a concern.
