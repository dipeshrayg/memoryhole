A former Hamas bodyguard, wanted in Germany over an alleged plot to murder Jewish people, has been found guilty of a knife attack on a bailiff outside his UK home.
Mohammed Alfatih, previously known as Mohammed Naim, was regularly seen acting as a bodyguard for a former leader of Hamas in 2006. He was granted asylum in the UK in 2015 and British citizenship in 2021.
The 40-year-old was found guilty by majority verdict of unlawfully wounding a bailiff who had come to repossess his wife's car, possession of two knives and criminal damage. He was cleared of wounding with intent.
Alfatih was under surveillance by a counter-terror unit at the moment he attacked the bailiff in Birmingham last year.
The son of a senior Hamas official, he could not be named during his trial due to reporting restrictions.
During the trial, footage was shown of Alfatih brandishing two large kitchen knives outside his home in the suburb of Kings Heath and trying to stab the bailiff as he drove away.
He told the court he chased the victim across the street to scare him away after his wife was asked to settle her debts of more than Â£2,250 linked to unpaid parking fines.
Her car had been clamped by the enforcement officer, who was involved in a struggle inside Alfatih's terraced home.
Jurors were not told of Alfatih's links to proscribed terrorist group Hamas or his work as a bodyguard to its now-deceased former leader Ismail Haniyeh.
But it can now be revealed that while detained for the attack in Birmingham, he was re-arrested when authorities in Germany said he had been part of an alleged gun plot to attack Jewish targets there.
It is alleged Alfatih was an organiser and was involved in the movement of weapons.
German authorities also allege he had pre-recorded a video wearing a balaclava and sunglasses claiming the attack was planned on behalf of the Izzedine al-Qassam Brigades, the military wing of Hamas. The alleged attack was prevented after a tip-off from the Israeli intelligence agency Mossad.
Alfatih, who was born in Germany, is expected to be extradited to the country after he has served his sentence in the UK.
During his evidence, Alfatih did not mention Hamas but told the court he had been tortured for 25 days while detained in Egypt in 2010.
The father-of-four, an IT worker, failed in a legal application to try to get the wounding charges thrown out on the grounds of prejudicial media coverage about the investigation in Germany.
During the two-week trial at Birmingham Crown Court, the jury was shown video footage of Alfatih lashing out with a knife at the broken window of a van on 14 October last year.
Bailiff Kevin Bucknall was left with cuts to his head and required hospital treatment, but the defendant had pleaded not guilty on the grounds of self-defence.
Alfatih told jurors it had happened a day after he had learned one of his brothers had been killed in Gaza.
He also said while giving evidence that traumatic experiences in his past included being shot and injured on two different occasions.
"I just want to be as honest as I can," he told the jury.
"I don't think I was even in my full mind. I didn't intend anything other than him moving away.
"I wasn't anything more than terrified."
Defence barrister Kate O'Raghallaigh said Alfatih had a heightened perception of danger, triggered by fear and desperation he had previously experienced.
Jurors deliberated for more than six hours over three days before convicting him, with Alfatih heard mumbling to himself as the verdicts were returned.
He has been in custody since the knife attack and now faces sentencing in Birmingham on 11 January, as well as separate extradition proceedings at Westminster Magistrates' Court.
The BBC understands that when Alfatih went by the name of Naim, he enrolled in a VIP protection course and was selected to accompany Haniyeh on trips to Egypt and other countries between 2006 and 2008. However, he was later removed from the role due to his behaviour.
The second son of former Hamas health minister Bassem Naim, he later spent time in Malaysia, Qatar and Turkey before arriving in Britain.
Following his conviction, a government source said it was "staggering" that he was granted asylum and later given citizenship.
A Home Office spokesperson added Hamas was a proscribed terrorist organisation and the government condemned "those who choose to support its activities and further its aims".
