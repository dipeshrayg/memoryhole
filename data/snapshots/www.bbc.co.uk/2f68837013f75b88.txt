People on prepayment meters will be hit as their spending comes when energy is needed, rather than the cost being spread over the year as with direct debits.
What is the price cap?
The cap sets a maximum price for each unit of energy, not the total bill. Those on fixed deals are unaffected until the end of their term, but for those on variable tariffs it means:
- For electricity, average unit prices will go up on 1 January from 26.32p per kilowatt-hour to 32.69p. The electricity price is often pegged to gas, owing to gas-fired generation.
- Gas will increase from 7.97p per kilowatt-hour to 10.96p, higher than in the wake of the invasion of Ukraine.
- Daily standing charges will be 54.77p for electricity and 29.65p for gas.
The cap is set every three months, based on the costs faced by suppliers as well as the cost of running the energy system.
It is based on a three-month period that ends about six weeks before the new cap comes into force. A fall in international wholesale prices in recent days will not be reflected in January's cap, but it will be in April's.
