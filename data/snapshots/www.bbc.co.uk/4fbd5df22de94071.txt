Over-70s will have to take eye tests every three years or face losing their driving licence under new road safety plans.
Mandatory eyesight tests are part of the Department for Transport's (DfT) road safety strategy, expected within weeks.
Around 6.5 million over-70s hold drivers' licences, with 1.6 million of those aged 80 or over.
To drive legally in the UK, you must be able to read a standard number plate from 20 metres away, wearing glasses or contact lenses if required.
Government sources told the BBC ministers will recommend over-70s be made to regularly prove their eyesight meets the minimum standards. Those who do not, or who fail to meet the minimum standard face losing their licence.
The change follows a consultation which launched early last year, set to recommend a shake-up of the rules for older drivers.
Under the current rules, over 70s have to renew their licence every three years, but only have to self-certify that their sight.
Dennis Reed, of campaign group Silver Voices, which represents older people, said it was "discriminatory to just pick an age out of the hat and say 'everybody over 70 has to go through this, but nobody else does'".
But he said the group is not opposed in principle to regular eye tests for older drivers.
The changes being considered were first reported by the Daily Mail, external.
The NHS provides free eye tests for over 60s, but they are not detailed enough to approve someone to drive.
The BBC understands this system is also being looked at, with the free tests likely to be expanded to become more rigorous.
A Government source said "beefed up measures" were long overdue.
"One life lost on the roads is one too many," they added. "There have been several arguably preventable fatalities on Britain's roads caused by drivers with failing eyesight in recent years.
The DfT said no decision has officially been made on mandating eyesight tests for drivers aged 70 and over.
A spokesperson said: "We recognise the importance of driving for older people's independence but need to balance this with our priority to keep everyone safe on our roads and avoid tragic accidents.
"Mandatory eye tests for drivers over 70 as part of our Road Safety Strategy are one way to do this.
"We are carefully reviewing consultation results," they added. "Older drivers who are fit to drive and have good enough vision have nothing to worry about."
