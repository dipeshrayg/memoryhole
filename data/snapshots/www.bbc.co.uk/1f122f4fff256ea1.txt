Third one-day international, Hobart
England 328 (48.3 overs): Duckett 133, Jacks 59; Edwards 4-31
Australia 231 (46.5 overs): Renshaw 55; Baker 4-45
England won by 97 runs
England confidently swept aside Australia in the third one-day international in Hobart to seal a statement series win.
At times the series decider felt inconsequential - the latest seismic turn in Australia's sandpaper scandal taking all attention during Australia's chase - but Harry Brook's side won by 97 runs in an important step as they build towards next year's World Cup.
Even when failing to fully take advantage of a fast start, England reached 328 before being bowled out in the penultimate over, with Ben Duckett making a fine 133 from 115 balls.
Duckett's century, after all of his struggles in last winter's Ashes, was his first for England on Australian soil. A 48-ball 59 from Will Jacks ensured his innings was not wasted as Australia's spinners slowed scoring through the middle.
Just as impressive was another exciting effort from England's new-ball pair Gus Atkinson and Sonny Baker.
Atkinson dismissed Travis Head for the third time in a row in the first over - something to be noted before next year's Ashes series. He also hit Cameron Green with a brutal bouncer that forced the all-rounder to retire hurt.
Baker had Australia captain Mitchell Marsh caught at second slip in the second over, soon added the wicket of Josh Inglis and also dismissed Green's concussion replacement Oliver Peake en route to figures of 4-45.
It helped England calmly close out the win and, after being dismissed for 131 in the series opener, their recovery is noteworthy.
Australia, meanwhile, have questions to ponder on and off the field - many of which are unlikely to be answered before a five-match T20 series begins between these sides on Saturday.
Baker a huge positive for England
On the field, Australia can at least point to the fact key bowlers â most notably under-fire captain Pat Cummins, Mitchell Starc and Josh Hazlewood â have been rested for this series as a caveat for their defeat.
On the evidence of this series, England, though inconsistent, look the more settled side.
England had taken strides in ODI cricket this year, beating Sri Lanka (twice) and India, only to be checked by their horror defeats in Pakistan last month. This corrects that course.
Duckett was impressive again but the most significant learning from this series may be the emergence of Baker in partnership with Atkinson.
The 23-year-old Hampshire quick was brought in after defeat in the first ODI and has added real threat with the new ball in his first meeting with Australia.
Bowling has been England's weaker suit in recent ODIs but, given another good start, Liam Dawson was again able to ease through his overs of left-arm spin, bowling Cooper Connolly, having Jack Edwards caught at short fine leg and taking Matt Renshaw caught and bowled with his second ball.
Dropped chances â Baker and Brook put down Renshaw in his 55 and Tom Banton put down a sitter late on â will frustrate, given Brook and the management have spoken of a wish of becoming the best fielding side in the world.
But, after poor run in ODIs which included a failed defence of the World Cup in 2023 and the dismal showing at last year's Champions Trophy, England's 50-over team ends in a much better place than it started.
By having Spencer Johnson caught, Brook even nipped in with the final wicket of the match - his first in ODIs.

Duckett breaks Australian duck
Duckett, whose difficult Ashes tour last winter included videos of him apparently drunk circulating on social media, averaged 18.33 across 12 previous innings in Australia before this match. His highest score was 42.
In Hobart, he found a pitch more to his liking â less seam movement, pace or bounce â and capitalised to score a fourth ODI century in a year in which he has switched between feast and famine.
After he and Tom Banton thrashed 73 in the first eight overs, Duckett took responsibility as others fell to Australia's part-time spinners.
Joe Root was bowled by Renshaw for 12, Brook hit Connolly to deep mid-wicket for 17 and Jordan Cox was stumped off Renshaw. Even Jofra Archer was pushed up to number six as a pinch-hitter, only to hole out for a run-a-ball seven off Travis Head.
Throughout it all, Duckett continued. Having driven the quicks early, he slog-swept the spinners until a top edge playing that shot became his downfall in the 39th over.
England's innings fell away slightly but Jacks â once again showing his value â ensured they got a good score.
Duckett's year has been peculiar â England's only Test centurion in their home summer while averaging 26.36 â but this hundred, which follows back-to-back tons on the flat pitches in Pakistan during the recent tri-series, secures his 50-over place going forward once and for all.
England are next in action in this format in January â a timely three-match series against tournament co-hosts South Africa during which they can test their progress in conditions they will face during the World Cup.
