Alan Milburn has said the country needs a "complete system reset" to bring down the number of young people not in work or education - known as "Neets".
In a major review, published on Tuesday,, external he suggested a raft of proposals to address the problem, which mostly cover England but he says can be adapted in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland too.
Each of his recommendations will need to be accepted by the government and implemented and paid for before they become reality - and that could take some time. So how might today's proposals affect you if you are Neet?
A new Youth Opportunity Service
At the heart of the recommendations, Milburn says, is a new Youth Opportunity Service. He wants this to be implemented locally, with each area drawing up a plan to help their own young people.
It would bring together employment, careers, health and practical support, with each young person getting a named adviser and a personalised plan.
Milburn wants areas to begin building their Youth Opportunity Service from 2027, with one in place everywhere by the end of 2029.
Within 14 days of entering the service, a young person would then meet with a named adviser. Within four weeks, they would have their "personalised participation plan", setting out what both the support services and the young person involved would do, and when.
Jobs wise, Milburn wants there to be initiatives like work placements of up to 12 weeks, structured volunteering opportunities, and help to create businesses.
He also wants those who remain Neet - despite enagaging with support - to be provided with a paid six-month job, funded by the government.
The government has already committed to a legal guarantee of job support in theory, but the exact workings still need to be worked out.
More apprenticeships and college places
Alongside more work opportunities, Milburn wants a "major expansion" in further education for 18- to 24 year-olds, as well as more apprenticeship places.
Over the next decade, he wants the number of young people completing an apprenticeship to double, and for apprenticeships to rival university as an option for school leavers.
Colleges should also get new funding for participation and work-readiness, the review says, initially targeted at 16- and 17-year-olds who are Neet or at serious risk of becoming so.
He also wants the government to develop new industry-led technical colleges and specialist campuses.
If the reforms are enacted, Milburn says young people should have better access to high-quality further education and apprenticeships by 2028.
Under the proposals, schools, colleges and universities would be judged by whether pupils make successful transitions into further learning and work, rather than just academic achievements.
By 2036, he wants 35% of each year group to have started a high-quality apprenticeship by age 25.
More mental health support
Milburn has said he wants a fundamental transformation of how mental health support for young people is delivered.
Young people who are Neet should be guaranteed fast-track access to services within three months, according to his recommendations.
This would include things like vocational rehabilitation, talking therapies and counselling as part of a young person's plan with their local Youth Opportunity Service - which would be rolled out between 2027 and 2029 under the plans.
There has been a big rise in mental distress among children and young people in recent years, and Milburn's report comes at a time the NHS is facing huge pressures.
Milburn wants mental health support to be available for all young people digitally, without needing a diagnosis.
Some areas are already using these approaches, but scaling them up would be a major challenge and would take a long time to introduce across the board.
Plus, Milburn wants "fitness to work" notes to be taken out of the hands of GPs and put into community work and health teams, and for them to be "a trigger for help - not simply a certificate of absence".
Milburn wants these changes to begin in 2027 and would be linked to the Youth Opportunity Service rollout, which would be complete by 2029 under the plans.
More incentives for employers to hire young people
Milburn has said he wants companies to be incentivised to employ more young people, with the report highlighting a competitive jobs market and applicants who often fail to hear back from potential employers.
The report includes three asks of employers: to create more genuine first jobs, more first-job apprenticeships and much wider access to meaningful work experience.
Plus, Milburn wants large employers to find ways of responding to rejected job applicants.
These changes should be felt by both employers and the young people looking for jobs by 2029, the report says.
