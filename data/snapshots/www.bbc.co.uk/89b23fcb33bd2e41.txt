Great Britain advanced to the semi-finals of the Davis Cup for the first time since 2019 as Henry Patten and Neal Skupski won a decisive doubles tie against third seeds Germany in Bologna.
Patten and Skupski, playing together for the first time since announcing their doubles partnership for the 2027 season, proved too clinical for Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz.
They converted two break points and saved five in the first set, before overturning a mini-break in the tie-break to win 6-2 7-6 (8-6).
Skupski was involved in a Davis Cup quarter-final loss playing alongside Joe Salisbury against the same German doubles pair in December 2021 - a defeat that was in his mind as Thursday's decider progressed.
"I thought about that match during the match today and I was determined not to let that happen again," Skupski told BBC Radio 5 Live.
"No matter what was happening today, we weren't going to come off the court very easily with a loss."
World number one Alexander Zverev had earlier sent the tie to its full distance by defeating British number one Arthur Fery in straight sets to cancel out Cameron Norrie's three-set victory over Jan-Lennard Struff.
Great Britain's semi-final is scheduled for Saturday, when they will face Spain.
Spaniards Daniel Merida and Rafael Jodar won their singles matches, meaning the team did not need to play doubles, allowing Carlos Alcaraz to rest.
Captain David Ferrer said Alcaraz may be in contention to play in the singles against Great Britain.
Defending champions Italy will play the Czech Republic in Friday's first semi-final.
Smith will step down as captain at the end of the tournament and Great Britain retain hopes of a second victory under his leadership, following the most recent of their 10 Davis Cup successes in 2015.
"I felt the same sort of pressure, nerves and butterflies beforehand and during the matches," said Smith, who has been captain since 2010.
"You sit for a long time in that chair on your own on a day like this, a lot of thoughts go through your head and one of the things I kept reminding myself is how much of a privilege it is to be in this situation.
"I loved it but, more importantly, I'm thrilled for the guys. They've committed so well to this, they've been to a few different places over the past 18 months to get to this opportunity and I'm delighted they've got to the final four now."
Norrie wins but defeat for Fery
World number 52 Norrie had got Great Britain off to the perfect start with a three-set win against Struff, a player 13 places above him in the world rankings.
The left-hander raced through the first set in just 23 minutes but a break late in the second set by Struff forced a decider.
The key moment came at 2-2 in the final set when Norrie saved four break points to hold serve, after which he won 12 of the next 14 points to complete a 6-3 3-6 6-2 victory.
Norrie's success meant a win for Fery against Zverev, a two-time major champion in 2026, would have been enough to secure Great Britain's passage through to the last four.
However, as he did in their Wimbledon semi-final in July, Zverev proved too strong for 24-year-old Fery and ended as a 6-3 6-2 winner.
Patten and Skupski have found themselves unusually in the spotlight over the past week following the former's "ruthless" split from Harri Heliovaara in favour of joining forces with his compatriot next season.
While their partnership is in its infancy, there were few signs of teething problems as they extended their Davis Cup record as a pair to 3-0.
Patten's pinpoint returns and sharp reactions at the net coupled with Skupski's impressive serving proved too strong for Krawietz and Puetz, who were left to rue missed opportunities.
In a sticky opening service game, Patten double-faulted on the opening point and faced a break point, while Skupski saved four in game seven of the opening set.
Neither pair earned a break point in the second set, with the British duo dropping just five points on serve.
Trailing 3-1 in what turned out to be a thrilling tie-break, they fought back to lead 6-5 before closing out the win at the second opportunity.
"It doesn't feel like the end of the season for those two because they've just paired up," Smith said. "There's been a buoyancy in their practice sessions because it's a new start.
"They were brilliant today. You don't know if you're going to get a match or not [as doubles players] but I saw them 20 minutes before their match and you could just see they were delighted to get this opportunity and that gave them a huge lift."
