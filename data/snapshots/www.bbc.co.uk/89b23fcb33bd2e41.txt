Great Britain advanced to the semi-finals of the Davis Cup for the first time since 2019 as Henry Patten and Neal Skupski won a decisive doubles tie against third seeds Germany in Bologna.
Patten and Skupski, playing together for the first time since announcing their doubles partnership for the 2027 season, proved too clinical for Kevin Krawietz and Tim Puetz.
They converted two break points and saved five in the first set, before overturning a mini-break in the tie-break to win 6-2 7-6 (8-6).
World number one Alexander Zverev had earlier sent the tie to a decider by defeating British number one Arthur Fery in straight sets to cancel out Cameron Norrie's three-set victory over Jan-Lennard Struff.
A nerveless Norrie saved four break points in the fifth game of the third set and carried that momentum throughout the remainder of the match to win 6-3 3-6 6-2.
Great Britain's semi-final is scheduled for Saturday and Leon Smith's team will discover their next opponents later on Thursday when Spain meet Austria in the last of the four quarter-finals.
Smith will step down as Great Britain captain at the end of the tournament and his side retain hopes of a second victory under his leadership, following the most recent of their 10 Davis Cup successes in 2015.
Defending champions Italy will play Czech Republic in Friday's first semi-final.
World number 52 Norrie had got Great Britain off to the perfect start with a three-set win against Struff, a player 13 places above him in the world rankings.
The left-hander raced through the first set in just 23 minutes but a break late in the second set by Struff forced a decider.
The key moment came at 2-2 in the final set when Norrie saved four break points to hold serve, after which he won 12 of the next 14 points to complete victory.
Norrie's success meant a win for Fery against Zverev, a two-time major champion in 2026, would have been enough to secure Great Britain's passage through to the last four.
However, as he did in their Wimbledon semi-final in July, Zverev proved too strong for 24-year-old Fery and was a 6-3 6-2 winner.
Patten and Skupski have found themselves unusually in the spotlight over the past week following the former's "ruthless" split from Harri Heliovaara in favour of joining forces with his compatriot next season.
While their partnership is in its infancy, there were few signs of teething problems as they extended their Davis Cup record as a pair to 3-0.
Patten's pinpoint returns and sharp reactions at the net coupled with Skupski's impressive serving proved too strong for Krawietz and Puetz, who were left to rue missed opportunities.
In a sticky opening service game, Patten double-faulted on the opening point and faced a break point, while Skupski saved four in game seven of the opening set.
Neither pair earned a break point in the second set, with the British duo dropping just five points on serve.
Trailing 3-1 in what turned out to be a thrilling tie-break, they fought back to lead 6-5 before closing out the win at the second opportunity.
