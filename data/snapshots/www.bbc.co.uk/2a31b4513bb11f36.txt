Travel disruption and power cuts are expected as Storm Austen hits Wales.
The Met Office said the storm, the first named one of the autumn, will bring a spell of strong winds up to 70mph (112km/h) overnight on Thursday and into Friday, mainly in coastal areas.
Two yellow warnings, external for Wales are in place for Friday, with periods of heavy rain also expected. The Met Office said injuries and danger to life from flying debris were possible.
Some roads and bridges may close, damage to buildings could happen, and longer journey times and cancellations to public transport are possible.

The Royal National Lifeboat Institution (RNLI) has urged anyone visiting the coast in south Wales to "to stay vigilant and not take unnecessary risks".
Gusts of wind of about 50 to 60mph are likely but some coastal or exposed areas could see gusts of about 70mph, the Met Office said, adding winds would slowly ease during Friday afternoon.
Ross Macleod, RNLI water safety manager, said: "If you plan to visit the coast or a waterside location, we urge you stay a safe distance from the water as conditions could knock you off your feet or wash you into the sea."
MacLeod said anyone in trouble in the water should "lie back in the water with your head so far back that your ears are wet, extend your arms and legs and try to relax as best as you can until you get control of your breathing before then calling for help or swimming to safety".
He added if anyone sees someone in danger, throw them something buoyant into the water and call 999 or 112 and ask for the coastguard.
Network Rail urged passengers to check their journey plans before setting out.
Transport for Wales' customer operations chief, James Gough, said: "Amended timetables will be in place on a number of routes."
