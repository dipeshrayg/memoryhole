A former Labour MP has said the Conservatives should not allow a former neo-Nazi who was jailed for antisemitic abuse against her to stand as a local election candidate.
Joshua Bonehill-Paine was convicted of racially harassing Luciana Berger, who is Jewish, in 2016, while serving time in jail for sharing antisemitic posts.
The Conservatives have picked him as a councillor candidate in Somerset and party leader Kemi Badenoch has defended the decision, saying she believed he had been rehabilitated and wanted to give him a second chance.
But Baroness Berger told the BBC Bonehill-Paine engaged in a "sustained campaign" against her that should rule him out of standing for public office.
Baroness Berger said Bonehill-Paine's harassment "affected my life in lots of different ways because of the volume of the material that he was disseminating and putting out, the extremity of it in terms of just frankly how shocking and awful it was".
The Labour peer added: "I think that we need to have a bar when it comes to standing for public office.
"I strongly believe that this individual today falls very, very short of that."
Bonehill-Paine spent three years in prison after he was jailed in 2015 for posting antisemitic material ahead of a planned neo-Nazi rally.
A year later in 2016, he was convicted of racially harassing then-Labour MP Berger, who is now a member of the House of Lords.
Bonehill-Paine has described his past views as "indefensible" and now speaks out against extremism.
Speaking to the BBC, Badenoch said that while Bonehill-Paine's crimes were "appalling", she felt she had served his sentence, showed he was "more than contrite" and was now committed to tackling antisemitism.
Badenoch said: "I am extremely worried about antisemitism and what I feel is that if we can't have the people who have previously been radicalised, who understand what went wrong and are part of the fight against antisemitism now, if we're saying: 'No, no, no, you're untouchable,' then how can we pretend that we believe in rehabilitation?
"I'm someone who's quite sceptical about rehabilitation and I see this man as somebody who has been rehabilitated."
The Board of Deputies of British Jews has called on the Conservatives to ditch him as a candidate, saying the selection "beggars belief".
It is understood the party has now offered the Board, as well as the Jewish Leadership Council and the Chief Rabbi Sir Ephraim Mirvis, a meeting with Bonehill-Paine so they can discuss their concerns directly.
When Bonehill-Paine's selection was announced, Karen Newman, vice president of the Board of Deputies of British Jews - the largest body representing Jews in the UK - said that the board had contacted the Conservatives to "set out our concerns and call on them to rescind his selection and review their vetting processes."
Bonehill-Paine also holds previous convictions for assaulting a police officer, burglary of a police station and malicious communications.
On Tuesday, he was selected to stand as the Tory candidate in the new Somerset Council seat of Crewkerne South in 2027. He said he wanted to "give back" to a community which had supported him.
Bonehill-Paine said he reformed his previous views when he learned of the murder of MP Jo Cox in 2016, and subsequently undertook a de-radicalisation programme. He now delivers counter-extremism sessions around the UK.
He said: "When I was first released from prison in 2018, I was supported by many of the people in my local community who understood that I had made some awful mistakes but was wanting to change my life around."
The decision to field Bonehill-Paine as a candidate has also been criticised by other political parties.
Liberal Democrat MP Adam Dance, whose Yeovil constituency includes Crewkerne, said the Conservative Party needed to explain "why it believes this candidate is fit to serve", while a Labour Party spokesperson said his selection was "a total disgrace".
