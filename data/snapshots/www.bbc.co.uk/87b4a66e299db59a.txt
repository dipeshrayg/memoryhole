Relationships and regulations
Chelsea co-owner Behdad Eghbali and Villa owner Nassef Sawiris have developed a close working relationship and have communicated directly over transfer business.
That relationship has spanned several transfer windows and included Rogers' move to Stamford Bridge this summer.
Chelsea did not have a clear run at Rogers, though - as Arsenal also wanted to sign him, and it was the player's decision to move to Stamford Bridge.
Uefa has the power to scrutinise transfers completed between the same clubs within a 45-day period. Garnacho's move to Villa - on an initial loan deal - came two days after Rogers signed for Chelsea. Jackson's permanent transfer went through 38 days after the Rogers one.
But a timeline on its own is not evidence that the deals were in any way related.
Villa, for instance, could point to their long-held interest in Jackson, who previously worked with head coach Unai Emery at Villarreal.
Chelsea, meanwhile, could argue their interest in Martinez was independent of previous dealings with Villa and would have been pursued regardless of the club he played for. The goalkeeper had attracted interest from Juventus earlier in the window, with a finger injury understood to have scuppered a move.
Why do Premier League clubs buy from each other?
There are broader reasons why domestic transfers remain attractive, but money is often the biggest factor.
Premier League clubs generally have greater spending power than most teams elsewhere in Europe, meaning another English side may be among the few buyers capable of meeting the fee and wage demands of a selling club.
That can make domestic deals particularly attractive when clubs are trying to move on high-value players. Signing from within the league also reduces some of the uncertainty, with recruitment teams already familiar with a player's qualities and performances.
Yet even in a league where domestic trading is commonplace, the frequency of deals between Villa and Chelsea stands out.
