Anthropic's share price has dropped back towards its initial public offering (IPO) price, after a soaring market debut, as the broader stock market continues to fall.
The artificial intelligence (AI) company's debut on the Nasdaq stock exchange on Thursday was a crucial test for the industry, and was largely seen as a success with trading starting at $111.50.
However, Anthropic's shares opened trading on Friday down 8% at about $88, close to its IPO price of $83, and the broader stock market falls seen on Thursday continued.
The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite Index opening down 1.3%. The S&P 500 also opened down 0.4% while the Dow Jones Industrial Average opened largely flat.
The maker of the Claude chatbot raised a record $81bn in its initial public offering (IPO), but its share price moved significantly in the first day of trading.
The shares peaked at $115 on Thursday, before easing to end the day up 17% at $97.17.
Analysts said volatility was to be expected in the first days or even weeks of Anthropic's trading, as the company was the first frontier AI firm to go public so had no direct peers.
Some have also noted the similarities with Elon Musk's SpaceX, which at the time of its debut in June was the largest IPO on record. Shares in the rocket and AI company experienced large swings in its first few weeks of trading.
"There's going to be some volatility like we had with SpaceX," Rob Hackel, chief executive of financial advisory and brokerage firm RF Lafferty & Co told Reuters.
"A lot of people are going to have FOMO [Fear Of Missing Out]. I don't think there's any need to get on it on the first trade."
Early trading for the largest US tech companies was mixed. Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon began the day in positive territory after falls on Thursday.
But Tesla shares opened down 0.3%, Meta was down 1.4% and Nvidia had fallen 2.9%. SpaceX continued its large falls, down nearly 4% in the first half hour of trading.
Europe's main stock markets - which contain fewer tech stocks - were also trading lower on Friday. Germany's Dax index fell 2% and the UK's FTSE 100 was 0.5% lower.
Earlier on Friday some Asian markets had seen big falls, with South Korea's Kospi and Japan's Nikkei 225 indexes both down about 5%.
