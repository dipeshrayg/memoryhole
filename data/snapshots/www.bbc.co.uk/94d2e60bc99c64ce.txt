More than 350 flights in the US have been cancelled so far on Thursday as bad weather coincides with busy travel on the Thanksgiving holiday.
Another 700 flights into, out of, or within the US have been delayed, tracking site FlightAware said.
On Wednesday, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) briefly grounded all flights heading for Miami International Airport after a FedEx aircraft overran a runway. No injuries were reported, and the FAA is investigating.
More than 80 million Americans were expected to be traveling this week at the same time the National Weather Service (NWS) has forecast storms along the western and eastern coasts of the US that could bring heavy rain and flooding.
The FAA previously warned that delays across the country would be possible "throughout the week" as a result of the rain and wind that are forecast. On Wednesday, FlightAware reported more than 6,800 delays and more than 160 cancellations.
The NWS forecast a strong storm will deluge the eastern US coast on Thursday, bringing high winds, heavy rainfall and major coastal flooding.
Ahead of expected severe weather in New Jersey, the state's governor, Mikie Sherrill, declared a state of emergency. Several coastal areas that experienced flooding earlier this autumn could be vulnerable again on Thursday.
In New York City, organisers of the annual Macy's Thanksgiving parade have postponed the event to Saturday.
Across the US, severe weather is also descending on the West Coast. The NWS has warned of a series of Pacific storms that are expected to bring rain, mountain snow and strong winds.
Many are bracing for potential flooding as king tides peak along the coast.
Water levels along California's vast coastline could reach over 8ft (2.4 metres) in some areas on Thursday, according to the NWS, breaking records set just last month.
Storms are also forecast for the Pacific Northwest, including the Canadian province of British Columbia.
In Miami, some travel delays persist after the FedEx plane incident prompted the closure of all but one runway at the airport for a brief period on Wednesday evening.
A statement FedEx provided to US media confirmed that one of its flights had "experienced an issue upon landing".
It added: "We are grateful that our crew is safe and there are no reported injuries. We are coordinating closely with investigating authorities, and any additional questions should be referred to the FAA."
An airport employee who spoke anonymously to CBS News, the BBC's US partner, raised the possibility that rainy conditions had been a factor.
"They tried as hard as they could to stop, but I don't know if it was the weather because the rain and the wind were really bad, and they just couldn't stop in time and went right off the runway and veered off into the grass," they said.
Wednesday's incident in Miami came 11 weeks after a cargo plane crashed on landing at the same airport, killing five people and injuring five others in vehicles on the ground with which it collided.
The airport is among the top five domestic destinations for travellers in the country over Thanksgiving, according to the American Automobile Association (AAA), which predicted that 81.5 million Americans will travel at least 50 miles (80km) from home for the Thanksgiving holiday over a seven-day period.
