England have been boosted by the recovery and return of George Furbank, Seb Atkinson and Ollie Chessum to their starting line-up to face New Zealand on Saturday in the Nations Championship.
The trio, who all missed last weekend's win over Japan with knocks, are joined in the starting line-up by Will Joseph, who crossed for five tries against the Brave Blossoms.
Furbank held off the challenge of Freddie Steward, who impressed against Japan, to regain the full-back slot, while vice-captain Chessum brings ballast to a back row that is completed by Ben Curry and Ben Earl.
Fin Smith returns at fly-half to reprise a 10-12 combination with Atkinson that has started six of England's last seven Tests and dates back to their days as teenagers in Worcester Warriors academy.
Barring Joseph's inclusion for the injured Immanuel Feyi-Waboso, England have named the same matchday 23 which combined excellently in the 45-12 victory over Australia a fortnight ago.
After last year's 33-19 success, England are aiming for successive wins over the All Blacks for the first time since beating them in November 2002 and June 2003 - five months before Rugby World Cup glory in Australia.
"There are few bigger occasions in international rugby than facing New Zealand," said head coach Steve Borthwick.
"It's a fixture that always carries a special significance, and one that the players are hugely looking forward to.
"We know the quality New Zealand have in their team. We've prepared for the very best version of them, knowing the challenge they will bring and the level we'll need to reach to get the result we want."
New Zealand, meanwhile, have also been bolstered by the return of big names.

Following their shock defeat by Wales in Cardiff last weekend, Jordie Barrett, Damian McKenzie and Cam Roigard are brought back in, while wing Will Jordan - his country's leading all-time try-scorer - has recovered from a hamstring issue to start.
Full-back Beauden Barrett, who skippered the side for the first time in Cardiff, will win his 150th Test cap, becoming only the fourth men's player - alongside Alun Wyn Jones, James Slipper and Sam Whitelock - to achieve the landmark.
Beauden and Jordie Barrett's brother Scott is in the second row, while Codie Taylor captains from hooker.
New Zealand lead the Nations Championship southern hemisphere standings by one point and any sort of victory over England is likely to be enough to book their place in the headline fixture of next weekend's play-off finals.
France, who lead the northern hemisphere table and play Argentina later on Saturday, would be their likeliest opponents.
A victory for England, however, could mean Borthwick's men forcing their way into the title play-off, while Ireland and South Africa, who meet in Dublin at 16:40 GMT, could also have a say in an intriguing finale to the group stage.
