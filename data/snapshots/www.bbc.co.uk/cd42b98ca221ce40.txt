Sportspeople are faced with a question when injuries hit again and again. Do I try one more time or should I walk away?
England's Katie George came really close to choosing the latter option last year.
"I had a couple of conversations with my close family and partner about whether it was taking too much out of me and whether I was happy with the person I was," she says. "I was pretty miserable."
George made her England debut in 2018 aged 18 as a tearaway fast bowler. She was quick. Some coaches believed generationally so.
She played seven internationals between March and July before the injuries first hit that summer. George is matter of fact about how many have followed. The list is enough to make the rest of us wince.
"Nine stress fractures in my back," she says. "I tore the cartilage off my rib.
"Bits and bobs. Ankle surgery. I have missed a lot of cricket."
Unable to bowl because of her back and little more than a tailender with the bat, George got into sides purely for her athleticism and a penchant for stunning catches. She was occasionally referred to as a 'specialist fielder'.
The label was meant as a compliment, but it still stung. She says she felt like an "empty uniform".
"For a long time I was worried about being the [injury prone ex-Liverpool and England striker] Daniel Sturridge - what could have been, all of that talent wasted," says George, who played football for Portsmouth in her youth.
"I found that really difficult. I didn't want to be that person.
"Over a period of time you get told you are really resilient and you have been through so much. Then when it keeps happening there is pressure and it gets harder.
"You need to look brave and be resilient and show less emotion than you are feeling. Anyone knows when you bottle that up it can get quite tricky."
Then came George's end-of-year appraisal at Warwickshire - that uncomfortable question. Quit or change something. George opted the latter.
Alongside coach Ali Maiden, an unlikely plan was hatched. George had more ducks than fifties at that point in her professional career but why not try and build on flashes of potential. Why not try and become a frontline batter?
Even George did not really believe.
"I always knew I was half decent at bowling but I never had that feeling for batting," she says.
"Right the way through age groups and my first fifty at Western Storm, there were moments where in a game it would come off but I would have no idea how to replicate it.
"I would always put it down to a fluke."
The turnaround has been extraordinary. Batting most regularly at number three, George topped the average tables for this season's One-Day Cup with a figure of 77.55.
A 98 against Blaze in April was followed by a first professional century against Lancashire in June. An unbeaten ton against Durham rounded off the campaign. She even opened in the T20 Blast.
"It is crazy it happened but there was a lot of hard work and I am very grateful to Ali," she says.
"He pushed me and challenged me as to why couldn't I change the narrative a little bit. I want to impact games.
"I don't want to sit on the bench. I can do that from my sofa."
As a result, George will travel to India for an England A tour that begins later this month.
She is there alongside youngsters tipped for bright futures but also established names like Lauren Filer and Tilly Corteen-Colman. Her partner Issy Wong was named in the squads but has withdrawn because of injury.
"We were doing a team thing a couple of weeks ago and we were asked what we are most proud of," George says.
"Until the last few months it probably would have been my England debut but, once I thought about it, it was actually being stood in that team room as a batter and wearing this kit again.
"If you said I would be here as a batter in England kit I would definitely have laughed and said you were smoking something."
Given her past, George would be wise not to look too far into the future. She has not given up on bowling again regularly, nor is she ready to listen to advice to stop.
"It would fall on deaf ears," she says.
More immediately, England coach Charlotte Edwards, selector Lydia Greenway and England's new managing director of women's cricket Leah Poulton will all be in India for the upcoming tour, providing George the chance to impress.
"My dream would be to play a red-ball game for England," she says. "That is what I set out for do.
"It is what I watched as a kid with my dad. I vividly remember my first ever Test match watching Jimmy Anderson running in and bowling.
"Whether I am able to, I don't know. Maybe it will be as a batter - that would be the biggest plot twist ever."
