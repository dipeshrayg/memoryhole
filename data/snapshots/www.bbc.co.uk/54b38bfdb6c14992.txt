The Premier League's Key Match Incidents Panel has ruled there were six on-field errors in the last round of games - the joint-highest it has recorded in one weekend.
Each week, the five members of the panel assess all the contentious incidents and vote whether both the referee and the video assistant referee (VAR) have got the decisions correct.
You have to go back to the early part of the 2023-24 season to find the last time there were six mistakes logged.
The statistic does not include correct VAR interventions to fix errors, such as the red card shown to Brentford's Igor Thiago.
In the last round of games almost every fixture had a contentious refereeing situation which was hotly debated.
It started on Friday with a potential penalty to Coventry City for a hair pull by Everton's James Tarkowski.
Arsenal were denied a penalty following a VAR review after it was deemed Kai Havertz was offside.
Tottenham lodged a complaint over a challenge by Leeds United's Jayden Bogle, who was not shown a red card.
And Liverpool argued against a late Crystal Palace penalty.
But which decisions did the KMI Panel rule were incorrect? Some of the votes might surprise you.
Two decisions were on-field and VAR errors
Such was the controversy across the weekend, it may come as a surprise that the KMI Panel ruled only two were missed VAR interventions. There have been six missed overall, the same number as this stage last season.
Incident 1: Coventry's Caleb Yirenkyi appeared to have his hair pulled inside the area by Everton's James Tarkowski. No penalty was awarded.
KMI Panel vote: 5:0 incorrect, for the on-field decision and the VAR.
What the KMI Panel said: "Tarkowski had a hold of Yirenkyi's hair and the panel unanimously felt that VAR should have intervened."
Incident 2: Arsenal's Kai Havertz was pulled down inside the area by Hull City's Nobel Mendy. The VAR, Michael Oliver, sent referee Farai Hallam to the pitchside monitor but it was not given due to an offside offence by the Germany international.
KMI Panel vote: 5:0 incorrect, for the on-field decision and the VAR.
What the KMI Panel said: "Although Harvertz is clearly in an offside position, there is a clear and impactful holding action by Mendy before any potential offside offence."
Four decisions were on-field errors, but not VAR mistakes
When the KMI Panel sits down to vote there are two separate considerations.
The first is on the decision of the referee, which is a basic judgement in law and the Premier League's expected interpretation.
The second is on the potential VAR intervention. This takes into account what the referee has said, the available replay angles and the high bar for intervention.
Incident 1: Thierno Barry put Everton in front against Coventry in the 61st minute, but there was a VAR check for a potential handball.
KMI Panel vote: 2:3 incorrect on the field, but 5:0 no VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "On the balance of probability the ball did accidentally strike Barry's hand. However, the readily available footage did not provide conclusive evidence."
Incident 2: Crystal Palace were awarded a penalty against Liverpool three minutes into stoppage time for a foul by Milos Kerkez on Dario Osorio. But was it just a clash of heads?
KMI Panel vote: 0:5 incorrect on the field, but 5:0 no VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "A penalty was incorrect with the contact being inevitable. However, it was not a clear and obvious error."
Incident 3: Ipswich were awarded a 72nd-minute penalty against Bournemouth for a foul by Lewis Cook on Abdoul Ouattara.
KMI Panel vote: 1:4 incorrect on the field, but 4:1 no VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "Ouattara made a clear step across Cook who didn't make a challenge and the referee's decision to award a penalty was incorrect. However, it was not a clear and obvious error."
Incident 4: Potential second yellow card for Bournemouth's Marcus Tavernier.
KMI Panel vote: 2:3 incorrect on the field; no VAR intervention possible.
What the KMI Panel said: "There was a clear, deliberate action by Tavernier to delay the restart."
The controversial decisions deemed to be correct
Some of the most high-profile decisions from across the weekend have not yet featured.
That is because they have been voted to be correct on the field and through VAR.
Incident 1: Leeds United's Jayden Bogle caught Tottenham's Mathys Tel on high on his ankle. There was no VAR review for a red card.
KMI Panel vote: 3:2 correct on the field and for the VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "The majority felt Bogle was looking to challenge with his right leg and his left leg came down on the back of Tel's ankle without the speed, force or intensity to be serious foul play. Two panellists felt that the challenge was sufficiently dangerous to merit a red card."
Incident 2: Chelsea's Cole Palmer went down on the edge of the area under a challenge by Sunderland's Luke O'Nien.
KMI Panel vote: 4:1 correct on the field and for the VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "Clear contact made by O'Nien on Palmer which the panel felt should have been penalised with a free-kick on the basis that contact was made outside the area. One panellist felt that the foul contact was inside the area."
Incident 3: Sunderland were awarded a penalty when Levi Colwill was ruled to have tripped Brian Bobbey.
KMI Panel vote: 5:0 correct on the field and for the VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "There was upper body holding from both players as they progressed into the penalty area before a careless trip by the Chelsea defender."
Incident 4: Possible foul by Senne Lammens on Aston Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki before Patrick Dorgu's 96th-minute equaliser.
KMI Panel vote: 5:0 correct on the field and for the VAR intervention.
What the KMI Panel said: "Although Lammens turned away from the ball, the contact he made on Suzuki was not impactful."
What does this tell us about VAR?
The KMI Panel was scrapped this summer in the Scottish Premiership because clubs became frustrated with the voting patterns of its members.
But it is more important in the Premier League, with the results creating a merit table which contributes to 50% of referees' salaries in bonus payments.
Many fans will feel there should have been more than two VAR errors logged in the last round of games.
Yet the KMI Panel does not exist to mirror the opinion of supporters.
Crucially, it uses the laws of the game and how the Premier League expects its games to be refereed as the barometer.
Still, six on-field mistakes - plus the Thiago red card - is clearly a high number.
And fans struggle to understand why it seems to be OK to have these on-field errors, and not correct them due to the high bar.
It all adds to the frustration of VAR.
