The Las Vegas Grand Prix will start with two drivers on the front row who dislike each other - and after qualifying one said of the other that "somebody has got to stand up to that guy".
"That guy" is Max Verstappen, who starts his Red Bull from pole position. The driver criticising him is Mercedes' George Russell, who lines up second.
Both were promoted after the fastest qualifier, new world champion Kimi Antonelli, was demoted by a five-place grid penalty for exceeding his engine allocation for the season.
Russell's beef stemmed from an incident in final practice, in which Verstappen overtook the Mercedes on the Las Vegas Strip, then veered in front of him into Turn 14.
Russell, unhappy with the Dutchman's behaviour, dived down the Red Bull's inside on braking for the corner, got alongside, and then made his displeasure known by pushing Verstappen wide, the two cars making light contact.
They were called to the stewards to discuss the incident. Verstappen was given a warning having been found to have made an "unpredictable change of direction under braking".
After qualifying, Russell told Sky Italia: "I think the warning was fine. There's nothing in the rules to say that you cannot overtake someone who is starting their lap but when you do this, you are screwing this person over.
"He does this week in, week out, with many drivers. Everybody accepts it and I am not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this and walk around like they are bigger and better than everyone else. Somebody has got to stand up to that guy."
Russell's comments were not put to Verstappen as he had finished his media commitments by the time they emerged into wider knowledge in Las Vegas.
Asked about the incident in the official news conference, Verstappen said: "Nothing happened."
When Verstappen spoke to the Dutch media, he was again reluctant to be drawn on it.
But asked whether it gave him extra motivation to beat Russell in the race, he said: "I don't need any extra motivation."
These two have history. This incident is almost exactly two years since they fell out in public following a row at the Qatar Grand Prix where Verstappen felt Russell had stepped over the line to get him a penalty that demoted him from pole position.
They exchanged insults. Russell, in remarks that echo those from Las Vegas, said: "People have been bullied by Max for years now."
Verstappen called Russell a "backstabber" and a "loser".
That was just the latest in a series of incidents - after a clash in Azerbaijan in 2022, Verstappen called Russell "Princess George" in a spat the Briton called "a little bit pathetic".
And then there was the 2025 Spanish Grand Prix. Late in the race, with Verstappen struggling with his tyres, they banged wheels as Russell attempted an overtake.
Verstappen retained the position after going off track and after being told by his team to hand the position to Russell, he moved over for the Mercedes and then appeared back on its inside and made contact with it.
He was penalised 10 seconds for that, and the following day issued a public apology for his behaviour.
All this certainly adds a hefty dose of extra spice to a race which, frankly, needs it.
This is the first of three 'dead rubbers' to close the season now both championships have been settled. There is little to fight for, and very little enthusiasm in the paddock for the final two races in the war-torn Middle East.
Despite widespread disquiet about going to Qatar and Abu Dhabi, everyone has been toeing the party line on that one recently in public, that they trust governing body the FIA and Formula 1 to have made the right decision.
After qualifying, though, Verstappen came close to becoming the first to break the omerta, when he told the Dutch media "of course I would rather be at home", before repeating that he trusted the sport's bosses on the matter.
BBC Sport has been told of at least one other major driver who would rather not be going to the Middle East specifically because of concerns about safety.
As for the racing here in Sin City, it is set up beautifully.
The two drivers on the front row, as well as not troubling to hide their distaste for each other, are also the only two to have won here.
Verstappen has won two of the three events on the Strip circuit, sandwiching Russell's victory in 2024.
In theory, that could give an opportunity to McLaren's Lando Norris, who qualified fourth, and starts third as a result of Antonelli's penalty.
But the 2025 world champion was concerned about the McLaren's lack of straight-line speed.
"The pace in the car is not bad," he said. "The car is performing well, but our straight-line speed is so far off that we are not going to be able to overtake someone, so I think it's going to be a tough day."
His team principal Andrea Stella was more optimistic.
McLaren's battle with Red Bull for third in the constructors' championship - the two are separated by just 17 points - is one of the few major issues still to be settled this year.
Stella saw the fact that Norris had missed out on beating Russell by just 0.02secs, despite having a plastic bag caught in his floor that he said had cost 0.1secs, as reason for optimism.
As were the lessons of the Italian Grand Prix - held on a track with similarly long straights and big braking zones.
"We saw that because of this towing effect you actually can stay tangled, linked to the other cars," Stella said. "So we are definitely looking forward to tomorrow and encouraged by the fact that qualifying has been competitive.
"Because the tow effect is so big and because it's bigger than Monza, racing will be difficult to predict.
"So we need to make sure that from a driver's team point of view, we are ready for all the situations.
"It's also a circuit that may offer quite a few safety cars, and will be interesting, for instance, restarting from safety car with a tyre warm-up. Definitely an interesting one to be part of and to follow."
