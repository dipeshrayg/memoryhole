An amber warning has been issued for parts of Northern Ireland during Storm Boelo on Saturday night.
The Met Office alert for counties Antrim and Londonderry lasts from 21:00 BST on Saturday until 10:00 GMT on Sunday.
The organisation is warning of widespread gusts up to 70mph (115kph), and up to 80mph (130kph) around exposed coastal areas.
Strong winds of up to 75mph could cause disruption across other parts of Northern Ireland.
Meanwhile, a yellow Met Office weather warning will be in place across Northern Ireland from 10:00 BST on Saturday until 12:00 GMT on Sunday.
The Irish weather service Met Ãireann has issued a series of orange and yellow warnings for heavy rain and damaging winds across parts of the Republic of Ireland.
Strong westerly winds are expected to become widespread on Saturday evening, with very windy conditions continuing overnight and through much of Sunday.
The strong winds will come with heavy, blustery showers at times.
Conditions are expected to gradually improve from the southwest during Sunday afternoon as the strongest winds begin to ease.
Firework displays cancelled
In light of the warning, there have been a number of closures and cancellations:
- Causeway Coast and Glens Borough Council has cancelled its fireworks display at Ballymoney on Saturday. In statement, the council said: "We understand how disappointing this will be, but the decision has been taken with the safety of visitors, staff and event personnel as the priority"
- Mid Ulster District Council has also cancelled their, external Halloween firework event which was due to be held at the Mid Ulster Sports Arena in Cookstown
- Antrim and Newtownabbey Borough Council has postponed their spooked-out event, external at V36 until Sunday 1 November
- The Antrim Senior Hurling Championship final, which was supposed to be on Sunday, has been postponed until 1 November
- The Sunflower Trophy motorcycle race meeting at Bishopscourt on Saturday has also been cancelled
- A number of StenaLine ferry sailings between Belfast and Cairnryan, and Dublin and Holyhead have also been cancelled, external on Saturday
- Derry City and Strabane District Council said cemeteries will be open on Saturday and closed on Sunday, except for funerals. Recycling centres will be open on Saturday but closed on Sunday, while outdoor sports facilities, parks and greenways will be closed on both days
- Brooke Park leisure centre will be closed on Saturday and Sunday
Risk of power cuts and travel disruption
The Met Office has warned that strong winds could cause disruption to transport networks and essential services.
Power cuts are possible, which could also affect mobile phone coverage and other services.
There is a small risk of damage to buildings, including roof tiles being blown off, while flying debris could pose a danger to the public.
Road, rail, air and ferry passengers could face longer journey times or cancellations, with some roads and bridges potentially closing.
Coastal areas could be particularly vulnerable, with large waves and beach material potentially being thrown onto seafronts, coastal roads and nearby properties.
