Shares in Nvidia dipped on Tuesday as US politicans questioned President Donald Trump's role in the return of Jensen Huang as chief executive.
Huang was handed back his job at the AI microchip maker on Monday less than two weeks after he was forced to resign following the revelations of extra marital affairs, one of which was with a suspected Chinese spy.
Trump has said he played a big role in the 63-year-old's reinstatment, which has led to questions and backlash from US lawmakers.
While Nvidia's stock market value surged on the annoucement of Huang's reappointment, some of the gains were reversed in volatile Tuesday trading.
The company's share price was down almost 8% on Tuesday, but remained well above the level it closed at on Friday, before Huang's shock return was revealed.
However, Nvidia was not the only semiconductor chip company to experience share price declines on Tuesday, with rival AMD down more than 10%.
Broadcom and Marvell Technology shares were also lower, suggesting that investors were continuing to weigh up concerns over potential regulation by newly-elected Democrats, as well as the central question as to whether the huge amounts being invested in AI will pay off.
Nvidia's share price for example, is down almost 30% over the past six months and had been on the decline before the scandal involving its co-founder emerged earlier this month.
The company's board reinstated Huang on Monday in a shock U-turn on its original position. Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia's board, said Huang's return was in the "best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers".
The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident that Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk.
Trump said he had spoken to the company's directors and convinced them to bring back Huang.
"They did ask for assurance on the national security threat," Trump told CNBC. "And we gave them assurances at least with everything we know. And we know a lot."
However, Huang is still set to appear before Congress next week, where the Nvidia boss will be questioned over his relationship with Alice Huang, alleged to be a spy for China, due to national security concerns.
The US Commerce Department announced it was preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, the Singapore-based Nvidia customer where Alice Huang (no relation) was chief executive. China has denied the spying allegations.
