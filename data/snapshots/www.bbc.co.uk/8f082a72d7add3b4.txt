Third T20, The Gabba, Brisbane
Australia 108 (16.5 overs): Overton 5-12, Dawson 3-16
England 93-3 (8.5 overs): Cox 33* (14), Donald 32 (13)
England won by seven wickets (DLS method)
England reaffirmed their advantage over a struggling Australia in white-ball cricket with a seven-wicket thrashing in the third T20 at The Gabba.
Fresh from their eight-wicket win on Tuesday, England bowled Australia out for 108 - the lowest total the hosts have been dismissed for in a T20 at home.
Jamie Overton had struggled to this point on the tour but claimed his first international five-wicket haul in taking a remarkable 5-12. Spinner Liam Dawson also chipped in again with 3-16.
From there, despite England slipping to 49-3, the most likely route to Australia escaping without defeat appeared to be the weather in Brisbane.
After three rain delays, Harry Brook's side reached their revised target of 93 with 7.1 overs to spare, claiming victory via the Duckworth-Lewis-Stern method.
Opener Aneurin Donald flickered again with a 13-ball 32 while Jordan Cox finished 33 not out as England romped to the finish line on the site of many an Ashes defeat.
Having won the 50-over series earlier this month, England can clinch this one with a match to spare by winning the fourth T20 in Sydney on Sunday.
Overton's day out
England's batters, namely Donald and Phil Salt, stole the headlines in Tuesday's win. Here the bowlers, in particular, Overton came to the fore.
Overton has had a mixed international career - useful contributions without match-winning performances and often criticised through 51 previous England appearances.
His day began with two catches off Dawson in the powerplay as the spinner dismissed Mitchell Marsh in his first over and Sam Harper for 26 in his second to continue England's good run with the new ball. They have taken two or more powerplay wickets in all 11 T20s since the start of the English summer.
After that the tall seamer truly got into his work. He found the fast, bouncy Brisbane conditions to his liking and hammered away at a shorter length.
The dangerous Jake Fraser-McGurk fell into the trap by sending one from that length to long-on, then Tim David and Nikhil Chaudhary were both hit - David on the arm and Chaudhary flush on the grille - before also being bounced out.
Joel Davies was another to fall to a bouncer while Ben Dwarshuis was obviously tentative when he nicked behind to give Overton, who also carried on after a nasty blow to the thumb when bowling, his fifth wicket.
Brook said afterwards Overton was rewarded for "extra graft" put in during training on Thursday.
Overton added: "The last couple of games I've been really disappointed with the way I bowled.
"Obviously we've not got a few players here who are experienced players so there's the responsibility to step up.
"I don't feel I have done that, so it was nice to put in a good performance today."
The all-rounder lost his place in England's 50-over side during the series win earlier this month.
He may never be a man for all seasons but this was a reminder, with the next T20 World Cup in Australia in 2028 and some bouncy surfaces to be expected in South Africa for next year's 50-over World Cup, of his success when conditions suit.
Australia struggling
In contrast to England, who have won 18 of 21 completed T20s this year and consolidate their place at the top of the rankings, Australia are struggling badly amid their off-field issues.
On the evidence of the previous two matches, the biggest surprise is that they managed to win the series opener in Melbourne.
They are without a number of first-team players - most notably Travis Head, Pat Cummins and Josh Hazlewood - but the next generation have shown little to suggest they are ready to step up. For all of Overton's success, this was a supreme batting wicket.
"A very disappointing night," said captain Mitchell Marsh. "We have to own that.
"I said to this young group at the start of the series I don't want them to fear making mistakes."
Seamer Riley Meredith offered some fight in a losing cause, bowling in a similar fashion to Overton and dismissing Phil Salt for five and Harry Brook for nine before adding the scalp of Donald.
But fielding has also been an issue for Australia.
After dropping six catches as a team in Melbourne and putting down Salt early on Tuesday, Chaudhary let a cut from Cox through his hands at backward point when England's wicketkeeper had only one.
Cox and Brook will leave Australia before the final T20 to join up with England's Test squad in South Africa.
England have called up all-rounder Rehan Ahmed and batter Asa Tribe to the T20 squad as cover for Brook, Cox and Will Jacks, who was ruled out of the rest of the series after breaking his hand in the first T20.
