Senate
The results in the Senate are beyond what the Democrats would have expected. North Carolina was a likely pick-up but the previously deep red state of South Carolina was a huge shock and Kansas had not returned a Democratic senator since 1932.
Texas, Iowa, Ohio and Maine also turned blue and the Democrats held Michigan. There are two seats remaining, Alaska and Mississippi, both are on a knife edge.
The shift from Republican to Democrat happened across the country. Some of the biggest swings since the presidential election in 2024 were in Texas, where the Democrats took the Senate seat and the governor. Several counties on the state's southern border swung by more than 50 percentage points.
House
The BBC's partner CBS News projected the Democrats would win the House just after 03:00 GMT on Wednesday (22:00 EST Tuesday).
Now it's a question of how big that win will be as counting continues.
Two significant seats are Florida 14 and Florida 25 which the Democrats have held. This is a blow to the Republicans' attempt to redraw the boundaries in their favour.
The redistricting saw both districts move from having a majority of 2024 Kamala Harris voters to having a majority of people who voted for Donald Trump.
Florida 22, which was also redrawn to favour the Republicans, saw a victory for the Democrats too.
What turned the vote?
Hispanic voters look to be one of the key groups in the elections in Texas. The poll suggests this group backed the Democratic Senate candidate James Talarico by 68% to 30% for the Republican Ken Paxton.
This is a turnaround from the estimated 50% of Hispanic voters who backed Donald Trump in 2024.
The poll also asked what issues voters were most concerned about. It suggests that across the country the economy was the biggest issue, followed by the state of democracy.
