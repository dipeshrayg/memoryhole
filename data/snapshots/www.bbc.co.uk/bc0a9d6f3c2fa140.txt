Former South Africa batter AB de Villiers has alleged Australia were ball-tampering from the first match of their controversial 2018 Test series.
Australia captain Steve Smith, vice-captain David Warner and batter Cameron Bancroft received long-term bans for their roles in using sandpaper to alter the ball during the third Test in Cape Town.
Former Australia batter Usman Khawaja, whose new autobiography has re-started scrutiny of the scandal, has previously claimed that sandpaper was also used during the preceding Test at Gqeberha, then known as Port Elizabeth.
Warner and Bancroft have both corroborated Khawaja's claims about that match, but De Villiers has now suggested the ball was altered during the series opener in Durban.
"It started in Durban, by the way, not in PE," De Villiers said on his YouTube channel ABD360. "I'm very happy for people to quote me on that.
"I see everyone is sort of admitting that in Port Elizabeth it also happened. I can very much tell you Durban was no different, in the first Test. Go look at the footage. Absolutely no doubt in my mind.
"We lost that first Test match in Durban with the ball reversing at a very, very interesting time. Way too early for anyone to reverse a ball at Kingsmead. Trust me, I've played a lot of cricket there.
"So whatever they did there, whether it was sandpaper or (a) nail, I don't care. The fact of the matter is they were caught and everyone knew. Just stand up and say 'we were wrong'. A lot is at stake now because they lied.
"The bowlers in particular knew because the ball doesn't just reverse. It's not your first Test match for Australia. So just stand up and say, 'guys, we were wrong'."
In a 4,000-word article last week, Bancroft said sandpaper wasn't used in Durban, where left-arm paceman Mitchell Starc finished with match figures of 9-109 as he bowled Australia to their one victory in the series.
"Casting forward to the first Test in Durban, sandpaper was not used," he wrote. "There were more traditional methods of maintaining the ball, including saliva. I cannot say with certainty whether mints or lollies were involved. Probably quarter-seam picking, this practice became so normal after my first Test."
Australia's bowling attack in the three Tests and Darren Lehmann, their head coach at the time, have consistently denied any knowledge of ball-tampering during the series.
Current Test captain Pat Cummins, who was part of the bowling attack, labelled Khawaja's claims as "100% wrong" and "utter nonsense" at a news conference earlier this month and has received backing from Cricket Australia to continue in his role.
Cricket Australia has launched an independent "forum" for players and staff involved in the scandal to voluntarily share their version of events, but it is not a full investigation and will not involve any new penalties.
"It's become really personal for all the people involved and I do have sympathy for them," added De Villiers, who played in every match in the series and retired from international cricket after its conclusion.
"Obviously not sympathy for what took place on that day and who knows how many games before then.
"I just feel they should have stuck together as a unit. They threw the guys under the bus and forced a situation like this to pop up again eight years later."
De Villiers also acknowledged that he was not an "angel" during his playing career but insisted his side had stayed within the rules of the game when working on the ball.
"I'm not playing all innocent and saying that I'm the perfect person," he said. "We all try and get the ball to reverse. We try and bounce it in. We try and do it in a legal manner and we try and push the boundaries as much as possible.
"We all have tried over the years to get the ball to do something, to get an edge, but obviously not being as stupid as to take an object onto the field and blatantly cheating."
Cricket Australia has been contacted for comment.
