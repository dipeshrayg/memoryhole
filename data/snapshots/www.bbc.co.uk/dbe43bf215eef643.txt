First T20, Melbourne Cricket Ground
England 187-9 (20 overs): Brook 65 (28), Banton 39 (22); Chaudhary 2-21
Australia 191-7 (19.5 overs): Chaudhary 66 (38)
Australia won by three wickets
Unlikely Australia international Nikhil Chaudhary rattled England and was the match-winner in a tense and tetchy final-over victory in the first T20 international in Melbourne.
Born in Delhi and a team-mate of India captain Shubman Gill in Indian domestic cricket, Chaudhary only switched to representing Australia after the Covid pandemic meant he got stuck in Brisbane celebrating his 24th birthday.
Progression through Australian club and domestic cricket followed - plus a stint working in a Mexican restaurant - before the 30-year-old played his standout performance to date in his fourth international at the Melbourne Cricket Ground.
Helping restrict England to 187-9 despite Harry Brook's fireworks in smashing 65 off 28 balls, Chaudhary took 2-21 with his mystery spin and then hit 66 to steer Australia within 20 runs of victory.
The elegant right-hander appeared to anger both Liam Dawson and Sam Curran during his knock and the MCG crowd loudly booed England as the tourists slowed the game in the final moments.
"You always know it is going to be a big game against England so there is going to be some chirp," said Chaudhary.
Curran was left with 11 to defend from the last over but all-rounder Jack Edwards finished with 25 not out as Australia completed their highest chase on home soil with three wickets and one ball remaining.
The win offers some relief to Australia - an XI featuring a host of new faces putting aside the ongoing sandpaper scandal to take a 1-0 lead in the five-match series.
England can level the contest in Gold Coast on Tuesday.
Chaudhary lets cricket do the talking
After all of the off-field issues that have overshadowed this tour, this was a welcome entertaining game of cricket.
England lost Phil Salt to the first ball of the match and afterwards there was boundary-hitting, comically bad fielding by Australia, a bit of needle and a grandstand finish.
Chaudhary should take the headlines. After opener Jake Fraser-McGurk fell in the first over and Mitchell Marsh was sluggish in making 37, he found scoring more straightforward.
He repeatedly targeted the off side and, after Australia lost 3-13 from 143-3, he pressed on the accelerator by hitting Will Jacks for six and Jamie Overton for two fours.
The incidents with Dawson and Curran appeared innocuous but words were exchanged. The umpire intervened in the Curran spat to encourage Chaudhary to continue his innings.
By hitting Overton to deep square-leg at the end of the 18th over, he allowed England back into the game.
However, Edwards kept his nerve, while England also had to deal with Jacks having to leave the field with a cut hand after four balls of the penultimate over.
Chaudhary once said he switched allegiance to Australia because he feels "more close to Australian culture than Indian culture".
Fittingly then, he experienced the wild cheers of an MCG crowd after the winning runs were hit.
"My parents came over from India last night," he said. "It is their first game watching me as well."
Brook knock in vain
When Brook had England 110-2 in the ninth over, the tourists looked on course for a score well over 200.
The four Australia wickets they took from the 16th over gave them a chance but it was their failure not to add another 15 or 20 runs earlier on that cost them.
England's skipper had a quiet ODI series with 23 runs across the three matches. Here he reaffirmed his position as one of the leading white-ball batters in the world.
He was dropped on two and three - both relatively straightforward chances among the six put down by Australia - but burst into life when hitting Riley Meredith for three consecutive sixes in the fourth over and was particularly destructive with his pull shot.
Brook's dismissal came when he tried to guide a cut off Chaudhary. Banton, who made 39 and shared a stand of 97 with Brook, followed in the next over and England never got going again.
After scoring 119 in the first 10 overs, they managed only 68 in the second, with Curran scoring 21 from 20, Jacks out for three and Jordan Cox never breaking free when making 27 from 20 balls.
With bowlers Jofra Archer, Gus Atkinson and Sonny Baker now preparing for the Test series against South Africa, England need more from Curran and Jamie Overton, who took 1-37 and 1-44 respectively.
