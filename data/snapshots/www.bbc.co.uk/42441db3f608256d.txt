How do you deal with the most horrendous conditions in track and field?
"I am absolutely slathered up in deep heat right now, my legs are tingling," said England's Amy Hunt after securing silver in the 100m.
"I am quite toasty and warm."
Hunt may have been the only one.
As other parts of the UK prepared for the fourth heatwave of the summer, the Commonwealth Games athletics competition was given a drenching in Glasgow.
Athletes shivered their way through their media interviews and high jump champion Eleanor Patterson of Australia was wearing a tartan scarf while officials struggled to clear water from the runway.
"This is the first time I have raced in these conditions," said Emmanuel Eseme of Cameroon, who powered through after a slow start to win the men's 100m.
"It was horrible. But we were all racing in the same conditions and I wanted to be that person with the least complaints. That is what worked for me."
There was a point midway through the session when the conditions threatened to breach into the ridiculous.
Persistent rain turned torrential and the first two throws of the women's hammer throw slipped from athletes' grip and clattered into the cage as thunder cracked above the arena.
Then, as the pace slowed in the men's 10,000m, three sodden runners came together. Uganda's Samuel Simba Cherop lost his shoe and had to run the remaining 15 laps with an increasingly soggy sock on his left foot.
He finished seventh in an admirable effort.
Bronze was taken by Isle of Man's David Mullarkey - the island's first Commonwealth medal in track and field - and he appeared colder than most.
Scotland's Andrew Butchart finished 13th, but he has seen this all before.
"The winters of Scotland in the cross-country are far, far worse than that," he said.
"It wasn't too cold which was nice. I'll have that again on Saturday [in the 5,000m event] if that's what comes."
Some athletes struggled in the conditions.
England's Morgan Lake did not clear the bar with any of her three efforts in the high jump.
Similarly, Scotland's Steven Stone had attempts with his glasses on his face and the top of his head in the men's T20 long jump but did not register a clean effort.
Northern Ireland's Kate O'Connor said she was "gutted" with her throw of 12.85m in the heptathlon shot put.
"It just kept slipping out of my hands," she said.
"When your chalk gets wet, it's like you might as well have dish soap on your hands and I just probably spent too much time and effort trying to stay dry rather than doing the cues that I work on in training.
"I can't blame anybody else apart from myself for not executing."
But there were also a number of impressive performances given the conditions.
O'Connor responded by running a dominant 24.14 seconds in the 200m to reclaim top spot of the heptathlon competition. England's Jade O'Dowda was only eighth quickest and slipped back to second, 49 points in arrears.
In the men's 100m, Eseme's winning time of 9.83 was a Commonwealth Games record, the third-fastest time this year and moved him third on the all-time list of African sprinters.
The hammer throw and the high jump were briefly paused but when the former did resume Canada's Camryn Rogers produced another Games record - 74.91m.
Even Hunt, who was slow out of the blocks and off the pace for the first 60m, finished 0.01secs outside her lifetime best of 10.97 in the women's 100m which was won by New Zealand's Zoe Hobbs.
"I am just proud of myself for gritting it out and trusting my long legs," Hunt said.
"Ultimately, we all have to race in the same conditions. We are all on the start line together freezing.
"You just have to get out there and get it done."
A smattering of hardy locals even stayed until the final anthem was sung as the clocked neared 11pm.
It was a night few will forget.
