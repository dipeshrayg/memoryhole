Poundland has collapsed into administration, putting 11,000 jobs at risk, after its owners failed to find a buyer to keep it in business.
The discount retailer formally entered administration on Wednesday afternoon, just weeks before Christmas.
Poundland's US owners held talks with several bidders over a last-minute rescue deal for the business, but no transaction was reached.
This led to Poundland's boss filing for administration, with consultancy Alix Partners appointed to oversee the process.
Alix Partners will pursue a sale of the business and its assets, with Poundland likely to be broken up and some of its stores sold off to rival retailers.
The adminstrators said it would look at "all options" for the business, "including sales of the business and its assets, either in whole or part".
"We appreciate this is a deeply concerning time for all involved - customers, suppliers and, in particular, Poundland colleagues," they said.
During the adminstration process, Poundland shops and distribution centres will remain open and continue to trade.
Its owner Gordon Brothers has been criticised over how it handled a potential sale of Poundland, with accussations the investment firm hoped to profit from selling off the chain's 600 shops and stock piecemeal.
Gordon Brothers, which specialises in buying and restructuring distressed companies, had denied such claims.
Mark Newton-Jones, Gordon Brothers' senior EMEA managing director, has been called to appear before a committee of MPs to give assurances that the firm will not act to liquidate Poundland's stock or act in the insolvency process.
'Sadness and intense regret'
Poundland boss Barry Williams said in a note to staff that the board of directors had "taken the difficult decision to appoint administrators" as a result of a deal not being agreed for the business.
"It is with sincere sadness and intense regret that we have to tell you that we had no alternative but to take this decision," he said.
He claimed owners Gordon Brothers had removed "all borrowing facilities and access to our bank accounts" on 27 October, and adequate funds had not been released since then to keep the business solvent.
Despite the board's "positive engagement with prospective bidders", Gordon Brothers had told the board there was "no prospect of an acceptable solvent offer" so the owner would no longer extend loans to the business," Williams claimed.
"Under these circumstances, it is impossible for the board of directors to see how Poundland can continue to operate as a going concern."
Gordon Brothers has previously stated Poundland was "challenged and vulnerable to any potential downturn in future trading".
But this was in stark contrast to Poundland's boss Barry Williams, who has said the business was making a "strong recovery".
