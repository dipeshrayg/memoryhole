Hackers who stole a record $6.3bn (Â£4.7bn) from Binance used fake job interviews and a compromised internal AI tool, the firm has revealed.
Initial findings published by the company, which operates the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, said hackers gained access to its systems through a "sophisticated, multi-stage campaign".
It said they used a range of techniques such as malicious software downloads to compromise the computers of Binance engineers and gain control over tools to drain its wallet of billions of dollars worth of crypto.
Binance called it a "serious and highly sophisticated security incident" and apologised, saying "this should not have happened".
In a post on X, external, Binance said hackers targeted its engineers using social engineering techniques.
These see hackers use approaches such as phishing to trick people into parting with sensitive or personal details, which can then be used to try and access their accounts.
Binance said as part of the hack, one of its engineers was contacted through a legitimate job-hunting platform and invited to bogus job interviews with fake company representatives.
It said the perpetrators used "more advanced and deceptive" methods to build trust with him, such as sharing social media accounts which contained personal images.
The engineer in question was instructed to download malware masquerading as an "assignment" during a second interview, which deployed malicious code with "obfuscation techniques" evading detection.
Binance said hackers then leveraged the engineer's compromised computer and an internal AI tool to gain control over two other engineers' laptops, and execute the next steps of the attack.
The incident led the firm to suspend withdrawals for customers globally, meaning users could not get their cryptocurrency out of the platform until the service was restored on Sunday.
Customers withdrew billions of dollars worth of assets after it reopened.
"We apologise for the concern, disruption and uncertainty it caused our users, partners and the wider industry," it said.
The company added it had taken a range of steps following the incident, including working with law enforcement to trace and freeze affected assets.
It said it had also set aside more than 100% of the funds needed to cover the loss, and said 98% of the token assets impacted had been "replenished" as of Saturday.
It is thought to be the largest crypto hack to date.
North Korea involvement
US crime agencies, including the FBI and Secret Service, have been assisting Binance in tracing the crypto taken from the platform's own wallets.
The agencies, along with a range of cyber analysts, said the hack - like similar breaches before it - bore the signs of having been orchestrated by North Korea.
Pyongyang hit back at the claims on Tuesday, denying it was behind the hack.
Kim Yo Jong, a top North Korean official and the sister of leader Kim Jong Un, said the accusations were another "sinister attempt" by the US to damage the counry's public image.
The country has consistently denied being behind a number of major cyber-attacks it has been blamed for in recent years.
The TraderTraitor unit, which is also known as the Lazarus Group, was blamed for a then-record $1.5bn hack of crypto exchange Bybit in 2025.
Binance had previously not responded to the BBC's queries about North Korea's alleged involvement in the hack.
But on Tuesday, the company wrote in its post on X, external: "The latest information released by the Federal Bureau of Investigation indicates that the Democratic People's Republic of Korea is responsible for this incident."
As part of its security efforts following the attack, Binance said it would also increase investment in AI tools which can detect security threats, as well as engage third-party cyber experts to carry out a full-scale review of the incident.
It also said that it would seek to establish a security research fund and work with leading AI and security firms on targeted initiatives.
