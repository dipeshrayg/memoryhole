A student who survived the Huntingdon train attack has met for the first time with the passenger she believes saved her life during the stabbings.
Astrid told BBC News that Stephen Crean's decision to confront Anthony Williams gave her time to escape. "I owe him my life," she said.
Williams was found guilty on Thursday of attempting to murder Crean and 13 others.
The then 19-year-old and 61-year-old Crean were just strangers on a train when she ran down the carriage screaming that there was a man with a knife.
Passengers on the Doncaster to King's Cross service fought off Williams, 33, with a frying pan and a toaster as the attack unfolded on 1 November 2025.
Crean ran towards Williams and was stabbed in the hands, back and neck. She was the last person to reach the buffet car and believes his actions gave her time to get in and shut the door.
Meeting after the guilty verdicts were delivered, the two train passengers hugged.
Astrid told the Nottingham Forest fan, who had been travelling home from a match: "It's brilliant to meet you... now we will know each other for a long time."
Crean said: "It means the world to me to meet her. Meeting her was the best thing, it was worth waiting for. I'm just so happy."
Astrid, who did not want to use her last name, said Crean was a hero for his actions that day.
"I just owe him everything," she said, before adding: "I wouldn't be here today without you, and to put your life on the line for someone else, a complete stranger, is just complete bravery."
She said Crean had a "split second to make a decision" and she owed him her life because of that.
"So I'd just like to say thank you," she said.
Crean praised her for alerting him to the danger of Williams as she ran past.
"You can be proud of yourself, and your parents will be proud of you," he added.
During the trial, Crean told the court he decided "to try to stop" the attack and "launched himself towards and at" Williams.
Crean and Astrid both faced terrifying moments as Williams rampaged down the train.
Crean said Williams asked, "Do you want to die?" as he stabbed him several times.
Crean then managed to lock himself in a toilet and call 999 telling them what had happened and that he was covered in blood and scared.
He said he remembered seeing Astrid crying with makeup running down her face.
The fashion student said she remembered seeing Crean throw himself at the attacker and put himself between her and Williams so she could escape.
"I was able to shut the door with the rest of the people behind me. If you hadn't been in between us... I have no idea what would have happened if you weren't there, literally in that second."
Astrid had seen an interview with the 62-year-old on the BBC two days after the attack and contacted the BBC to say he was the man on the train who had helped protect her and others.
Only now the trial has finished have they been able to meet.
Crean said he is pleased the jury found Williams guilty of attempting to murder him as he did not believe he should be allowed to get away with the vicious attacks on him and the other passengers.
On Thursday Cambridgeshire police apologised for failings in stopping Williams after another assault the month before the Huntingdon attack.
As well as the train attacks, Williams was found guilty of attempting to murder four other people in separate incidents and will be sentenced at Cambridge Crown Court on 4 and 5 February.
Crean believes more should have been done to prevent these further offences. "I think they should have had him before. He shouldn't have been allowed to go running around like this. And if they'd picked up on it, we'd have had him before."
Having survived the attack, Astrid is running the London marathon for Crimestoppers as part of her efforts to turn around the harrowing situation she found herself in.
She can, she says, because of Stephen Crean's actions. And he has promised to be there for her again: this time on the finish line.
