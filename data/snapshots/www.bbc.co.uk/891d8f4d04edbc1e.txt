Pat Cummins will retain backing from Cricket Australia (CA) to remain as Test captain despite ongoing and explosive allegations about the sandpaper controversy.
A detailed testimony from Cameron Bancroft supported previous claims made by Usman Khawaja that the entire Australia team - including Cummins - knew of the plan to tamper with the ball during a Test match against South Africa in Cape Town in 2018.
Cummins has always denied involvement in or knowledge of the cheating plot, and reiterated his stance in a 68-minute address to the media last week.
CA has regularly spoken of its support for Cummins, and the fast bowler will be allowed to continue in the captaincy role he has held since 2021. A statement could be issued over the weekend.
Cummins, 33, is due to lead Australia in the first Test against New Zealand on 9 December. A squad for the series opener against the Black Caps has not yet been named.
With the support of CA, a decision on Cummins' future as captain would now appear to rest with the player, who has shown no indication he will resign.
The row over the sandpaper saga, one of the biggest controversies in Test history, has engulfed Australian sport since Khawaja's claims first emerged two weeks ago.
In 2018, Bancroft, then-captain Steve Smith and his vice-captain David Warner were punished for Bancroft's act of rubbing sandpaper on the ball in the third Test against South Africa at Newlands.
Khawaja, who retired from international cricket earlier this year, wrote in his autobiography that the entire Australian team knew and should have taken responsibility.
The former batter's account was supported by his childhood friend Warner, along with Chadd Sayers and Jon Holland, both back-up bowlers on the tour.
In response, Cummins called a news conference where he promised to answer all questions about his part in the controversy, in the hope of "never having to speak about it again".
He called Khawaja's assertions "100% wrong" and "nonsense".
However, Cummins' address to the media motivated Bancroft to publish his own account of more than 4,000 words in the West Australian newspaper.
"The final straw for me to write this account was Australian cricket captain Pat Cummins' press conference," said Bancroft.
"It made me feel angry, betrayed and alone."
Bancroft alleged he had conversations with all of the bowlers about how much the ball was swinging, and that the entire group of players and coaches celebrated when the ball-tampering yielded success.
Batter Bancroft also implicated Darren Lehmann, who was then the Australia head coach, and David Saker, the bowling coach.
Lehmann, now the coach of county side Northamptonshire, is due to commentate for the BBC and Australian radio station ABC on the first T20 between Australia and England in Melbourne on Saturday.
Saker was part of the England coaching staff for the one-day tri-series in Pakistan in October.
The 60-year-old has been employed by England on an ad hoc consultancy basis, an arrangement that has now ended.
England have firmed up new coaching teams under Test head coach Stephen Fleming and his white-ball counterpart Brendon McCullum.
