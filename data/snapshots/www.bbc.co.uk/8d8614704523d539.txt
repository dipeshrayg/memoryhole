Two officers who lied about carrying out missing person searches linked to a crash in which three people died have been sacked after being found guilty at a police misconduct hearing.
PC Richard Hardwick and PC Lauren Livingstone faced gross misconduct allegations over their actions before a car carrying five people was found crashed two days later in St Mellons, Cardiff, in 2023.
The hearing in Newport was told they were "dishonest" in official logs and to a senior officer by claiming searches of the homes of Eve Smith, who died, and Sophie Russon had been carried out.
Members of Smith's family described the verdict as "an important step towards accountability".
Following the proceedings, Smith's mother Emma Borg said the family had to repeatedly revisit the "most painful period" of their lives.
She said her family felt their honesty and credibility was being "questioned" at a time when they "trusted the police to do their jobs".
"Instead, the dishonesty established during these proceedings undermined the missing persons' investigation and the trust our families desperately needed to have in those responsible for helping us," she added.
Lauren Doyle, Smith's sister, said they "never wanted to spend years scrutinising police decisions" and called the outcome of the proceedings an "important step".
She added: "Our search for answers continues. We will never lose sight of why we started."
The Independent Office for Police Conduct (IOPC) called the officers' actions "inexcusable with both failing in the fundamental basics of policing".
Panel chairman Ian Arundale said a final written warning was not an option because the level of harm and culpability was too high. He also said there was a lack of mitigating factors.
Hardwick and Livingstone had deliberately misled a missing persons investigation, he added, which may have compromised the investigation, and had undermined public trust in the police in a high-profile case.
Hardwick faced six allegations, including that he lied about searching the homes of crash victims Smith and Russon, and that he also lied to his supervising officer and the police watchdog.
Livingstone faced four allegations, including that she lied about searching the two houses, as well as making a dishonest entry on the log and lying to her supervising officer.
The police disciplinary hearing at Rodney Parade, which lasted four days, was told that both officers were part of the team assigned to look into reports that Smith, Darcy Ross and Russon had gone missing after a night out on 3 March 2023.
The three women were reported missing to Gwent Police on 4 March, but were not found until 30 hours after the initial contact by the families.
The car was found in a wooded area off the A48, 46 hours after it had crashed.
Smith, Rafel Jeanne, and Ross had died, while Russon and Shane Loughlin were seriously injured.
Hardwick and Livingstone went to Russon's home and then on to Smith's house, and as part of Gwent Police's missing person policy were supposed to search the address to look for clues for what might have happened to them.
Hardwick wrote in the official log afterwards "premise search conducted" and told the police sergeant overseeing the case the search at Russon's house had been carried out. He also said searches had happened when he spoke to the police watchdog who later investigated.
This was in contrast to what Russon's father, Nathan, told the hearing, that the officers were at the house for a very short period and did not ask to search the bedroom.
Livingstone said she could not remember whether searches had taken place, but wrote what she agreed was a "poorly written" report in the official log, which the panel head heard was "deliberately vague" but suggested a search had taken place.
The panel found Hardwick and Livingstone had failed to conduct a search at Smith and Russon's homes.
Hardwick was found to have provided a false entry into the police log and had provided a dishonest statement when interviewed by the IOPC.
He had been found, in part, to have given a dishonest account of the search to his supervisor and found in part to have provided a dishonest account in a signed statement to the IOPC.
Livingstone was found to also have provided a false entry into the police log and to have had given a dishonest account of the search to her supervisor.
Arundale said their behaviour had caused harm because of the impact on the family and had undermined public trust in the police in a case which resulted in significant levels of concern from the public.
Derrick Campbell, from the IOPC said the officers failed to "act honestly and with integrity", saying property searches were a "basic but essential part in the early stages of a missing person investigation".
"PC Hardwick deliberately lied during the course of the missing person investigation, claiming on police records and to his supervisor that searches had been completed.
"PC Livingstone had ample opportunity to tell the IOPC the required searches had not been conducted.
"Instead, she did not give an account and stated she could not remember the specifics of her visits to the homes of two of the young women who had been reported missing," Campbell said.
