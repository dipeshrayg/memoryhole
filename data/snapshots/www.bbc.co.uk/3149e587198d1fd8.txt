A gunman suspected of killing six people in central Krakow, as well as his parents and sister at their home, died in hospital early on Monday, Polish police have said.
The 24-year-old, who has been named as Michal Oksinski by local media, was injured after opening fire in central Krakow, the second largest city in Poland, on Saturday, police said.
Armed officers raced to the scene and engaged in a gunfight with the suspect, who was shot within three minutes of the attack beginning, preventing a further massacre, officials said.
Authorities initially reported that the gunman had apparently been inspired by "far-right ideologies", but have since said they are investigating any possible motivation.
After the shooting, which began shortly before 15:00 local time (14:00 GMT) in Krakow's busy Main Market Square, police identified the attacker as a 24-year-old Polish man.
In the hours after the shooting, a minister said that early investigations indicate he was inspired by "far-right ideologies". The minister also described him as an "ultra-nationalist" without giving further details.

Six people - four women and two men - are confirmed to have been killed in the attack.
Four victims are Polish, while a British national and an Austrian woman were also killed.
Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk said three bodies - two women and one man - were found at the gunman's house when it was searched after the attack.
On Sunday, Polish police confirmed those three victims had been identified as the alleged gunman's 53-year-old father, 52-year-old mother and 21-year-old sister.
Polish media has reported the suspect had a firearms permit and may have obtained the weapons from his family home.
Four firearms were found in the gunman's car, including two AR-15 rifles and two handguns, along with several hundred rounds of ammunition, Nowak said. Authorities also seized at least four phones from his car and person, and electronic equipment from his home.
Sixteen people, including five foreigners, two police officers and the attacker, were taken to hospital. The interior ministry later said they were from Austria, Britain, Germany, Portugal and Russia.
