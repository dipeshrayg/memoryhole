Speaking to the BBC's Laura Kuenssberg, the Conservative Party leader said: "What we want to see is that people who work harder don't get punished for doing so."
The earnings limit means only families where no parent earns more than this threshold are eligible for 30 hours of government-funded childcare or tax-free childcare.
The announcement comes as Conservative members gather for the party's annual conference in Birmingham.
Badenoch, who arrived in the city on Saturday, has vowed the party was new and different from the one that lost the 2024 general election.
The Conservatives - who have been polling behind Labour and Reform UK over the past year - are expected to use the four-day event to lay out more of their policy agenda ahead of the next general election.
Ahead of the conference, the Conservatives produced a 100-page essay setting out their principles, external including a "central" belief that business, rather than governments, create growth.
It argues that governments should create the conditions for growth, particularly through the tax system.
In her interview with the BBC, Badenoch pitched herself as a "classic old-school Conservative" adding: "I believe in common sense - people working hard and being rewarded for that effort."
She insisted the Tories would keep the state pension triple lock, which Prime Minister Andy Burnham has pledged to change if Labour wins the next election.
She said the policy, under which state pensions rise each year by the highest of inflation, average wages, or 2.5%, was "the right policy for pensioners".
She added: "What we don't want to see is people going after the elderly, chasing after vulnerable and disabled people, and then the extremely large cohort of people who can work, who are not working, are left alone."
The Conservatives have long called on the government to cut the growing welfare budget including by implementing tighter restrictions on claimants.
