Sir Nick Faldo has suggested, external those who financially benefited from LIV Golf should "chip in and take care of" businesses owed money by the Saudi-backed tour.
His comments have been welcomed by UK companies still waiting to be paid.
A BBC Sport investigation found that LIV paid millions to players and executives in the weeks before filing for bankruptcy, while bills from suppliers were already overdue.
Faldo - arguably Britain's greatest golfer - said the controversial breakaway tour risked "ruining the small businesses that help make their events possible".
"British suppliers who cleaned venues and kept events running are still owed hundreds of thousands of pounds," added the six-time major winner.
"Maybe if those on and off the golf course feel they were overcompensated for their worth, they should chip in and take care of this matter."
Since the investigation was published, more firms have contacted us about money they are owed by LIV.
They include sports communications company Soapbox, which works with the likes of Anthony Joshua and Luke Littler and is owed tens of thousands of pounds.
"I'm incredibly grateful to Sir Nick Faldo for speaking out," said Krissi Foskett, whose cleaning company BD365 Clean is Â£75,225 out of pocket.
"For the last couple of months I've felt powerless. Having someone of his standing recognise the injustice of what's happening to businesses like ours genuinely means a lot.
"Sir Nick's suggestion that those who have benefited financially could contribute towards paying the outstanding suppliers is something I wholeheartedly welcome."
LIV Golf has not responded to requests for comment on the original investigation or Faldo's remarks.
In August, chief executive Scott O'Neil said he hoped the tour would be able to pay its suppliers.
"What I would say to them is we're doing everything we can to make sure we can do right by them and the work they committed.
"I come from a family of entrepreneurs, a family of small business entrepreneurs and know the challenge."
