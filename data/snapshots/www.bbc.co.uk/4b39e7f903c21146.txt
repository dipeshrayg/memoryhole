The prime minister is expected to argue the UK has lost more than it has gained from leaving the European Union in a speech ahead of a UK-EU summit later this week.
Speaking at the Lord Mayor's Banquet on Monday, Andy Burnham will compare the UK's Brexit deal to the "emperor's new clothes" - saying "a needlessly hard Brexit" created barriers for businesses and the promise to "take back control" was not delivered.
He is due to sign new agreements on food, energy and youth mobility with the bloc at Friday's summit, after the substance of those deals was set by his predecessor Sir Keir Starmer.
The Conservatives have said Labour are "dragging us into the European Union via the back door".
Separately Burnham has said he wants to examine the "pros and cons" of different options for Britain's long-term relationship with the EU.
Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told the BBC the prime minister would lay out these potential options after the UK-EU summit.
However, Burnham has ruled out major changes before the next general election.
Burnham is expected to say on Monday: "It's like the emperor's new clothes â where no-one is prepared to say the obvious truth. Well, I say it as I see it. And to be frank, the emperor has nothing on.
"The Brexit we were given has not worked. We have lost more than we have gained."
Ahead of Friday's UK-EU summit, Burnham will set out three tests for any agreement.
Firstly, that it must be consistent with Labour's 2024 general election manifesto pledge that there would be "no return to the single market, the customs union, or freedom of movement".
He will also say it must improve the original Brexit deal and bring it closer to what people thought they were voting for in 2016.
Finally, he will say any deal must pass the "Makerfield test" of providing tangible improvements to people's everyday lives - a reference to voters in Burnham's Greater Manchester constituency.
He will say the prospect of a new deal is a "win-win" and illustrates a "new approach".
"Not adversarial. No longer burning bridges. But being constructive and positive - showing that we can come together to confront shared challenges."
This week's summit will see the UK seek to agree further changes to former Prime Minister Sir Keir Starmer's deal, struck in May 2025, covering fishing, trade and other areas.
The UK and EU have agreed a draft deal on removing post-Brexit checks from food and agriculture exports ahead of the meeting.
The government argues the agreement will help cut costs for businesses and could bring down prices for shoppers.
However, critics say the deal will make the UK a "rule taker" from Brussels, aligning with EU regulations without being able to vote on them.
Similar texts for deals on emissions trading and a youth travel and work scheme have also been circulated to EU nations.
These deals were close to being finalised under Sir Keir, with only some details still to be worked out.
As a candidate in the Makerfield by-election that propelled his return to Parliament, Burnham had promised not to "re-run" Brexit arguments and said he was "not proposing that the UK considers rejoining the EU".
However, since becoming prime minister he has said he wants to explore a different long-term relationship with the bloc.
He has suggested potential options for the future could range from no changes to the UK-EU relationship, to rejoining the customs union, the single market or a fresh referendum on full EU membership.
The prime minister has stressed any changes would require a mandate from the public at a general election.
Boris Johnson, who was prime minister when the UK left the EU in 2020 and negotiated the original Brexit deal, has warned Burnham against "wasting his premiership" on Europe.
He told the Daily Telegraph, external he believed the public would back Brexit again if Burnham forced another vote on the issue.
"It is madness to escalate the Brexit issue as your political priority," Johnson told the paper.
"He will impale himself and his party on this point, and he needs to move off it because he won't win â because the reality is that people are not fools."
Deputy Conservative leader Alex Burghart accused Burnham of "reopening a debate the country settled long ago, while ignoring the challenges facing Britain today".
Reform UK has said one of the "key benefits" of Brexit is that Britain no longer has to "adhere to ridiculous EU rules", and argued that any form of alignment could have a negative impact on the country's trade policy.
The Liberal Democrats have said the draft deal on food exports is a "patch, not a fix" and that the "real solution is to re-join the single market and to establish a customs union with the EU".
The Green Party of England and Wales has called for the UK to re-join the customs union, with leader Zack Polanski previously saying "full membership of the EU remains the best option for the UK".
