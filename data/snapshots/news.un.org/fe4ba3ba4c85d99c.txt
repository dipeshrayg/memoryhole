Peacekeeping: With 500 paternity and child support claims unresolved, UN scales up accountability
The UN is adding new accountability measures to better support children fathered by peacekeepers, the Under-Secretary-General for Management Strategy, Policy and Compliance, Catherine Pollard, announced Thursday.
Since 2006, the organization has received some 800 paternity and/or child support claims involving peace operations personnel in cases of sexual exploitation and abuse (SEA), the Secretary-General's Deputy Spokesperson Farhan Haq told journalists in New York.
Children 'left behind'
"Claims of paternity are one of the most serious consequences of SEA, and it impacts not only the victim but also the children left behind," Ms. Pollard said.
"Their fathers have abandoned them and have left the peacekeeping mission, often before they were even born. These fathers normally do not provide child support unless they're forced to do so," she emphasised.
While progress has been made to prevent and respond to sexual exploitation and abuse, around 500 of the claims remain unresolved, including 400 where paternity has yet to be determined, according to Mr. Haq.
'Unjust situation'
"It is an unjust situation, and we are doing everything in our power to change this, which is only something we can do together with Member States," Ms. Pollard stressed.
Indeed, the first step in responding to paternity and child support claims is to make sure the accused peacekeeper is the biological father, which requires investigation and evidence - usually DNA paternity testing.
Member States may have to compel accused fathers to submit to these tests in line with national legislation, Ms. Pollard said.
Building on previous work
In late 2024, the UN launched a Public Paternity Dashboard, which shows the status of the resolution of child support and paternity claims involving "blue helmets".
It shows what claims involve, how long they've been pending, and their status.
"But we thought more detail was required," Ms. Pollard explained.
"So, we expanded the public reporting to more clearly show the status of the claims, including to clearly identify where Member States are working with us to resolve claims and where they are not."
These assessments may inform future deployment decisions, Mr. Haq noted.
