Mozambique: More than 7,000 flee fresh violence in Cabo Delgado
More than 7,000 people have fled their homes in northern Mozambique's Cabo Delgado province since late September, escaping fresh violence and fear of attacks by non-state armed groups, according to new data from the International Organization for Migration (IOM).
Nearly 80 per cent of the latest arrivals are women and children. Many are seeking safety in communities that already host people displaced by years of conflict.
"These communities need help with food, shelter and healthcare," said Dana Graber Ladek, IOM Chief of Mission in Mozambique.
"Without timely support, life could get much harder for displaced families and the people hosting them."
Forced to flee again
Between 21 September and 13 October, 5,794 people were displaced, followed by another 1,235 between 14 and 27 October. Some have had to flee more than once.
In the most recent two weeks, those displaced included 87 pregnant women, 58 older people, 18 people with disabilities and four unaccompanied or separated children. Many men stayed behind to keep earning a living, despite the danger.
Cabo Delgado already hosts more than 205,000 displaced people across 84 sites. More than half of them are children.
Food is the most urgent need, reported by 88 per cent of those assessed, followed by shelter (71 per cent) and healthcare (44 per cent).
The UN migration agency is providing emergency shelter, household items, water, sanitation and hygiene, protection and psychosocial support. But it warned that continued displacement and falling funding are stretching the response just as needs grow.
Affordability of mobile internet reaches new milestone
Mobile internet has become affordable in the world's landlocked developing countries and small island developing States for the first time, according to new figures released on Tuesday by the UN's International Telecommunication Union (ITU).
The median price of a basic mobile data plan in both groups has fallen below the internationally agreed target of 2 per cent of average income.
"In the age of AI, being offline means being shut out of countless opportunities, from education to employment," said ITU Secretary-General Doreen Bogdan-Martin.
Some 6.3 billion people - 76 per cent of the world's population - are online this year, up from 6.1 billion in 2025, though 2 billion remain unconnected.
Price and speed gaps
Prices remain stubbornly high in the least developed countries, at 6.1 per cent of income, and three in 10 economies meet neither the mobile nor the fixed broadband target.
Connection quality varies too. 5G networks reach 86 per cent of people in high-income countries but just 4 per cent in low-income ones. There, the average mobile user gets through 3.4 GB of data a month - around six times less than in richer nations.
"Now more than ever, we need to expand connectivity and make it meaningful for everyone, everywhere," Ms. Bogdan-Martin said.
