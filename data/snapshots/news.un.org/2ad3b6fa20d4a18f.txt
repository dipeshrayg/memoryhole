World News in Brief: Vaccine crisis in Yemen, aid for Ukraine, another UNRWA staffer killed
More than 364,000 people have been displaced in Yemen since the beginning of August as fighting and insecurity continue, the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs (OCHA) said on Tuesday.
In encircled Taiz, some 350,000 children missed the national measles and rubella vaccination campaign because shots could not be delivered safely, UN Spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric told reporters in New York.
More than 30,000 babies under the age of one also risk missing routine immunization.
No access
"Our colleagues at WHO and UNICEF have the resources and vaccines needed, but they cannot currently reach children that they need to reach in Taiz," he said.
Since August, more than 25 humanitarian partners have provided health assistance to over 100,000 displaced people. Emergency food, hygiene supplies and other relief have reached more than 300,000.
The UN renewed its call for civilians to be protected and for safe, unhindered humanitarian access.
Ukraine: Aid convoy reaches frontline Kherson community
An interagency convoy facilitated by OCHA delivered food, hygiene items, medicines and warm clothing on Tuesday to a frontline community in Kherson where 600 people remain.
The community has had severe power disruptions for nearly a year, and hostilities and mine contamination restrict both aid access and civilian movement.
Attacks continue to kill and injure civilians across the country, Mr. Dujarric told reporters in New York.
Zaporizhzhia bears brunt
Over the past day, at least seven civilians were killed and nearly 80 injured, according to the authorities. Zaporizhzhia region was hardest hit.
On Monday in Kramatorsk, in Donetsk region, a drone reportedly struck a humanitarian evacuation vehicle run by a local NGO. Two civilians were killed and two others injured, including the driver.
In Odesa, some 80,000 households and businesses are without heating. More than 460 warming centres are open.
Gaza: UNRWA staff member killed near water well
A staff member of the UN agency for Palestine refugees (UNRWA) was killed and three others were injured in Gaza on Monday after an Israeli strike near a water well in Jabalya, the agency said.
The staff were at the well carrying out their regular duties, Mr. Dujarric told reporters at Tuesday's briefing.
"We of course condemn the killing and injuring of our UNRWA colleagues," he said.
Nearly 400 staffers killed
The incident brings the number of UNRWA personnel killed since October 2023 to 397. In that time, 600 aid workers have been killed across Gaza.
Over the past week, the UN Children's Fund (UNICEF) brought in trucks of school chairs and desks for temporary learning centres.
The UN has also pressed the Israeli authorities to let in a wider range of critical supplies, including heavy machinery, engine oil and spare parts for vehicles and generators.
