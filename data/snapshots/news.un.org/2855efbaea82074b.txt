Ukraine: Civilian casualties in 2026 already far exceed previous years' tolls
This year has seen dramatically higher civilian casualties in Ukraine than any other year since the full-scale invasion of the eastern European country by Russia in February 2022, the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission in Ukraine (HRMMU) said on Tuesday.
In October 2026, the number of civilians killed (372) and injured (1,981) was 95 per cent higher than in October the previous year.
The first ten months of 2026 have seen more civilian deaths than all of 2025, which in turn was worse than the year before, the head of the HRMMU noted, urging the world not to "become numb to the deadly toll the war exacts on Ukraine's civilians."
In all, almost 18,000 civilians have been killed and almost 58,000 have been injured in Ukraine since February 2022.
Deadly November attacks
The alarming trend continued in November, which saw one of the deadliest single attacks on civilians since the start of the war.
On 2 November, multiple bombs struck the premises of a school in Komyshuvakha in south-eastern Ukraine, including an underground shelter where civilians had gathered to receive humanitarian aid.
72 people had been killed and four remained missing, Ukrainian authorities reported on 16 November.
The UN human rights mission is still verifying the victims' identities and status, but has confirmed that dozens were civilians, including local residents, administrative staff and emergency workers.
Moreover, on Monday, a drone struck a humanitarian vehicle evacuating civilians with limited mobility from the city of Kramatorsk in eastern Ukraine. The driver was seriously injured, one evacuee sustained minor injuries, and other passengers suffered hearing damage, the UN Secretary-General's spokesperson told journalists on Tuesday.
The UN's humanitarian coordination office (OCHA) renewed its call for the protection of civilians, including aid workers.
Missiles and drones
Long-range weapons - missiles and drones - remain the leading cause of civilian casualties, accounting for more than half of those killed and injured.
Casualties from aerial glide bombs - bombs fitted with wings that allow them to travel long distances after being dropped from aircraft - also rose sharply in October. The number of civilian deaths they caused doubled compared with September, reaching 92 killed and 331 injured.
Aerial glide bombs accounted for 18 per cent of civilian casualties that month, while short-range drones were responsible for 19 per cent.
Energy infrastructure
Russian forces intensified attacks on Ukraine's energy infrastructure in October as the country prepared for its fifth winter at war.
Strikes damaged electricity and heating facilities, disrupting power supplies and, in some cities, water services.
Ukrainian authorities were forced to introduce rolling power outages nationwide.
Ukrainian armed forces also attacked electricity infrastructure in Russian-occupied territory of Ukraine.
In occupied territory and Russia
According to Russian authorities, Ukrainian attacks in October killed at least 90 people and injured 306 in Russian-occupied Ukrainian territory, while 59 were killed and 402 injured in the Russian Federation - though HRMMU could not independently verify these figures due to restricted access and a lack of independent sources.
In Russian-occupied areas of Ukraine, Ukrainian forces also targeted electricity infrastructure and the UN mission independently verified that at least 12 civilians were killed and 18 injured.
