Security Council LIVE: Can conflict resolution keep pace as AI and drones reshape war?
When the UN was founded in 1945, computers, the internet and satellites were the stuff of science fiction. Today, artificial intelligence (AI) is already assisting in targeting, drones are reshaping warfare and disinformation is spreading in unprecedented ways, fuelling record levels of conflict. The Security Council on Tuesday is weighing these changes, with UN chief António Guterres telling ambassadors, "killer robots must have no place in our future."
Key Facts
- AI and other new technologies now spread globally within months, not decades
- The UN chief told council members they have "a special duty to ensure that competition over emerging technologies does not destabilise international peace and security."
- Last week in Geneva, a push by around 100 countries to negotiate a legally binding instrument on lethal autonomous weapons fell short
- The Secretary-General's July review of UN peace operations made closing the technology gap a priority
- A new informal council expert group on technology, peace and security met for the first time on 18 November
- In 2021, the council recognized technology as a potential "force multiplier" for UN peacekeeping
