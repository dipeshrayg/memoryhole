Lebanon leading the way in mental health support
Mental health support must be a core part of the humanitarian response, said the UN Deputy Secretary-General during a visit to Lebanon over the weekend, where efforts to address the psychological toll of years of conflict and instability offer valuable lessons for other crisis-affected countries.
"What I heard in Lebanon is a powerful reminder that the impact of conflict does not end with the physical destruction we can see," said Amina Mohammed.
"The effects on people's mental health, families and communities can endure long after."
Lebanon is facing mass displacement and acute food shortages as Israeli strikes continue, particularly in the south.
Lebanon 'has the expertise'
In the face of growing needs, though, Lebanon has increasingly integrated mental health support into its public healthcare system.
The country's National Mental Health Programme has developed a strategy to make mental health and psychosocial support an integral part of healthcare services.
In Beirut's primary health centre in Chiyah, for instance, routine health checks include mental health screenings and support.
Lebanon has also recently made its national suicide prevention and emotional support hotline, Lifeline 1564, toll-free.
"Lebanon clearly has the expertise, integrated systems and proven models; what it urgently needs is more investment to bring mental health well-being services to more people and communities and ensure its long-term sustainability," she said.
The 'courage' of providers
In the southern city of Tyre, Ms. Mohammed met mental health professionals and patients, hearing how local teams are working to ensure uninterrupted care despite difficult conditions.
"I was deeply moved and inspired by their courage, especially women, and how they lean into each other's kindness to build strength," she said.
In Naqoura, also in the South, the Deputy Secretary-General met with personnel of the UN's peacekeeping mission in Lebanon, UNIFIL, and expressed her thanks for the contributions and sacrifices of its staff as the mission's mandate comes to an end.
A wider mission
Lebanon's efforts are part of a broader UN push to make mental health support central to humanitarian responses worldwide.
In 2024, more than 300 million people needed humanitarian assistance and protection globally, including an estimated 66 million experiencing adverse mental health conditions.
Yet services remain severely underfunded, leaving millions without adequate care.
To help close this gap, the UN launched the Greentree Acceleration Plan, backed by $13.2 million in initial funding from the independent charitable healthcare foundation, Wellcome.
The initiative, being rolled out first in Lebanon and Chad, aims to strengthen locally led mental health services, expand access to proven treatments and improve psychological support for humanitarian workers.
Lessons beyond Lebanon
Annemarie Hou, Executive Director of the UN Office for Partnerships, the body responsible for the Greentree Acceleration Plan, said Lebanon offers valuable lessons for humanitarian operations elsewhere.
"People affected by crisis need more than survival. They need the support and dignity that allow them to recover, reconnect and rebuild their lives," she stressed.
