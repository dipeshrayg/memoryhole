Japan's exports in October surged 20.5% from a year earlier, snapping a two-month slowdown in shipments, official data released Wednesday showed.
Exports growth beat expectations of economists polled by Reuters that had pegged it at 20%, and it was sharply higher compared to a revised 16.5% in September.
Shipments of semiconductor equipment that have been propping up Tokyo's exports jumped 49.7% by value. Exports of motor vehicles climbed 19.9% year on year.
In terms of destinations, shipments to China rose 13.3%, while they climbed 23.9% to the U.S.
Exports have been the main driver of Japan's growth, although a surge in the value of imports of petroleum due to the Iran war has offset some the impact of the exports surge on its economy.
Import growth in Japan for October came in at 21.6%, sharply missing analysts' estimates of 25.8%, with petroleum imports climbing 60.5% by value, even as volumes slipped 0.7%.
Analysts previously told CNBC that the surge in value was because Japan was attempting to switch sources to more expensive American oil as shipments from the Middle East stalled.
Imports of petroleum from the U.S. jumped more than 1,100% year on year by value and 738% by volume. In contrast, shipments from the Middle East dipped 16.5% by volume, while higher prices pushed up import value by 37.7%.
The country posted a surprise contraction in its third quarter GDP, which shrank 0.1% on an annualized basis.
Following the data, Japan's Nikkei 225 was up 0.91%, while the yen traded at 159.50 to the dollar.
