U.K. inflation rose to 3.6% in the 12 months to October, its highest level in a year, as fuel costs continued to rise.
The reading was in line with the expectations of economists polled by Reuters and marked an increase from September's print of 3.5%.
October's reading puts U.K. inflation at a one-year high.
Britain's Office for National Statistics said the largest contributor to the rising inflation rate was motor fuel costs, which rose 33.2% year-on-year. It was the largest one-year rise in motor fuel prices since July 2022, when energy costs surged in the wake of Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
The U.K. is a net energy importer, making it vulnerable to external energy shocks such as the one sparked by the disruption of trade through the Strait of Hormuz.
Oil prices have retreated from their wartime highs of well above $100 a barrel, but remain elevated.
The ONS said Wednesday that the average price of petrol rose by 5.2 pence (7 cents) per liter between September and October this year, compared with a 0.7 pence per liter rise in the same period a year earlier. The average petrol price stood at 174.7 pence per liter in October, the highest it's been since Aug. 2022.
Diesel prices rose by 7 pence per liter in October, with the average price reaching 199.5 pence per liter - the highest since ONS records began in 1996.
The Iran war has seen energy costs surge in various major economies. U.S. diesel prices hit $6 per gallon for the first time ever in September.
Since the U.S.-Iran war began in late February, inflation has risen sharply after a brief dip thanks to an energy price cap that was revised upward in July.
Earlier this month, the Bank of England hiked interest rates for the first time in three years, warning that inflation could hit 5% by mid-2027.
Governor Andrew Bailey said in the bank's November Monetary Policy Report that without "real progress towards settling the conflict in the Middle East... risks to energy prices, and so to inflation, are to the upside."
Markets are currently pricing in around an 80% chance of a rate hike at the Bank of England's final meeting of the year.
Scott Gardner, investment strategist at JPMorgan Personal Investing, said in a note following the release that a recent climb down in oil prices following the U.S. midterm elections could offer some relief in future inflation readings.
But he added: "While this may be a short-term reprieve for motorists, the broader outlook for UK inflation remains challenging as we approach 2027. After hiking interest rates at the start of the month, the Bank will be wary of a sustained uptick in inflation that could be here to stay."
