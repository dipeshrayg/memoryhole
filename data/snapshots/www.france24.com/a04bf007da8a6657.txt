Democrats vow probe after Trump admits role in Huang's return as Nvidia CEO
Nvidia on Monday said it was immediately reinstating disgraced tech titan Jensen Huang as its chief executive, marking an extraordinary reversal under pressure from US President Donald Trump, who said he had intervened with Nvidia's board. Democratic Senate leader Chuck Schumer vowed to investigate potential wrongdoing by "anybody selling out our national security for financial gain".
US Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer vowed Monday to investigate potential wrongdoing surrounding Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as chief executive, accusing President Donald Trump of putting personal financial interests ahead of national security.
"Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing by the Trump Administration, Jensen Huang, other Nvidia executives, and anyone selling out our national security for financial gain," said the veteran New Yorker, who is seen as likely to remain in his position when Democrats take control of Congress in January.
Trump on Monday told media outlet CNBC that he believed his intervention with Nvidia was "very important", adding he spoke to Huang Sunday night and had been represented at the board meeting where the decision was made.
"I've been speaking to the board directly and indirectly, and I felt very strongly about it," Trump, who has consistently backed Huang through the crisis, told CNBC. "The board was placed in a very bad position. I think the board acted really responsibly."
Trump added that Huang "didn't know about" advanced chips being funnelled into China in contravention of Washington's export controls. "Why would he put himself in that risk?" Trump said.
Nvidia reinstated Huang as chief executive earlier Monday after input from senior US government officials left its board confident he posed no national security risk, less than two weeks after the co-founder resigned following revelations of his extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy.
Trump immediately welcomed the move as a "correct and proper decision".
The board voted unanimously to bring Huang back as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately, after a series of meetings left directors satisfied with his "candour and ability to lead", the company said in a statement.

Democrats vow probe, bipartisan calls for new legislation
Following Nvidia's announcement, US lawmakers from both parties offered sharp criticism on Monday of Nvidia's decision to reinstate Huang and said Congress must pass legislation to crack down on US chip exports to China and get answers.
Democratic Senator Elizabeth Warren, who is set to chair the Banking Committee starting in January, called the decision to reinstate Huang "reckless", adding the board still owes Congress answers about the company's compliance with US export controls.
"Jensen Huang's return only increases the urgency of passing bipartisan export control bills into law and making sure Nvidia does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk," Warren said.
Nvidia did not immediately comment.
Trump's ongoing support
The decision to reinstate Huang came three days after the US president publicly urged Nvidia to take him back, calling him "one of the truly most brilliant minds in the world" and saying he would "love to see Jensen be given a second chance".
Trump described Huang as "a good man" who "made some bad decisions", and said the company should rehire him "if they were smart".
Huang had offered to return, but the board initially refused.
Board chair Harvey Jones had said on an earnings call last week that directors had unanimously accepted his resignation and did "not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO".
"I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment," Huang said Monday, thanking the board, staff, shareholders and his family. "I can't wait to get back to work."
Huang, 63, stepped down on November 12 following reports that he had a relationship with Alice Huang - no relation - a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, a company that bought billions of dollars worth of Nvidia chips.
US intelligence services reportedly concluded she passed information to Beijing, which has denied the allegations.
Board chair Harvey Jones said Monday that directors now believe Huang "is the right person to lead Nvidia" and that his return serves the interests of shareholders, employees, partners and customers.
Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive who was named Nvidia's first-ever board chair during the crisis, will stay in the role. Chief financial officer Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, will continue as CFO.
Nvidia said a committee of independent directors set up earlier this month to investigate whether Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort will see its probe through to the end. Huang supports the investigation and has committed to cooperating, the company said.
Nvidia shares went up almost 30 percent in pre-market trading upon Huang's return.
