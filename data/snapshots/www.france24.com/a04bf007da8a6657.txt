Democrats vow probe after Trump presses Huang's return as Nvidia CEO
Nvidia on Monday said it was immediately reinstating disgraced tech titan Jensen Huang as its chief executive, marking an extraordinary reversal under pressure from US President Donald Trump, who said he had directly intervened within Nvidia's board. Democratic Senate leader Chuck Schumer vowed to investigate potential wrongdoing by "anybody selling out our national security for financial gain".
US President Donald Trump praised Nvidia's decision Monday to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive, as Democratic leaders vowed investigations and prominent Republicans questioned whether his return posed a national security risk.
Trump also posted a doctored version of the famous photograph of him raising his fist after surviving a 2024 assassination attempt, replacing himself with Huang, who brandished a red bra beneath an Nvidia flag.
The image appeared to allude to the scandal that forced Huang, co-founder of the AI chip giant, to resign less than two weeks earlier following revelations of extramarital affairs, including with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy.
"The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO," Trump wrote on Truth Social, adding that the government had no reason to believe his return posed a national security risk.
In a telephone interview with CNBC, Trump suggested the scandal could prove to be "a net positive" by highlighting Nvidia's technological advances.
"I don't think he's going to be diminished at all," he said, predicting Huang would return at "an almost higher level".
He said the attorney general had confirmed that US authorities possessed no evidence Huang had engaged in unlawful conduct, but urged Nvidia to continue investigating its compliance with restrictions on advanced chip sales to China.
Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer and House speaker-designate Hakeem Jeffries vowed sweeping investigations into Nvidia, Trump and his senior officials, with Jeffries singling out Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.
"We will not allow America's national security to be sold out by the Trump administration for personal financial gain," Jeffries said.

'What changed?'
Huang is already scheduled to face questioning behind closed doors by three House committees on December 1 over his relationship with the suspected Chinese intelligence operative and concerns about Nvidia's chip exports.
And the Republican-led Senate Banking Committee has announced that his appearance scheduled for December 3 will be livestreamed.
"We still don't know what information Chinese intelligence may have obtained from him," said House Intelligence Committee Democrat Raja Krishnamoorthi, demanding a full public congressional hearing.

Senator Elizabeth Warren called the decision "reckless" and demanded details of what input senior administration officials had provided in support of Huang's return.
Republican Senator John Curtis raised similar questions, noting that Nvidia's board had reversed its opposition to reinstating Huang while an independent investigation remained unfinished.
"Who in our government weighed in? What did they tell the board?" he wrote on X. "I'd like to know what changed."
Senator Pete Ricketts, also a Republican, said Huang's behavior did not warrant reinstatement and insisted the move did not excuse him from testifying before Congress.
Former Republican House speaker Newt Gingrich called for scrutiny of what he described as a national security issue "of enormous proportions".
Outgoing Republican congressman Don Bacon alleged Huang had been "compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy", demanding answers about who had authorised advanced chip sales to China.
And Republican Senator John Cornyn offered a terse response to Trump's assurances.
"No national security risk? Based on what?" he wrote on X.
Trump declined earlier Monday to comment on a New York Times report that the woman at the center of the scandal had been in contact with an official from China's principal intelligence agency during her affair with Huang.
