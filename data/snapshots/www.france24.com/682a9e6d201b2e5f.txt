'My door is open and my hand is extended': Eisenkot begins trying to build a coalition government
Israeli President Isaac Herzog formally gave opposition politician Gadi Eisenkot the first chance to form the next government Sunday evening.
Complex coalition negotiations still lie ahead, but it marked a significant step in the former military chief's effort to oust Israel's longest-serving prime minister, Benjamin Netanyahu, after neither bloc emerged from the October 27 parliamentary elections with a majority.
"I hope with all my heart that whatever government is formed will lead to unity, connection and healing among all segments of our people - for our very existence depends on it, and we have only one country," Herzog said at the ceremony at the presidential residence in Jerusalem.

Iran FM says no negotiations since 'shock' of US midterms
Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said that mediators striving for a peace deal between the US and Iran had not travelled at all since the "shock" of the US midterm elections.
"We now have to see how long it will take for them to digest these results, in order to determine how we are going to move forward," the top Iranian diplomat told state TV.
Various regional powers, including Qatar and Pakistan, have been trying for months without success to bring a definitive halt to the war launched by the United States and Israel in late February.
Outright fighting has largely subsided, but Iran continues in its efforts to blockade the Strait of Hormuz, vital to global energy supplies, while the US Navy maintains its own counterblockade of Iranian ports.
"For the past few days, there has been no more travel (by mediators), and the reason for this is the shock that the election results in the United States have caused to everyone," Araghchi said.

The economic challenges ahead for Israel
Israel's next government will face several major economic challenges, and the outcome of the coalition negotiations could have a direct impact on how they are addressed.
The central issue is how to finance the country's security needs while dealing with the economic consequences of prolonged conflict, rising public debt and pressure on the cost of living.
For Gadi Eisenkot, if he succeeds in forming a government, the challenge will be to balance defence spending, economic recovery and the demands of potential coalition partners.

Iran says 3 tankers caught fire in Hormuz
Three oil tankers have caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, where Iran regularly targets ships that transit without its authorisation, the Revolutionary Guards said in a statement.
"Since the early hours of this morning, three non-compliant tankers have exploded and caught fire while they were sailing in the illegal channel in the Strait of Hormuz," the ideological arm of Iran's military said in a statement. It did not specify the names or nationality of the tankers.
Also on Sunday, British maritime agency UKMTO reported that a tanker had caught fire in the strait - off the coast of Khasab, Oman - a day earlier.
"Military authorities reported the tanker is on fire which has resulted in the evacuation of the crew," the agency said in a statement, also reporting another tanker struck by an "unknown projectile" on Saturday.

US launches task force to disrupt land transit routes into Iran
The US military has announced it will create a 20-member task force to find ways to disrupt land routes into Iran that it says are used for shipments of weapons and illicit materials.
The restrictions would expand on a US naval blockade of Iranian ports during the two countries' eight-month war, in which Tehran has also restricted passage through the critical Strait of Hormuz trade route, choking global energy supplies.
The task force's work "will further constrain Iran's ability to finance terrorism, rebuild weapons factories, and replenish their military", US Central Command chief Brad Cooper said in a statement posted on X on Saturday.
The team of both civilian and military personnel based in Florida would coordinate with US government agencies on "preventing the movement of advanced weapons systems, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials, and illegal revenue streams across Iran's land borders and the Caspian Sea," the statement added.
At least 33 people killed in Saudi air strikes in Yemen on Saturday, health ministry says
At least 33 people were killed and dozens more were injured in Saudi airstrikes on a village near Sanaa on Saturday, Yemen's Houthi-run health ministry said.
The ministry said the strikes hit homes in the town of Bani Hushaysh, a Houthi stronghold, and that most of those killed were women and children.
Abdul-Aziz al-Dailami, director of Sehatuna Hospital in Sanaa, said many victims remained trapped under the rubble, including first responders.
