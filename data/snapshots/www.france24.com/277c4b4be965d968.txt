Lost homes, stolen freedom: National Book Award winners on exile and imprisonment
Issued on: Modified:
Two books about losing everything have just won America's most prestigious literary honours. Hannah Lillith Assadi took the National Book Award for fiction for "Paradiso 17", a novel inspired by her late father, who was driven from Palestine in 1948 during the Nakba. Abdelrahman ElGendy won the non-fiction prize for "Huna", his memoir of six years as a political prisoner in Egypt, arrested at 17 alongside his father. ElGendy couldn't be in New York to collect his award: his visa was denied.
In this edition of arts24, both writers join us to talk about displacement, memory and holding on to who you are when everything is taken away.
