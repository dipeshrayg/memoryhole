Macron vows 'concrete' measures to address student demands after unrest
French President Emmanuel Macron on Monday said the government will announce "concrete and strong" measures to address the demands by high school students after their protests demanding better education conditions triggered weeks of nationwide unrest. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu is set to hold a news conference after meeting with high school student unions.
French President Emmanuel Macron said on Monday that his Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu will announce "strong measures" to assuage French student protesters, while noting that next weekend could nevertheless bring the risk of more unrest.
"I have also asked the government to respond to the legitimate demands that lie at the root of this social movement," he told reporters in Marseille on Monday.
Lecornu is set to hold a news conference later Monday after meeting with unions of high school students.
During a visit to Marseille, Macron repeated what Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said earlier on Monday, namely that more protests are expected next Saturday.
The French government is seeking to draw a line under weeks of protests that have been marred by violence due to widespread anger over the rising cost of living, with nationwide demonstrations on October 17 degenerating into some of the country's worst rioting in years.
The demonstrations come at a critical moment for France, six months ahead of a presidential election to replace centrist Macron.
The frontrunner - far-right leader Marine le Pen - held an election rally in Orleans Sunday, a day after yet more demonstrations also saw some violent clashes on the sidelines.
She vowed to "restore the rule of law" if elected next year. Polls show the protests and ensuing disorder have boosted her support, as the government and Macron faced sharp criticism over the unrest.
Authorities have admitted to security and intelligence failures that paved the way for the clashes, large-scale looting and vandalism of public property that erupted on October 17, and had stepped up security for Saturday's protests.
While Nunez said the outcome of Saturday's rallies was more "positive" than a week ago, street violence still erupted in the French capital and a number of other cities including Marseille.

During his visit to Marseille, France's second-largest city, Macron made a stop at the city hall after it was damaged in the protest violence on October 17.
He also meets with shopkeepers who had fallen victim to looting and vandalism, according to the French presidential palace.
After this weekend's protests, many shopfronts in the city centre were still boarded up early Monday, just hours before Macron's arrival.
Macron's visit was intended to demonstrate that "the state is present, standing firm and fulfilling its mission to protect", an aide said.
'High level of violence'
A total of 1,435 people were arrested on the sidelines of the most recent weekend protests, Nunez said on Monday.
"The day was brought under control and there were no serious incidents," he told broadcaster BFMTV.
"Nevertheless, we saw a very high level of violence directed at the internal security forces," Nunez added.
He said that 267 police officers and gendarmes were injured, along with 150 protesters, including three who were seriously hurt in Toulouse, Reims and Clermont-Ferrand.
The unrest followed weeks of demonstrations by students campaigning for better learning conditions.
Several teenage protesters have been seriously injured and thousands arrested.
The police response to their demonstrations, which included blockades of schools, has been criticised as heavy-handed and sparked a broader movement against the cost of living and police violence.
In Paris on Monday, Lecornu meets student leaders, with National Education Minister Édouard Geffray and Higher Education Minister Philippe Baptiste joining him for the talks. Following that meeting, Geffray and Baptiste will hold a news conference.
Macron already met representatives of secondary school pupils last Friday, but they emerged from the talks urging students to keep protesting until the government put forward concrete proposals to address their demands.
On top of the protests, the country was last week shaken by a shooting that killed one person and wounded five, including a 12-year-old girl, outside Paris.
Macron has branded the shooting a "racist terrorist attack", as most of the victims were from foreign backgrounds.
