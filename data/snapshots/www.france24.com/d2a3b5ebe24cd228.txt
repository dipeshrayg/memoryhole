Yemen's army says Houthi fighters closing in on government stronghold of Aden
Yemen's military on Tuesday said that Houthi fighters were closing in on the internationally recognised government's stronghold of Aden from about 60km (40 miles) away and vowed to repel any assault. A military spokesman said defences were prepared following Houthi advances and government pullbacks near the Red Sea.
Yemen's armed forces said Tuesday that Houthi fighters were ready to attack the internationally recognised government's stronghold of Aden, vowing to head off any potential assault.
"Yes, Houthi forces are about 60 kilometres (40 miles) from the city. We take that seriously, and we are ready for it," said military spokesman Colonel Majid al-Nuzaili during a briefing.

"They are now ready to attack. We have made our preparations to defend Aden."
The comments come on the heels of recent advances by the Houthis this month, as the Yemeni government pulled back its forces from areas near the Red Sea to reinforce troops "where current circumstances require", with securing Aden and nearby provinces taking priority.
On November 15, six Yemeni sources told AFP the Houthis had seized control of the Al-Maqatirah and Al-Madaribah districts and positioned themselves 60 kilometres from the southern city.
"The situation is not where we want it to be. But it's not as dire as reported," Nuzaili insisted.
"Taking Aden is a hypothetical situation. It will not happen," he said. "Our forces and our government will make sure that Aden is not at risk."
On Tuesday, Yemen's leader Rashad al-Alimi - the head of the country's presidential council - struck a defiant tone in the face of the Houthi threat.
"We trust in the ability of the Yemeni people and their armed forces to end the Houthi threat, no matter how the front lines shift," he wrote in a post on X.

The Houthis have repeatedly struck the city of Aden in recent weeks, including an assault on October 15 that targeted the presidential palace and left four people dead, according to government military sources.
The Iran-backed group previously launched an unsuccessful bid to seize Aden early in the war in 2015.
Yemen's internationally recognised government has been based in Aden since it was driven from the capital Sanaa by the Houthis in 2014.
Yemen, embroiled in more than a decade of civil war, was sucked into the wider US-Iran conflict in July when a truce between the Houthis and the Saudi-backed government unravelled.
Since then, the fighting has left more than 1,600 people dead and over 300,000 displaced in the country, the poorest on the Arabian Peninsula, according to the latest UN toll.
