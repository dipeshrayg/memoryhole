Live: Microsoft reports 'improvements' to Outlook services in North America, Europe
Microsoft's Outlook email service has been disrupted since late Thursday as the tech giant struggles to fix issues with its servers, hitting businesses and government agencies that use the popular work platforms in 74 countries and territories. The company said Friday it was making progress in restoring Outlook services in North America and Europe, with users reporting improvements in mailbox access. Follow our liveblog for the latest updates.
Microsoft said that efforts to repair a major outage of Outlook were not going as expected and that the firm was seeking alternative solutions as users around the world were left without its crucial services Friday.
The disruption, which Microsoft attributed to "elevated load" on its infrastructure, hit email as well as products such as Teams and the AI assistant Copilot for more than 12 hours.
It said it was "not aware of any malicious activity" that could have caused the outage.
The impact of the outage has been felt globally, with users ranging from companies to governments reporting that they were unable to communicate via Outlook.
"We've monitored the status of the changes applied to improve serviceability and confirmed they're not taking as expected," Microsoft said on X.
The disruption went global within an hour, knocking out Microsoft products in 74 countries and territories, outage tracker Downdetector reported.
A map posted by Downdetector on X showed the outage affected users across the Americas and parts of Europe, Asia and Africa.
One of the impacted countries was New Zealand, where the foreign ministry sent a notice saying its "ability to receive emails has been affected by the global Microsoft outage. This also impacts all New Zealand Embassies, Consulates and Representative Offices around the world."
Microsoft initially said it was investigating outage reports "in the Americas", but later confirmed that the issue apparently affected "Microsoft 365 service connectivity for users in any region".
Microsoft 365 is the company's cloud-based subscription service that includes popular applications such as Outlook and Teams.
"We've determined a portion of infrastructure is experiencing elevated load across multiple regions, which is likely contributing to the connectivity errors," the company wrote on X.
"We're aggressively pursuing all pathways to reduce impact," it added.
Downdetector, which tracks online outages, showed a sharp decline around 0450 GMT in users' reports of disruptions in Washington and Hong Kong.
