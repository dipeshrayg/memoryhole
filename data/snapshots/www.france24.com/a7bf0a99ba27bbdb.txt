Jamaica's beaches are increasingly off limits to locals
Issued on:
Jamaica usually conjures up images of white sandy beaches, Rastafari culture and Bob Marley - but another reality exists. On this small Caribbean island, less than 1 percent of the coastline is freely accessible to local people. The rest has gradually been privatised, mostly for large hotel developments and private homes.
Yet access to the sea is vital to Jamaican society and the local economy - fishing and selling crafts to tourists are two industries that depend on it. The Jamaica Beach Birthright Environmental Movement, or JABBEM, has taken five hotel projects to court in an effort to protect public access to the beaches. Jamaicans are particularly vulnerable to property developers, with the vast majority lacking formal titles to the land they live on.
Our regional correspondents Laurence Cuvillier and Matthieu Comin report.
