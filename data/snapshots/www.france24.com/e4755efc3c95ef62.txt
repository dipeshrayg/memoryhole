French student groups call for protests to continue after meeting with Macron
Representatives from French high school student groups called for "continued mobilisation" after meeting with President Emmanuel Macron at the Élysée palace for the first time since protests started more than a month ago. Earlier, Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said some 70,000 security forces would be deployed to keep order throughout France for planned protests on Saturday. Read our blog to see how the day's events unfolded.
- Lyon and Marseille banned all demonstrations on Saturday ahead of a new round of planned nationwide protests against France's rising cost of living. French President Emmanuel Macron said after meeting with intelligence services that all demonstrations not organised by workers' unions would be banned.
- French Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu met with representatives from France's political parties. Manuel Bompard, party coordinator of the left-wing France Unbowed, accused the premier of having wasted their time by refusing to address the demands of the weeks-long student protest movement.
- A French administrative court suspended a Paris police order banning a demonstration organised by university students. Hundreds of students took part in the peaceful march.
(FRANCE 24 with Reuters, AP and AFP)
