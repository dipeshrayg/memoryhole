Romelu Lukaku, Belgium's all-time leading goalscorer, has announced his retirement from international football after 16 years, saying he was "at peace" with his decision.
"These 16 years I gave all I had for the national team. It was a nice ride and I enjoyed every single moment," Belgium's record goalscorer said on Instagram on Monday.
With 94 goals in 135 games, Lukaku is the fifth-highest scorer in men's international football history and featured in four World Cups for the Red Devils, helping them reach the quarterfinals of this year's tournament before losing to eventual winners Spain.
The 33-year-old, who now plays for Turkish club Fenerbahce, made his debut in 2010 as a teenager and was an unused substitute in Sunday's 4-1 Nations League win over Italy.
"To the Belgium fans, thank you for the support you gave our team. I am proud to have represented my country in 4 World Cups and 3 European Championships.
"I will never forget those moments. I'm at peace with my decision to stop here," Lukaku said.
The former Chelsea, Manchester United and Inter Milan forward was part of Belgium's so-called "golden generation".
Lukaku, alongside Kevin De Bruyne, Thibaut Courtois and Eden Hazard, finished third at the 2018 World Cup, a best-ever finish for Belgium in the tournament, but they failed to lift a trophy during the period.
"I played with amazing teammates that I will always be grateful for," Lukaku said.
"I want to thank them all. I want to thank all the coaches as well for pushing and trusting me to help the team," he added.
Inspiration to generations of football fans
The national team paid homage to Lukaku shortly after his announcement.
"More than a goalscorer, you have been a leader, a strong personality and an inspiration to generations of Belgian football fans," said a post on Instagram by the Red Devils. "You have written an incredible chapter in the history of the Red Devils."
A host of fellow Belgian players also paid tribute to Lukaku.
"Legend, my brother," midfielder Axel Witsel posted on Instagram.
"Thank you for everything my bro," Courtois said.
"So many amazing memories," the Real Madrid goalkeeper added.
Lukaku's last appearance for his country came in October's 4-1 loss to France.
"Beyond his exceptional performances on the pitch, he has been a true leader, with a strong personality and an essential role in the dressing room," Lukaku's former teammate and now Belgium's technical director, Simon Mignolet, said in a statement from the Belgian football association.
"We respect his decision and are deeply grateful to him for everything he has brought to our national team," the former Liverpool goalkeeper added.
Belgium's first game without Lukaku will be a Nations League last-eight fixture in March.
"We must now turn our attention to the future and prepare for the Nations League quarterfinals ... with the ambition of continuing to make progress as a team," Mignolet said.
