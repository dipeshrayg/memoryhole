When Israel launched a sweeping military assault on Iran with the United States in February, the Israeli government enjoyed overwhelming support from the Jewish Israeli public.
Opposition parties lent their support to the army and backed the audacious step. Enmity towards the government in Iran is an issue mainstream Zionist parties agree on.
Then in June, Israel's role in the confrontation halted abruptly with an announcement from US President Donald Trump, who said Iran and Israel had agreed to stop attacking each other.
That announcement - and Netanyahu's decision to abide by it at the time - were unpopular in Israel. According to the Israel Democracy Institute, 65 to 70 percent of Jewish Israelis believe that stopping the war did not serve Israel's national security interests.
As Israelis prepare to vote in national elections on October 27, Iran remains an important election issue for many. Opposition parties present Iran as an example of what they say are Netanyahu's many failures. They promise to reset security and foreign policy if they're elected.
The frontrunner, Gadi Eisenkot, says the conflict is unfinished. While saying that a change in Iran's government was an unrealistic goal, Eisenkot accuses Netanyahu of doing little to dismantle Iran's nuclear programme. In September at the inauguration of his new party, Yashar, Eisenkot told supporters: "Ultimately, it must end in the destruction or dismantling of the Iranian nuclear programme, a goal not yet achieved with 440kg [970lb] of highly enriched uranium already there, which could quickly be turned into several nuclear devices."
Former Prime Minister Yair Lapid, who is now the head of the opposition in the Knesset, accuses Netanyahu of a strategic failure. He recently told the Atlantic Council that toppling the administration in Tehran is a realistic objective that requires a military escalation: "What Israel should do or the alliance should do is bomb all the energy facilities, especially on Kharg Island, but other places as well in order to make sure that the Iranian economy is demolished. And the reason is that it's the only thing that will overturn the regime."
Bombing this civilian infrastructure was actually something Netanyahu had lobbied for himself. An international outcry about such bombings likely constituting war crimes and Trump's refusal to offer backing put the brakes on the plan.
Naftali Bennett, another former prime minister, says Israel must ensure its military dominance over the entire region. For Bennett, toppling the government in Iran and crushing it is a must. But he says that is not enough. The right-wing politician calls Qatar an "enemy" and says Turkiye is "the new Iran", going as far as saying that military confrontation with the NATO member was inevitable.
"We will build an alliance of moderate forces in the region against the Islamist forces, against Iran, against Qatar, Turkiye and all the radical forces. We will take proactive actions to shape the region," Bennett recently told supporters.
Eisenkot and other opposition leaders do not rule out renewed confrontations. They focus on their confidence in the military and security establishment in assessing the dangers and dealing with them appropriately.
But opposition leaders have voiced concern that Netanyahu might resort to military escalation before the elections to tilt the balance in his favour or even disrupt them altogether - even though the prime minister said last week that he did not plan to attack Iran before the vote.
Those fears were elevated after Netanyahu posted a video from the Tel Nof Airbase warning that Israel's enemies might attack before the elections. Other news reports said Netanyahu has shared intelligence on new threats from Iran with the US president.
Netanyahu was quickly rebuked by opposition leaders, who accused him of "political fearmongering".
Lapid openly questioned Netanyahu's motives. Lapid also accused him of using the Israeli military and bases as "backdrops" for election campaign videos.
Ultimately, most Israeli opposition parties and leaders aren't opposed to war with Iran, and many are in fact even more hawkish in their approach towards Tehran than Netanyahu. They just don't want a new military escalation before the elections.
