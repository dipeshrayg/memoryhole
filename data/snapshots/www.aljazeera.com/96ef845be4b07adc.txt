French president Emmanuel Macron has announced on X that European Union leaders have agreed to appoint Pablo Hernandez de Cos as the next head of the European Central Bank.
The 55-year-old will become the first Spaniard to take the role, and succeeds France's Christine Lagarde at the Frankfurt-based institution at a challenging time for the eurozone.
Diplomats have described the economist as a 'steady hand' to steer the institution, with a reputation for pragmatism and consensus-building.
Currently head of the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), the Basel-based forum for the world's major central banks, the Spaniard will take over in February.
As the Bank of Spain's chief economist and later governor, he navigated the aftermath of Spain's 2010s banking crisis, years of ultra-low inflation, COVID-19 and the 2022 inflation surge that followed the pandemic and Russia's invasion of Ukraine.
He also chaired the Basel Committee on Banking Supervision, the global standard-setter for banks.
