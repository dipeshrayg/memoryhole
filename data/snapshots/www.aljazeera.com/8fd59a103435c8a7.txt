A bomb attack at a mosque in Pakistan's restive north-west has killed at least 15 people, with more than 50 reported injured.
An explosive-laden car was rammed into the entrance of a mosque at a police headquarters in Kohat, in the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa near the Afghan border, as people gathered for Friday prayers.
At least 15 bodies and more than 50 injured people were taken to hospital in the town, Dr Tahir Shah said, according to the dpa news agency. The death toll could rise as several injured people were in a critical condition, he added.
There was no immediate claim of responsibility for the attack. However, both a regional affiliate of the ISIL armed group and the Pakistani Taliban (TTP) are active in the region and regularly target security forces.
Violence has surged in Pakistan's remote regions near the Afghan border in recent years. Islamabad accuses Afghanistan's Taliban rulers of allowing militants who orchestrate attacks in Pakistan to operate from Afghan territory.
Officials in Kabul deny the accusation. However, the issue has seen the pair swap strikes across the border across recent months.
