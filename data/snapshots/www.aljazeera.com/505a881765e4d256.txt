Pakistan had all but lost hope of winning their One-Day International (ODI) cricket match against England when all-rounder Shadab Khan walked onto the pitch to bat and stormed off it with an unlikely come-from-behind win in the bag, in front of a raucous home crowd in Lahore.
The hosts were reduced to 154-9 in the chase of 242 on Sunday, and England were in sight of their first win of the tri-nation series when Shadab and fast bowler Haris Rauf came together for the last-wicket partnership.
From there on, Shadab put on a run-chase masterclass, hitting an unbeaten century to give Pakistan a one-wicket victory and book a spot in the series final.
The 28-year-old launched a surprise counterattack on England, ending up with 113 not out off 96 balls alongside Rauf (7 not out off 19). Shadab and Rauf's partnership was the highest last-wicket stand in a successful run chase in ODI history.
Earlier in England's innings, Ben Duckett was the lone batting saviour, scoring 105 off 111 for his fifth ODI century. Once Arafat Minhas dismissed him, England looked hopeless as Shaheen Afridi's impressive bowling cleaned up their batting lineup.
He took his fourth wicket (4-46) to remove Henry Crocombe. England's last-wicket pair added 39 to post a below-par total of 241 all out from 46.4 overs.
England then decimated Pakistan's top order as Jamie Overton (3-43) and Liam Dawson (2-19) removed Fakhar Zaman, Saim Ayub, Babar Azam and Saad Baig cheaply.
The hosts ultimately slumped to 154-9, having been 74-3. By this point, England had all but secured victory as the home crowd began leaving Gaddafi Stadium after the ninth wicket fell.
Shadab stepped up to flip the script and produce the batting performance of a lifetime when he prised victory away from England, particularly after his leg-before-wicket dismissal was overturned on review.
"I dedicate this win to the two most wonderful women in my life: my mother & my wife," Shadab wrote on social media following the victory.
"Special mention for [Haris Rauf], we win only as a team and he showed that today in the partnership. Thank you to my entire team."
'Won it for Pakistan out of nowhere'
The victory went some way towards restoring faith among Pakistani cricket fans, who have endured months of dismal performances at global tournaments, as well as bilateral series.
It was "an innings to remember", as former Pakistani cricketer Mohammad Hafeez put it, while teammate Imad Wasim praised Shadab for handling the extreme pressure.
"That was a sensational innings by Shadab Khan," Sri Lanka's veteran cricketer Angelo Mathews wrote on X. "Won it for Pakistan out of nowhere."
Barbadian wicketkeeper-batsman Shai Hope called the match "absolute cinema" while former West Indies cricketer Daren Sammy termed it "another thriller", referring to Sri Lanka's thrashing of England on Thursday when they recorded the second-highest successful run chase in ODI history.
Praise and memes kept pouring in on social media as one user joked about making a documentary of the game, while others reiterated the need for more trilateral series in ODI format.
Speaking after the match, Pakistan's captain Afridi said he was pleased with the team's overall performance, especially the way Shadab and Haris finished the game.
"We thought we had a lot of variation in the team, so that's why we brought Shadab in," the fast bowler said.
"We need top-order runs; we're struggling. I hope in the next few games we'll bring that energy.
"I think tonight we'll be celebrating; tomorrow back to normal."
'Incredible run chase'
English cricket commentator Nasser Hussain praised Pakistan's "incredible run chase" as he credited Shadab's change of intent in the heat of the moment.
"Shadab Khan and Haris Rauf; that partnership, you had to be really switched on and smart how you played it," Hussain said on Sky Sports.
"The moment they changed intent, the moment Shadab said 'right we've gone one way, it's gone nowhere, we're going to play our shots', there's a lesson to be learnt," Hussain said, referring to the previous game when England were put under pressure.
"Brilliant change of intent from Shadab Khan; that game had gone from them. Took one individual backed up by Haris Rauf to bring it back."
Former Pakistan captain Mohammad Yousuf echoed a similar sentiment.
"Superb display of positive intent! Fearless, aggressive and full of belief. Take a bow," he wrote on X.
Speaking alongside Hussain, retired English cricketer Chris Woakes agreed on Shadab's spectacular performance: "A hundred not out at number eight is pretty special!"
