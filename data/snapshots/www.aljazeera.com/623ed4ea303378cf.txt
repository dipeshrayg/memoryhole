The crew of a Russian ship in the Gulf of Finland, hit by drones likely launched by Ukraine, has now been evacuated to a nearby cargo ship and will reportedly be taken directly back to Russia.
Finnish authorities say the Sparta II has links to Russia's armed forces and is currently anchored off the southern Finnish town of Loviisa, where firefighting efforts are still ongoing at sea.
Operations at the scene, involving extinguishing the fire on board the Sparta II and cooling the vessel, are being carried out by rescue units and patrol ships from the Finnish Border Guard, a patrol aircraft and helicopters. Two Finnish civilian rescue tugs are also involved, as well as an Estonian navy patrol ship.
"At the request of the Border Guard, the Russian rescue ship Murman is also participating in the rescue operations," Finnish authorities said in a statement.
Finnish Defence Minister Antti Hakkanen told the Helsingin Sanomat newspaper that the Sparta II would be towed out of Finland's territorial waters on Saturday night.
How the attack unfolded
On Saturday morning, Finland's Ministry of Defence said its forces detected a drone attack on a Russian vessel in international waters, with initial reports saying the ship had sustained damage. Finnish Border Guard units were dispatched to the scene, and a photograph taken by a Finnish patrol plane showed flames and thick smoke coming from the rear of the ship.
The attack happened west of Gogland Island, off the coast of the Finnish town of Loviisa, and Finland quickly implemented temporary air and sea traffic restrictions in the eastern part of the gulf.
A Russian helicopter has since violated Finnish airspace, venturing more than 4km (2.5 miles) into Finnish territory.
"The suspected airspace violation is possibly related to rescue or tracking operations of a damaged cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland. The Defence Forces and the Border Guard are handling the situation in cooperation with other authorities," said Hakkanen.
By Saturday afternoon, the Finnish Defence Forces said the strike was likely carried out by Ukraine. Earlier, Finland's Prime Minister Petteri Orpo wrote on X that "war is spilling over into our immediate vicinity. The Finnish authorities are handling the situation in the Gulf of Finland, where a drone strike has hit a Russian merchant vessel. Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor".
What do we know about Sparta II?
Finland's Border Guard said the Sparta II is known to have links to Russia's armed forces. The roll-on/roll-off ship was built in the year 2000 and sails under a Russian flag, according to the VesselFinder website.
"At the moment, there is no confirmed information about what cargo is on board, but it cannot be ruled out that there ‌‌‌‌is military materiel on board," said Commander Ilja Iljin from the Finnish Border Guard.
The governor of Russia's Leningrad region, Alexander Drozdenko, said earlier on Saturday that damage to the ship had yet to be fully assessed.
"Three nautical miles [5.6km] from the Russian Federation's border, a cargo vessel bound for Kaliningrad was attacked by two drones," he wrote on the messaging app Max, shortly after the attack.
Kaliningrad's Governor Alexey Besprozvannykh said supplies to the Russian exclave were not at risk.
One of the major risks of attacking any vessel is environmental damage if it leaks or sinks, a scenario that Baltic Sea nations treat with high importance. That risk is "further amplified by the poor state of some of the so-called 'Shadow Fleet' tankers" that Russia operates, according to Robin Haggblom, senior adviser for Risk Intelligence and an expert in Finnish maritime issues.
"Actions to prevent environmental damage has also been emphasised in Finnish Border Guard communication regarding this event. Luckily in this case, we don't yet have ice coverage in the Gulf of Finland, which would impact any oil spill recovery efforts that may be needed," Haggblom told Al Jazeera.
Crowded shipping lanes
The narrow and shallow waters of the Baltic Sea are some of the most crowded shipping lanes in the world. The Helsinki to Tallinn ferry route had 7.5 million passenger crossings last year. Other passenger routes link Baltic ports and capital cities, and cargo vessels and military shipping contribute to the total number of vessels in the relatively small body of water.
So if Ukraine is using drones - maritime or aerial - to hit Russian ships, there could be risks to civilian vessels.
"There obviously always is a risk of collateral damage. However, so far Ukraine does seem to be taking a number of steps to minimise the risks involved, with the Sparta II, for example, apparently having been identified and hit a handful of kilometres from Russian waters," added Haggblom.
Earlier this month, Finland's military said it had detected a strike on a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland, which Ukraine took credit for. Helsinki later said it had raised concerns with Kyiv about strikes in the area.
"It is the same with the strike on the Russian corvette earlier, where it is reported the Ukrainians were able to pick it out and target that specific vessel among perhaps a dozen other ships. There are also unconfirmed indications that Sparta II was targeted specifically, and not just as a general Russia-associated merchant vessel," Haggblom added.
Ukraine and Russia have attacked each other's shipping throughout the war, mostly in the Black Sea.
