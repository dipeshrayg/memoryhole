CNC climbs six places in national workplace inclusion ranking
CNC rises to 30th in the Inclusive Top 50 UK Employers List, recognising progress in workplace culture, inclusion and colleague voice.
The Civil Nuclear Constabulary (CNC) has risen from 36th to 30th in the Inclusive Top 50 UK Employers List for 2026/27.
The result recognises progress across the force to strengthen workplace culture, remove barriers and give officers and staff a greater voice in decisions that affect them.
The national ranking assesses organisations on evidence including recruitment, training, workforce data and diversity initiatives.
Over the past year, more than 300 recommendations and areas for improvement have been brought together in a single Integrated Cultural Action Plan, with clear ownership and oversight. A new Positive Workplace Forum also gives representatives from operational locations a direct route to raise issues and comment on organisational proposals.
Other changes include stronger equality assessment of people policies, more inclusive recruitment and development practices, and additional support for colleagues with caring responsibilities, disabilities and health conditions. Four employee-led Affinity Networks also use colleagues' lived experience to help shape policy and improve workplace support.
CNC's latest figures show its mean hourly gender pay gap has fallen from 5.56% to 2.95%, while the median gap has reduced from 6.92% to 1.06% compared with the previous reporting year.
Assistant Chief Constable Kerry Smith said: "This recognition reflects the contribution of colleagues who have spoken up, shared their experiences and challenged us to improve, as well as the teams across the CNC who have worked hard to turn that feedback into meaningful change.
"We have made real progress in how we listen to our people, assess the impact of our decisions and respond to barriers in the workplace.
"I want to thank everyone who has contributed to that work. Every improvement, whether through our policies, staff networks, recruitment or the support available to colleagues, helps us build a stronger and more inclusive CNC."
