Minister acts to keep exquisite Mughal calligraphy collection from leaving the UK
The government has temporarily blocked two volumes of Mughal calligraphy, considered amongst the finest surviving specimens, from being taken out of the UK permanently
- The export bar allows time for a UK gallery or institution to acquire this national treasure
UK galleries and institutions have been given time to save a collection of intricately detailed Mughal calligraphy for the nation, thanks to an intervention from the UK Government.
The Mughal dynasty ruled much of the Indian subcontinent during the 16th to mid-19th centuries, creating an artistic legacy spanning architecture to portraiture. This calligraphic work was created during a time of Mughal expansion under Emperors Akbar and Jahangir.
The two volumes of calligraphy include work from leading royal calligraphers, including Emperor Akbar's preeminent calligrapher Muhammad Hussain Kashmiri, who was renowned for the outstanding intricacy and detail of his work. Rare work from celebrated calligrapher Abd al-Rahim al-Haravi is also included.
The government has decided to place a temporary export ban on the calligraphy volumes in the hope of keeping them accessible to the UK public. The deferral period gives time for museums and galleries to raise the funds needed to acquire the volumes, for example through philanthropic support, or for a private individual to purchase them and donate them to the nation.
The calligraphic works are thought to be over 400 years old. They are spread across 29 leaves, featuring gold decorations depicting plant and animal motifs.
By keeping the volumes in the country, it is hoped researchers can study how the construction of such calligraphic collections has evolved. Given so few works of this kind survive intact, its preservation makes it especially valuable.
Heritage Minister Baroness Twycross said:
Calligraphy of this quality and from such prestigious calligraphers is extraordinarily rare. These sheets hold secrets that researchers are desperate to unlock.
Keeping these volumes in the UK, where the public and researchers can access them, will create a perfect opportunity to discover more about how they were created and the vital role they played in the history of diplomacy.
RCEWA committee member, Caroline Shenton, said:
The sublimely beautiful leaves in these two volumes were created by the most outstanding imperial calligraphers and illustrators of the Mughal Court. There is much still to be discovered about the techniques used in their production, and the subsequent history of their dispersal and rebinding (including their relationship to similar folios now in the Royal Collection) which may only be undertaken if the physical volumes remain in this country for research. I therefore very much hope that a UK institution will step forward to ensure that they are not lost to the nation and to scholarship.
The Minister's decision follows the advice of the Reviewing Committee on the Export of Works of Art and Objects of Cultural Interest (RCEWA).
The Committee made its recommendation on the basis that the volumes met the second and third Waverley criteria for their outstanding aesthetic importance and significance to the study of the work of royal Mughal calligraphers, their studio practice, and the role they played in diplomacy and international relations.
The decision on the export licence application for the volumes will be deferred for an initial period ending on 15 February 2027 inclusive in which we welcome expressions of interest from an appropriate purchaser to try and acquire them. At the end of the first deferral period owners will have a consideration period of 15 Business Days to consider any offer(s) to purchase the volumes at the recommended price of £900,000. The second deferral period will commence following the signing of an Option Agreement and will last for four months.
Notes to editors:
- Organisations or individuals interested in purchasing the album should contact the RCEWA on 02072680534 or rcewa@artscouncil.org.uk.
- Details of the ITEM are as follows: Two volumes prepared from a more extensive imperial album of calligraphic specimens dated to c. 1595-1605. It is believed that the original album was prepared for Mughal Emperor Akbar (r. 1556-1605) or his son Prince Salim (d. 1627) at the turn of the 16th century at the royal workshop either in Lahore or Agra. The original imperial album was disassembled prior to 1728 and collectively, these two volumes consist of twenty-nine folios featuring calligraphic specimens and one drawing. Each folio is adorned with illuminated margins.
- Provenance: Private collection, England, 1980s; Private collection, London, 1980s-2020; Sotheby's, London, 27 October 2020, lot 435; AeR Gallery, London
- The Reviewing Committee on the Export of Works of Art and Objects of Cultural Interest is an independent body, serviced by Arts Council England (ACE), which advises the Secretary of State for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport on whether a cultural object, intended for export, is of national importance under specified criteria.
