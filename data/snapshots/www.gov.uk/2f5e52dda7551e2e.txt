MHRA approves zanidatamab (Ziihera) for the treatment of gastroesophageal cancer
The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) has today, 27 November 2026, approved a variation to the existing authorisation for zanidatamab (Ziihera) for the treatment of gastroesophageal cancer in adults
The Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) has today, 27 November 2026, approved a variation to the existing authorisation for zanidatamab (Ziihera) for the treatment of gastroesophageal cancer in adults whose cancer has high levels of a protein called HER2.
Zanidatamab was previously approved for the treatment of adults with bile duct cancer.
Zanidatamab is used when the cancer cannot be removed by surgery and has spread to nearby tissues or other parts of the body. It is used alongside chemotherapy, with or without another cancer medicine called tislelizumab, depending on the level of HER2 in the cancer.
Zanidatamab is administered by an infusion drip into a vein every two or three weeks.
In a study of 914 patients with HER2-positive gastroesophageal cancer, patients who received zanidatamab alongside chemotherapy and tislelizumab lived on average around seven months longer, and around four months longer without their cancer getting worse.
Julian Beach, Executive Director, Operations, said:
"This approval provides a new treatment option for adults with HER2-positive gastroesophageal cancer, where the cancer cannot be removed by surgery or has spread to other parts of the body.
"This approval reflects the MHRA's commitment to ensuring patients can access safe and effective medicines where there is an unmet clinical need.
"As with all approved medicines, we will continue to monitor the safety and effectiveness of zanidatamab as it is used more widely."
For the full list of side effects and restrictions with zanidatamab, see the package leaflet.
