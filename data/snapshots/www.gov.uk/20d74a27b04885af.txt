SFO charges eight over London Capital & Finance collapse
Investment firm's collapse in 2019 led to loss of £251 million.
The Serious Fraud Office (SFO) has today charged eight individuals in connection with its investigation into the collapse of London Capital & Finance Plc (LCF).
Michael Andrew Thomson (53), Simon Hume-Kendall (73), Elten Barker (53), Paul Careless (51), Robert Sedgwick (78) and John Russell-Murphy (52) have each been charged with conspiracy to defraud. Helen Hume-Kendall (64) has been charged with concealing criminal property.
An eighth defendant has been charged with conspiracy to defraud and acting in contravention of a director disqualification undertaking. It is understood this individual's legal team will make an application for anonymity at the first magistrates' court hearing.
The charges follow the SFO's investigation into LCF, which collapsed in 2019, leading to more than 11,200 investors losing an estimated £251 million.
LCF was a Tunbridge Wells-based investment company that raised money by selling mini-bonds to retail investors, marketed as a way to earn high returns.
All eight defendants are due to appear before Westminster Magistrates' Court on 7 December.
Emma Isaac, Head of Division for the Serious Fraud Office, said:
Thousands of investors believed backing London Capital & Finance would yield high returns, instead they lost over £250 million. Today's charges mark a significant step forward in our investigation into the collapse of the firm and ensuring those responsible are held to account.
