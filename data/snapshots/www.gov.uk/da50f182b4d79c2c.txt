Research project: exploring the effects of Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards in the private rented sector on fuel poverty, household energy costs, and pathways out of fuel poverty
This research explores the effects of minimum energy efficiency standards in the private rented sector on fuel poverty, household energy costs, and pathways out of fuel poverty.
The Committee on Fuel Poverty is delighted to announce the launch of its latest research project, in collaboration with the Centre for Sustainable Energy and IPSOS.
This study, 'Exploring the effects of Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards in the private rented sector on fuel poverty, household energy costs, and pathways out of fuel poverty', will generate critical evidence providing insight into how landlords, tenants, and the retrofit industry are expecting to adapt and meet the government's PRS MEES policy.
The government's PRS Reforms are a flagship part of its efforts to tackle fuel poverty, improve the quality of English housing stock, and ensure nobody lives in a cold, draughty home. These reforms involve a myriad of stakeholders, from government departments and Local Authorities, to tenant and landlord associations, and an extensive retrofit supply chain. Current modelling suggests the updated regulations could lift around 415,000 households out of fuel poverty by 2030. However, these projections are dependent on a range of assumptions, including the proportion of landlords that will comply with the regulations and invest in energy efficiency improvements, the capacity and capability of the retrofit supply chain to deliver upgrades at scale, the extent to which costs are passed on to tenants through rents, and how tenants respond to improved energy efficiency through changes in energy consumption and heating behaviours.
The research will be delivered through a sequential mixed-methods programme over 2 years. In the first year, the focus will be on quantitative and qualitative research examining the behaviour and decision-making of landlords, tenants and supply chain actors, while subsequent place-based case studies will explore how different local contexts may affect policy delivery and outcomes in practice.
The Committee on Fuel Poverty (CFP) is an Expert Advisory Committee sponsored by the Department for Energy Security and Net Zero (DESNZ). The role of the Committee is to provide advice and challenge the government on reducing fuel poverty in England. There are seven members of the Committee, including a Chair, who are appointed through a public appointments process. The Committee is allocated funding by DESNZ each year for research. The relationship between the Committee and DESNZ is set out in a framework agreement.
Previous research commissioned by the Committee has examined the barriers and enablers to fuel poor households participating in energy efficiency schemes, as well as the experiences of households using low-carbon heating technologies such as heat pumps and their implications for energy affordability.
