UK and US deepen transport partnership to boost growth, strengthen aviation and back next-generation technology
By combining expertise, British and American businesses can lead the transport technologies of the future, while providing safer and more reliable journeys now.
- Transport Secretary meets US counterpart Secretary Sean P. Duffy as the pair agree to deepen cooperation on modernising airspace and emerging technologies
- discussions with automated vehicle industry leaders covered the future of self-driving vehicles in Britain
- meeting with major US airlines focused on aviation growth, innovation and the success of One Stop Security - the scheme making journeys smoother for hundreds of thousands of passengers between the UK and US
Transport ties and cooperation between Washington and Westminster have been strengthened this week as Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander met US Transportation Secretary Sean P. Duffy and sector partners in Washington DC.
The pair agreed to deepen cooperation on crucial transport issues, including addressing challenges around crowded airspace and making passenger journeys more seamless, without compromising safety or security.
The 2 transport leaders discussed how UK-US aviation links underpin trade, tourism and investment between our countries.
Discussions also covered recent air traffic control outages in the UK and US, with both sides agreeing on the importance of sharing lessons and expertise to improve the resilience of aviation systems, minimise disruption and support passengers.
The UK Transport Secretary met with key US stakeholders, including Airlines for America, United Airlines, American Airlines and Delta Air Lines to further discuss these priorities.
Conversations with airlines focused on aviation growth, innovation and the success of One Stop Security - the scheme making flights with transfers smoother for passengers at Atlanta, Dallas and Heathrow airports.
One Stop Security streamlines transfers by removing the need for eligible passengers to undergo a second security check when connecting between flights.
Since February 2025, over 310,000 passengers have benefited from the scheme at Heathrow alone, saving on average 20 minutes when transferring.
Heidi Alexander, Transport Secretary, said:
The UK and US have a strong history of transport cooperation, helping drive innovation and modern infrastructure.
It was a pleasure to meet with Secretary Duffy to discuss leadership in aviation, emerging technologies, maritime and how we can deepen collaboration.
By combining expertise, we can unlock growth, create jobs and help British and American businesses lead the transport technologies of the future, while delivering safer and more reliable journeys today.
During the visit, the UK Transport Secretary also met with self-driving vehicle company Waymo to discuss trials taking place across London and Washington DC and see some of their new technology up close.
This follows the introduction of the UK government's self-driving vehicles pilot scheme - bringing cutting-edge technology to British roads, creating thousands of jobs, and unlocking billions for the economy. Safety and impact on local roads, jobs and communities will remain at the heart of these trials.
As part of the visit, the Transport Secretary toured Union Station with Amtrak to see first-hand the progress of a £350 million refurbishment. This comes as the UK continues its own train station transformations, including a £303 million overhaul for Leeds station, to accommodate millions more journeys.
Leaders in next-generation aviation technology - including Wing, Amazon Prime Air, Joby, Vertical Aerospace and BETA - also outlined how to unlock growth in drone and zero-emission aircraft on both sides of the Atlantic.
This includes cutting red tape, streamlining regulation and helping innovative businesses bring better technologies to market. The sector could contribute up to £103 billion to the UK economy by 2050.
The discussions come as government invests £219 million to boost British production of sustainable aviation fuel, alongside £43 million to power up greener aviation.
Finally, the visit recognised the successful removal of all 3 masts from the Second World War-era US-owned shipwreck SS Richard Montgomery, which sank in the Thames Estuary in 1944 with over 1,400 tonnes of munitions remaining on board to this day.
The operation, delivered through close collaboration between the UK and US, will see the historic masts initially preserved at The Historic Dockyard Chatham, allowing future generations to discover the story of the sailors who helped supply the Allied war effort.
