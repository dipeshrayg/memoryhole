"Maintain a clear purpose", RSH Chief Executive tells landlords
RSH Chief Executive Jonathan Walters was speaking at the Social Housing Leaders Conference in London today.
RSH Chief Executive Jonathan Walters reminded landlords of the need for strong governance, a clear strategic direction and the need to take a longer-term view in a rapidly growing and changing sector.
Addressing the Social Housing Leaders Conference, he reiterated that the challenge for leaders was "not simply managing change but maintaining a clear sense of purpose through it."
Jonathan said landlords had a central role to play in delivering more social homes, and given the economic backdrop and the importance of continuing to invest in existing homes, he said it would require use of "innovative approaches" including working in partnership or through new funding models.
And there were no easy choices as leaders must "carefully balance competing priorities, manage risks effectively and ensure their organisations remain financially resilient."
He said social landlords faced a series of "complex interlocking risks" including a difficult economic environment, pressure to deliver new homes, cyber and AI, aging homes and weak financial positions and "innovating to move through these challenges" was "critical."
Whilst Jonathan spoke about RSH's fundamental objectives of supporting a viable sector and ensuring safe and decent homes for tenants remaining the same he acknowledged adaptions were required to meet the needs of a much-changed sector.
He said RSH wanted to ensure the sector was financially resilient, well governed and able to invest in existing and new homes confidently for the long term, and so was working towards a formal consultation next year to put needed updates in place to the economic standards.

Notes for editors
- RSH promotes a viable, efficient and well-governed social housing sector able to deliver and maintain homes of appropriate quality that meet a range of needs. It does this by undertaking robust economic regulation focusing on governance, financial viability and value for money that maintains lender confidence and protects the taxpayer. It also sets consumer standards and may take action if these standards are breached and there is a significant risk of serious detriment to tenants or potential tenants.
