UK creative businesses to benefit from funding package
Government funding will help businesses grow, create jobs and compete globally, in the Government's latest vote of confidence in the creative industries.
- Recipients of the second wave of Creative Industries Clusters funding announced - with five projects sharing a pot of £34 million to support growth in the creative industries
- £10 million also awarded to 40 ambitious businesses shaping the next generation of creative technology across sub-sectors like video games and film
- Government invests £2.2 million in the Great North Creative Corridor, to bolster regional investment in creative industries in identified high growth areas
More than £45 million of government funding will help creative businesses from South Wales to Glasgow grow, create jobs and compete globally, in the Government's latest vote of confidence in the creative industries: the UK's calling card to the world.
Games studios, music tech start-ups, film and TV innovators and publishers are set to share over £45 million of government funding, as part of the wider £500 million investment in research, development and innovation committed to by UK Research and Innovation (UKRI) in the Creative Industries Sector Plan.
Announced today at the BEYOND conference in Manchester, the investments will fund the new products and services that will power tomorrow's creative industries, from the latest developments in creative technology to improvements in green design. The funding aims to support the regional growth of the creative industries across the country, not just in London and the South East.
It supports the government's aim to make the creative industries a strong investment opportunity and a good place to work, whoever you are and wherever you live. It will also help create the origin story of the next GTA VI: where DMA Design, a British start-up, became Rockstar North - a global video game industry titan which created the biggest entertainment release of all time.
The package includes:
- £34 million for the second wave of five creative industries clusters (Amgen, Green Design North, CreateSci, ScreenJourneys and UK Publishing Futures)
- £10 million for 40 high-growth 'createch' (creative technology) businesses through the Next Wave pilot
- £2.2 million for the new Great North Creative Corridor - building on the work of the £150 million investment of the Creative Places Growth Fund
- £5 million to extend the Creative Industries Policy and Evidence Centre, which is generating evidence to shape the future of the UK's creative industries
Creative Industries Minister Ian Murray said:
As we set out in our Creative Industries Sector Plan, this Government is committed to ensuring that the creative industries can thrive in all corners of the UK by making businesses stronger and improving access to creative careers. This package of investment speaks to that through decentralisation and innovation.
These are projects and organisations with major growth potential, and we're excited to see what they can achieve with this boost.
The Government announced the Creative Industries Sector Plan in 2025, published as part of the Industrial Strategy, as a new 10-year plan to tackle barriers to growth and maximise opportunities across the creative industries sector. The Sector Plan committed £380 million of Government funding to the growth of the creative industries. As one of the Industrial Strategy's eight growth-driving sectors, the creative industries are central to the Prime Minister's economic agenda, creating good jobs, developing globally competitive businesses and turning British ideas into growth across the country.
£34 million for five creative industries clusters
The second round of recipients for the Creative Industries Clusters programme have been announced today. The programme brings together universities, businesses, local and regional policymakers and private funders to drive research, innovation and growth in the creative industries. It can also give students real world experience on live projects, which can lead to future job opportunities.
The five new clusters are:
- Amgen, led by University of South Wales: an industry-led cluster for Wales (amgen is Welsh for 'alternative' or 'better'). Through a bespoke innovation programme combining live performance and creative technology, Amgen will enable collaboration across rural, coastal and post-industrial microclusters, building capacity within local ecosystems and creating testbeds for innovation for creative R&D.
- Green Design North, led by Manchester Metropolitan University: led with Lancaster, Sheffield Hallam and Northumbria universities, with the Design Council as a strategic partner. It links research, industry and civic leaders across the north of England to grow design businesses in the region.
- CreateSci, led by University of East Anglia: a cluster that will grow East Anglia's creative industries through upskilling, and work with the life science and clean energy sectors. It aims to tackle two barriers to creative industry growth: slow creative technology adoption due to limited capacity and facilities, and dispersed geography that discourages investment. Led by the University of East Anglia in partnership with universities across the region, CreateSci will provide the shared facilities, networks and partnerships that creative businesses need to innovate and attract investment.
- ScreenJourneys, led by King's College London: The first four-nations creative industries cluster, drawing on the strengths of partners in London, Edinburgh, Cardiff and Belfast while working with destinations across the UK. It will focus on screen tourism - travel inspired by destinations across the UK made famous on film and TV screens - aiming to establish the infrastructure and innovation to make screen tourism a recognised part of the UK creative economy.
- UK Publishing Futures, led by Northumbria University: Publishing Futures will help publishers, small businesses and freelancers to grow, develop new products, reach new markets. It will also test how AI can be used responsibly in publishing and how UK publishing become more sustainable as a sector. The cluster will create skilled jobs across the region and build on investment from major publishers including Hachette, Pan Macmillan and Bonnier.
£10 million to help createch businesses grow
The UK's next generation of createch innovators are being backed to scale through Next Wave, run through UKRI's business-facing agency Innovate UK, and bringing together financial and non-financial support for ambitious businesses to help strengthen the UK's createch sector.
Createch combines creative innovation and cutting-edge technology to generate novel products, services and experiences, like immersive gaming, virtual try-on tools for fashion and interactive museum exhibits.
Following a competitive selection process, each of the 40 small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), based across nine UK regions including Scotland, the North East, Wales and the West Midlands, have won a grant for projects costing between £110,000 and £500,000. The businesses will contribute a minimum of 30 percent match funding.
The recipients* operate across a number of sectors, from video games and visual arts to music and film & TV. Their innovations are redefining what's possible, whether it's turning real human scans into lifelike digital characters, streaming immersive 4D performances live, making learning interactive through voice-powered gaming, or protecting children's identities and artists' creative rights in the age of AI.
£2.2 million to connect the north's creative sector
The Department for Digital Culture, Media and Sport will contribute £2.2 million to the Great North Creative Corridor, set up by nine northern mayors and regional leaders. It aims to connect the region's creative businesses and make the most of the north of England's cultural and economic potential. The Corridor is made up of the North East, Liverpool City Region, Greater Manchester, West Yorkshire, Hull and East Yorkshire, South Yorkshire, York and North Yorkshire and Tees Valley.
