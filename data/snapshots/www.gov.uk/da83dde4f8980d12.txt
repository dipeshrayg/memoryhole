Nuclear power site transfer programme wins national award
Award-winning team recognised for successful AGR site transfer to the NDA group.
The team behind the first transfer of an Advanced Gas-cooled Reactor (AGR) nuclear site from EDF to the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority (NDA) group has won a major national project management award.
The Nuclear Restoration Services (NRS) and EDF Transition and Transfer Programme won Programme of the Year at the Association for Project Management Awards 2026. It was also a finalist for the Innovation in Project Management award.
The judges praised the programme's joint governance, collaborative culture and success in bringing hundreds of stakeholders together around a shared goal.
Having completed their electricity generation and defueling phases, the first two of seven AGR nuclear sites, Hunterston B in North Ayrshire and Hinkley Point B in Somerset, transferred from EDF ownership to the NDA earlier this year. NRS is now the site licensee responsible for decommissioning. Five further sites will follow over the next decade.
Sohail Ashraf, AGR Transfer Programme Director at NRS, said:
Being named Programme of the Year recognises what we achieved by working collaboratively together across NRS, EDF and with our many stakeholders.
Thank you to everyone who helped transfer Hunterston B in April and subsequently Hinkley Point B in October, including our highly skilled workforce on those sites who will now take forward the decommissioning mission.
These sites have supported jobs, businesses and local life for generations. As they move into decommissioning, we remain committed to supporting future employment and making a positive contribution to the communities around them for years to come. I'm proud that we've been able to deliver this work with people at the heart of our approach.
A complex first-of-a-kind programme
Transferring a nuclear power station, its workforce and associated responsibilities to a different company is a complex undertaking.
Years of preparation and joint working across NRS, EDF, government, regulators and partners brought together the people, systems and approvals needed for NRS to take responsibility for decommissioning the two sites. This included regulators issuing new nuclear site licences and transferring environmental permits. Around 500 staff also transferred from EDF into NRS, alongside changes to IT systems, contractual and funding arrangements.
EDF Nuclear Decommissioning Director, Clare Sharpe said:
The transfer of two nuclear power stations into new ownership was a hugely complex undertaking, involving hundreds of employees, critical IT infrastructure and close collaboration across multiple organisations.
What makes me most proud is not just that we delivered a first-of-a-kind programme on time and on budget, but the way people came together to make it happen. This recognition from the APM reflects the dedication, professionalism and teamwork shown throughout the programme. I am incredibly proud of everyone who contributed to this achievement.
The AGR transfers represent the NDA's first significant new mission since it was established, expanding the group's nationally important decommissioning work. The work will be funded by the Nuclear Liabilities Fund, an independent ring-fenced fund set up to meet the costs of decommissioning the AGR sites.
Chief Executive Officer of NRS, Rob Fletcher said:
This award is an important recognition of what we've achieved with these first two transfers.
Being trusted to take on responsibility for the AGR sites is hugely significant for NRS and the wider NDA group. We're bringing together our decommissioning experience with new skills, ideas, and perspectives.
Our focus now is on building on that strong start, preparing for the remaining transfers and delivering our responsibilities safely, securely, and efficiently.
Sites set to transfer in the future include Dungeness B, Heysham 1 and 2, Hartlepool and Torness.
