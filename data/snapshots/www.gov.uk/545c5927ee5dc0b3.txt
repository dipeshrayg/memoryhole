Mini lab-grown human organs to boost search for new treatments as £20m Cambridge lab opens
As the £20 million UK Preclinical Translational Models Hub opens its doors, the laboratory will help to usher in a world of new possibilities for patients.
- New £20 million Cambridge hub opens today, where lab-grown mini human organs are helping scientists test new medicines
- One year on from the Replacing Animals in Science Strategy, Government is backing projects to speed up the search for new treatments
- Builds on ten UK projects supported by £2m to better predict how medicines move through the body - boosting patient treatment
Faster access to treatments, a better understanding of how medicines behave inside the body, and fewer animal experiments will all be supported through a new lab opening in Cambridge today (27 November).
As the £20 million UK Preclinical Translational Models Hub opens its doors, the laboratory will help to usher in a world of new possibilities for patients. The site will use organoids - tiny versions of human organs made from tissue donated by NHS patients - to help researchers build up a more accurate picture of the most promising new drugs to take forward for clinical trials.
Some studies show that lab-grown organs could actually reduce total pharmaceutical R&D costs by between 10% to 26%. It also has the potential to slash the time it takes for the most promising drugs to move into full scale development by nearly a fifth - meaning a quicker route to get cutting-edge treatments into the hands of health workers.
Science Minister Chris McDonald said:
Growing tiny organs from donated tissue may sound like the domain of science fiction writers, but this hub highlights the very best of British science, research, and innovation.
The work being led here will deliver faster and more effective treatments which could transform outcomes for patients, while backing a growing British industry which has the potential to attract investment and sell its expertise around the world.
A year ago, we set out to make Britain a world leader in replacing animal testing in science, and it's through projects like this that we're now working to make that vision a reality."
Marking one year since the launch of the Replacing Animals in Science Strategy, the UK's moves to find alternatives to using animals in research and testing are gathering speed.
While animal studies continue to play an important role in many areas of research and testing, they cannot always fully predict how the human body will respond. The government's strategy which launched last November is backed by £75 million and looks to tackle this problem head on - with research carried out on human tissue giving earlier, potentially more accurate insights.
Other steps to support replacing animal testing include:
- A £2m investment from Innovate UK and the National Centre for the Replacement, Refinement and Reduction of Animals in Research (NC3Rs) into ten organisations this summer, supporting research from beating human heart cells in a dish, to advanced software that predicts how a drug moves through the body.
- Earlier this year, the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) published guidance on how companies can receive regulatory feedback on studies applying non-animal approaches before they formally submit them for approval - providing medicines developers with greater clarity on the evidence it expects.
- Confirmed today, the UK Centre for the Validation of Alternative Methods and the Alternative Methods Expert Committee will be hosted by UKRI and coordinated by the NC3Rs. A key commitment from the Replacing Animals Strategy with £30m of government backing, this paves the way for work to begin next year to build further evidence, confidence and international recognition needed to support wider adoption of alternatives to animal use across research, innovation and regulation.
Today's opening of the UK Preclinical Translational Models Hub builds on the strengths of a thriving UK life science's sector.
Worth £147 billion, it currently employs some 360,000 people and has received £4 billion in private backing since July 2025.,
As the UK continues to deliver its strategy on replacing animals in science, our booming life sciences sector is firmly establishing our place as the global destination to design, develop, and sell the next generation of testing technologies.
Professor Matthias Zilbauer, Director of the Preclinical Translational Models Hub, said:
The UK is already at the forefront of developing innovative models of human biology and disease. The challenge now is to turn these scientific advances into technologies that can be reliably used in drug development.
Our Hub will bring together expertise from across academia, healthcare and industry to accelerate the development, validation and adoption of these new approaches. By doing so, we aim to improve how medicines are developed, reduce reliance on animal testing and establish the UK as a global leader in translating scientific innovation into real-world patient benefit."
Dr Cynthia Bullock, Life Sciences Programme Director at UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), said:
The opening of the £20 million UK Pre-clinical Translational Models Hub, core-funded by the Office for Life Sciences in partnership with UKRI, will help strengthen the UK's ability to turn scientific discoveries into new treatments.
It will support the development and use of new innovative methods such as human-relevant models and computational approaches that can generate better evidence, help promising innovations move more quickly towards clinical trials and improve how potential new medicines are assessed and developed.
By bringing together expertise from academia, industry and the NHS, the Hub will support alternatives to animal use in research and strengthen the UK's position as a global leader in life sciences.

The establishment of a UK Centre for the Validation of Alternative Methods (UKCVAM) and an Alternative Methods Expert Committee (AMEC) are important new additions to the UK infrastructure to help drive and coordinate the development and use of alternatives both in academic research and regulatory testing. Notes to Editors
The government will replace animals wherever the science allows. Each replacement will be backed by robust validation so patients and the public can trust the alternatives. Responsible, justified use of animals will continue to be supported where alternatives are not yet available.
A full update on progress against the Strategy's 26 commitments has been published on GOV.UK
With co-leads at the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology and the Wellcome Sanger Institute, the Preclinical Translational Models Hub will draw on a Cambridge research community of around 60 principal investigators. Based on the Cambridge Biomedical Campus it will:
- Create more accurate predictive pre-clinical models of human disease to lower the attrition rate of new therapeutics and accelerate medicines discovery for patient benefit
- Be a national resource for in vitro model development and increase access to state-of-the-art technology, human tissue and patient samples and data across the UK
- Develop cutting-edge AI science agents to transform target discovery
- Enable additional methodological innovation through the co-location and collaboration of expertise across bioengineering, biological, clinical and computational science from academia and industry
- Position UK at the forefront of human in vitro model development for medical research, making it an attractive destination for global talent and pharmaceutical investment
- Increase the range of robust alternative methods available to support the reduction in the use of animals in science
