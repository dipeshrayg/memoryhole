Faster planning decisions as new rules and AI tackle delays
Homeowners and small businesses to get quicker answers as new rules put expert officers in charge of straightforward applications
- Homeowners and small businesses to get quicker answers as new rules put expert officers in charge of straightforward applications
- New AI planning tool cuts time spent on key tasks by 58%, halving processing times for routine householder applications
- Over 400 planners recruited through government-backed schemes, exceeding manifesto commitment of 300
Homeowners looking to extend or convert their loft, and small businesses improving their premises, could get faster planning decisions under new rules to cut unnecessary delays.
From today (Saturday 31 October) straightforward applications will be decided by expert planning officers rather than waiting for a planning committee slot, helping families, shopkeepers and pub landlords get decisions sooner.
The new National Scheme of Delegation will give greater certainty for applicants, while freeing up councillors to focus on the most significant developments.
Families and businesses could also get quicker decisions thanks to 420 newly recruited planners through government-backed programmes, surpassing the government's 2024 manifesto commitment to recruit 300 additional planners more than two years before the end of this Parliament.
New AI technology designed to take time-consuming admin off planners' desks will also help homeowners get quicker decisions on their applications, like getting permission for a house extension or a conservatory. Developed with Google DeepMind, Google Cloud, Faculty and local authorities, PlanAssist brings together applications, policies and site constraints into one place for trained officers to review. Early testing cut time spent on key planning tasks by 58% - helping us halve processing times for routine householder applications from eight weeks to four.
Following a successful trial in Camden, Barnet and Dorset Councils, the tool will now expand to 15 councils for 12 months, with the government intending to make it available to every council in England by the end of 2027.
With householder applications accounting for nearly 70% of applications each year, the changes will get more everyday applications moving, delivering a faster, more efficient planning service for residents and businesses and giving planners more time to tackle complex cases.

Further information
PlanAssist brings together planning applications, local policies and site constraints in one place, helping officers identify key information more quickly and efficiently. Planning officers remain responsible for reviewing applications and making decisions.
The tool compiles the information officers need and gives an evidence-backed initial assessment, speeding up routine householder applications which make up 70% of planning officers' workload.
Following successful initial testing with the London Borough of Camden, London Borough of Barnet and Dorset Council, the government has now expanded testing to a further 12 councils, bringing the total number involved to 15. The 12 additional councils are: Ashford Borough Council, London Borough of Lambeth, Newcastle City Council, Nottingham City Council, Bristol City Council, Cumberland Council, North Lincolnshire Council, Rochdale Borough Council, London Borough of Redbridge, South Gloucestershire Council, Stoke-on-Trent City Council and Westminster City Council.
Officers will use PlanAssist alongside their normal process, to test its performance at scale
PlanAssist will not be mandated software and the government is removing blockers to AI adoption by mandating the digitisation and publication of key planning documents as open data by the end of 2027, which will support all property technology tools with better quality data.
In 2026/27, the Department is investing over £14 million in two major routes into the profession: the Pathways to Planning graduate scheme and Public Practice.
- Pathways to Planning, delivered by the LGA, provides a structured two-year placement and fully supported master's-level training for graduates working in local planning authorities. Over £12 million has been allocated this financial year, including £8.5 million for salary bursaries.
- Public Practice, a social enterprise specialising in placing mid-career built-environment professionals into the public sector, is receiving over £2 million this year, including £1.6 million in targeted salary support for eligible councils. Alongside direct placements, Public Practice is helping authorities strengthen recruitment processes, address organisational barriers and promote more inclusive hiring practices.
