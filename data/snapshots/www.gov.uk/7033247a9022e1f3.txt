AI in Mental Health Airlock to support safe innovation
A new regulatory sandbox will explore how safety approaches in innovative digital mental health technologies can be effectively evaluated and regulated.
As AI plays an increasing role in mental health support, a new regulatory sandbox launched today (23 November) by the Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) and National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE), will support safe innovation in AI-enabled mental health technologies, helping promising technologies reach people sooner.
As an expansion of the MHRA's AI Airlock programme, in partnership with NICE's Health Technology Innovation Assessment (HTA) Lab, the AI in Mental Health Airlock will bring regulators and innovators together to explore the specific evaluation needs for AI-enabled mental health technologies and develop on the recommendations of the National Commission on the Regulation of AI in Healthcare. The project will help increase confidence among patients, the public and the NHS and to help realise the potential benefits of AI in healthcare.
The AI in Mental Health Airlock is part of phase two of a Wellcome-funded programme, first launched in 2023, to explore how innovative digital mental health technologies can be effectively and proportionately evaluated and regulated.
Working with innovators, clinicians, experts and people with lived experience, the sandbox will help to identify the regulatory, health technology assessment and evidence challenges presented by these rapidly developing technologies and explore proportionate approaches that provide appropriate safeguards and instil confidence while enabling innovation.
The project will be in partnership with NICE's HTA Lab, which is a safe space for NICE to test and develop new evaluation approaches, without affecting live NICE evaluations or business as usual. For NICE, the AI in Mental Health Airlock is an opportunity to explore how AI-related methodological challenges should be approached within its own evaluations of mental health contexts, as they increasingly incorporate AI.
Digital mental health technologies (DMHTs) are increasingly being used by individuals and the NHS to support mental health, from therapeutic AI support to AI-integrated virtual reality for mental health therapy. Recent research also suggests more than one in three adults (37%) have used an AI chatbot to support their mental health or wellbeing. Some of these chatbots may have medical purposes and be subject to medical device regulation, while others may not.
These technologies could offer new ways for people to access support and potentially improve how mental health services are delivered. Evaluation approaches that can respond to the specific challenges of AI are needed to support developers in realising these benefits for users of their technologies.
The aim is to generate practical learning that can give innovators greater clarity, support proportionate regulation and evaluation, and help safe and effective technologies to deliver benefits for individuals and the health system.

Health Innovation Minister James Frith said:
"AI has the potential to open up new ways for people to access mental health support, but innovation must go hand in hand with safety and public confidence.
"This new initiative will bring regulators, developers, clinicians and people with lived experience together to help promising innovations reach patients safely and responsibly.
"We want to support the development of AI that makes a real difference to people's lives, while ensuring patients can have confidence in the technologies designed to support their mental health."
Chief Executive of the MHRA, Lawrence Tallon, said:
"AI technologies have the potential to transform access to mental health support, enabling more people get help when they need it. The AIMH Airlock will help us better understand how AI mental health technologies can be practically assessed and managed, supporting safer innovation, timely access and informing the UK's wider approach to responsible AI in healthcare."
Chief Scientific Officer of NICE, Dr Nick Crabb, said:
"Part of NICE's role is to scan the horizon for transformational care and drive innovation into the hands of health and care professionals. Digital mental health technologies powered by AI have real potential to improve access to support, but that potential can only be realised if we have robust ways of identifying and managing the risks they present. The AIMH Airlock gives our HTA Lab the opportunity to explore practical approaches to these challenges alongside the MHRA, developers and people with lived experience."
Director of Mental Health at Wellcome, Miranda Wolpert, said:
"We are living through a revolution in mental health science. New treatments, tools and technologies could help us reach millions more people, and AI can play a significant part in that. But innovation must go hand in hand with robust evidence, appropriate safeguards and the trust of the people these technologies are intended to support. By bringing together regulators, researchers, innovators and people with lived experience, the AI in Mental Health Airlock can help create a clearer path towards AI-enabled technologies that are safe, effective and able to deliver meaningful benefits."
Alongside this work, earlier this year the MHRA published free practical advice to help the public, parents, carers and professionals make informed choices about digital mental health technologies. It sets out five simple things to look for when choosing a product, including what it claims to do, who it is for, the evidence behind it, how personal data is used and whether it is regulated as a medical device, supporting informed and confident use of these products.
The work reflects the MHRA's 2030 Strategy focus on using regulatory science and proportionate, innovative approaches to support the safe adoption of emerging technologies. The initiative also supports the direction set by Government response to the recommendations the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare in providing a practical opportunity to explore how proportionate approaches can support beneficial AI innovation while maintaining the trust of patients and the public.
Up to five organisations will be selected to take part in the sandbox, working directly with the MHRA, NICE's Health Technology Assessment Innovation Laboratory, other relevant regulators, subject matter experts, clinicians and people with lived experience.
Through workshops and testing activities, participants will be able to generate practical insights into how AI-related risks in mental health contexts can be identified, assessed and managed, helping inform future regulation and health technology assessment approaches in the UK and internationally.

NHS Doctor and Professor of Regulatory Science and Innovation, University of Birmingham, Prof Alastair Denniston, who chaired the UK's National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare, said:
"There is enormous potential for AI technologies to transform mental health care for the benefit of patients, but the message we took away from the AI Commission was loud and clear that patients and the public need to feel confident that these treatments are safe and effective.
"This new regulatory sandbox is an opportunity to test approaches that safeguard these innovative technologies in a safe environment and ensure they are regulated appropriately, providing the reassurance patients, NHS professionals and the public need."
Dr Sarah Hughes, Chief Executive of Mind says:
"The public are at risk of harm if AI regulation isn't fit for purpose and doesn't put their safety first. People are increasingly turning to AI - including general purpose chatbots - for mental health support and so it's vital that we are testing, learning, and evaluating how these tools are being used and regulated.
"It's great to see experts being brought together to shape these conversations. Patient choice and people's lived experience must be central to this. It's one of the reasons why Mind has launched our own AI commission, so we can better understand how people are using this technology for their mental health and contribute to meaningful change that keeps them safe."
