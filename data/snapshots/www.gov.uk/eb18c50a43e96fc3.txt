Mr President,
The United Kingdom welcomes this opportunity to mark the sixtieth anniversary of the two International Covenants.
When these Covenants were adopted in 1966, it was recognised that, together, they gave effect to the vision of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights. By enshrining civil, political and economic, social and cultural rights in binding international law, they affirmed the dignity and worth of every individual.
This anniversary is an opportunity to reflect on the progress achieved and reaffirm our shared commitment to uphold these rights and to remain steadfast in the face of any efforts to weaken them.
As we look to the next sixty years, the United Kingdom is committed to working with the UN and partners worldwide to promote and protect human rights for all.
And as noted in the agenda, "showcasing" the real-world value of these rights is essential to building and sustaining commitment to them.
Mr President,
We therefore welcome reflection from the panellists on what more we can do collectively to bring to life how these rights support people's everyday experiences, making clear their relevance, impact and importance for communities around the world.
