New panel to give child sexual abuse and exploitation survivors a voice
New Victims' Commissioner panel, backed by £1 million in Home Office funding, will give adult survivors of child sexual abuse and exploitation more say in decisions.
Adult victims and survivors of child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) will have a stronger voice in shaping decisions that affect them, as the government sets up a new advisory panel of people with lived experience of these horrific crimes.
Operated by the independent Victims' Commissioner for England and Wales, the new CSAE Victim and Survivor Advisory Panel will provide a safe, supportive forum for victims and survivors to share their experiences, insights and recommendations directly with government - helping to bring better outcomes for all victims and survivors of these terrible crimes.
The government is investing up to £1 million over the next 3 years to fund the panel.
Satvir Kaur MP, Minister for Safeguarding and Violence Against Women and Girls, said:
Victims and survivors know better than anyone what changes we need. Yet too many decisions are still being made for them, instead of with them.
This panel will ensure survivor's voices are where they need to be: right at the heart of decision-making for tackling child sexual abuse and exploitation and supporting victims.
The panel delivers on a government commitment to ensure people's real, lived experiences shape how it tackles CSAE. It was developed in partnership with victims and survivors and draws on evidence and expertise from the child sexual abuse sector and successful international examples.
The Victims' Commissioner for England and Wales, Claire Waxman OBE said:
Victims and survivors have been clear that they want to be listened to, not spoken for. This independent panel is an opportunity to do things differently, moving beyond tokenistic engagement to give survivors a meaningful role in shaping the policies, practices and services that affect them.
I am proud that my office will host this panel. It will be built on independence, trust, transparency, and proper support, so that members can contribute with confidence, knowing their voices will influence change. Real reform means not just listening but acting.
If you are an adult survivor of child sexual abuse and exploitation and feel able to do so, I encourage you to consider applying. Your experiences, insights and expertise are vital to helping deliver a better response for victims and survivors.
The Victims' Commissioner will open the recruitment process to the public shortly, so the panel can begin its work in spring 2027.
Victims and survivors have long asked for a more active role in influencing decision-making. By establishing this new panel, this government is going further than ever before in including their voices in a meaningful way.
Minister for Victims, Tackling Violence Against Women and Girls and International, Justice Alex Davies-Jones said:
No child should ever have to go through the horrors of sexual abuse and exploitation. But when they do, as adult survivors they deserve a seat at the table in shaping the decisions made to support them and tackle these abominable crimes.
We will always do everything we can to support survivors, and that's why we're investing an unprecedented £550 million in support services over the next 3 years, which includes holistic, trauma-informed support to victims of sexual abuse - helping deliver the justice they deserve.
