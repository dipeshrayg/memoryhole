Major boost to local housing and regeneration ambitions as regional leaders strengthen co-delivery ties with Homes England and National Housing Bank
Mayors and leaders central to new wave of Place Delivery Agreements that back local priorities through joint commitments, resources and expertise.
Mayors, regional leaders, Homes England and the National Housing Bank have agreed multiple ground-breaking collaborations in the last two months that will accelerate co-delivery of local housing and regeneration plans across England.
This includes the first wave of new Place Delivery Agreements (PDAs), first outlined by the housing minister in September, which give mayors a stronger role in shaping housing delivery, regeneration and growth.
Individual agreements with South Yorkshire Combined Mayoral Authority, East Midlands Combined County Authority, West Midlands Combined Mayoral Authority, North East Mayoral Combined Authority, West of England Combined Authority and Liverpool City Region Combined Authority have now been published, with more partnerships to be announced shortly.
Each PDA will see the respective strategic authority work together with Homes England and the National Housing Bank to deliver a locally led plan, with a shared pipeline of priority schemes and strengthened local oversight.
Dedicated joint delivery units (JDUs) will bring together delivery expertise and specialist capability from all three partners, including through co-location where possible, alongside access to investment, land and regeneration tools.
Mayor of the West Midlands Richard Parker will officially sign his region's agreement today (19 November) in Birmingham at Homes England Live, a national event attended by hundreds of housing and regeneration experts from across the country.
In tandem with its collaboration with mayors, Homes England continues to strengthen vital ties with partners in non-mayoral areas, ensuring every place can access the support, investment and expertise needed to achieve local ambitions.
Angela Rayner, Secretary of State for Housing, Communities and Local Government, said:
Local leaders know what their communities need and that's why we're giving them a greater say on the housing, regeneration and growth priorities that matter most in their regions.
We're establishing new, co-delivery partnerships that will unlock greater levels of private investment, ramp up new housing supply and create thriving communities for local people.
Amy Rees CB, Chief Executive of Homes England, said:
These ground-breaking agreements signal a new chapter of co-delivery firmly rooted in local priorities and backed by national expertise, investment and resources. They will see colleagues from local, regional and national organisations take long-standing collaboration to new heights to accelerate delivery for local communities across the country.
Mayors and local leaders know housing and regeneration opportunities and challenges best. The agency's regional operating model means our teams can provide tailored support to places, including those not represented by mayors, to help deliver against locally driven priorities the length and breadth of England.
Claire Ward, Chair of the UK Mayors' Network and Mayor of the East Midlands, said:
Mayors across the country are determined to build the homes their residents need, as these are the bedrock of a strong society and economy. Delivery on this scale can only be done in partnership, and public money needs to work smartly to unlock further private investment.
That's why these new agreements with Homes England are so important. They give mayors a stronger voice in shaping housing and regeneration projects, while bringing together the expertise, investment and support needed to make things happen. As Chair of the UK Mayors' Network, I want to see this kind of partnership become the norm, so that wherever people live, local leaders have the backing they need to deliver the homes and thriving communities their places deserve.
PDAs are one example of how Homes England and the National Housing Bank provide tailored support to places, with Strategic Place Partnerships and Memoranda of Understanding being two other active mechanisms supporting locally led housing and regeneration ambitions across England.
Richard Parker, Mayor of the West Midlands, said:
This PDA will help focus investment on the region's four major Growth Corridors: Birmingham and North Solihull Gateway; Coventry Growth Arc; Black Country Growth Corridor and Wolverhampton-Walsall Growth Cluster.
We've already shown what we can achieve when powers and funding are put into the hands of the people who know this region best. Over the last year we smashed government targets on homes, jobs and business support and this new agreement is a further step along that devolution journey.
It will bring together investment, expertise and local knowledge to breathe new life into derelict sites, generate thousands of new jobs and create vibrant places that people can build their lives around and be proud to call home. By working together in this way, we can give local people more opportunities, more genuinely affordable homes and a brighter, more prosperous future.
Will Jeffwitz, Head of Policy at the National Housing Federation, said:
These place-based agreements will help ensure the right homes are delivered in communities up and down the country. Housing associations, which have deep ties with their communities, will be crucial partners for mayors and local authorities as we work together to turn the tide on the housing crisis.
James Stevens, Director for Cities at the Home Builders Federation, said:
Place Delivery Agreements should help bring more sites forward more quickly to meet local housing need. It will require all public agencies working closely and effectively with the private sector. A more coordinated approach will enable the mayors to have a direct and positive effect on housing and investment in their areas and should ensure that sites earmarked for new homes can be processed more efficiently.
House builders will welcome the opportunity they provide to accelerate housing delivery. Government needs to ensure that as they develop the proposals, they create opportunity to accelerate the delivery of all types of housing, on different sized sites across the local area such that all parts of the industry can play their part in delivering the homes the country needs. This should include allowing opportunities for SME builders to develop of parcels of major schemes.
