Local Police Commands created to tackle everyday crime
The Home Secretary announced around 200 new Local Police Commands will be created across England and Wales at the annual APCC/NPCC summit.
The biggest reforms to policing in two centuries will see the creation of around 200 Local Police Commands (LPCs) to tackle the everyday crime blighting communities.
Since the government unveiled plans in January to fundamentally restructure the policing model, much focus has been on reducing the number of forces at a regional level.
However, delivering a keynote speech to police leaders today, Shabana Mahmood announced she will build a structure from the bottom up with a larger number of smaller Commands that will focus solely on the needs of their local area.
LPCs will be the foundation of the new policing model, focused on keeping neighbourhoods safe and tackling crimes like burglary, vehicle theft, phone theft and antisocial behaviour.
Each one will be situated closer to the areas they serve with decision making driven by local knowledge and understanding.
Broadly aligned to the geographical boundaries of councils, LPCs will cover towns or rural areas. This will also allow police to work in closer partnership with other local services such as probation, health and housing and improve outcomes.
A new Local Police Guarantee will ensure every LPC delivers the same high-quality local policing, no matter where in the country they serve.
This is the first step in a wider programme of reform to modernise policing and ensure the system is better equipped to tackle the crimes and threats facing communities today.
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said:
Local policing is the bedrock of all policing. Where it is visible and effective, a sacred bond between officers and communities is built. Where it is absent, distrust takes root.
That is why we are reforming policing with a structure that is bottom-up, starting with Local Police Commands relentlessly focussed on keeping their neighbourhoods safe.
We will deliver root-and-branch reform which will set up policing to succeed, restoring public trust and confidence across the country.
The Home Secretary outlined her vision for a new three-layered policing system, with Local Police Commands as the foundation.
Every LPC will have a named Commander, accountable to their local community and elected officials, with communities able to have a role in setting their local priorities.
Above them will sit specialist services that will lead investigations on the most serious and complex crimes. They will be responsible for delivering specialist functions such as public order, forensics and firearms.
This will guarantee that no matter where anyone is, they will have access to expert specialist policing which the current system does not provide.
The new system will also remove the administrative duplication that exists across the existing 43 police headquarters, freeing up thousands of police officers to return to neighbourhood policing.
The final structure within policing would be the National Police Service which will tackle nationwide and international threats.
It will bring together the world-leading capabilities of Counter Terrorism Policing and other agencies, ensuring that policing has the technology, intelligence and resources needed to tackle crime in the 21st century.
The Home Secretary also announced today:
- A new impartiality standard for police to tackle head-on the "corrosive and growing perception" of two-tier policing. This new guidance, which will be developed by the College of Policing, will reinforce that police must apply the law consistently and base decisions on evidence and professional judgement, without the influence of assumptions or stereotypes.
- A new fast-stream programme which will see 200 talented recruits each year placed on a path towards police leadership. The programme will raise standards and strengthen police forces across the country.
Blair Gibbs, Director of the Police Foundation, said:
Policing is fundamentally a local service, and so re-establishing a clear link with local areas is the right organising principle for new policing structures. If larger regional forces are eventually created, they must ensure that Local Police Commands retain real autonomy to align with the different needs of towns and boroughs, because crime and safety priorities do differ across the country.
A devolved policing system with Local Policing Commands will necessitate an even stronger focus on having enough skilled future leaders in policing to provide visible and effective community leadership at that town or borough level. We welcome the pledge to develop a new leadership fast stream programme in policing to help the service attract and bring on the talented leaders of the future. It is a long overdue recognition of the need for the service to invest more in the development of its own people and how they are trained and led.
Dal Babu, former Chief Superintendent at the Metropolitan Police, said:
The introduction of Local Police Command Units will reduce bureaucracy and duplication, and focus in more sharply on criminal activity that sits at the heart of our local communities. For too long we have been tinkering at the margins of police reform. I am therefore excited by the boldness these reforms promise and I believe they will have support from those who understand policing and have successfully delivered reductions of crime.
Andrew Goodacre, CEO of the British Independent Retailers Association (BIRA), said:
Retail crime is a national problem but is fundamentally committed locally in communities and on high streets. BIRA is therefore very supportive of an improved localised approach to policing as laid out by the Home Secretary, which independent retailers have long been calling for. Our hope and belief is this will result in an increased specialist focus in addressing the core issues and challenges affecting our streets today.
