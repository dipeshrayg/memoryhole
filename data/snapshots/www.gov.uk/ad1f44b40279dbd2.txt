Defence Secretary meets President Zelensky and Minister Khmara in Kyiv as UK deepens industrial support for Ukraine
Defence Secretary meets President Zelenskyy in Kyiv as new agreements strengthen UK industrial support for Ukraine and expand drone production
- UK deepens industrial support for Ukraine with new agreements to boost drone production, and Russia step up attacks on civilians and critical infrastructure.
- Ukrainian-designed strike drones will be produced in Britain for Ukraine, adding manufacturing capacity and strengthening supply chains.
- New agreements come as Defence Secretary visits Ukraine after Contact Group and Coalition of the Willing meetings this week where the UK announced £500 million for drones, air-defence missiles, radars and other critical capabilities.
The UK has strengthened support for Ukraine, signing new agreements to enable Ukrainian-designed strike drones to be produced in the UK for the Ukrainian Armed Forces.
On his second visit to Ukraine as Defence Secretary, Wes Streeting announced the strengthened industrial support as he met President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, and later Defence Minister Khmara. The meetings discussed the UK's ongoing steadfast support for Ukraine, as Russia's brutal invasion continues in its fifth year.
The new agreements come after the UK announced how the £500 million of further military support set out at the Budget would be spent to help Ukraine withstand Russian attacks. This support alongside commitments from allies and partners is essential as Russia seeks to cut of heat and water supplies to civilians as winter sets in.
The industrial arrangements set out today mean that Ukraine will be able to set up production facilities in the UK, increasing production capacity and improving resilience.
The UK is committed to putting Ukraine in the strongest possible position to secure a just and lasting peace. £3 billion a year of military support is committed for as long as it takes and the UK leads the Ukraine Defence Contact Group and Coalition of the Willing.
Speaking at the top of the meeting in Kyiv, President Zelenskyy said:
We are very proud that we have such strong relations between our countries, especially your visit during such massive attacks. It's a huge signal of your support, and of the support of the United Kingdom, for the people of Ukraine. We are very thankful for this.
We are also thankful for your winter package. It's not about words. It's about real steps to strengthen our army first, and of course civilians. These decisions, totalling £500 million, are really big packages.
What is very important is that, after such difficult nights in Ukraine, after such massive attacks, including ballistic and cruise missiles, visits like this give us hope that we are not alone, and show the real fact that we have strong partners.
Also speaking at the top of the meeting, Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said:
I don't think there is a European leader since Winston Churchill who has done more for the freedom of their people and their country, but also of our continent thank President Zelenskyy. It's my honour and privilege to be here to talk about what more we can do to support Ukraine in its fight.
Ukraine's front line is our front line for freedom and democracy. I'm here to make sure that we lead by example, to discuss what more we can do to help Ukraine through this winter. Putin isn't just trying to break bridges, he's trying to break the spirit of the Ukrainian people. We are united behind Ukraine and will not let you down.
Streeting's visit to Kyiv comes after he joined French and Polish counterparts at Exercise Koalicja, the Multinational Force for Ukraine's first major exercise in Poland, led by the UK and France. It tests how a force would move, protect and sustain itself to help rebuild Ukraine's Armed Forces once fighting ends.
The industrial agreements build on the £500 million package this week, including £240 million announced by Wes Streeting as he co-chaired more than 50 nations for the Ukraine Defence Contact Group in Brussels. The support includes more than 1,000 air-defence missiles and accompanying launchers, and over a dozen radars, responding directly to Ukrainian requests for greater protection against Russian long-range strikes.
Co-chairing the Coalition of the Willing Leaders meeting on Thursday, the Prime Minister set out a further £260 million package that will provide more than 1,000 long-range strike drones, alongside maritime, logistics and surveillance drones and supporting communications and electronic-warfare capabilities. More than 80 per cent of that package is expected to be spent with UK companies, including small and medium-sized businesses.
The support announced this week demonstrates the UK's continued determination to support Ukraine .
