Sovereign AI and British Business Bank invest in British firm developing the next generation of AI hardware
UK Government's Sovereign AI and the British Business Bank back Fractile - a Bristol and London based frontier AI hardware firm developing advanced chips
- UK Government's Sovereign AI and the British Business Bank back Fractile - a Bristol and London based frontier AI hardware firm developing advanced chips, as part of a funding round worth more than £500 million
- Investment supports the government's ambition to make Britain a world leader in developing the technology that powers artificial intelligence
- Backing British companies will strengthen sovereign capabilities, reduce reliance on overseas technology and support highly skilled jobs and industrial renewal across the country
Fractile, a startup based across London and Bristol developing advanced chips - the physical hardware that powers artificial intelligence - has secured backing from the UK Government's Sovereign AI Venture Fund and the British Business Bank as part of a round worth over £500 million.
The investment, announced today [Wednesday 11 November], is another step in the government's ambition to make Britain a world leader in the technologies that will shape the future of AI, ensuring that British scientific excellence translates into British companies, highly skilled jobs and industrial strength.
It will also help strengthen Britain's sovereign capabilities in critical technologies, reducing dependence on fragile overseas supply chains and ensuring the UK has a greater stake in the hardware that will underpin its future economy
Fractile is re-inventing the hardware that AI runs on, so that it is faster, more energy efficient, and less costly. It is taking a unique approach, looking at the full stack of technologies that AI rests upon: from chips, to software and fundamental research into machine learning (the mathematical algorithms that underpin AI). Today's funding round values the company at over £5 billion. Besides Sovereign AI and the British Business Bank, it is also being backed through this Series C round by investors including CapitalG, Lightspeed and Redpoint.
From Alan Turing's pioneering work to the creation of Arm in Cambridge, Britain has a proud history of laying the foundations for modern computing. Yet too often, the industrial and commercial rewards of British breakthroughs have been realised elsewhere.
The government is determined to change that, backing ambitious homegrown companies to grow into global technology leaders while keeping more of the jobs, expertise and economic value they create here in Britain.
Through Sovereign AI, the government is backing British chip designers, advanced hardware companies and AI entrepreneurs. By anchoring these critical capabilities on home soil, the government is safeguarding the UK's critical infrastructure, keeping British data secure and ensuring the economic returns of technological breakthroughs directly benefit British workers.
The British Business Bank is the UK's economic development bank, helping innovative businesses access the finance they need to start up, scale up and stay in the UK. Their support will help the company access the long-term capital needed to scale its breakthrough AI hardware technology in the UK.

Fractile represents the very best of British ambition, bringing together world-class chip design, engineering and AI expertise to develop faster, cheaper and more energy-efficient hardware.
By backing brilliant British companies like Fractile, we're making sure Britain has the industrial strength and sovereign capability to shape the future of AI, with the jobs and economic rewards coming home to communities across our country.

This news comes a fortnight after the Budget, where the Chancellor confirmed a further £140 million backing for Sovereign AI, with an additional £60 million to support the Fund's investments, plus £80 million for Sovereign AI's R&D Procurement Scheme. The Chancellor also announced that Sovereign AI would expand into a new focus area of Physical and Industrial AI, building on the UK's leading research base and growing strengths in areas such as robotics, autonomous systems and advanced manufacturing. Meanwhile the UK is also pushing with plans to build and deploy the compute infrastructure it needs for the AI era, and ensuring that Britain is home to next wave of leading AI and compute companies, with up to £2 billion of investment over the coming years, under the AI Compute Roadmap.
Sovereign AI is the government's big bet on promising early-stage AI companies to help them grow, scale and succeed from Britain, offering a range of backing to high-potential AI businesses that are starting out on British shores.
Combining the pace and ambition of venture capital with the weight of the state behind it, Sovereign AI is moving fast, backing ambition and cutting through the red tape that so often holds brilliant ideas back, so they can go on to succeed globally.
All of the startups Sovereign AI supports have a meaningful presence here in the UK, and create well paid jobs right across the country - supporting the Government's ambition for good growth in every postcode. These startups are all firmly anchored in the UK with either their founders, leadership, operating headquarters or a large proportion of their talent based here in Britain.
The Chancellor also expanded the British Business Bank's investment capacity by £5 billion to £30.6 billion, giving it more firepower to back innovative British firms as they grow. The package unveiled at the Budget will back growing businesses across every part of the UK and is expected to support more than 24,600 existing jobs and create more than 15,800 new jobs over the lifetime of the finance.
