Britain's biggest hospitality brands partner with Government to deliver 100,000 jobs for NEET young people
As part of a new partnership with UKHospitality announced today (15 October 2026), major employers will provide paid employment as well as work experience and training opportunities for 18 to 24 year olds who are not in education, work or training.
- Britain's leading hospitality businesses including Whitbread, McDonald's and Nando's, commit to hiring 100,000 young people not in education, employment or training through a major partnership with UKHospitality.
- Review of school exclusions launched to stop more children falling out of education, with persistently absent pupils nearly four times more likely to become NEET.
- Comes as part of wider reforms to ensure young people get the support they need to take up education, training and work opportunities.
Young unemployed people are set to benefit from a major new partnership with Britain's leading hospitality businesses, targeting 100,000 paid jobs in the sector.
The hospitality industry is one of the biggest employers of young people, and some of Britain's biggest names, including Whitbread, McDonald's, Nando's, Marriott, Compass, Burger King, Parkdean Resorts, Haven, Fuller's, Merlin Entertainments, Stonegate Group, Mitchells and Butler, Wagamama, Sodexo, WSH Limited and De Vere, will commit to 100,000 paid jobs over three years - offering these young people the chance to gain skills, confidence and experience.
For some, a job in hospitality will be the start of a long-term career in the sector and for others, it will be a chance to discover what they want to do in life, build their CVs and develop the experience to take their next step.

The Government will seek views on measures to strengthen support for pupils at risk of exclusion, improve reintegration into school following suspension, and ensure children continue learning while out of the classroom. The reforms are designed to help more children stay engaged in education, prevent problems escalating and reduce the risk of young people becoming disconnected from opportunity later in life.

Additional Information:
- Today's announcement builds on wider reforms to strengthen routes into work. Through an additional £2.5 billion investment in the Youth Guarantee and Growth and Skills Levy, the Government will support almost one million young people and create up to 500,000 opportunities to earn and learn.
- The Government is introducing a Jobs Guarantee expected to support more than 90,000 young people, alongside 300,000 additional work experience and training placements, the ambition to see 50,000 additional young apprentices and an expansion of Youth Hubs to more than 360 communities across Great Britain.
- Every young person across the country will also be able to pursue prestigious new Vocational GCSEs and access work experience, as part of the Prime Minister's commitment in his first week in the role to put technical education on an equal footing with the traditional academic path. The new national qualifications will be created to sit alongside traditional GCSEs and will replace the current Technical Awards.
- The Government has also introduced £3,000 hiring incentives for employers and stronger partnerships with mayors, local leaders and businesses to help more young people move into sustained employment.
- A new National Mission on Work Experience will work with employers, mayors and local leaders to create 200,000 additional work experience placements, while expanding technical and vocational pathways.
- Together, these reforms will help ensure more young people leave education with the skills, experience and opportunities they need to succeed in work.
Early intervention and prevention
- Today's announcement also builds on the Government's wider programme of early intervention, recognising that tackling youth inactivity means acting earlier to prevent young people becoming disconnected from opportunity.
- The Government is reforming SEND provision, with £4 billion being invested in SEND reform over the next three years including a new £500 million annual Inclusive Mainstream Fund to help schools better support children with additional needs.
- Expanding opportunities for supported internships and other specialist pathways for young people with additional needs, with supported internships providing 6-12 months of work-based learning for 16 to 24 year olds with SEND and tailored job-coach support to help them progress into paid employment.
- Responding to the findings of the Independent Review into prevalence and support for mental health conditions, ADHD and autism, the Government has committed to a major overhaul of mental health support through the forthcoming Mental Health Strategy, with a renewed focus on prevention and early intervention.
- The Government has also expanded mental health support in schools, colleges and communities. Nearly six million children and young people can now access support through Mental Health Support Teams in their school or college, 800,000 more than last year.
- Extending free school meals to all children in households receiving Universal Credit, making over 500,000 additional children eligible and backed by more than £1 billion of Government funding.
- Together, these reforms are designed to break the link between child poverty, poor educational outcomes and youth inactivity, ensuring more young people get the support they need to thrive and reducing the risk of them becoming disconnected from education, training or work later in life.
