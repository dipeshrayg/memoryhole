Reviewer of state threats and terrorism legislation appointed
A new independent reviewer for both state threats and terrorism legislation has been appointed, succeeding Jonathan Hall KC.
Former judge Rupert Jones has been appointed as the government's new Independent Reviewer of Terrorism Legislation and Independent Reviewer of State Threats Legislation.
He succeeds Jonathan Hall KC, who has served as both reviewers since May 2019 and February 2024, respectively.
Rupert has had a distinguished career as a barrister and judge, specialising in areas including proceeds of crime and national security. In addition to appearing as counsel in cases before the House of Lords, Supreme Court, and Privy Council, he was a judge of the Upper Tribunal, authorised to sit as a judge of the High Court, and a legal member of the Investigatory Powers Tribunal.
Home Secretary, Shabana Mahmood, said:
The threats we face from terrorism and foreign states are growing. So, it is vital we have the best independent and expert scrutiny of the powers we use to keep the public safe.
Rupert's deep legal expertise and sound judgement will be vital in ensuring our response remains effective and proportionate.
I would also like to pay tribute to Jonathan Hall for his long and distinguished service in the role. His rigorous oversight and analysis have improved our laws and made our country safer.
Rupert Jones said:
I am very grateful to have been given the opportunity to serve in this role and provide an independent contribution to an evolving area of the law which is of significant public importance.
When reviewing the operation of the relevant provisions, I hope to inform public debate, scrutinise the use of the legislation and make appropriate recommendations, paying due regard to fairness and effectiveness, liberty and security.
During his tenure, Jonathan Hall KC has made a significant contribution to the development and scrutiny of the UK's counter-terrorism and national security framework. Notably, he helped shape the government's approach to youth diversion orders, introduced in response to the growing number of young people involved in counter-terrorism casework.
More recently, as the first Independent Reviewer of State Threats Legislation, his recommendation led to the introduction of proscription-like powers for the Home Secretary to designate state linked organisations involved in hostile activity against the UK.
Jonathan Hall KC said:
The independent reviewer carries out a unique and, I believe, an important role. I am very grateful to all the members of the public, officials, police, civil society organisations, academics, journalists, Parliamentarians, and ministers who gave up time, information and expertise to allow me to review how national security laws operate in practice. After more than seven years as independent reviewer, it is time for a fresh pair of eyes, and I wish Rupert Jones every success.
Both roles are responsible for assessing the fairness, effectiveness and proportionality of UK state threats and terrorism legislation, acting as important oversight and scrutiny. The reviewer is required to provide separate annual reports covering both pieces of legislation, of which their findings and recommendations will be reported to Parliament.
Rupert Jones will also be appointed to carry out future reviews for the Treasury and Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office, as required under the Sanctions and Anti-Money Laundering Act 2018, a role that Jonathan Hall KC had previously been appointed to, and delivered a report under in December 2023.
His appointment follows a robust competition in accordance with the requirements of the Governance Code of Public Appointments. He will begin his tenure on 23 November 2026.
