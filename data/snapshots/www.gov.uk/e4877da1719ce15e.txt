Company director sentenced after liquidators unable to verify more than £1 million
Director sentenced for failing to provide company records
- Jenna Lennon, 41, was handed a suspended sentence for an offence under the Insolvency Act 1986
- Liquidators could not verify the source of more than £1 million paid into Hope & Pride Limited's bank accounts, where Lennon was sole director
A director has been sentenced after failing to provide accounting records which left liquidators unable to verify more than £1 million passing through her company.
Jenna Lennon, 41, former sole director of Hope & Pride Limited, was sentenced to six months in prison, suspended for 12 months, at Southwark Crown Court on Thursday 26 November.
She was also ordered to complete 100 hours of unpaid work.
Lennon pleaded guilty in September to failing to deliver up the company's books and records to the liquidators following a prosecution by the Insolvency Service.
She was also disqualified as a company director for seven years in March 2025 over the same misconduct.
An Insolvency Service spokesperson said:
Company directors have clear legal responsibilities to keep proper records and provide them to liquidators when a company enters insolvency.
Without adequate records, liquidators can be prevented from establishing what happened to company money, what is owed to creditors and whether funds were used for legitimate business purposes.
Lennon's failure left significant questions about Hope & Pride Limited's financial affairs unanswered. Her seven-year director ban and this criminal prosecution demonstrate the consequences directors can face when they fail to meet their legal obligations.
Lennon's failure to provide sufficient records meant the liquidators could not verify the nature and source of more than £1.1 million paid into Hope & Pride Limited's bank accounts.
HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC) had established that £396,630 was owed on a Director's Loan Account for money paid out to Lennon, but the liquidators were unable to verify the balance because of the missing records.
Hope & Pride Limited, described on Companies House as providing "other information service activities not elsewhere classified", entered liquidation in September 2023. The missing records meant the liquidators could not establish the company's true liabilities.
The liquidators repeatedly requested the company's books and records from Lennon. However, Lennon did not provide any company books and records.
