Background
In May 2026, the Evaluation Task Force (ETF) announced Phase 5 of its Evaluation Accelerator Fund (EAF). The EAF is a competitive grant fund and supports research and evaluation projects that:
- create actionable evidence in government priority areas that informs public spending or policy decisions;
- tackle evidence gaps in government priority areas; or
- provide robust evidence of financial or efficiency savings from new policies, interventions, or innovative approaches to service delivery
Phase 5 of EAF had an overall value of £6.5 million for 2026/27 financial year. Its areas of priority focus were:
- Projects designed to improve the evidence base around Violence Against Women and Girls (VAWG).
- Projects related to artificial intelligence, local government and other government priorities.
In total, £4.7 million were awarded to 40 projects via a competition, this total includes a contribution of £1.1 million from UKRI to support projects related to VAWG and £590,000 from DWP for projects related to employment. £1.4 million were awarded directly to What Works Centres and a further £376,000 were awarded to Mayoral Strategic Authorities.

Measuring What Matters: Building the Evidence Base to Evaluate the Neighbourhood Policing Guarantee
- Organisation: London School of Economics
- Funding awarded: £83,152
- Fund priority area: General
Public confidence, trust and legitimacy are headline outcomes of the Neighbourhood Policing Guarantee (NPG), yet we lack a tool to track them as the Guarantee rolls out. This project funds Wave 1 of a three-wave panel: a 40-item survey of 1.7 million users of VISAV's Neighbourhood Alert platform. Wave 1 tests whether confidence, trust and legitimacy can be measured reliably, sets force-level baselines and estimates how far perceptions of neighbourhood policing predict the outcomes. Two partner-supported waves then re-invite Wave 1 respondents and compare matched early- and late-adopter areas, estimating whether the NPG is associated with improved outcomes.
Evaluating the wider economic impacts of Income Tax changes
- Organisation: London School of Economics
- Funding awarded: £124,481
- Fund priority area: General
This project evaluates the wider economic impacts of Income Tax changes, using quasi-experimental methods and the development of new data linking infrastructure. Using difference-in-difference designs, the project leverages two past changes in Income Tax rates (in 2010 and 2016) to study the causal effect of these reforms on a wide range of linked firm-level outcomes, including financial and taxable profits, capital investment, employment, and indicators of innovation. The findings provide new estimates of the direct fiscal effects of changes in Income Tax rates, as well as (for the first time) estimates of their wider economic impacts via spillovers at firm-level.

Evaluation support to the OPSI programme
- Organisation: Liverpool City Region Combined Authority
- Funding awarded: £80,000
- Fund priority area: General
The Office for Public Service Innovation (OPSI) is a partnership between the Liverpool City Region Combined Authority, local authorities, public service providers and the third sector. It brings organisations and communities together to improve public services and tackle long-term, complex challenges, using a Test and Learn approach. This project will gather initial evidence of whether OPSI has established a convincing proof of concept in its first year, and conduct a feasibility study to assess options for future impact evaluation. Lessons from this work will be of value to other local areas adopting and evaluating Test-and-Learn approaches to address public service challenges.
Operationalising the West Yorkshire Outcomes Framework: Evaluation, Evidence, and Strategic Decision-Making
- Organisation: West Yorkshire Combined Authority
- Funding awarded: £80,000
- Fund priority area: General
This project aims to research and develop an Evaluation Strategy aligned to the newly created West Yorkshire Outcomes Framework. The strategic framework will be devised following a literature review and alongside consultation and engagement with internal and external stakeholders. The output will enable the design, implementation and realisation of key portfolio evaluations involving focussed place-based public policy and financial interventions by the Combined Authority, supporting evidence-based decisions, policy, and delivery. The work will also establish operational and methodological models of good practice, of wider relevance to other MSAs facing similar challenges under the Integrated Settlement.
Retrospective evaluation of the Adult Skills Fund and development of a future M&E Framework
- Organisation: West of England Combined Authority
- Funding awarded: £66,200
- Fund priority area: General
This project evaluates the effectiveness of Adult Skills Fund provision in the West of England Combined Authority region by assessing how successfully it has contributed to the priorities of the Adult Skills Fund and wider Combined Authority. The project combines a retrospective analysis of reach, strategic alignment and commissioning outcomes with the development of a Monitoring and Evaluation framework to address key evidence gaps and strengthen future decision-making. Together, these outputs will be used to support improvements to the design, delivery and evaluation of Adult Skills Fund provision, as well as to inform future commissioning and investment decisions.
Improving the evaluation of educational improvement programmes and understanding the early signs of promise and opportunity for the Excellence in Education programme
- Organisation: North East Mayoral Strategic Authority
- Funding awarded: £80,000
- Fund priority area: General
This project delivers two interdependent outputs: an Evaluation & Measurement Framework Report for Education Improvement and a report on the Results of a Midpoint Evidence Sprint for the Excellence in Education programme. The first report seeks to identify robust, feasible measures in the area of education improvement that are relevant to this and successive programmes. The second will test those measures in practice and provide insight into their feasibility. By working with participating schools, the project gathers school leader's views on relevant programmes against indicative measures, utilising follow-up case study interviews to further contextualise the data, test assumptions and build upon the theory of change.
