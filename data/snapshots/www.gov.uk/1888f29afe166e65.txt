Home Secretary welcomes new Migration Advisory Committee chair
Professor Jackline Wahba OBE appointed chair of the Migration Advisory Committee (MAC).
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood has welcomed Professor Jackline Wahba OBE to her new role as chair of the MAC.
The MAC is an independent non-departmental public body that advises the government on the immigration system. It is sponsored by the Home Office.
Professor Wahba takes over as full-time chair from Dr Madeleine Sumption, who has been interim chair since March 2026. She has been appointed for a 3-year term to November 2029.
Dr Sumption will return to her term as deputy chair.
Professor Wahba was appointed after an open and fair recruitment process. This followed the Governance Code on Public Appointments, the process for which is regulated by the Commissioner for Public Appointments
The MAC carries out research and analysis on migration. It has worked with Skills England, the Department for Work and Pensions and the Industrial Strategy Council.
Together they have developed an evidence-based approach to building up the homegrown workforce and reducing reliance on international recruitment.
Net migration under this government has fallen by 74%.
Last year, the government kept its manifesto promise on the MAC by giving it more money and staff. This lets the committee forecast future migration trends and make policy recommendations more strategically.
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said:
I welcome Professor Wahba's appointment and look forward to working with the committee as it continues to provide expert advice on migration issues.
Our approach to migration policy must be based on thorough, factual analysis. That is why this government has given the MAC the resources it needs to make timely, evidence-based recommendations.
Chair of the MAC, Professor Jackline Wahba OBE said:
I am delighted to have been appointed Chair of the Migration Advisory Committee. The MAC plays an important role in providing independent, evidence-based advice to government on migration and its impacts on the UK economy and labour market.
I look forward to building on the MAC's important work and ensuring that our advice continues to be informed by rigorous research and robust evidence.
Professor Wahba is a Professor of Economics at the University of Southampton.
She is a leading economist, and her research focuses on the economics of international migration and refugees.
She was awarded an OBE for services to economic policy in 2020. She has advised governments and international organisations, including the World Bank, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, the International Labour Organisation and the European Commission.
