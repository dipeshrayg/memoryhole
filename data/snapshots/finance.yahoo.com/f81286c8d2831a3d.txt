Gen Z is taking job interviews on their phones - hiring managers call it 'incredibly disrespectful.' What to do instead
Gen Z already has a public-relations problem at work. Taking job interviews from an iPhone is probably not going to help.
The pandemic normalized remote hiring, giving applicants the freedom to log on from almost anywhere. For Jason Rogers, that meant using his phone while interviewing for his first job out of college as a content writer.

"I thought I was underprepared, but the setup was more solid than I imagined," he told MarketWatch. "The pressure was different as well, since holding the device in your hand changes the way you hold yourself in the conversation."
Rogers landed the job and continued taking interviews from his iPhone without receiving any criticism. But as phone interviews become more common among younger applicants, the practice is starting to irritate hiring managers who see it as yet another sign that some members of Gen Z are treating the job hunt a little too casually.
First impressions
That is not exactly the reputation the generation needs right now. Gen Z workers have already been accused of arriving late, ghosting recruiters and refusing to complete lengthy screening assignments, all while older workers complain that they are unwilling to "bust their behinds" like previous generations.
The criticism comes at an especially unforgiving time. Recent graduates are entering the weakest entry-level job market since the pandemic, while the underemployment rate reached 41.5%.
With fewer openings and more applicants competing for them, seemingly minor details can carry more weight. Joining a job interview from a phone may feel no different from hopping onto FaceTime, but to some hiring managers, it can read as unprepared or overly casual.
Sophie Rocha, a 23-year-old marketing professional and Gen Z career influencer, admits she is beginning to understand where some employers are coming from. After interviewing an applicant who joined the call from a phone, Rocha posted a TikTok suggesting her generation's workplace reputation may not be entirely undeserved.
"I fear that the people who say that Gen Z aren't getting jobs because of their attitude are slightly accurate," Rocha, who goes by @Sopharoch, said in a TikTok.
