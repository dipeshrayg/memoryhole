Stock market today: Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq rally as Nvidia earnings revive AI optimism, software stocks roar back
US stocks climbed on Thursday after earnings from Nvidia (NVDA), Salesforce (CRM), and CrowdStrike (CRWD) lifted the tech trade and investors awaited the Fed's Jackson Hole gathering.
The Dow Jones Industrial Average (^DJI) advanced 0.2%. The S&P 500 (^GSPC) gained 0.7%, and the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) rose 1.5%, building on Wednesday's gains.
Nvidia (NVDA) stock leaped 8% higher after the company reported an earnings beat and signaled strong AI demand throughout next year, easing concerns that the AI chip king wouldn't be able to sustain its torrid pace of growth.
On Wednesday night, the Information reported that Nvidia agreed to buy Hugging Face, an open-source model repository, for $12.9 billion. The reported move comes after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang touted the company's ballooning AI investments, saying, "the only regret that I have is that I didn't invest more and sooner" in AI labs.
With the AI bellwether's report in the rearview mirror, the focus now turns to the Federal Reserve's Jackson Hole Symposium, where central bank officials will debate the path of interest rate policy. Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh is expected to deliver a speech on Friday, though his message is anyone's guess.
Bond yields have stabilized after spiking last week as investors continue to weigh Treasury's intervention in the bond market and the likelihood that the Fed would hold rates steady. On the economic front, data on jobless claims ticked down to 203,000 in a positive sign for the labor market.
Investors also got a glimpse of consumer trends when Dollar General (DG) and Dollar Tree (DLTR) reported earnings. The two discount retailers reported better-than-expected earnings as they have attracted higher-income shoppers, but their stocks diverged due to differences in outlooks.

- Jake ConleyJPMorgan in talks with lenders on $5 billion debt package to fund Volta data center buildoutJPMorgan (JPM) is talking with lenders about a $5 billion debt package to fund data center construction by Volta Infra Holdings, Bloomberg reported Thursday - another headline on an increasingly debt-funded AI build-out. The news comes after Volta this month raised $300 million in venture capital funding, valuing the company at $2.4 billion, as it looks to build out and provide AI infrastructure to firms looking to purchase Nvidia chips that may lack the capital to do so outright. Though the company was founded only a few months ago, Bloomberg reported that Volta has already made a deal with Anthropic to provide $10 billion in compute capacity. The headline also comes as investor worries have ballooned on the shifting funding models of AI. While earlier Big Tech waves were largely funded by free cash flow, the AI boom has increasingly shifted toward the debt market as companies such as Alphabet have begun reporting negative free cash flow. The hyperscalers alone have issued more than $150 billion in USD investment-grade debt through 2026, per Bank of America, with more than $60 billion issued in other currencies, and analysts expect those numbers to only grow. Taking a wider read, companies have borrowed roughly $600 billion to fund the AI build-out since last year, per Bloomberg.

China remains a quagmireNvidia (NVDA) painted a bullish picture for investors when it reported its earnings after the bell on Wednesday, raising its guidance for the third quarter and beating analyst estimates across the board on revenue, earnings per share, and a host of other metrics. Missing from that forward guidance, however, were sales projections into the world's second-largest economy - China, where Nvidia's ability to sell its chips has been snarled by state politics and national security concerns. I report: That potential sales to Chinese buyers were left out of forward guidance from Nvidia gets at how complex the picture has become for one of the world's most valuable companies in its dealings with the US's largest AI competitor. Nvidia said in its earnings report that it had stepped back into the Chinese market, if in a small way, with the first sales of its H200 chips, the leading semiconductor design from the company's "Hopper" line, a step down from its state-of-the-art Grace Blackwell chips. Yet those sales accounted for less than 1% of the $89 billion Nvidia recorded in data center revenue in the second quarter. For Jensen Huang's company, complications come from both sides of the US-China AI arms race. Since 2022, Nvidia has wrestled with the Trump White House over sales of its chips to China, where even models such as the H200, which are now multiple generations behind Nvidia's flagship products, remain competitive with options available in China from companies like Huawei Technologies.
