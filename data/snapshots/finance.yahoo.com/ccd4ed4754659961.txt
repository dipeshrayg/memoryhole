Tech stocks climbed higher Friday after a rocky week that saw SpaceX (SPCX) and AMD (AMD) post earnings that failed to impress Wall Street's lofty expectations and Google (GOOG, GOOGL) announced major changes to its AI division.
Nvidia (NVDA) closed out the week on a hot streak, climbing more than 10% as concerns over the broader AI trade seemed to subside for the time being.
The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (^SOX) also rose for the week, rising more than 8% over the last five trading days.
SpaceX (SPCX) closed out a pivotal week solidly in the green, with the stock fighting higher over the past five sessions after reporting its first earnings report as a public company and the largest share unlock in its brief trading history.
The two events amounted to back-to-back stress tests, either of which could have sent the stock sharply lower. Instead, the Elon Musk-led rocket company weathered both.
SpaceX stock jumped 15.8% on Friday for its best day ever, continuing the late-week momentum to finish up nearly 23% for the week. It was SpaceX's highest close since July 15th.
Google said on Wednesday DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis is transitioning to a new role as chair of DeepMind and chief scientist for all of Alphabet. DeepMind CTO and Google chief AI architect Koray Kavukcuoglu will become senior vice president for DeepMind and report directly to Alphabet CEO Sundar Pichai.
The company is also losing chief scientist Jeff Dean, who is founding a new company called Discovery Loop with Google senior fellow Sanjay Ghemawat.
Elsewhere, Meta (META) was ordered to pay $567 million into a mental health fund for teens in New Mexico, after the social media giant lost one of a number of lawsuits alleging its apps cause harm.
