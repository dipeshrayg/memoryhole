"Everyone does it now, so I'm not picking on you," begins Michael Quinnell with an ominous glint in his text, "but where did this infuriating trend come from to summarise the game by stating that the side batting second is "trailing" when they haven't yet competed their 1st innings. I'm sure someone today said 'Australia are 0-0 at the start of play today, trailing by 329'. What utter nonsense! They cannot possibly be trailing until they are all out - if they pass the SA score with wickets in hand they would then be 'leading'."
It seems linguistically straightforward to me, Michael: the team batting second are trailing until they're in front, in the same way that footballers are 28 until they're 29.
If Australia are at some stage on 330 then they'll be leading by one. If they're on 328 they'll be trailing by one. It's not a permanent state while the innings is still live, and as you say, won't be formalised until the innings is complete, but the relative state still exists in the moment.
I agree it's redundant to give that number when a team is 0-0, but when partway through an innings, it's relevant to know the batting team's score in relation to the team that has already batted. Not sure how else to describe it.

Australians won't take it well! People older than my vintage, who remember that era, still talk about a very poor years in the mid-80s like they were a source of generational trauma. So painful were those years to Australians that they fired Allan Border's furious determination into building the era of dominance that followed, picked up by Mark Taylor, then Steve Waugh, then Ricky Ponting. The intensity of Australian players through that later era was all fuelled by remembering a brief time when the team was not very good. Not dismal, just not great.
It was a little similar between about 2008 and 2012, perhaps, with some middling sides turning out. People hated that, too. Remember the Argus Review?

There was a fair bit of Travis Head correspondence during the first session, so let's have a look. Starting with Michael Hall regarding the first-wicket stand. "South Africa didn't bowl badly but Australia took their chances well. Is this the opening partnership the Australians have been looking for?"
I'm still not sold on Head opening, simply on the basis of probability: he's such a proven damaging player in the middle order, and playing him up top means that he's more likely to get out cheaply more often. You'll get more bang for buck from him by playing him where it's safer to bat, and using someone more defensively capable where it isn't.
Yes, he has some amazing opening innings, but a lot of those were beating up a broken England attack that was tailor made to feed runs to that style of player. And it doesn't help a team if their opener regularly makes low scores when conditions are tough batting first. You need someone who'll survive that.
In short, you get value from him opening, but probably more if he were in the middle behind a good opening pair.
