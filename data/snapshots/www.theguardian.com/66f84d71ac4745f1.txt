Ministers have "failed to make the case" for a third runway at Heathrow, according to a damning report by MPs.
The Commons transport committee said on Thursday that the detailed policy papers laid out by the government showed expansion of the London airport would fall short of pledges on the economy and climate.
The chair of the committee, Ruth Cadbury, said plans for the third runway "should be grounded" and the government needed to "go back to the drawing board".
Heathrow's owner is seeking to build a third runway and expand its terminals at a cost of about £33bn. It says its plan would almost double passengers numbers and add up to 276,000 flights a year.
The committee was tasked with examining the government's Heathrow expansion national policy statement (HENPS) - but Cadbury said there was a "complete disjoint" between the plans and a lack of wider aviation strategy.
The report found the case was not made across the four tests ministers had set for expansion: that it generated growth across the country, without harmful impacts on climate, noise and air quality.
The report said that while the government was designating the runway as a critical national project, the economic growth projections in modelling commissioned by the Department for Transport were a fraction of previous estimates - a 0.05% boost to GDP rather than 0.5% - and did not justify the designation.
It said the expansion was "not currently compatible with the UK's legally binding climate targets", adding: "It seems impossible for the additional carbon dioxide emissions it will generate to be offset elsewhere in the economy."
The case for "potential compatibility rests too heavily on delivery of technologies" such as greener fuel and carbon capture, which was "nowhere near the necessary trajectories", MPs said.
The committee said it did not agree that reversing hard-won improvements in local air quality was an acceptable consequence and the HENPS did not take into account World Health Organization guidelines or emerging pollutants.
It added that the government still needed to set out how commitments relating to noise mitigation were "secured and delivered".
"It is highly concerning that there are no clear, committed, funded plans in place to improve surface public transport to an expanded Heathrow," the report said. "Expansion should not go ahead at any price, or at any cost to society or the environment."
Cadbury said: "Our report should be a wake-up call for ministers. We found that the government should have set out its overall strategy for aviation before publishing the draft HENPS. It did not do so and now it has failed to make the case for Heathrow expansion.
"Plans for a third runway should be grounded for now and the government should go back to the drawing board before proceeding any further."
The government under Keir Starmer had strongly backed a third runway, particularly the then chancellor, Rachel Reeves. However, Andy Burnham has historically opposed the third runway and while he had stated it was "a matter for Londoners", continued support for the scheme appears in doubt.
Responding to the committee's report, Heathrow's chief executive, Thomas Woldbye, said the third runway still had strong support.
"The overwhelming weight of evidence and support remains strongly behind Heathrow expansion. Trade unions, business groups, airlines, regional airports, senior cabinet ministers and the majority of MPs back expansion because they recognise it is vital to creating quality jobs and supporting long-term economic growth across the UK," Woldbye said.
He added: "The case for expanding Heathrow has been examined more thoroughly than almost any infrastructure project in modern Britain ... The focus now should be on delivering this privately financed project which will unlock opportunity across the country while meeting clear tests on affordability, financeability and sustainability."
Heathrow on Wednesday released polling that suggested three-quarters of Labour MPs backed the expansion plan. It hopes to secure planning permission for a third runway by 2029, building it over the course of the following decade.
Despite Burnham's ambivalence, the chancellor, John Healey, and the transport secretary, Heidi Alexander, have said it remains government policy, with the Conservatives and Reform UK recently speaking out strongly in favour of the third runway.
However, campaigners said the transport committee report showed that an expansion of Heathrow should not go ahead. Friends of the Earth said continued government support for the plan "would be flying in the face of the evidence".
Greenpeace said it would be the antithesis of Burnham's stated aims of good growth in every postcode, "draining jobs away from the rest of the country while bringing more traffic and pollution to south-west London".
