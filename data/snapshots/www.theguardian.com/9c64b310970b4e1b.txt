At the end of the 2021 season, the Cincinnati Bengals came up three points short in Super Bowl LVI against the Los Angeles Rams. Near the end of the 2022 season, the Bengals came up three points short - a 23-20 score line again in the AFC championship game against the Kansas City Chiefs. But from 2023 through 2025, those Bengals had to walk the desert for a while. Between injuries to quarterback Joe Burrow, an offensive line that was problematic at best, and one of the NFL's worst defenses from year to year, Cincinnati had a combined regular-season record of 24-27, and no playoff appearances.
Now, the new Bengals are 6-2 after their 34-17 shellacking of the Atlanta Falcons on Sunday at the Santiago Bernabéu stadium in Madrid. And Burrow is on a tear we've rarely seen from any quarterback. Over the last four weeks, his 16 touchdown passes is the most in a four-game span in Bengals history (which goes back to 1968). He is the second quarterback in pro football history with 16 or more passing touchdowns and two or more rushing touchdowns in any four-game span, putting him up with Tom Brady in 2007 - the season in which the New England Patriots' offense scored 36.8 points per game, and Brady was at his best. Burrow has been pressured on just 30.2% of his dropbacks this season, as opposed to 33.8% in 2025 and 34.2% in 2024.
When healthy, Burrow is as good as any quarterback you'll see. At this moment, he's better. Against a Falcons team ranked fifth in pass defense DVOA entering Sunday, Burrow completed 24 of 29 passes for 271 yards, three touchdowns, no interceptions and a passer rating of 140.1. The Falcons had no answers against Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins, Burrow's dynamic receiver duo, and unlike in previous seasons, Cincinnati's defense is prepared to ... if not match the offense's efforts, at least hold things up. The Bengals are average on defense, but when you have an offense like this, that's good enough. The only time anyone was able to dim Burrow's brightness was when the Bernabéu lights briefly went out in the fourth quarter.
Add in the fact that Cincinnati's run game is nothing to write home about (their 3.87 yards per carry and minus-0.13 EPA per run are each among the league's worst), and it's clear that as much as any NFL player, Burrow will be required to take his team as far as they can go. That's good news for the Bengals, because Burrow has traveled to the realms of the near-unstoppable.
Burrow has never won the NFL MVP award. (He came in fourth in both 2022 and 2024 when Patrick Mahomes and Josh Allen won it, respectively.) But at this rate, the trophy seems his to lose.
MVP of the week
Since Burrow is the MVP of the season's first half, how about Myles Garrett for Week 9? The Rams' edge rusher had been out since Week 1 of the 2026 season with a knee injury, and in his return to action, per Pro Football Focus, he recorded three sacks, nine total pressures and a forced fumble in LA's 20-13 win over the Washington Commanders. Garrett, of course, was supposed to be the centerpiece of the Rams' dream team following a blockbuster trade with the Cleveland Browns, and his presence was desperately needed for a franchise that was just trying to get above .500 on the season. Garrett did his level best for the now 5-4 Rams, and it wasn't just that the sacks happened, it was when they happened.
Garrett's first sack came with 2:20 left in the first half and the Commanders up 10-3. That was the forced fumble, recovered by linebacker Omar Speights. That led to a Rams drive that ended with a field goal at the end of the half. The second and third sacks came in the fourth quarter as the Rams were trying to hold their 20-13 lead.
The Rams are no longer in that dream team conversation, but maybe they can at least ride the wave to a playoff berth. If Puka Nacua can stay healthy, that is. Nacua, the Rams' top receiver, caught 14 passes on 19 targets for 150 yards and a touchdown in the win, but also suffered a knee injury that may keep him out for a while.
Video of the week
Nico Collins of the Houston Texans is easily one of the NFL's best X-iso receivers, which means that he can line up against the opponent's best cornerback in one-on-one situations, and what would usually be a 50-50 contested catch turns into 90-10 in Collins' favor because of his skillset. Collins made that abundantly clear with 8:16 left in the third quarter of Houston's 44-22 win over the beleaguered 0-8 Los Angeles Chargers.
In this case, cornerback Tarheeb Still was the unfortunate victim of Collins' rare gifts. Texans quarterback CJ Stroud threw to Collins, but Still seemed to have this ball at least deflected, if not outright intercepted. Until Collins somehow one-handed the catch to Still's right side, and that was that. Collins caught four passes on four targets for 55 yards and a touchdown, and the touchdown was certainly the catch of the day.
Runner-up: The corgi race during halftime of the Denver Broncos' 26-21 win over the Carolina Panthers at Bank of America Stadium in Charlotte, North Carolina, took an interesting turn, as Lennox the Corgi went rogue from the vertical race path and decided instead to become a canine Barry Sanders. By our estimation, Lennox evaded seven attempted tackles, and even stopped to ask his pursuers at one point, "Are we playing or what?"
With such lateral juice, contact balance, and swagger, Lennox could have a bright future ahead of him.
Stat of the week
86-2. With 26 seconds left in the first half of the New York Giants' 19-11 win over the Philadelphia Eagles, Philly punter Braden Mann booted the ball from his own eight-yard line to the New York six-yard line. That was an 86-yard punt, which tied for the seventh-longest in pro football history.
However ... with 6:53 left in the game, Mann fumbled the center snap, and had a punt of minus-two yards. It is, of course, the only time in a game that a punter had one punt of more than 80 yards and another with negative yardage. Mann also had punts of 63, 62, 60, 54, 44 and 17 yards, so you could say that he painted with every color in the palette.

The Bears didn't always make it easy on themselves - quarterback Caleb Williams ended three of four second-quarter drives with interceptions after throwing two touchdown passes in the first quarter - but the Bears' defense, with new acquisition Jeffery Simmons, was a monster.
