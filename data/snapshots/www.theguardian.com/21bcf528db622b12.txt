Today's puzzle played a pivotal role in the birth of classical physics.
The tower drop
An object is dropped from a high tower. Bearing in mind the rotation of the Earth itself, where will the object land?
-
slightly to the west
-
slightly to the east
-
exactly at the bottom
The answer is counter-intuitive. Or, in the words of Isaac Newton, who posed the problem in a letter he wrote to Robert Hooke in 1679, the answer is "quite contrary to ye opinion of ye vulgar."
Some hints. Think about how the spin of the Earth might affect the downwards trajectory. Also think about what happens when a spinning figure skater pulls in their arms.

Today's puzzle was suggested by David Acheson, emeritus professor of maths at Oxford and the author of several excellent popular maths books. His latest is Wonders in Motion, The Story of Dynamics, a beautifully accessible introduction to the science of motion.
Acheson's book includes more background about the tower drop puzzle. Hooke's correspondence with Newton rekindled Newton's interest in gravity and motion, which ultimately led to his formulation of the mathematical foundations of classical physics in the Principia.
