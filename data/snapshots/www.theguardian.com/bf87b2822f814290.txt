Stock markets on both sides of the Atlantic are dropping deeper into the red.
The DowJonesIndustrialAverage is now down 1%, having lost 511 points to around 51,096 points.
And in London, the FTSE100 index is down 1.4%, having dropped by 154 points to 10,726 points.
Investors may be getting jittery ahead of Nvidia's results tonight, to be followed by Anthropic's IPO on Thursday.
Reminder: Wall Street will hear from Nvidia's interim CEO, ColetteKress, tonight, following the resignation of JensenHuang last week after allegations that he conducted an affair with a suspected Chinese spy.
Nvidia will also face questions about claims that billions of dollars of its chips were sold to China, and about the implications of the US closing a loophole that allowed Chinese companies to access Nvidia-powered data centres in other countries.
Joe Mazzola, head trading & derivatives strategist at CharlesSchwab, says:
The chip giant typically releases results about half an hour after the close. Nvidia's call follows at 5 p.m. ET, led by CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress. If a board member or executive besides Kress speaks prominently, speculation might rise about where they stand in the Huang succession stakes. Investors could hear plenty of scuttlebutt on the CEO topic after the call.
The biggest question is whether Nvidia can keep growing parabolically without Huang at the helm. Outlook is paramount, and a devastating outcome would be if Nvidia pulls guidance. Back in August, Nvidia forecast fiscal 2028 revenue growth of 70%. See if the company sticks to that. Export controls on advanced chips are another topic, especially after the U.S. took new measures to limit remote access to advanced AI chips.
Shares fell more than 4% Monday as investors prepared for worst-case scenarios, and big share moves are priced in for Nvidia depending on the outcome. If the call gets an optimistic take, the entire sector could feel relief. Though quarterly results might seem secondary, earnings are seen up 90.4% to $2.48 per share and revenue is seen up 91.3% to $109.1 billion.

Jensen Huang and Nvidia agree to appear before Congress
Ousted Nvidia CEO JensenHuang, and the chipmaker's general council TimTeter, have both agreed to testify to three congressional committees next month.
Huang and Teter have voluntarily agreed to appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and the House Intelligence Committee for transcribed interviews, the chairs of the three committees say.
House Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman BrianMast, Select Committee on China Chairman JohnMoolenaar, and House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence Chairman RickCrawford had invited the two men to testify last week.
Chairmen Mast, Moolenaar, and Crawford said in a statement, just released:
"We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security.
"The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and Nvidia's role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal."
Huang will appear before the committees on December 1st, and Teter will appear on December 2nd.

Here's a great chart from JimReid at DeutscheBank, showing how Nvidia's earnings have soared since the AI boom began, and how they are expected to keep accelerating:
He explains:
Forget the macro, the biggest event of the next few weeks is probably tonight's Nvidia earnings. It's a good moment to remember that the company's transformation is arguably one of the most extraordinary in corporate history.
When ChatGPT launched in November 2022, Nvidia was earning around $5bn a year. That was a respectable number for a company still best known to gamers and crypto miners. Today, on a rolling four-quarter basis, it's likely to be nearer $240bn, and consensus has it reaching around $625bn by 2031. If that happens, Nvidia will have gone from $5bn of net income to over $600bn in under a decade.
I can't think of anything that comes close. The biggest annual profit ever reported is generally thought to be Saudi Aramco's roughly $160bn in 2022, when oil was last above $100 and Europe was scrambling for energy. Nvidia is now ahead of this. Its 2031 forecast would be about the size of the current annual GDP of Thailand, the UAE or Austria. Those countries have populations of around 72m, 11.5m and 9.2m respectively.
Nvidia, at its last filing, employed around 42,000 people. Yes, it's apples and oranges, or perhaps chips, but it shows the sheer scale of what's going on.

US government confirms investigation into Megaspeed over Nvidia chip exports
Two US government departments have confirmed they are investigating computing company Megaspeed, suspecting it of violating US export controls by helping to ship tech giant Nvidia's chips to China.
A joint statement issued by the Department of Commerce and the Department of Justice explains that Megaspeed has been under investigation since 2024, for "potential export control violations."
In May 2025, Department of Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed. In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its "no sell" list, they explain.
And they also state that commerce department agents conducted an on-site, in-person, end-use check at Megaspeed in June 2025 and interviewed CEO Alice Huang.
The Department of Justice then took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed.
Tooting their own trumpets, the departments add:
This investigation is the result of the excellent work of the Department of Commerce under Secretary Howard Lutnick and the law enforcement efforts of the Department of Justice under Attorney General Todd Blanche.
That, presumably, is a response to suggestions that Lutnick could be fired following accusations that he had shielded Nvidia from federal oversight, and had failed to blacklist Megaspeed.
