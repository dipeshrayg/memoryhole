Australia's sandpaper soap opera has become one of alternative facts, but recollections of the second game in this one-day international series are unlikely to differ too greatly. For Tom Banton and Gus Atkinson, architects of an emphatic 135-run victory for England under lights on Sunday it was a match to remember.
Banton was sublime after England were asked to bat, unfurling a crisp 121 from 99 balls to underpin an imposing total of 355 for six. No touring side had gone bigger against Australia in their own back yard - the ideal platform for Harry Brook's pace attack to release the handbrake and level the three‐match series.
Not that it felt a done deal at halfway, with the pitch true, the square boundaries short, and two dangerous openers in Mitch Marsh and Travis Head. England failed to defend 429 during their tri-series blowout against Sri Lanka in Pakistan, while Adil Rashid, middle overs maestro for the past 11 years, was once again ferrying drinks.
But up stepped Atkinson with a sizzling three-wicket new-ball burst that derailed Australia's chase and a career‐best four for 28 followed. From their initial 29 for four, half‐centuries from Cameron Green and Matt Renshaw spared Australia's blushes. That said, their 220 all out owed plenty to three dropped catches.
"I've watched a lot of cricket in Australia over the years - the Ashes and white-ball series - so to perform here today is a pretty special feeling," Banton said, having earlier shared a run‐a‐ball stand of 137 with Joe Root to start the fightback after Perth. "To be able to stand at the other end and watch [Root] go about his business is pretty cool."
The two teams travel to Hobart for a decider on Wednesday and the kind of scenario that Brook's team have been craving a year before the World Cup. They are far from the finished product, clearly, but Banton's second century since his recall in the summer sits among the positives.
Conditions were a world away from the bounce of Perth, while Mahli Beardman, the six-wicket star 48 hours earlier, was rested. After shaking off an early blow to the head from Spencer Johnson, and the loss of Ben Duckett, Banton enjoyed punching fours through cover and used the ground's dimensions to propel four sixes.
Another plus was that England's batting fired without Brook contributing and his dismissal, bowled for five trying to ramp Riley Meredith, will not have changed his reputation in this part of the world. Root had just departed for a fluent 62 to make it 156 for two in the 28th over, while Banton was motoring at the other end.
Even with fine leg up, it was a poor option, and after Banton holed out Brook was shown up by Jordan Cox and Will Jacks in a stand of 102 from just 70 balls. Cox had been happy to tick along at first, with his 49-ball 67 a case of blossoming when set. It also featured one absurd reverse-scooped four with both knees on the turf.
Jacks likewise built towards a climax with an unbeaten 54 from 40 balls, while Atkinson cracked a couple of late sixes. The latter was just getting started, it turned out, wiping out Head, Cooper Connolly and Josh Inglis cheaply in a seven-over spell and the recalled Sonny Baker striking at the other end.
As well as the stash of runs staring back down from the Adelaide Oval's historic scoreboard, there was a bit more zip for both men under lights. They make for a contrasting new‐ball pair, too, with the quietly spoken Atkinson tight in on the stumps and the more outgoing Baker sending down fast, late outswing.
Baker's removal of the in-form Marsh for two - one of two fine slip catches by Root -justified his ousting of Jamie Overton from the XI, as well as being preferred to Jofra Archer up top. Archer bowled well through the middle - it looks a good blend overall - even if he had to wait until the very end for his reward.
Renshaw and Green profited from early lives as England's hands suddenly went from adhesive to Teflon, before the pair rebuilt with a 98‐run stand that briefly raised Australian hopes. These were doused by Liam Dawson trapping Renshaw lbw from around the wicket and the hosts subsided once more.
Most eye-catching among the second collapse of four for 46 was Sam Curran bowling the giant Jack Edwards with a truly glacial slower ball before inflicting a first-baller on Xavier Bartlett. By the time Dawson had Green stumped, a 31,000-strong crowd had largely evaporated and the win was sealed in near silence.
A day to forget for Australia, therefore. Albeit with Chadd Sayers giving a public account of events in South Africa eight years ago before play and slightly undermining that of Pat Cummins last week, it is unlikely to be.
