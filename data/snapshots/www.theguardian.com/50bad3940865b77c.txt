Renée Slegers has left her job as Arsenal Women's head coach after a dismal set of results, with the club saying she has gone by mutual consent.
The 37-year-old had been under contract until 2029 after signing a three-and-a-half-year deal in January but six consecutive home draws in the Women's Super League this season have left Arsenal sixth, 12 points behind the leaders, Chelsea, and eight points adrift of a European place.
Arsenal's assistant coach Aaron D'Antino has also left, having been on the backroom staff since 2018. Arsenal's director of women's football, Clare Wheatley, told the club's website a "change was necessary", saying: "It was an extremely difficult decision to part ways with Renée, who has led the team with dedication and commitment throughout her time here.
"In leading us to glory in the Champions League and the inaugural Fifa Champions Cup, Renée has made a significant and lasting contribution to the history of Arsenal Football Club, with close support from Aaron. After careful reflection, it is clear that results have been below the expected level, and change is necessary."
Arsenal's chief executive, Richard Garlick, described Slegers as a "wonderful person" but said a new head coach was required to give the club the best chance of meeting the ambition "to compete for and win major trophies consistently".
It is understood Arsenal were determined to give Slegers time and so stood by her earlier in the season when speculation about her future was mounting but that eventually the two parties concluded a change was needed.
Arsenal's search to find Slegers' replacement is under way but it is understood the club will not rush to make an appointment, because their priority is to find the best person rather than ensuring somebody is in place for their next match, at Everton on 11 December in the WSL.
Melissa Clarke-Reed, the former Brighton and London City Lionesses head coach, has been placed in interim charge. The club are believed to be comfortable with her, if necessary, overseeing the three games that precede the winter break. Arsenal then return to action on 10 January.
Slegers took charge in October 2024, initially as interim head coach, after the resignation of Jonas Eidevall, under whom she had worked as an assistant. She oversaw a run of 10 wins in 11 matches without defeat in caretaker charge, prompting the club to appoint her as the head coach on an 18-month deal.
The Dutchwoman then led Arsenal to one of the greatest moments in their history as they won the Women's Champions League title on 24 May 2025, stunning Barcelona with a surprise 1-0 victory in Lisbon to land the club's second European title and their first for 18 years.
The 2025-26 season ended with Arsenal second in the WSL, four points behind the champions, Manchester City. They were semi-finalists in the Champions League and League Cup and quarter-finalists in the FA Cup, but Slegers won - on home soil - the inaugural Fifa Women's Champions Cup, a competition between the winners of the respective continental titles.
Arsenal had held high hopes of mounting a WSL title challenge this term, vying to win their first domestic league title since 2019, and had what appeared to be a strong summer transfer window, signing high-calibre internationals such as the England midfielder Georgia Stanway and the Spain full-back Ona Batlle.
They have had injuries, including to their integral midfielder Mariona Caldentey, who is sidelined with a hamstring problem. The team's inability to break down opponents set up in a low block has been a repetitive issue, and on Sunday there were loud boos at the Emirates when they were held to a goalless draw by West Ham.
Slegers, a former Netherlands midfielder, managed the Swedish club Rosengård and Sweden's under-23s before joining Arsenal in 2023 to work under Eidevall.
