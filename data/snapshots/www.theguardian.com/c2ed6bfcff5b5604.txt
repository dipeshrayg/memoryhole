30 mins. The All Blacks lose the ball again, Vaa'i coughing up a McKenzie pass that for once had them nearly on the front foot. If you're up in the middle of the night to follow this in Aotearoa then you have my sympathies for what your team is serving up.
28 mins. On a scrum penalty advantage NZ move the ball again, but it's very lateral with McKenzie crabbing across the field to make it easy for the England defence. Nothing comes of it so the All Blacks will go again back from the penalty on the right touchline.
25 mins. The NZ threequarters finally have the chance to warm their hands with some clean possession from a lineout. They run a nice pattern, but England cover it with little issue before Jordan spurts the ball out of his hands out on the wing under zero pressure.
23 mins. Back England come, however, with Furback running the ball back with far too much ease, cantering 30 metres into the NZ half. McKenzie adds to the problems by tackling Atkinson off the ball and the England pack drive to the line from the resulting 5m lineout.
Coles drives over but the black defence engulfs him to hold the ball up.
20 mins. England stroll into the 22 again, but in the final phases the All Blacks are that little bit more keen at the breakdown and spoil possession, this time keeping the ball off the ground to turn it over. There's another small victory for the visitors when they win a scrum penalty to once again relive the pressure.
18 mins. McKenzie scrambles to gather the ball from a Slade grubber that once again has NZ under pressure in their 22. They are calm enough to reset the ball and clear to touch. But the relief doesn't last long as Scott Barrett is penalised or collapsing a post-lineout maul.
The All Blacks are getting thwacked pretty much everywhere and England should be fuming they are not further ahead.
15 mins. A clean lineout win from Itoje is moved quickly to Smith who finds another kick in behind for Joseph to chase. It's a decent foot race but Bauden Barrett is first to it.
13 mins. Sititi has a dart from the base of the scrum to set up an attack in the middle of the England half before Ref Berry blasts on his whistle as Heyes is suspected of a hip-drop tackle. The opportunity is wasted as Talyor overthrows the 5m lineout and Earl snaffles it. Van Poortvliet clears his lines.
10 mins. There's no real benefit from the penalty lineout as the NZ defence are first to the breakdown to win a penalty of their own for England holding on. Roigard punts the ball upfield and a handling error in midfield hands a scrum to the All Blacks in enemy territory.
8 mins. The trademark of England's attack recently has been the speed of recycling and then the absolute confidence to use the ball when presented to the backs. This is evident again on the latest possession before Furbank drills an exquisite 50:22 down the right touchline with barely 5 metres to play with.
5 mins. Possession is retained by England from the penalty and some fast recycling hand Fin Smith an age to pick an angled grubber in-goal that Slade grounds.
Conversion is pulled left, but still a great start by the home side.
3 mins. England have their first attacking platform from a scrum just inside the NZ half. Joseph takes a solid carry into the line on first phase and this is enough to get the visiting defence moving backwards. The next few phases feature a rumble up by Earl before the ball is moved across the field and a desperate defence is already offside.
2 mins. Jordie Barrett is this close to grasping a long clearing kick from McKenzie after the All Blacks received the kick off. However, it bumps off his fingertips under a mid-air challenge from Will Joseph.
Lewis Moody has joined the England team as they line up to face the haka. The home side move into a V formation to receive the challenge led by NZ captain Codie Taylor.
Moody sees off each England player in turn as he leaves the field with emotion clearly running high with each of them and he embraces them in turn. Quite the build up.

Alastair Campbell has a book out called Persevilience, the title apparently a mash-up of perseverance and resilience (see what he did there) to form the worst compound noun since dorkburger. Although, one imagines the creator of the latter wasn't trying to make it "the word the world needs now" like Big Al is on the cover of his volume.
However hateful the word, a still injury ravaged New Zealand need to show some of it today as they attempt to shrug off their seventy-three years in the making dorkburgering in Cardiff last week.
Since 1996, where the All Blacks have played tests with no more than a 21-day gap they suffered defeat in 52 first-up fixtures, with only 18 of the follow-up matches being a loss. Fourteen of those next-up losses were against either South Africa (10) or Australia (4), and they have only lost two consecutive matches on their November tours on one occasion in 2021 (Ireland, then France). On the 31 occasions they followed a defeat with a win, the week one loss margin average is seven points (sound familiar?) and the week two win margin is 26.
Thus, history tells us that such a situation tends lead to a rage filled bounceback being the order of the day, but matching the historic 26 point next-up winning spread will be a tougher ask than usual. The Dave Rennie led 2026 All Blacks are not the team of yore, and that's before we consider their medical list, plus they must deliver it at SponsorDome Twickenham versus an England side on a decent run of form and results.
Exactly how good the home side's run is can be debated; they are more fluent, confident and organised in attack than at any time during Borthwick's tenure, but their wins this Autumn have come against an indisputably wretched Australia and a weak Japan. They are yet to be tested in defence and New Zealand's attacking shortcomings vs Wales will surely not be repeated; but the squad is settled and consistent, with their tails up and no small amount of power and ability.
The sun is out and there's a fascinating test match incoming.
