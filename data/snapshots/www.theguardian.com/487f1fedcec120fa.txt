I've wondered that too, and also whether this chop-change thing is making him less effective. There are mutterings that he's contributing in the margins, and his last bag of wickets was over two years ago in Wellington. But by leaving him out this many times recently, is Australia hurting themselves by having a less confident, less settled bowler in their line-up when they do need him?
In Jamaica or in Brisbane, did the fourth seamer win them the Test? No. Would they have won with Lyon in the team? Probably. So is there actually a benefit to the configuration without him? Debatable, but there is an observable negative if you're unsettling a vital player in the medium term.

This was the risk of Test cricket in October, we're told by South Africans. More likely to have bad weather. In 2018, of course, this tour was played in March and even strayed into the first days of April. Seven of the last eight Australian visits started in February or March. But that can't happen anymore, because that would displease the IPL, whose owners want Australian players, and would displease Australian players who want to earn the extra slice from the IPL.
Well, perhaps more importantly it would deny that same chance to South African players, who need that extra payday much more urgently given how little they comparatively earn from their home board, or more realistically it would see a Test series shorn of anyone who got picked up by the IPL.
Does the rain mean we're doomed to have a draw? Not necessarily, given the speed at which Test cricket moves in this era. Even in Durban, which was an uncharacteristic grind of a match relative to many recently, they only played about 360 of a possible 450 overs. All the time lost to bad light didn't matter, and it wasn't a close-run thing to finish the game on day five. If wickets keep falling when we get some play, the rain may not matter.
