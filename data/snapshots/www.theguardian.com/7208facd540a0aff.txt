Senegal have been reinstated as the champions of Africa, and their controversial 1-0 victory over Morocco confirmed in the record books, after the scandal over January's Africa Cup of Nations final was resolved in a Swiss courtroom.
The court of arbitration for sport (Cas) ruled that a decision to award the 2025 Afcon title to Morocco on appeal should be set aside. Senegal celebrated with a social media post proclaiming the team to have won the title twice: first on the field and then at Cas. Morocco, who are due to co-host the World Cup in 2030, declared their intention to "pursue all legal avenues" to challenge the ruling.
A Cas panel of three legal experts agreed unanimously that the original result should stand because of the primacy of decisions made on "the field of play". The referee's decision to complete the fixture was held to be a definitive act that could not be overturned by Senegal's breach of Afcon's rules when they walked off and caused a 15-minute delay in one of the most notorious encounters in the competition's history.
The final was held in the Moroccan city of Rabat on 18 January. With the match goalless in second-half added time, the then Senegal coach, Pape Thiaw, withdrew his team from the field in protest at Morocco being awarded a penalty, shortly after Senegal had had a goal disallowed. After Senegal returned to the pitch, Brahim Díaz missed the spot kick with a Panenka. Senegal went on to win with a goal in extra time.
The result caused huge ructions in African football and became a media sensation. An initial appeal by the Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) to the Confederation of African Football (Caf) argued that Senegal's behaviour had contravened Afcon rules which prevent teams from leaving the field without the referee's authorisation. The argument was accepted by Caf, which overturned the result in March, awarding Morocco the title in a 3-0 walkover.
Senegal responded with an appeal, and an in-person hearing took place behind closed doors at Cas on 8 October. Cas verdicts can be challenged at Switzerland's supreme court only on limited procedural grounds.
The judging panel concluded that the "field of play doctrine" which prevents the court from reviewing decisions taken on the pitch by match officials must hold sway, and that because referee, Jean-Jacques Ndala, had not chosen to call the match off the result should stand.
"By blowing his whistle to resume his match at the 109th minute the referee considered that the conditions for resuming the match had been met," said Matthieu Reeb, the director general of CAS. "Both teams were on the pitch again and both expressed their willingness to resume. The Cas panel found that the referee's decision fell solely within the scope of the rules of the game and was therefore within his sole authority. The Caf rules [Afcon regulations] would have been applicable if the referee had blown the final whistle which would have resulted in Senegal's forfeit loss, but this was not the case."
In a trenchant statement the FRMF announced its determination to continue fighting the result, saying it "reaffirms its determination to pursue all available legal avenues to challenge this Cas decision".
The FRMF argued that the Cas decision "failed to take into account the stance recently adopted by both the International Football Association Board (Ifab) and the International Federation of Association Football (Fifa) regarding players leaving the field of play without the referee's authorisation, as well as the associated responsibilities and disciplinary sanctions."
It also expressed "astonishment" that Cas had not delayed its verdict after being presented with claims by the FRMF that one of the three panel members, professor Dr Ulrich Haas, had had a private telephone conversation with the president of the Senegalese Football Federation on Sunday.
Cas said: "The [international council of arbitration for sport] challenge commission found that there was not enough evidence at the time of the challenge to delay the issuing of the decision. Ulrich Haas strongly denies any direct contact at any moment with the parties involved in this procedure."
Senegal's Sadio Mané said on Instagram that the Cas decision "fills us with joy". He spoke of the "efforts and sacrifices everyone has put into for this second star" and thanked fans for their support. He also expressed solidarity with Morocco, saying: "Senegal and Morocco are two brother peoples. May the passion of football continue to bring us closer."
