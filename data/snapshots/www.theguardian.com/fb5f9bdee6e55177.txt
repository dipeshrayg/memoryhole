The boss of the British Gas owner Centrica has told hundreds of staff at its largest gas storage facility that he was seeking urgent talks with Andy Burnham and would do everything he could to save jobs.
Chris O'Shea, the chief executive of Centrica, reportedly told staff at the Rough gas storage facility off the east Yorkshire that the government had rebuffed the company's plea for short-term support to run the store over the winter.
In a memo, seen by Sky News, O'Shea is understood to have expressed disappointment that the government had delayed its response to a consultation on whether to support the ageing underground gas stores over the longer term.
The Guardian understands that the government delivered its decision to Centrica in the last few weeks and the internal company memo was intended to update the facility's staff about continuing talks.
The Rough gas storage facility supports about 400 jobs, including direct employees and contractors, but is scheduled to close at the end of March - unless the government intervenes - because it has become uneconomic to run.
Facilities typically store gas in the summer when the market price is lower, and sell it in winter when the price rises due to higher demand. But successive energy crises in recent years have upended this pattern, making it difficult to make enough money to invest in the upgrade work needed to keep the facility running.
"No decisions about roles have been made," O'Shea wrote in the memo, according to Sky News. He reportedly added that the company was "doing everything we can to save your jobs" and that he would seek urgent talks with trade unions, Whitehall officials and the prime minister on the issue.
It is the latest in a series of warnings by Centrica over the future of the site. Centrica is listed on the FTSE 100, valued at nearly £7bn.
The jobs warning has emerged less than a fortnight after the energy company SSE told investors it would shut its Atwick gas storage site in east Yorkshire next summer unless the government offered support.
SSE said although the site continued to have "strategic importance" to the energy system as the North Sea's domestic production declined, it was "increasingly clear" that the market would not support its continued operations.
Earlier this month, the National Energy System Operator (Neso) used a second consecutive annual report to warn that the UK was at risk of a gas supply crisis as the North Sea's production wound down.
The report underlined concerns raised in Neso's first annual report which found that while the UK has enough access to global gas markets to cover demand when weather is normal, if even a single piece of Britain's ageing gas infrastructure suffered an "unlikely" outage during a very cold spell, then the UK could run out of gas.
A spokesperson for Centrica declined to comment.
