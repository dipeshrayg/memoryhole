Nvidia board reinstated Huang without consulting management - report
Nvidia's board of directors' surprise move to reinstate ousted CEO Jensen Huang was done without consulting the company's senior leaders, who were informed of the plan at the last minute, Bloomberg is reporting, citing people familiar.
Per Bloomberg's report: "The decision was made Sunday afternoon after board members faced pressure from US President Donald Trump, according to the people, who asked not to be identified because the discussions were private. Some candidates to replace Huang had also ruled themselves out of the process, the people said.
"The plan to bring back Huang, announced shortly before markets opened in New York on Monday, came together in mere hours on Sunday. The deliberations involved Trump but excluded interim CEO Colette Kress and other top executives, according to people familiar with the matter."
US reportedly set to blacklist Megaspeed chip firm linked to Nvidia scandal
The United States on Monday is set to add Singapore-based chip firm Megaspeed, which is closely tied to an espionage investigation involving Nvidia, to its trade blacklist, a source has told Reuters.
It follows revelations that Nvidia's freshly reinstated CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and Megaspeed's controlling shareholder who is alleged to be a spy for Beijing.
US companies cannot ship goods, software and technology to companies on the list ‌without a license, ‌which is likely to be denied.
The New York Times reported last ‌month that US authorities had concluded Alice Huang was a Chinese intelligence asset who passed information to Beijing while developing a close relationship with the Nvidia CEO.
Reuters could not independently verify the intelligence allegation. China has called the spy accusations "groundless".
Nvidia lists Megaspeed as an official cloud partner in its directory. Megaspeed attracted public attention in October 2025, when the NYT reported it was under US investigation over whether it was helping Chinese customers obtain advanced Nvidia chips subject to Washington's export controls.
The last time the US commerce department placed any new entities on the list was October 2025.
As Reuters reported in June, the US has held off adding China's AI startup DeepSeek, memory ‌chipmaker CXMT and more than 100 other companies flagged as national security risks to the trade blacklist, as the Trump administration tried to avoid escalating tensions with Beijing.

Republicans question Jensen Huang return and Trump's involvement
Ariana Baio
Several US Republican lawmakers have raised questions and concerns about Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang after Donald Trump admitted that he "convinced" Nvidia's board to bring Huang back.
Pete Ricketts, a senator from Nebraska, wrote on X that Huang's "behavior does not support reinstatement".
Ricketts, who sits on the Senate foreign relations committee, said Huang still needed to testify before Congress next week and that Nvidia still needed to answer questions about national security concerns.
John Curtis, a senator from Utah who sits on the Senate commerce committee, noted that Nvidia's board relied on information from a government official to reinstate Huang.
"Nvidia's board says it relied on input from senior U.S. officials to conclude that bringing Jensen Huang back posed no national security risk... Who in our government weighed in? What did they tell the board? And on what basis did the board conclude there was no risk?" Curtis asked.
John Cornyn, an outgoing senator from Texas, posted several times on social media, questioning whether the FBI did a defensive briefing on Huang, whether that information was ignored, Nvidia's corporate governance and more.
Another outgoing representative Don Bacon, of Nebraska, wrote: "Jensen Huang was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy. Nvidia should have moved on without Huang and ignored the Administration's inexplicable desire to save Huang. Congress needs to get to the bottom of who approved high end computer chips sales to China. We cannot say we want to win the AI race and then give China our best technology. We need sunlight on who is responsible."

After Fathom Consulting made an AI stock market bust its central forecast for 2027 (see previous post), rival analysts at BCA Research say the collapse in share values and investment next year will be bad enough to trigger a recession.
The return of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's boss has cheered the markets for now, but the bust is coming.
BCA's chief economist Peter Berezin, says that while much of the investment in data centres and new chips by Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet (Google), Meta and Oracle - the so-called hyperscalers - is already set in train and will run into 2028, stock market investors will smell trouble ahead and sell in 2027.
Berezin says:
Stocks usually peak well before [capital expenditure] peaks. Thus, even if AI capex does [plateau or decline] until 2028, financial conditions could still tighten next year as the stock market begins to price in a capex bust. As such, AI represents the biggest macro risk for 2027.
He adds that there will likely be "violent countertrend rallies in tech stocks" until the end of the year, mimicking the fits and starts that characterised the 2000-2001 stock market crash.
A deeper recession that the US experienced in 2001 will then likely follow.
