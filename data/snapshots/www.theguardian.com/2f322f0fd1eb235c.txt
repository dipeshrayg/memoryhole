Newsflash: Jensen Huang has been sensationally reinstated as president and chief executive officer of Nvidia, less than two weeks after he resigned.
In a surprise statement, Nvidia announced that its board of directors voted unanimously to reinstate Huang following a series of discussions and meetings, "after which the Board was satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead Nvidia".
In a hint to the political presure put on the company, Nvidia added:
In the process of reaching its decision, the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.
Harvey C. Jones, Chair of the Board, says:
"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.
"We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company. We are grateful to Colette Kress for her outstanding service as interim CEO, in addition to her continuing responsibilities as NVIDIA's Chief Financial Officer."

After Fathom Consulting made an AI stock market bust its central forecast for 2027 (see previous post), rival analysts at BCA Research say the collapse in share values and investment next year will be bad enough to trigger a recession.
The return of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's boss has cheered the markets for now, but the bust is coming.
BCA's chief economist Peter Berezin, says that while much of the investment in data centres and new chips by Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet (Google), Meta and Oracle - the so-called hyperscalers - is already set in train and will run into 2028, stock market investors will smell trouble ahead and sell in 2027.
Berezin says:
Stocks usually peak well before [capital expenditure] peaks. Thus, even if AI capex does [plateau or decline] until 2028, financial conditions could still tighten next year as the stock market begins to price in a capex bust. As such, AI represents the biggest macro risk for 2027.
He adds that there will likely be "violent countertrend rallies in tech stocks" until the end of the year, mimicking the fits and starts that characterised the 2000-2001 stock market crash.
A deeper recession that the US experienced in 2001 will then likely follow.
