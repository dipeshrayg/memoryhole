Rassie Erasmus is facing the prospect of a lengthy suspension with World Rugby expected to launch an investigation after he questioned whether officials are "match-fixing" and took aim at the refereeing standards in the Nations Championship.
Amid a clampdown on comments that call officials' integrity into question, a disciplinary process is expected to take place in the coming weeks. If concluded next week and Erasmus is found guilty, he may be banned from Twickenham for the Nations Championship finals weekend.
After targeting the Australian referee Nic Berry during the 2021 British & Irish Lions tour of South Africa, Erasmus was given a ban of two months from all rugby involvement and 10-and-a-half months from involvement with the Springboks on matchdays. If a disciplinary panel decides to throw the book at South Africa's head coach again, his involvement at the start of South Africa's World Cup defence next year could be jeopardised.
In July, World Rugby launched a new match officials abuse disciplinary process in an effort to "reduce comments which often are the basis of the unacceptable increase in abuse and threats towards match officials". Sanctions range from a warning to a fine and a lengthy suspension. Over the summer Italy's head coach, Gonzalo Quesada, received an automatic ban for two matches - later reduced to one - for saying "the refereeing team was partly to blame for the score. They made a lot of mistakes today", after his side's defeat by New Zealand. Erasmus is expected to face the same disciplinary process where a panel would take a dim view of his suggestions of "match-fixing".
Erasmus' remarkable tirade came after last weekend's 21-19 loss to France. The South Africa head coach claimed that officials had missed 17 knee-drop tackles committed on his side, suggesting that incidents of foul play are not being picked up in what he described as a "manipulation of the game" and that officials are showing more leniency to northern hemisphere teams. To illustrate his point he predicted Ireland's fly-half Sam Prendergast would be available to face South Africa on Saturday despite being cited for a hip-drop tackle.
Referring to the 17 knee-drop tackles he claims went unpunished, Erasmus said: "The TMO doesn't come in once. I'm not saying he's wrong. I'm saying if he didn't see it, it must be a technology problem. If he saw it and he decided not to act on it, is that not match-fixing? Is that not interfering with the outcome of a game if you see this injury that happens there?"
Erasmus added: "If the TMO didn't see it, maybe then technology must be better. If he saw it and he ignored it, what does that boil down to? Manipulation of the game or the result, or injured players getting injured." Erasmus said he was not making an "accusation" about the officials, and acknowledged there was "a drive in World Rugby that the TMO should stay out and not interrupt the game".
Given the new sanction process, it is unclear whether Erasmus' previous suspensions will count against him. As well as his lengthy ban after the 2021 Lions tour, Erasmus was banned for two matches in November 2022 for a series of social media comments about the English referee Wayne Barnes, who revealed he was on the receiving end of death threats.
A World Rugby statement issued in July read: "Under the process, an independent panel comprising the independent judicial panel chair and two experienced judicial panel members consider whether any conduct or comments made by team personnel includes among other things, allegations that a match official acted with bias, prejudice, improper motive, external influence, or deliberate intent to affect an outcome and/or conduct which, viewed objectively, they knew or ought to know would undermine public confidence in match officials.
"Assessment of comments takes account of a full range of factors including the nature and reach of the comments, the timing, the role of the speaker and any aggravating factors, such as whether the conduct led directly to an increase in personal abuse against a match official. Outcomes range from "no further action" to a guidance letter, formal warning, or automatic sanction under the framework."
