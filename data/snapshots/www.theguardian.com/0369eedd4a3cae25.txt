Rassie Erasmus has been banned for six international matches with immediate effect by World Rugby after an investigation into his remarkable rant on Monday in which he questioned whether officials were match-fixing.
The global governing body has moved swiftly with a disciplinary panel determining "that the threshold for serious misconduct had been met" by Erasmus, who also took aim at the standard of refereeing in the Nations Championship, suggesting a bias towards the northern hemisphere.
Erasmus will be forced to sit out South Africa's clash with Ireland on Saturday as well as the Springboks' match on finals weekend. It is likely to expire at some point during next year's Rugby Championship, meaning it will not affect South Africa's defence of their World Cup title. In 2021, Erasmus was handed a 10-and-a-half-month ban for his verbal attack on the Australian referee Nic Berry during the British & Irish Lions series. If a similar ban had been introduced this time he would have missed the Springboks' entire World Cup warm-up campaign.
South Africa Rugby has exercised its right to request a full misconduct hearing, issuing a statement to outline how it had "requested an opportunity for its coach to contextualize his comments and assure the panel that it was not his intention to allege that any match official had acted with ulterior motives".
Erasmus's ban applies to international matches, and includes any match-day involvement including coaching, direct or indirect contact with team management or players on match days; entering the stadium, coaches' box, playing enclosure, technical area, approaching and/or communicating with match officials on match day; engaging in any "feedback" process with match officials; and engaging in media duties during the suspension.
The 54-year-old's tirade came after last Friday's defeat by France. The South Africa head coach claimed officials had missed 17 knee-drop tackles committed against his side, suggesting that incidents of foul play are not being picked up in what he described as a "manipulation of the game" and that officials are showing more leniency to northern hemisphere teams. To illustrate his point he predicted Ireland's fly-half Sam Prendergast would be available to face South Africa despite being cited for a hip-drop tackle.
Referring to the 17 knee-drop tackles he claims went unpunished, Erasmus said: "The TMO [television match official] doesn't come in once. I'm not saying he's wrong. I'm saying if he didn't see it, it must be a technology problem. If he saw it and he decided not to act on it, is that not match-fixing? Is that not interfering with the outcome of a game if you see this injury that happens there?"
Erasmus added: "If the TMO didn't see it, maybe then technology must be better. If he saw it and he ignored it, what does that boil down to? Manipulation of the game or the result, or injured players getting injured." Erasmus said he was not making an "accusation" about the officials, and acknowledged there was "a drive in World Rugby that the TMO should stay out and not interrupt the game".
In July, World Rugby launched a match-officials-abuse disciplinary process in an effort to "reduce comments which often are the basis of the unacceptable increase in abuse and threats towards match officials". The disciplinary panel determined that Erasmus's comments constituted "disrespecting the authority of the match officials by impugning their integrity. Therefore, the panel reviewed a number of elements of the commentary including the timing and reach of the statements, combined with the allegations of bias or bad faith, and found that the comments attracted a six-match automatic suspension."
As well as his lengthy ban after the 2021 Lions tour, Erasmus was banned for two matches in November 2022 for a series of social media comments about the English referee Wayne Barnes, who revealed he was on the receiving end of death threats.
"Respect is fundamental to rugby, and match officials are central to the sport," read a World Rugby statement. "Protecting their wellbeing and integrity, and their right to perform without abuse or intimidation, is a responsibility World Rugby and unions take seriously. Coaches, players, unions and everyone in the game must uphold these standards.
"Robust, respectful debate is natural, but teams have access to well-established and supported channels and protocols for raising officiating matters before and after matches. Challenging a decision is not the same as questioning an official's integrity or making comments that expose them to personal abuse."
