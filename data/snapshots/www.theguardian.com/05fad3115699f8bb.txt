Michael Carrick talks to TNT Sports. "We didn't really get going in the second half ... we should have been up in the first half ... it was as positive as we could have hoped for ... we looked really dangerous ... we found a way of unravelling in the second half ... a bad goal to concede ... then the penalty and we were right up against it ... I don't think the heads dropped ... there's no hiding from the fact we haven't won enough games ... the boys were in a good place in the first half ... they made the first blow in the second half and that made the difference ... I've not looked at [the Martinez penalty incident] ... I've not seen it."
Do you feel let down by Martinez? "No."
Do you think the fans (who sang Carrick's name) will feel let down by the players? "There's a lot of emotion ... we want to win football matches ... the fans pay a lot of money to watch us play ... a lot of emotion running around right now ... we've got to move forward ... take a bit of belief and confidence ... a lot of the good things from the first half."
Do you expect to be in charge for Roma in the Champions League on Tuesday? "Yeah."

Xabi Alonso speaks to TNT Sports. "We are happy ... the first half was close ... a few chances for us and them ... we spoke at half time ... more control with the ball ... the start of the second half was much better ... a mature performance ... a great win for sure ... more stability ... more control ... [a clean sheet] was a big part of our improvement ... we can do better ... we have taken a step forward, but I don't know how far we are ... my expectations are really high ... I am sure the last three months of this season we will be much better."
Manchester United were decent in the first half. The better team, in fact. But Lisandro Martínez butchered their chances in the second half, run ragged by Morgan Rogers, then drawn into an idiotic challenge by Wesley Fofana, picking up two yellow cards in under two minutes, and that was that. Martínez didn't cover himself in glory for Rogers' opening goal, either. You have to wonder what Michael Carrick is thinking, given the opinionated defender's role in drawing further attention to United's plight. Speaking of which, they'll be in the relegation places tonight should there be a winner between Coventry City and Sunderland later, and Crystal Palace (at Tottenham Hotspur) or Nottingham Forest (at Brentford) draw.
In other news, next Friday will be the 40th anniversary of Alex Ferguson's appointment as United manager. That it's come to this.
Morgan Rogers, who opened the scoring with a Paul Gascoigne style sashay down the middle of the park, and has been named player of the match, talks to TNT Sports. "It was massive ... a difficult week ... a poor result last weekend ... OK in the cup but not the win we wanted ... so we knew we had to come out strong ... first half was tough ... they played well ... second half we really stamped our authority ... it was a massive conscious effort to go again ... we deserved the win ... I'm loving [playing for Chelsea] ... it's about the team coming together as a whole ... I'm going to try to be a leader ... I need to keep going ... friendly competition [with his pal Cole Palmer] ... if we're both doing well, the team are doing well, and that's the most important thing!"
Does that represent Michael Carrick's last act as manager of Manchester United? Everyone was saying that last weekend, following the Bournemouth debacle, so let others speculate, we'll just have to wait this out. But he goes over to the travelling support and offers some more apologetic applause, just as he did at Old Trafford last Sunday, before disappearing sadly down the tunnel, hands in pockets, a mournful trudge. Bruno Fernandes follows him over to the United fans, some of whom deliver a full and frank appraisal of his recent efforts. He looks on the verge of tears. Then he takes his leave as well. A grand old club in deep crisis.
