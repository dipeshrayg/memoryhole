A mere seven years since their debut, Dogrel, Fontaines DC have enjoyed a virtually seamless passage from Dublin dive bars to arenas, with stadiums to follow next year. Each step has been taken seemingly effortlessly in their stride and this latest outing is no exception. Bathed in shimmering blue light, the band are well into hymnal opener Six Shot Morning before we even see sight of singer Grian Chatten. He wanders on while singing, an entrance worthy of Frank Sinatra.
Chatten has previously seemed to distrust showbiz, but if anything the singer seems far more comfortable on the bigger stage than he ever did on smaller ones. With his Korn T-shirt, unfeasibly baggy trousers and tiny tattoo reading "deadly" in capitals, he leaps around, raises his arms to whip up the crowd and points the microphone towards them to sing key lines. Similarly, music this intense, dark, elegiac and even bleak - which speaks to the void at the heart of much modern life - is hardly typical arena fare but works brilliantly by taking on a more anthemic, celebratory aspect. It's certainly weirdly thrilling hearing Chatten's lines such as "We're all depressed, running on a wheel" or "I don't feel anything in the modern world" - or a song as plainly dark as Jackie Down the Line - become enormous, arena-uniting sing-songs.
The band's racing progress is demonstrated by the setlist: just one song remains from each of their first two albums (Boys from the Better Land and A Hero's Death respectively) and there are a whopping nine from Dopamine Chamber, their rave-reviewed latest. Driven by the band's powerhouse rhythm section and walls of guitars and electronics, the new songs are already crowd favourites. Marianne is haunting and Mimesis is epic, gothic, European-sounding rock. A sea of phones illuminates Japan, a spectral near-ballad, and the crowd's massed voices fill in for Rosalía (who duets with Chatten on the record) for Where Is Gone? It's another unabashed triumph from a band who are yet to put a foot wrong.
