In April Freddie Potts was absorbing the disappointment of being substituted at half-time during West Ham's FA Cup quarter-final defeat by Leeds. The midfielder was out of favour under Nuno Espírito Santo and could not have imagined that he would be preparing to face Liverpool in the Champions League seven months later.
It is the 23-year-old's reward for taking the difficult decision to leave his boyhood club last summer. Potts noticed his minutes were drying up in pre-season, even though West Ham were not exactly stocked with talent after relegation, and he was ready for a new challenge when Club Brugge offered about £10m to sign him.
"I thought this was the perfect place for me to take the next step," Potts says of his move to the Belgian champions. "I'm not going to shy away - it was very hard. West Ham have been my club for my whole life. I still have a lot of love for West Ham. But I had to take into account what was best for my career and I'm loving it at Brugge.
"Every player wants to be in the team expected to win every week and seeing how Club Brugge have performed in the Champions League before shows the project and how they want to develop the team and players. It was a very exciting place for me to come. They look to develop young players."
Potts was six when he joined West Ham. His father, Steve, made 506 appearances for the club and is part of Scott Parker's backroom staff now. Freddie's brother, Dan, came through the academy before moving to Luton in 2015. This is a West Ham family; it is no surprise that moving to Belgium was emotional for Potts.
Yet it was the right move. Brugge are second in the Belgian Pro League, a point behind Union Saint‐Gilloise, and have impressed in the Champions League. They have nine points from four games in the league phase and caused a stir when they beat Inter 4-3 at San Siro last month.
Potts calls it "one of the best nights of my career". It was quite the turnaround for a player who mostly watched from the bench as West Ham tried to save themselves during the second half of last season. It helps that Potts knows how to stay calm. He was talked up as a future West Ham captain when he made his full debut and impressed in a 3-1 win over Newcastle last year. He had a run in the team but lost his place when Nuno switched to a 4-4-2 in January and settled on Mateus Fernandes and Tomas Soucek as his preferred partnership in central midfield.
That was when Potts remembered how he grew during loan spells at Portsmouth and Wycombe. "I know that you can have games where you feel like you are on top of the world and then you can come straight back down," he says. "The loans helped me out massively with West Ham because I managed to cope with the highs. The Newcastle game was a real high, but there were some lows after that and I feel like I was coping with it."
Potts saw the reaction on social media when he left. Some West Ham fans were not happy with the club for allowing a homegrown player to depart. This season has been tough for West Ham, who are 10th in the Championship after a disappointing run and replaced Nuno with Parker this month. One problem has been the lack of a No 6. Potts, who can snap into a tackle and link play from deep, has been watching from afar.
"I still think they're playing well," he says. "They've got a great team, great players. I think Scott Parker is a really good manager and he's got three teams promoted from the Championship. I don't think it will be a problem again. West Ham will get promoted in the end."
Potts has glitzier assignments and is adjusting to his surroundings. He cannot speak much Flemish, but he has sipped a couple of Belgian beers and is enjoying life in Bruges. "I'm not going to lie, when I first joined I found it difficult. It's completely different when you move country. But I was open to the challenge. At the start it was a challenge, but now it's really smooth."
One of the biggest challenges has been adapting to a new style. Given the intensity of the Premier League it is a surprise to hear Potts say Brugge, managed by the 48-year‐old Croat Ivan Leko, have helped him become more physical.
"Straight away in training programmes it was a real surprise how hard everyone works," Potts says. "It's in the nature of the club to really work hard and give 100% every day. You see that straight away as you come in the door. No matter how hard it can be everyone gets on with it. No complaints. We're a running team and everyone gives it their all. That's the message from the top. It's a real positive environment for someone of my age to be part of."
Brugge have a couple of rising stars in attack: the rapid Portugal winger Carlos Forbs and the 22-year-old striker Nicolò Tresoldi, who scored a hat-trick in the win against Inter. There is a feeling Leko's side could go on a run in Europe. They do not fear Liverpool. "It'll be difficult for them coming to our place," Potts says. "They're a great team, but we're going to approach it like we have been doing with every game.
"Inter are one of the top teams in the competition. PSV are a great team and we got the win there. Liverpool will be tough, but we've proved it against top teams before."
