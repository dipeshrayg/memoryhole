No ground in Australia is more associated with ritual guttings of English cricket teams than the so-called Gabbatoir. They usually leave in a state of disarray, beaten up on the field and with ears still ringing from abuse on the boundary's edge.
Yet on a muggy Friday night on Vulture Street, the locals had precious little material to work with and the traditional script was flipped on its head. Driven by Jamie Overton's fiery five-wicket haul, England wiped the floor with their hosts for the second match in a row to claim a 2-1 lead with two to play.
The pockets of England supporters inside the Gabba would sooner this scoreline had come during that ill-fated Ashes tour 12 months earlier, clearly, not a low-key T20 affair against a barely semi-skimmed Australian XI. For all the sandpaper-related schadenfreude, this tour is not proving a great return on their investment.
Still, they got to watch an English fast bowler release the handbrake as Overton's five for 12 rolled the hosts for a sorry 108 all out in 16.5 overs. A few brief rain showers were pretty much the only thing stopping England's march from there and an adjusted 93-run target from 16 overs was wiped out with 43 balls to spare.
The initial source of the runs was again Aneurin Donald, with the self-professed slogger following Tuesday's fireworks display on the Gold Coast by cracking 32 from 13 balls.
But this match was settled by Overton's remarkable first innings, claiming two catches inside the powerplay and then unleashing with ball in hand for the best T20 figures by an Englishman against Australia. Only Sam Curran - five for 10 against Afghanistan in Perth four years ago - has better overall.
"It has actually been a frustrating tour," said Overton afterwards, revealing some back-to-basics drills with the bowling coach, Tim Southee, were behind his personal turnaround. "It's funny how quickly things can change. And T20 can be a funny game. Obviously it's nice to get five but I feel like I've bowled better at times previously."
The daft thing was, Australia had actually started reasonably well after being inserted. Despite being pegged back twice by the wily Liam Dawson en route to personal figures of three for 16, the hosts had put 64 runs on the board after six overs and so were tracking for a 200-run total that looked like par.
Enter Overton. With Adil Rashid spinning a thrifty web at the other end, Surrey's giant Devonian struck in each of his first three overs and then finished off with a double-wicket maiden. As is the way with T20, these were not classical dismissals but still owed plenty to classical pace on a typically rapid Gabba surface.
Jake Fraser-McGurk sent one down the throat of long on, while Tim David was cracked flush on the elbow before top-edging his next ball to deep fine leg. Joel Davies wafted tamely to midwicket and while Nikhil Chaudhary scrapped his way to 19 - wearing one nasty blow to the grille - he eventually went the same way as Fraser-McGurk.
Chaudhary middling one straight back into Overton's left thumb the ball before could easily have ended his night early. But much like an earlier near-miss with the one of flamethrowers on the boundary's edge, the 32-year-old shrugged it off and made Ben Dwarshuis his fifth via a regulation outside edge behind.
With the remainder soon mopped up - including one farcical run out that snuffed out the last recognised batter Mitch Owen - Australia had conspired to lose eight wickets for 43 runs and were staring at their lowest T20 total on home soil.
Even with the slippery Riley Meredith then unleashing with three wickets in the reply, and big Billy Stanlake flooring Harry Brook with a brutish delivery that the England captain did well to evade, the match was long since over as a contest.
After Donald's eventual demise the most eye-catching strokeplay in between the stoppages for rain came from Jordan Cox and an unbeaten 33 from just 14 balls.
An anticlimax in some regards overall, not that Overton - or any previous English visitors to the Gabbatoir - had too much cause for complaint.
