At the end of a bruising week for Australian cricket came a few hours of respite and a night that ended with cheers echoing around the MCG. Set 188 to win, a home side light on star names beat England by three wickets and with one ball to spare.
The off-field developments kept coming before the start of play. Cricket Australia publicly threw its support behind Pat Cummins as Test captain, while its broadcast partner Fox Cricket decided it was in everyone's interest to stand down Usman Khawaja and David Warner from commentary duties.
ABC had no such qualms about Darren Lehmann continuing behind the microphone, however. And before the start of the match - the first of five to round off England's white-ball tour - the former Australia head coach once again denied any prior knowledge of the plan to use sandpaper in South Africa eight years ago.
But when Jack Edwards drilled the penultimate ball of the match from Sam Curran for four to seal the deal, events in 2018 - and who has been economical with the truth since - were far from the minds of the 39,000-strong crowd. Twenty20 is intended to be lighthearted fun. Sticking it to the old enemy is even better.
The star of the evening was the little-known Nikhil Chaudhary, an India-born all-rounder who was visiting Australia on a travel visa in early 2020 when the Covid-19 pandemic struck. Chaudhary stayed on, worked his way up through club cricket and became a match-winner in just his fourth outing for his adopted country.
Chaudhary's two wickets - with just 21 in the runs column - were key to derailing England's effort with the bat which, despite being a record total on this ground, was still light at 187 for nine. The wrist-spinner shutting down Harry Brook's 28-ball 65 via a fine edge was arguably the moment this game turned.
The 30-year-old was not done there, however, going one better than England's captain with a 38-ball 66 from No 4 that broke the back of the chase. Edwards, 25 not out from 11 deliveries, was the finisher after two wickets earlier on himself.
England overcame a 1-0 deficit during the recent one-dayers and will not be beating themselves up too much. Of greater concern will be the injury to the vice-captain, Will Jacks, who was struck on his bowling hand by a rasping drive from Ben Dwarshuis and walked off with blood pouring out of the wound.
Jacks is due to captain England in the fifth match of this series when Brook flies off to join the Test squad in South Africa. Speaking after the defeat, and bemoaning yet another slow start, Brook conceded it did not look too promising.
Brook also accepted that England ultimately let this match slip with the bat, with their innings one that started chaotically, promised plenty at the halfway stage, but ended up fizzling out as Australia's spinners applied the squeeze.
After Phil Salt fell to the first ball of the match and his fellow Welshman Aneurin Donald departed soon after, Brook put on a display of remarkable ball-striking alongside Tom Banton (39 from 22). Their third-wicket stand was worth 97 runs from 41 balls and drew plenty of gasps from the locals.
That said, Australia should have shut him down far sooner, granting lives on two and three and putting down six catches in the innings overall. Brook is not a player to hand a life, let alone two. And his 20-ball half-century, having been seven off nine, included surely one of the flattest, longest square sixes on this ground.
Adam Zampa was among those manhandled initially but, after an electric catch from the debutant Lachlan Shaw on the rope removed Banton, the leg-spinner then returned to pin Jacks lbw for one. Sitting 123 for five at the start of the 12th over, England were suddenly mindful of burning more resources and finished short.
Liam Dawson struck in the first over of the reply by bowling Jake Fraser-McGurk as the opener danced down the pitch. At the other end Henry Crocombe saw his first ball on debut swivel-pulled for six by Mitch Marsh. The Australia captain's 32-ball 37 was slightly laboured and was ended by an athletic take by Dawson.
Impetus instead came from another newcomer in Sam Harper and a frisky 15-ball 32 from No 3 that saw Jamie Overton taken for 21 off his first over. Chaudhary then made good use of the crease to take the game away, striking six fours, sending two over the rope, and enjoying some running verbal battles along the way.
