England's bid for a fourth successive Commonwealth Games men's artistic gymnastics team title ended with silver after a dramatic final overshadowed by an injury to the teenager Gabriel Langton, who was taken from the arena on a stretcher after a heavy fall from the high bar.
The 19-year-old fell head and neck first on to the mat during the final rotation of the team final, bringing competition to a halt as medical staff rushed to his aid. The arena fell silent while Langton received treatment, with fellow gymnasts watching on in visible concern.
After initially lying motionless, Langton responded to medical staff, moving his legs and later his hands. A dozen medical personnel carefully stabilised the England gymnast before lifting him on to a stretcher about 11 minutes after the fall. He was applauded by the crowd as he was taken from the competition floor.
The incident came with England locked in a battle with Canada for the team title. At the time of the stoppage, Canada led the standings, narrowly ahead of England, while Australia occupied the bronze-medal position. Scotland remained within touching distance in fourth, with Cyprus fifth and Wales sixth.
After Langton's fall and the lengthy delay, Luke Whitehouse faced the daunting task of competing on the same apparatus immediately after his teammate's accident. Despite a composed routine under difficult circumstances, he lost his balance on the dismount and finished on the mat, visibly disappointed as he walked away.
"When something like that happens, you've just got to forget about it and reset," Whitehouse said on performing after his teammate's fall. "I just had to focus on my routine one skill at a time.
"Gymnastics is such a dangerous sport and things like this remind us of that. It's one of those things you have to put out of your mind. From what we've heard [Langton] is doing OK."
England finished with 238.250, leaving Canada needing 36.450 points from their final three floor routines to secure gold. The Canadians held their nerve with the veteran René Cournoyer opening with 13.500 before Félix Dolci, one of the standout performers of the day, followed with 13.700 to effectively seal the title. Canada finished on 241.400 to claim their first Commonwealth men's team gold since 2006, upgrading the silver medals they won in 2018 and 2022.
England took silver on 238.250, while Australia secured bronze with 235.650 after holding off a stirring late challenge from Scotland, who finished fourth on 234.300. Cyprus placed fifth on 228.350, with Wales sixth on 227.000.
Whitehouse, expected to spearhead England's title defence, made an excellent start by posting an impressive 14.400 on floor exercise. However, halfway through the contest England found themselves fourth behind Canada, Australia and Cyprus after three rotations, with the Canadians leading on 122.200 points.
England steadily clawed their way back into contention as Australia encountered problems on the high bar. Alexander Yolshin-Cash played a key role in the resurgence, producing the best parallel bars score of the evening with a 14.100.
Canada's grip on gold briefly looked vulnerable when the experienced Cournoyer came off the high bar and scored 11.550, allowing England to close the gap. However, Dolci immediately steadied Canadian nerves with the apparatus' highest score of the night, 14.250, to preserve their slender advantage heading into the final rotation.
England responded admirably after Whitehouse's slight stumble, with Yolshin-Cash producing another confident high bar routine. His score of 13.250 ranked fifth on the apparatus, secured qualification for the individual final and added valuable points to England's overall total.
Scotland also mounted a late push for bronze. Cameron Lynn thrilled the home crowd with a near-flawless high bar routine worth 13.500 before Hamish Carter added 12.350 after sticking his landing. Reuben Ward then scored 13.150, leaving Connor Sullivan needing something special in the final routine. Despite another excellent performance, Scotland fell just 1.350 points short as Australia's consistency on pommel horse proved decisive.
