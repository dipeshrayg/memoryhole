Maro Itoje has said he will never adopt a win-at-all-costs mentality as England captain as he prepares to lead his side against the All Blacks at Twickenham on Saturday after a week in sport dominated by Rassie Erasmus's "match-fixing" rant and sandpapergate.
Itoje has called on his "very good" side to take the next step towards greatness by clinching back-to-back victories over the All Blacks for the first time since 2003 but, guided by his Christian faith and moral compass, he is adamant there are lines he will not cross.
This week Erasmus was banned for six matches for questioning whether officials are match-fixing and suggested they had "manipulated the game", comments widely perceived as a cynical attempt to exert pressure on officials in the Springboks' forthcoming matches. The resurfacing of the Australia cricket team's use of sandpaper in 2018, meanwhile, has brought back into focus how low another elite sporting side were willing to stoop in pursuit of victory.
"There's a line," the England captain said. "I don't believe in the metric of win at all costs because, depending on what that cost is, it can be extremely damaging. Integrity is really important. I'm Christian as well, so there are certain things which I wouldn't do, or [positions] I wouldn't put myself in. There's a passage in the Bible that says something along the lines of 'what does it gain for a man to gain the world but lose his soul?'. Part of that is why I don't believe in that kind of thing.
"Don't get me wrong, I want to win. I want to do everything within my power to win. But if that power is taking performance-enhancing drugs or betting on the game - I can list a number of other examples - if that is the cost of winning, if I did those things and then win, what does the win really mean? I don't believe in that."
Itoje also echoed the views of Steve Borthwick and a number of other head coaches around the world in insisting that officials must be protected in the wake of Erasmus's comments. Like Borthwick, Itoje chose not to directly address the Springboks' head coach but added: "We should always respect referees and officials. You said [Erasmus] has been banned three times - that obviously means World Rugby has taken action. It's not something that has gone without action.
"It's really important as a global game that we try and exhibit the behaviours and values that we want to see continued when we're no longer in these positions and other people are taking these things forward. World Rugby has taken action and I think it's just important for us as players to really hold those values strong.
"Often in this game referees get a lot of stick. It's a tough old job, to be honest. But when you're out and talk to people from different sports - particularly footballers - what people commend rugby for is how much respect we have for the referees. I think it's really important for us as a game and a sport to make sure we continue to respect the referees.
"It's a tough job, it's not easy, they open themselves up to a lot of online criticism. We've seen cases, whether it's football or in rugby, where referees have got a lot of flak online. I just think one of the strengths of rugby is that we respect the referee and each other. At Twickenham on Saturday there will be New Zealand fans sitting right next to England fans. I think that value of respect is something that as a community we need to hold high."
England come into the match on the back of four straight wins and are still in with a shout of reaching next week's Nations Championship final while next year's World Cup looms large. "These are the sort of challenges we need to face," said Itoje. "These are the sort of games we need to be in if we are going to be a great side. There are going to be times where we are playing highly motivated, very good teams. This week we are playing both."
