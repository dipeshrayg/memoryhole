Michael Carrick has been dismissed by Manchester United. He departs after a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa at Old Trafford last Sunday that left the team in the Premier League's bottom three. United twice fought back from two goals down for a late point against Villa, after a dramatic recovery to defeat Roma in the Champions League five days earlier, but that wasn't enough.
United are third-bottom in the Premier League, a point behind 17th-placed Coventry, and 17th in the Champions League league phase table. His departure follows a review led by the director of football, Jason Wilcox, and a recommendation to Sir Jim Ratcliffe, who controls sporting policy.

Pochettino's situation, though, does feel different with the longer contract - certainly to him. "Now it is a different thing," he says. "When we gave our word to the federation [in August 2024], it was about starting and finishing after the World Cup. For us, it was the moment to say: 'If some club comes, it's impossible.' Always you can break a contract, say you stop and that's it ... you agree something. But in the moment it wasn't right for us to create all these situations and expectations.
"Now it's a four-year contract, it's different. We can next season lose the Copa América or the Gold Cup, they're not happy with us and they can sack us. Or we can say: 'OK. It's not in the way that we think.' Four years is different. Maybe now if someone calls in the future, it's a different, different type [of thing]. We are excited, we are thinking to spend four years to the next World Cup. But now it's a long period."
