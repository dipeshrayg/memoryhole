Raye is leading this year's female-heavy Grammys race with 11 nominations.
The British singer-songwriter, who has received four nominations in the past, picked up nods in categories including song of the year, songwriter of the year and album of the year.
The other artists nominated for album of the year are Phoebe Bridgers, Olivia Dean, Ella Langley, Bruno Mars, Raye, Olivia Rodrigo, Taylor Swift and Rosalía, who came in second with nine nominations.
The Spanish star received nods in categories including best alternative music performance, best music video and record of the year for Berghain.
The other nominees for record of the year are Tame Impala and Jenny - Dracula, Olivia Dean - Man I Need, Ella Langley - Choosin' Texas, Bruno Mars - I Just Might, Raye - Where is my Husband?, Taylor Swift - The Fate of Ophelia and Olivia Rodrigo - The Cure.
Rodrigo, who has won three Grammys previously, scored seven nominations putting her in third position with Bridgers, Miranda Lambert and Langley, who broke out this year with record-breaking hit Choosin' Texas.
Langley leads the category of best new artist alongside Angine De Poitrine, Geese, Slayyyter, Sienna Spiro, Don Toliver, Remi Wolf and The Womack Sisters. Last year's award was won by Dean, who received six nominations this year.
Beyoncé received her 100th nomination, this year for best R&B solo performance. She remains the most nominated artist of all time at the Grammys. She is also the most awarded with 35 wins.
The most nominated male artists are Leon Thomas and Bruno Mars, with six nods apiece. Mars has previously won 35 awards.
Madonna, who has won seven Grammys in her career, picked up four nominations including best dance/electronic album, and best pop duo/group performance with Sabrina Carpenter. Other artists who received dance/electronic nominations include Slayyyter, Zara Larsson and Tinashe.
Swift received five nominations for both her The Life of a Showgirl album and I Knew It, I Knew You, her Toy Story 5 song.
Despite ongoing controversy over his antisemitic comments, Kanye West (Ye) picked up a nomination for best rap album for Bully - Deluxe. He was nominated alongside Busta Rhymes and J Dilla, Cardi B, Rapsody, Don Toliver and Wale.
Dolly Parton, who died in August, also received three posthumous nominations, including best country duo/group performance. Tim Curry, who also died in August, was also nominated in the audiobook category.
Morgan Freeman, who has previously been nominated for a spoken word album, picked up a nomination for his blues album.
The newly introduced category of best Asian pop music performance has already led to controversy with members of BTS refusing to submit their music as a result. "We hope our music can be heard and loved for what it is, rather than being divided by region or language," a joint statement read.
The nominees included Bini, Ive and Lisa.
Other first-time categories include best Latin song, which was led by Karol G and Rosalía; best traditional pop vocal performance, which featured Olivia Dean and Raye; and best R&B collaboration or duo/group performance, which saw nominees including Chris Brown featuring Leon Thomas and Jill Scott featuring JID.
Notable snubs included Harry Styles, Justin Bieber, Gracie Abrams, Sam Smith, Luke Combs, Katseye and Ed Sheeran, who all received no nominations. Despite acclaim for her album West End Girl, Lily Allen also received just one nod for the artwork.
There are now 100 categories at the Grammy awards.
Harvey Mason Jr, CEO of the Recording Academy, referenced concerns over artificial intelligence in his introduction, stating that at this time it is "so important that we honour the human creators behind the music".
Last year's awards were dominated by wins from Bad Bunny and Kendrick Lamar.
The ceremony is scheduled to take place on 7 February at the Crypto.com arena in Los Angeles. A host has yet to be announced.
