Many Microsoft 365 services are affected, for example:
Problems with Exchange Online mean users may be unable to access their mailbox using Outlook on the web
Microsoft365Education features including Assignments, Reading Progress, and Speaker Progress may be impacted.
MicrosoftCopilot365 - users may be unable to load Copilot Chat or receive responses in the web and desktop apps
The Microsoft ToDo app may not load, and users may see operation failures.
On Teams, Microsoft says:
Most of the core functionality including joining calls, meetings, group chats, and channel messages are not impacted. Scenarios that depend on Exchange Online, including Calendar related failures with scheduling, invites, viewing, and meeting edits. Creation of Live or Virtual events fail. Failures with Copilot responses for outlook, calendar, or M365 files, read history, or adding to new chats.
Navigating to App chats, adding new contacts or syncing contacts in settings fail. Standard channels are working fine but users may experience the inability to create or delete private or shared channels.
And it warns it could take up to 48 hours to restore services fully:
City of Tampa offices are open and operating on normal schedules. Walk-in services, phone lines, and emergency operations (911) remain fully operational.
However, residents and external partners may experience delays or failures when sending emails to City of Tampa accounts or receiving emails from them. Technology teams are actively monitoring the Microsoft issue and based on vendor guidance, full service restoration is anticipated within 48 hours.
The Microsoft outage may be an - unwelcome - opportunity to test how companies and public services would cope under a serious cyber attack.
Forrester's principal analyst Dario Maisto epxlains:
"The Microsoft outage, for the little we know so far, represents the perfect quasi-natural experiment for stress testing an organization's - and even an entire continent - digital sovereignty posture.
"Although this outage does not strictly pose a sovereignty question, as this seems to be more an accident than the consequence of a government's will, it does allow us to test what would happen if interruption of mailing services and AI tools like Copilot would be the direct consequence of a political act from the government.
Microsoft has now declared that service availability continues to improve - but dome users may still have problems accessing their email.
In an updated Current Status on the outage, it says:
We're continuing to see improvements in service availability, with email queues significantly reduced and approaching normal levels. Some users may still experience intermittent access issues or delayed email delivery as recovery continues.
Simon Azzopardi, CEO of security firmCoreView,says IT teams need to do three things following the Microsoft outage:
"Confirm that security and compliance controls such as data loss prevention, sensitivity labelling and Intune compliance policies are working as intended.
Review every emergency change made during the outage, because workarounds made under pressure tend to become permanent gaps.
And treat delayed email with extra caution: Microsoft warned that Safe Links might not be able to check URLs during the disruption, and attackers routinely use outages as cover."
Azzopardi adds:
"Our sympathy goes out to every IT team that has spent their time keeping their organisation running without email, calendars and, in many cases, their security and compliance tools."

More people who've been affected by the outage have been getting in touch with The Guardian, including students, teachers and others who work in education.
Jenny is a teacher in SEN [special educational needs] school in Norfolk, England and teaches a four day week. Ordinarily Friday is her rest day and in the morning, she says she logs in and gives access to her colleagues to teach lessons she has pre-prepared and also responds to emails.
"I realised there was a problem at 5:30am when I tried to give access to a powerpoint presentation my TA [teaching assistant] needed for a maths lesson. I had to download this, recreate it as a pdf and then upload it to the cover planning folder. It was not a quick fix."
She also had to keep in contact with my TA via WhatsApp rather than email.
It's made her rest day a lot more stressful, Jenny explains:
"I was wrestling with this issue until 9am when I gave up as school had then started."
Jenny believes we should invest in European technology firms. "I find it very worrying that we are so reliant on US technology as we are seeing this has affected so many areas of our society today."
Meanwhile Tracy, a nursing student at the UniversityofSouthWales, says the outage has disrupted her studies, "particularly as university emails are not working".
This has made it difficult for her to access important communications from lecturers, receive updates about assignments, and stay informed about placement arrangements. "As nursing students, we rely heavily on timely communication," she says, "especially when balancing academic work with clinical placements. The outage has caused uncertainty and made it harder to keep up to date".
On the bright side, this might help nursing students to prepare for similar situations in the future, she says.
"We can see how to handle not being able to access the information. We can write everything down, we can pass on notes verbally or on paper - it's back to the old-school days."

UK house moves are being disrupted by the Microsoft outage, it appears.
DavidStrank, who runs Kent-based Stranks Removals & Storage, said that the moves of two customers had been disrupted today while the exchange of another client was also delayed. The hold up could derail their planned move date.
"We've got two crews that are waiting for keys at the moment," Strank told the Guardian.
"They are loaded and sitting outside but can't do anything. We've got a move that we're supposed to start on 23 November and he's rung to say that they can't exchange today because they can't send any emails."

The UK government say it is seeking to establish "the full picture" of Microsoft outages.
The prime minister's deputy spokeswoman said:
"We're working with departments and with Microsoft to establish the full picture, and where services are disrupted, departments have contingency arrangements in place.
"You'll obviously appreciate this is an ongoing situation, but in the meantime, people are encouraged to follow advice on the services they require for alternative ways to get in touch."
Some UK doctors have been getting in touch, reporting that their emails are now coming back online after being disrupted by the Microsoft technical problems today.
That suggests there's progress bringing Outlook back online.
Ofqual, the exam regulator for England, is the latest educational institution to be hit by the Microsoft outage. This morning it advised users that its complaints service was not functioning:
"We are currently experiencing issues with this service, related to the global Microsoft outage.
"We are currently unable to receive complaints, whistleblowing and malpractice submissions."
It said any urgent enquiries should come by telephone.
The continued disruption at Microsoft shows the importance of building some resilience into your IT setup, points out StevenWatt, Professor of Practice in Cybersecurity at the University of St Andrews:
Organisations should be able to answer some basic questions. Which of our critical processes depend on a single supplier? How long could we operate if that supplier were unavailable? What is our fallback for communicating with staff, customers and regulators? Have we tested it, or do we simply assume it exists?
A continuity plan that relies on the same platform that has just failed is not a plan. Remember manual workarounds - these don't work any more!
