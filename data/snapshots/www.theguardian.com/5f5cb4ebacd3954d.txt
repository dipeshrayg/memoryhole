Newsflash: Nvidia's shares have dropped sharply at the start of trading in New York, as Wall Street gives its verdict on the company's spying scandal.
Shares in the chip maker fell by almost 7.5% as soon as the opening bell rang out, as investors respond to the news that CEO JensenHuang has admitted having an extramarital affair - reportedly with a Chinese spy.
That knocked the shares down to $172.75 each, down from $186.64 on Friday night.
That, by my maths, wipes $335bn off Nvidia's valuation, following the board's decision to launch an independent investigation into whether Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort.
It lowers the company's value to around $4.16tn, down from $4.5tn on Friday night.
Briefing.com analyst Patrick O'Hare said that Nvidia was being treated as a one-off story by investors for the moment.
"In other words, the stock is being penalised for Jensen Huang's personal transgression, which has invited a great deal of uncertainty into the mix, but the market isn't caught up in the notion that this situation is going to have a systemic domino effect," he said.

As if Nvidia didn't have enough to worry about, it also faces a new challenges from Alphabet.
Google's parent company is rolling out a broader array of artificial intelligence chips, Bloomberg reports, adding:
The company is introducing two new variations of its tensor processing units, or TPUs, custom-made semiconductors that help develop and run AI software. The latest chips will address the need for confidential computing and help models access a wider range of information when handling queries.
FT: Alleged Chinese spy linked to Nvidia chief pitched AI chips deals to investors
The plot thickens!
The FinancialTimes are reporting that AliceHuang pitched an AI chips deal to China-based investors, touting her ability to secure Nvidia's coveted GPUs and her commercial relationship with Chinese tech giant Alibaba.
They say:
In early 2025, Alice Huang and her business partner Kang Wei sought investment for a south-east Asia data centre project worth several hundred million dollars, according to two investors pitched on the deal.
The pair said they had "no problem securing GPUs" as a key pillar of the deal and cited Alibaba as a client of the project, according to one investor present.
As covered earlier (here), a US hedge fund claimed six months ago that Alibaba had been secretly funding Megaspeed - Alice Huang's Singapore-based cloud computing company....
Tom Stevenson, investment director at Fidelity International, says:
"Stock markets ended last week digesting one AI-focused crisis and they have started the new one assessing another.
"AI has driven markets higher and it has started to drive them lower again. For good or ill, it is certain to be the dominant story for the foreseeable future.
"Renewed pressure on Nvidia shares has added to the uncertainty surrounding the sector, just as markets were starting to show tentative signs of stabilising after the most significant wobble to date in the ongoing AI boom. With investors already questioning the scale of spending on AI infrastructure and the returns it will generate, sentiment towards technology stocks remains fragile.
