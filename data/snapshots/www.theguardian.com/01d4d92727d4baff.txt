"I would imagine that many of the Essex partnership records against each county are held by Gooch and McEwen, who seemed to score huge numbers of runs in the 1980s.
"I do recall one match I attended, a JPL 40-over match, in which Gooch scored 175 and Essex 310 in 40 overs, both of which I think were records at the time. Gooch may well have gone on to make 200 had Pringle not run him out. This was against Glamorgan at Southchurch Park, Southend, the very same ground where the 1948 Australians hit 721 in a day. Sadly, the Southend Council recently saw fit to demolish the old pavilion there."

"Abell, in partnership with Lammonby and Pretorius, has set all-time Somerset records for the 4th and 9th wickets in the same innings - astonishing when you consider Somerset have been playing since 1882.
"Has anybody, or for that matter any team, set two new partnership records in the same innings or even the same match before ?
Have a vague memory of Glamorgan, with Northeast and Cooke involved, coming close a few years back, but can't pin it down, and you'd guess Durham might also have done it in their early days with records much more attainable. But there's probably somebody out there - Gary Naylor perhaps ? - who knows the answer...."
The 18 county scorers run a competition each year and an actual wooden spoon gets presented to the bottom club by the previous owners. We think the handover happened at Canterbury this week, where Kent passed over safekeeping to Gloucestershire.
Lancashire will finish 17th for the first time this season, though they have finished 16th in a 17-team competition five times: 1962, 1976, 1977, 1981 and 1984. The annual awards dinner has been cancelled for this year.

Somerset, 731 all out, have a first-innings lead of 301. Do Surrey have one last collapse in them?
A standing ovation for Migael Pretorius, as he is lbw having an ugly slug across the line to Dan Worrall. Somerset decide that 727 for nine is not enough and Jack Leach comes out.
Abell becomes the eighth Somerset cricketer to make 300 in an innings -after Justin Langer (twice), Tom Banton( last year), Viv Richards, Jimmy Cook, Graeme Smith, Harold Gimblett and James Hildreth.
At a warm Taunton, where Surrey are still fielding in sunhats, some succour for Somerset fans. A first triple century for Tom Abell, 30 fours, eight sixes, 402 balls, and nearly nine hours of batting. Bravo!
Good morning for one final time this season and welcome to the morning after the day before. We have our Champions, all hail the mighty Bears, our promoted clubs, Kent and Durham, and our sad duo bound for Division Two, Hampshire and Leicestershire.
The Manchester sky is slate, the air sea-spray damp but, like my stoical geraniums, there's just about enough life for one more day.
Three games plod on, a slow draw at Old Trafford, a high-scoring not-this-year goodbye at Taunton and a more intriguing match at Sophia Gardens where great escapees Essex have made a better fist of their second innings.
We'll be keeping an eye on all those, but also looking back over the summer, to the hat-tricks, the hundreds, the hiccups and the howlers.
