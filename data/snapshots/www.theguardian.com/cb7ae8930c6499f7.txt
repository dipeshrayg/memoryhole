Sandra Yarwood has a file "about 2in thick" of paperwork relating to the 32 driving fines she received this summer after her car's number plate was cloned.
Yarwood's black Volkswagen Golf did not move from outside her London home while she was on holiday in Canada, but a similar vehicle with a fraudulent copy of her registration racked up penalty notices totalling £2,560 across five London boroughs.
Criminals are using cloned plates to evade detection and avoid paying penalties for dangerous or illegal driving.
A record 11,400 drivers contacted the DVLA last year after being issued with fines for offences they said they did not recognise or accept responsibility for: most of which are believed to be a result of cloning. The number of complaints was 9% higher than in 2024 and almost double the number recorded in 2018.
To help victims, a new reporting tool has been launched in England and Wales. The tool is designed to make it easier to alert the authorities by allowing victims to report a cloned plate directly to the police online who will also inform the DVLA. There is a new section on the government's website that explains how to appeal against incorrect fines or charges.
The government has also issued consumer guidance on how to avoid falling foul of driving with an illegal number plate after it emerged that a third of motorists stopped in number plate operations this year had no idea they had one.
Early findings from the police suggest that, as well as deliberate criminal use, the public are unwittingly being sold incorrect plates. Stolen secondhand cars can be fitted with cloned plates and the government advises that you check the car's registration number is consistent with information held by the DVLA.
The roads minister, Justin Madders, said: "Illegal plates are being used by criminals, but it is also the case that innocent motorists can have illegal plates on their vehicles without their knowledge.
"We are going to make it easier for victims of cloned plates to report them as well as [launching] webpages to help drivers when they buy number plates and used cars from reputable sellers. These are small everyday fixes from the government which could help you avoid unwelcome attention from police."
It took Yarwood months to sort out the mess left by the criminals who racked up the fines in six days.
"I never paid any of them ... but the difficulty contesting them is gobsmacking," she said. "You have to prove your innocence and the fact that I was not in the country was not enough."
She said after she had reported the cloned plates her husband was stopped while driving the car. "But not in an unpleasant way," she added. "It was fine."
If it happens again, Yarwood has been advised to change her registration which she says is a "whole thing I didn't want to have to do. So I'm waiting. I hope they don't."
