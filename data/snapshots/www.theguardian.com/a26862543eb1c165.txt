ECB's Lane: Framework guidance works best when rate are above lower bound
Finally at the Bank of England and its Watchers conference, the eurozone's chief economist is riffing about central bank guidance.
PhilipLane is explaining that "framework guidance" - the strategic approach that central bankers take - is more effective than rate forward guidance when policy rates are away from their lowest point (the 'effective lower bound').
He also points out that communicating with households and firms presents different challenges to communicating with markets.
This, Lane explains, is made harder because their attention to inflation rises and falls with inflation itself!
He says:
Unlike professional market participants, most households do not closely follow central bank communication.
And even when they do encounter central bank messages, they may interpret their implications differently to market analysts.

Lombardelli: AI will make junior economist jobs much more interesting
Q: Does the Bank of England agree with US economist Jason Furman who gave a provocative estimate last week that AI could displace at least half of analytical economics jobs?
ClareLombardelli agrees that Furman's piece was "very provocative."
She then explains that it's "quite astonishing" what AI can do.
She says it will fundamentally change what economics jobs are, and make them rather more interesting.
It will mean that junior economists doing a lot less chart formatting, and more time thinking about the economy.
Does it mean fewer people? Or does it mean people doing much better work?
Lombardelli says there will be "a balance between the two".
