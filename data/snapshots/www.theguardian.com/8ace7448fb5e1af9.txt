Raheem Sterling has avoided being sent to jail after he received a suspended sentence of 12 months for pleading guilty to a prolonged period of reckless driving while impaired by the drug nitrous oxide.
The former England international, who five years ago was leading the Three Lions' charge to a European Championship final, stood expressionless in the dock as the sentence was read out by district judge Kirsty Allman at Basingstoke magistrates court.
Sterling was sentenced to 18 months in prison, reduced to 12 months, for dangerous driving on the morning of 28 May this year. He was awarded a sentence of two months for a failure to provide a specimen of blood for analysis, which is to run concurrently. He received no punishment for possession of a class C drug. Canisters in Sterling's possession were seized and destroyed.
In sentencing, Allman found the first two offences to be of the highest levels of culpability but argued that Sterling's charitable work, and his award of an MBE in 2021, acted as "significant and substantial" mitigation. Sterling was also commended for having voluntary submitted to addiction treatment, spending 12 days at the Banbury Lodge addiction treatment centre in June. Under the sentencing act of 2026, there is a presumption that any custodial sentence of 12 months or less be suspended.
Sterling must complete 100 hours of community service and has been banned from driving for 24 months.
Sterling was first observed behaving erratically at 1.30am on 28 May, when he was spotted inhaling from a large pink balloon while behind the wheel of his black Lamborghini Urus outside McDonald's in Hayes. After a night without sleep, Sterling got behind the wheel again at 7.45am. Four police forces received reports concerning Sterling's driving along the M25, M40 and M3, with one witness describing it as "absolutely horrendous". Forced to overtake, the witness saw Sterling inhaling from a large white balloon.
On leaving the M3, the Lamborghini veered off the carriageway on the A327, crashing into the central reservation and causing "substantial damage" to the vehicle which "was no longer drivable", according to Allman's sentencing remarks.
Sterling complied with initial roadside breath and saliva tests, which proved negative for alcohol, cannabis and cocaine. At Basingstoke police station Sterling admitted he "had a problem with nitrous oxide" and went on to fail a preliminary impairment test. He was also asked to judge 30 seconds with his eyes closed but recorded 52. Sterling then refused to give a blood sample for further testing until he was accompanied by a lawyer.
Allman said the nature of the offences were such that she had considered the possibility of passing sentencing on to a crown court. "These offences are so serious that neither a fine nor a community order can be justified," she said. "The custodial threshold is clearly crossed, and custody is unavoidable."
Custody was avoided, however, after Allman took into consideration Sterling's receipt of an MBE for services to racial equality in sport in 2021 and his ongoing commitment both to the Raheem Sterling foundation and the RS7 football academy. "I attach substantial weight to your personal mitigation," she said.
"I further accept that nitrous oxide became a maladaptive coping mechanism during a difficult period in your career," Allman went on. At the time of his arrest, Sterling had just completed an unsuccessful period at Feyenoord, having spent the previous six months training alone, excluded from Chelsea's first team.
Speaking for the defence, Jason Bartfeld KC, described the incident as "extremely sad" and said for Sterling it was "a matter of his own intense personal regret" which had "tarnished his personal reputation". Sterling left the court without making comment.
