Maro Itoje has called on his side to show their ruthless streak as they seek to become the first England side in history to clinch a third straight victory over New Zealand on Saturday.
Itoje and co earned a record 37-12 win over the forlorn All Blacks last Saturday and have been urged to go for the jugular with Steve Borthwick's side hoping to round off their 2026 campaign with a sixth consecutive victory.
New Zealand have an ever-growing injury list and are looking to avoid a sixth defeat of the year as they return to Twickenham with Codie Taylor, Will Jordan, Tupou Vaa'i and Anton Lienert-Brown all missing from the side that was downed last weekend. The head coach, Dave Rennie, has said he wants to show some "light at the end of the tunnel" before ending their most disappointing northern tour in recent memory.
"We always want to be ruthless," said Itoje. "We want to be aggressive. We want to be the confrontational team on Saturday. But we'll be fools to think that the All Blacks aren't going to come out firing and come out with an aggressive mindset for themselves. But for us, of course, we need to be physical. We need to be aggressive. We need to be ruthless. All of those things. If I were to speak to you guys after the game, if I could describe our team in those three words, we would have had a great game.
"We're playing the All Blacks. We're playing the All Blacks in what essentially is a type of final. It's going to be a hotly contested game. If we want to win, then we have to show up."
Of the potential to beat New Zealand for the third time in a row, he added: "It is something that we can aim to do and aim to move forward with. That's important and I would love to achieve that. But again I'm thinking about the direction of travel for this team."
Saturday's showdown with New Zealand is England's 12th match of the year. They began with a Six Nations victory over Wales, which was a 12th win in a row, but slumped to five straight defeats thereafter. Since July's loss against South Africa, however, Borthwick's side have won their last five matches.
"We started the year with the Six Nations not how we wanted to," added Itoje. "But I think this team has just stayed consistent. We stuck at it over the summer and made progress, and we're making progress now. I think there's just a lot of buy-in. We have definitely stayed together as a team. There is a collective spirit within the group, we have a good opportunity here, let's pull it together."
