In other news, the Aberdeen chair, Dave Cormack, has admitted the club's financial performance "falls short of the standards we set ourselves" after their latest accounts showed an operating loss of £6.2m.
Aberdeen reported a record turnover of £24.4m for the year up to 30 June 2026, which the club attributed to participation in the Conference League last season alongside "strong performances across commercial, retail, ticketing and hospitality operations".
But they posted an Ebitda (earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation) loss of £4m and an operating loss of £6.2m, compared with a loss of £1.5m in the previous financial year.
The club finished ninth in the Scottish Premiership last term after sacking their manager, Jimmy Thelin, in January and put their financial losses down to increased investment in football operations, a "disappointing" season on the pitch, increased operating costs and lower gains from player sales.
"While we are pleased to report record turnover, the overall financial outcome falls short of the standards we set ourselves and reflects the significant challenges experienced during the 2025-26 season," Cormack said. "The board made a conscious decision to invest heavily in the football department with the aim of driving success on the pitch. Unfortunately, the performances and results achieved did not deliver the return we expected from that investment, and that is reflected in these results.
"That said, the underlying strength of the club's revenue streams continues to be encouraging. Achieving record turnover and record partnership revenue demonstrates the continued loyalty of our supporters, the commitment of our commercial partners, and the hard work of our staff across all areas of the business." Don't worry, human rights, Uefa's got your back. Here's Reuters:
Uefa has established a Human Rights Advisory Board to provide independent oversight and advice during the planning and delivery of Euro 2028, the governing body said on Friday.
The board will serve as a platform for constructive dialogue, external scrutiny and specialist guidance on identifying, preventing and mitigating human rights risks linked to the tournament.
The initiative builds on Uefa's previous work with Human Rights Advisory Boards established for Euro 2024 in Germany and Women's Euro 2025 in Switzerland.
"The Human Rights Advisory Board brings valuable independent perspectives and constructive challenge, helping us stay proactive as preparations progress and ensuring our commitments translate into meaningful action," Uefa's vice-president and board chair Laura McAllister said.
Euro 2028 will be jointly hosted by England, Ireland, Scotland and Wales.

Will Carlos Baleba become a key man for United under Pochettino? Much has been made of the Argentinian's desire to see his teams run, run, and then run some more. In the space of one season at Chelsea, Pochettino improved their running stats.
Baleba looks to be the only current United midfielder with the legs to cover large spaces. Pochettino often prefers a 4-2-3-1, so surely it stands to reason that the former Brighton player will fit in as one of two deep-lying midfielders? Kobbie Mainoo would appear to be the ideal partner for Baleba - and we know Pochettino likes to promote young homegrown talent. What that means for summer signings Andrey Santos and Youri Tielemans, it remains to be seen. But the news that Bruno Fernandes probably won't sign a new contract, could mean a shift in the structure of United's midfield.
Santos was loaned out by Chelsea to Nottingham Forest and Strasbourg during Pochettino's sole campaign at Stamford Bridge. United may well look to recruit more hard-running midfielders in January or the summer to suit Pochettino's preferences.
Japan head coach Hajime Moriyasu will step down next week, the country's football association revealed today, cutting short his tenure in the wake of the 2027 Asian Cup's postponement.
Moriyasu, Japan's longest-serving coach, was originally set to lead the team at the Asian Cup in Saudi Arabia in January before handing the reins to current Under-23 boss Go Oiwa.
But the Asian Football Confederation announced last week that the tournament would be pushed back to December 2027 due to "exceptional circumstances" in the region, following Houthi attacks on the host country.
That left Moriyasu in limbo, with Japan Football Association (JFA) chiefs last week declining to clarify when the coaching handover would take place.
They said that Japan's friendlies in Singapore against Brazil on Saturday and Paraguay on Tuesday would be Moriyasu's last games in charge. AFP
