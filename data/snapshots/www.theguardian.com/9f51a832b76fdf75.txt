20. You Can See Everything (2026)
It's breakfast time at the California home of Elizabeth Holmes, the disgraced former CEO of blood-testing company Theranos, and as the camera follows her lightly forking some eggs, boyfriend Billy Evans swoops in, grabs the bowl off her and begins demonstrating how it should be done.
On the one hand, who among us hasn't backseat-cooked their partner in the kitchen? On the other, is this the most insightful moment of Nathan Fielder's mind-blowing treatise on "radical transparency"? We'll never know if this is Holmes weaponising incompetence because she hates making breakfast, showing us how easily she can be controlled by men - or staging the whole thing to curry sympathy (the woman can't make breakfast let alone dupe anyone out of millions). But it does prove that Fielder needed to break some eggs in order to make his own omelette.
19. Funny Games (1997 and 2007)
It doesn't really matter which version of Michael Haneke's fourth wall-breaking nightmare you watch, there are few scenes so low on violence yet so high on chills than the opening gambit involving some broken eggs. A wealthy family arrives at their summer house. While Anna/Ann, the mother, is unpacking their groceries, a gloved teenager called Paul turns up to borrow eggs. Seconds later, he's dropped them on the floor - then he asks for four more. Hovering just a hair too close for comfort as she cleans the broken shells, it's a moment that upends an everyday act of kindness into the beginning of the end for her family. Each time you watch it, you wish she'd forgotten to go shopping.
18. Alien (1979)
Eggs of a less savoury kind are central to the Alien franchise, though it's Kane's descent into the alien egg chamber on LV-426, discordantly scored by Jerry Goldsmith, which gets the biggest scare. Approaching one of the large, leathery spawns, he is excited to discover there is "organic life" inside. Indeed. Pregnant and now back on the ship, Kane's last words before the alien explodes from his chest, are grimly prescient: "The first thing I am going to do when I get back is get some decent food." That the whole scene takes place in the canteen is another pleasingly gross touch.
17. Howl's Moving Castle (2004)
Few cartoons meals are as appetising as the thick-cut bacon and fried-egg breakfast cooked by Howl in his meet-cute with Sophie in Hayao Miyazaki's Howl's Moving Castle. This dark fable about a cursed milliner and a sexy half-man, half-bird magician is heavy on visuals, but it's the simpatico banter between Howl and Calcifer, voiced in the dubbed version by Billy Crystal, which is most fun. In an early example of nose-to-tail eating, Howl insists on cooking a whopping six eggs because Calcifer eats the eggshells.
16. We Live in Time (2024)
I dare you to sit through this unexpectedly moving weepie without immediately making an omelette using two bowls. Starring Andrew Garfield and Florence Pugh, and set over various timeframes, breakfast food is a recurring theme. Almut is a chef and, early on, she whips up some perfect eggs for Tobias, who himself works for Weetabix. Various sad things happen, but it's Almut's insistence that the best way to crack eggs is flat, not on the side, and to place each raw egg in a small bowl first so you can spot and extract any stray shell, which stays with the viewer - as well as Tobias and their daughter.
15. Phantom Thread (2017)
"Welsh rarebit with a poached egg on top, not too runny, and bacon, scones, butter, cream, jam - not strawberry ... a pot of Lapsang ... and some sausages." Never mind that scones and eggs are a weird pairing, Reynolds Woodcock's flirty breakfast order to waitress - and later lover - Alma, has become the stuff of culinary lore. That is until she makes him one of her special mushroom omelettes and the darkly mutual dependency of their relationship is laid bare on a plate.
14. Rocky (1976)
In what is probably one of cinema's most famous sport-training moments, Rocky Balboa wakes up at 4am, cracks five raw eggs into a glass, gulps them down, then heads out into the freezing Philadelphia streets for a jog in his Converse sneakers. This pivotal scene not only pre-dated today's protein-maxxing phenomenon, it also reflected its star Sylvester Stallone's pre-fame reality: it's thought that he only drank raw eggs because he was too poor to own a hob.
13. Withnail & I (1987)
It's pretty hard to eat a fried egg sandwich without thinking about Bruce Robinson's bleakly comic film about two young actors in 1969 London. Having fled his grotty little flat for a local cafe, Paul McGann's I watches with disgust as an old woman bites into her egg sandwich and drips yolk all over the place. The insipid yellow yolk, the spitting oil, the loveless, wonky sandwiching together of cheap bread: no wonder it became cinematic shorthand for the almost-rock-bottom lifestyle.
12. Runaway Bride (1999)
The versatility of eggs is the saving grace of this bland romcom starring Julia Roberts as Maggie, a would-be bride who simply can't make up her mind about eggs, let alone men. With every partner, she changes her serving preference, though it isn't until Richard Gere's journalist, Ike, suggests this indecisiveness could be a sign she has lost touch with her own identity, that she decides to find out whether it's scrambled, poached or fried that she really likes. Women, eh?
11. Angel Heart (1987)
There's a lot of egg imagery to unpack in this thriller starring Mickey Rourke as a PI and Robert De Niro as creepy client Cyphre. It all comes together in the restaurant scene where we watch De Niro plucking hard-boiled eggs out of a silver grail before rolling their shells with his unnaturally manicured fingers. Offering him an egg, Rourke declines, saying he has "a thing about chickens", before tossing some spilled salt over his left shoulder to ward off the devil who, unbeknown to him, is sat opposite.
10. Ghostbusters (1984)
Sigourney Weaver has had to endure more preternatural eggs on film than most. In this cartoonish sendup of Poltergeist, her cellist Dana is unpacking groceries - lettuce, a bag of marshmallows (ha) and a box of eggs - when four inexplicably leap out and begin cracking themselves on the counter. The creep factor is PG but it's a well-executed example of how the most uncanny moments involve the most ordinary things. Just don't open the fridge!
9. Harry, He's Here to Help (2000)
Whenever he has sex, the titular Harry (Sergi López) in Dominik Moll's 2000 French nail-biter swallows raw eggs to improve his orgasms - and makes sure everyone knows about it. It's one of his many weird and inveigling moves in this creepy Hitchockian thriller in which a man called Harry inserts himself into the life of a man called Michel like a cuckoo into another bird's nest. Still, Harry's obsession with the foodstuff somehow shifts Michel's writer's block and inspires him to write a new book Les Œufs. Swings and roundabouts.
8. Five Easy Pieces (1970)
It's hard to choose between Jack Nicholson's best on-screen egg moments. As the abrasive Melvin in As Good as it Gets, his horrifying speech to waitress Carol (Helen Hunt) in her diner begins with his disarming order of "three eggs over easy". But it's probably his failed attempt to order a plain omelette as Bobby in Five Easy Pieces (no potatoes, tomatoes instead, a cup of coffee, and wheat toast) as a way of showing the film's tension between his individualistic streak in a world of rigid conformity, which is his most memorable.
7. The Substance (2024)
The opening egg scene of Coralie Fargeat's body-horror film shows a raw egg yolk being injected with a green liquid before dividing into two. Never mind that a yolk couldn't undergo mitosis - given nobody has ever birthed their alternate self from their back either, we know this is not a film about scientific accuracy. It's a pretty if blunt bit of foreshadowing, and the yolk theme is also nicely carried through via Elisabeth Sparkle's (Demi Moore) fab yellow coat.
6. Big Night (1996)
It's a simple but remarkable shot: the real-time making of an omelette which ties up surely one of the most stressful nights in restaurant history. This near-silent, five-minute dawn scene in which Secondo (played by Stanley Tucci) cooks an omelette for waiter Cristiano and his brother Primo in the kitchen of their failing Jersey Shore restaurant is about reconciliation and brotherly love, and no doubt served as a prelude to Tucci's second life as a food host. It was also nicely homaged by Sydney in season two of The Bear.
5. Cool Hand Luke (1967)
Lucas "Luke" Jackson had nothing on Gaston in Beauty & the Beast, who eats 60 eggs every morning. But the scene in which Paul Newman's defiant inmate eats 50 in an hour, thus earning the respect of his fellow prisoners, is a welcome moment of gung-ho respite in an otherwise pretty serious drama about freedom, oppression and institutional authority. In reality, it's thought Newman ate around only eight and spat out what he didn't swallow after filming. According to his memoir, when he was asked whether method acting was just doing the real thing, Newman replied: "Not if you have to swallow eggs."
4. We Need to Talk About Kevin (2011)
The scene in which Tilda Swinton's Eva discovers another high school mother has smashed all her eggs at the supermarket, buys them anyway, and goes home to make a rather masochistic omelette because she believes the actions of her son, Kevin, are all her fault is one of quiet devastation in the 2011 film adaptation of Lionel Shriver's 2003 novel. Coming a close second is the discordant use of Lonnie Donegan's jolly Ham 'N' Eggs, played by Eva in her car as she drives around town as the local pariah.
3. The Ipcress File (1965)
Watching someone break eggs with one hand is never not cool, as proved by working class agent Harry Palmer (Michael Caine) in The Ipcress File. His intention is to seduce fellow agent Jean (Sue Lloyd), by rustling up one of his famous omelettes. But as the sort of chap who also grinds his own coffee beans and listens to Mozart, the film unintentionally led to his character becoming an all-round domestic god in the real world too. Never mind that the eggs were actually cracked by Len Deighton, author of the 1962 novel on which the film is based, who was also a bit of a foodie, because Caine wasn't quite up to the task.
2. Airplane! (1980)
This slapstick classic is the definitive send-up of a disaster movie, and has a lot of fun on board a hellish flight hit by a serious case of food poisoning. As the crew and passengers are incapacitated one by one, it's the bizarre moment in which a gagging woman begins producing eggs from her mouth that sticks in the mind. The whole scene was done by sleight of hand, including the moment when Leslie Nielsen cracks the third egg on a teacup, a live bird flutters up into the cabin, and yet somehow everyone keeps a straight face.
1. Tampopo (1985)
If you haven't been brought to orgasm by eating a raw egg yolk, have you even lived? This so called "ramen-western" follows a widow and her attempts to turn around her run-down noodle shop. But it's the comic culinary scene - one of several - in which a gangster and his moll pass a raw egg yolk back and forth between their mouths until it bursts at the opportune moment, and dribbles all over her pretty white dress, which suggests that for some, eating and sex are interchangeable.
