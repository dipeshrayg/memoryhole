London's stock market has been dealt a further blow as the catering company Compass Group confirmed plans to move its main listing to the US, saying it had become "a predominantly North American business".
The world's largest catering group said the move would provide better access to "the world's largest and deepest pool of capital," as it kicked off a consultation with its shareholders, two-fifths of whom are based in the US.
"This isn't something we've taken lightly and has been under consideration by the board for some time now," the Compass chief executive, Dominic Blakemore, told analysts. "It's not just about the next few years or a short, rating opportunity. This is about the right long-term decision for Compass over many years to come."
The £43.6bn ($57bn) catering company is among the biggest on the FTSE 100 and has been listed in London since 1988 after a management buyout, described as the largest in UK history. It gained a foothold in the US in 1994 when it bought Canteen, a vending and food service company.
North America generates 75% of Compass's underlying operating profit and two-thirds of its revenue. The region is home to its largest client base and 325,000 employees, more than half of its global workforce.
The London Stock Exchange has experienced similar moves from the online betting group Flutter Entertainment, the building materials company CRH, and the financial technology firm Wise switching their primary listings to the US in recent years. Three FTSE 100 companies - the fund manager Schroders, the insurer Beazley, and the product testing group Intertek - have left the stock market after being acquired by overseas groups.
Compass reiterated its commitment to the UK, where it employs 55,000 people and is a big government contractor. It provides meals in more than 2,000 schools, academies, colleges, and universities across the UK, and in some NHS hospitals, as well as offices in the private sector. It declined to say whether it would also move its domicile to the US.
Greg Johnson, an equity research analyst at Shore Capital, said: "This is yet another blow to the UK market. Subject to consultation and shareholder approval, the transition is expected to complete in 2028. Au revoir or adieu?"
The company, which has 600,000 staff across 25 countries, said it was the market leader in the US, where it provides catering to Microsoft, Google and Amazon. It also supplies vending machines and hotel services such as checking in guests and cleaning rooms.
Compass received a boost from catering to stadiums hosting the men's football Fifa World Cup this year and is cashing in on the AI boom by providing food and services to datacentres. It is also pushing into catering for airline lounges and the US military.
"You are seeing the ramping up of our defence efforts ... We have recently won some datacentre construction contracts in both North America and international," Blakemore said.
"Second, the operational phase where we have worked with our hyperscale clients for some time," he added. Datacentres currently generate "a few hundred million dollars of revenue", and Compass has created a new brand, Data Center Life.
The group traces its origins to 1941, when the Croydon-based entrepreneur Jack Bateman founded Factory Canteens Ltd to feed wartime workers. It changed its name to Compass in 1984.
Compass made revenues of $50.8bn in the year to 30 September, up 10% year on year. Underlying operating profit rose 12% to $3.7bn.
