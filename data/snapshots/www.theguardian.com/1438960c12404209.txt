Harry Brook pulverised his way to the fastest double hundred in ODI history and it still was not enough for England. Sri Lanka were the victors in this absurd contest, hauling down a target of 430 in Rawalpindi, the second-highest successful chase in the history of the format. Only that magical 438 game, 20 years ago, trumps it.
Pavan Rathnayake, Dasun Shanaka and Avishka Fernando were the headliners, somehow turning it around for Sri Lanka after they were reduced to 24 for two. Rathnayake top-scored with 130, Fernando struck 97, and Shanaka - with 97 of his own - was the most brutal of the lot. After being taken apart with the ball, his revenge act included eight sixes. None of the trio were there at the close, as England suddenly found themselves back in it.
Up comes a crestfallen Harry Brook, who said his record-breaking knock now has "no value whatsoever" to him.
It was an incredible game of cricket - both teams were exceptional. Sri Lanka outplayed us unfortunately, we couldn't hit our straps with the ball, but fair play to them. [My own batting] was a nice innings - unfortunately it does not mean anything now. It has no value whatsoever in my eyes. We need to get together and go back to the drawing board a little bit. We know where we've gone wrong.
Kusal Mendis:
We've won the toss in our last five games and every game we've chased because of the dew. It was a very good game today; as a captain I really enjoyed it. Me and the coach talked to the players before the chase to play the first couple of overs as normal, then we decided Dasun [Shanaka] would come in earlier and everyone batted really well and took responsibility. Everyone showed their characters and belief in themselves.

It took time, it gently frayed nerves, but ultimately South Africa sealed the second Test at Gqeberha deep into the final day, clear by an innings and 31 runs, levelling the series with one to play. In truth, although Australia made 295 and got within 28 overs of safety, they did not deserve to save this game, even if Scott Boland and Travis Head did. Letting South Africa escape from 40 for four to pass 400 was the first offence, then came Australia's capitulation for 99 to be forced to follow on. The salvage operation was in waters too deep.

Sri Lanka pull off second highest ever successful ODI run chase
If England's batting brought almost unprecedented levels of brutal pyrotechnics, Sri Lanka's was an extraordinarily well paced and judged. Avishka Fernando and Rathnayeke laid the foundations for Shanakas and then Liyanage's extraordinary finishing.
Conditions were a batter's dream, for sure, but England did not bowl, looked bereft of cutting edge for long spells, and poor Sam Curran has conceded the most expensive ever figures by an England bowler in an ODI. Josh Tongue's excellent death bowling nearly won it before Liyanage held his nerve and won it.
