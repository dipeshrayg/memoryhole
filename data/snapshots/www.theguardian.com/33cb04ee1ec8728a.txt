Bryson DeChambeau will add star quality to the DP World Tour next season after it was confirmed the two-time major winner has joined the circuit after the demise of LIV Golf. The American heads a list of high-profile names who will perform regularly in Europe in 2027 following LIV's bankruptcy.
Intrigue had surrounded DeChambeau's future. As a poster boy for LIV and its Saudi Arabian-backed disruption scheme, he appeared unwilling to feature again on traditional tours. Yet it became increasingly clear what limited options he had. The PGA Tour has been unwilling to offer an easy pathway for the 33-year-old, who in theory can now play his way back there via the DP World Tour. Cards will be available on the PGA Tour for 2028 through DP World Tour rankings. DeChambeau has become increasingly keen on content creation, which fuelled speculation he may skip mainstream competition entirely.
There is now the enticing prospect of DeChambeau facing off regularly with Rory McIlroy. The pair have endured a mixed relationship over the years, with McIlroy winning a record-equalling eighth Race to Dubai title on Sunday. McIlroy said in advance of the final event of the season that DeChambeau would be a welcome addition to the DP World Tour.
DeChambeau has joined the DP World Tour for the first time as a "category one member", which applies to him as a recent winner - 2024 - of the US Open. Jon Rahm, who had already confirmed a DP World Tour return post-LIV, is in the same category as a past Masters champion, having won it in 2023. Graeme McDowell, Martin Kaymer, Sergio García, Louis Oosthuizen, Henrik Stenson, Lee Westwood and Ian Poulter have also rejoined the DP World Tour. Cameron Smith, Dustin Johnson and Bubba Watson will play on the DP World Tour in a set, unspecified number of events next year.
"There has been significant change in the landscape of men's professional golf over the past 12 months," said Guy Kinnings, the chief executive of the DP World Tour. "As golf's global tour, we have taken a pragmatic approach to this unique and unprecedented situation as we go into next year, always with the best interests of our entire membership at the forefront of our minds.
"We realise there are different viewpoints but this approach, discussed and agreed with our tournament committee and allied to this responsibly managed programme of invites, will add value which, in turn, will benefit the Tour as a whole."
The sentiment of Kinnings provides a tacit admission he had to balance bringing eyeballs to his business with the demands of rank-and-file members.
