Midge Ure, singer, songwriter
Visage were an amalgamation of three bands: Rich Kids, Magazine and Ultravox. Since all three were touring, getting everybody in the studio at the same time was nigh on impossible. Still, we were making our debut album and were a track short, when along came this gift from God.
We had no money so I'd been moonlighting with Thin Lizzy, and keyboardist Billy Currie had been playing with Gary Numan. Fade to Grey started life as an instrumental that Billy had been jamming with Chris Payne from the Numan band in soundchecks. They'd tried to release it but nobody was interested. I thought it was wonderful and suggested writing lyrics so we could use it on the Visage album.
The instrumental created this brilliant atmosphere - mysterious, melancholy, dark, dystopian, film noir - which put pictures in my head. I imagined a guy sitting on a train station with a suitcase, trying to escape his life, hence: "One man on a lonely platform, one case sitting by his side ..." There's a term in video-making, "fade to black", but I changed it for the song because it gave the impression of a transition to something else. I imagined the man getting on a train not knowing where he'd end up.
The music was already recorded so we didn't do a huge amount to it. I think Rusty Egan played some electronic drums. Steve Strange was the frontman but wasn't a natural singer. So I put down a vocal, then he sang while listening to my vocal in headphones - what came out of his mouth was the same melody, but in his voice. You can hear me on the "aahs", the "fade to greys" and some of the harmony parts. Rusty had a Belgian girlfriend, Brigitte Arendt, so someone - probably Rusty - suggested she read the title in French along with a translation of the lyrics. It was the icing on the cake.
Godley and Creme wanted to showcase themselves as video-makers so they made one for us for just £1,500: most of that went on makeup artists for Steve. The song and the video just seemed to capture a moment: the Blitz club, New Romantics, synthesisers. Then suddenly it was in the charts all over Europe.
Chris Payne, keyboards, songwriter
Fade to Grey started off in 1976 or 77 when I was a music student at Chichester and we had to compose something. I was really into pre-Raphaelite paintings - Millais' Ophelia and the like - so I wrote a romantic study with the chords that eventually became Fade to Grey, but played on piano in a different manner. After I joined the Gary Numan band, I used to mess around playing the chords on a Polymoog synth and a bassline on a Minimoog. One day Billy and drummer Cedric Sharpley joined in. As the tour progressed, we developed it in soundchecks.
After the tour, Billy was due to go back to Ultravox, so we wanted to record it as a keepsake. We used producer Martin Rushent's studio in London, but when we set up the gear the Minimoog wasn't there, just the Polymoog. However, the brass setting gave the bassline the distinctive attack on Fade to Grey. Cedric started the song with a CR-78 drum machine and put the drums down. Billy sprinkled synth over it and that was it. The arrangement was created on the fly. Then Billy called asking: "Do you mind if it goes on the Visage album?"
And the rest is history. The next thing I knew my publisher called me to say it was at No 45. Then my neighbour came in going: "It's on Top of the Pops! And there's this most amazing video." Then it was in the Top 10 - and No 1 in some countries.
I'm really happy with its success. On YouTube, there are loads of people doing different takes. I've just done a version for Midge's new album, and my wife sang the French bit. It's come full circle.
