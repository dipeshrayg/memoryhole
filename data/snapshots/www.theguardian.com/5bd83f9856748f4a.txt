Chip sell-off drags South Korea's Kospi index to six-month low
Oof! South Korea's benchmark Kospi share index has closed at a six-month low, as inflation fears and worries about the IT boom triggered a sell-off.
The Kospi ended the day down 7.5% at its lowest closing level since April, after a day of wild trading that briefly caused circuit breakers to be triggered. At its worst point, the Kospi was down over 9%.
Chipmakers drove the selloff - SamsungElectronics fell 9.5% and SKHynix plunged by 11.1%.
It's been a staggering year for South Korean traders. In the first half of 2026 the Kospi roughly doubled in value, as investors flocked to buy shares in chipmakers whose products were in hot demand with AI companies.
But that boom began to fade as we entered July. And now, the Kospi is 'only' up 30% for 2026.
The South Korean stock market boom was driven by a surge of interest from retail investors, many of whom bought shares on margin (with borrowed money), and could have lost their stakes as the market turned sour.
As covered earlier, today's tech rout is partly due to worries over US inflation, which may trigger interest rate rises.
There's also concern about the high amount of borrowing which AI firms are taking on as they roll out data centres and train new AI models.
