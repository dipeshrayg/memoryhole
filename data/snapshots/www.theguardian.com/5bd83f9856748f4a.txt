EU pushing for action over trade deficit with China
Jennifer Rankin
The EU is set to call for further action to curb the bloc's vast trade deficit with China, widely blamed for job losses and factory closures across the continent.
EU leaders meeting in Brussels on Thursday to discuss the trade deficit with China - which hit €360bn in 2025 - are expected to call for "new instruments to better address these imbalances" according to a draft statement.
The European Commission, which negotiates trade policy on behalf of the 27 member states, agreed a deal this month with Beijing that will see the EU limit hybrid car exports from China to 350,000 vehicles a year, potentially halving Chinese market share.
EU countries have welcomed the deal, but think it is not enough and want steps to protect other industrial sectors, such as chemicals, plastics and machinery.
The debate has sharpened since Germany - long reluctant to use trade defences against China - converted to a more hawkish position amid pressure from German industrial leaders, who are laying off workers and closing factories.
Germany and France last week called for better use of existing trade defence measures, including urgent action in the chemicals and plastics sectors.
The EU's two biggest economies are also seeking "a diversification instrument" that would cut off access to the EU market when faced with severe imbalances. Dubbed the "kill switch", the law is intended to be country agnostic, but is clearly aimed at China.
Member states have welcomed the deal negotiated by EU trade commissioner Maroš Šefčovič on hybrid cars, but are sceptical as to whether Beijing will stick to it. China would adhere to the agreement because they began to realise "they are killing their customers" , Šefčovič was reported to have said, according to a senior EU diplomat.
The EU diplomat highlighted the urgency of the situation from the EU's point of view: "We don't have much time because every month that goes by, thousands of jobs are lost."
