Tyson Fury has been cleared to fight Anthony Joshua in Cardiff next month despite concerns about his mental health.
The British Boxing Board of Control (BBBofC) indicated this week that it would investigate comments from Fury's wife, Paris, that the boxer had been diagnosed with schizophrenia, and had the powers to deny him a licence to fight.
Paris Fury retracted her claim in a statement issued on Thursday night, and the BBBofC's general secretary Robert Smith told Sky Sports: "Fury cleared last night. All up to date medicals cleared. Promoter told this morning."
Paris Fury had said in an initial version of a Netflix documentary released on Friday that Fury was diagnosed with schizophrenia just months after beating Wladimir Klitschko to win the world heavyweight title in Düsseldorf in November 2015.
She said in Thursday's statement: "I want to clarify a statement I made in the original version of the Tyson Fury documentary.
"I recalled a conversation from many years ago when discussing Tyson's condition and I used the word 'schizophrenic'. Whilst Tyson did show some symptoms associated with schizophrenia, he doesn't have this condition and has never received a diagnosis.
"Mental health is complex, and it isn't always easy to find the right words, especially when you're living it with someone you love. I'm so proud of Tyson for really opening up in this documentary and want to say thank you for all the support we've received as a family."
Netflix indicated it had edited the footage as a result, saying in a statement: "Following new information shared by the family relating to Tyson's official mental health diagnosis, we have worked alongside them to update the documentary accordingly ahead of launch."
Fury himself had also told the Guardian last week that he had been diagnosed with the condition, saying: "I went to a leading specialist and was diagnosed with schizophrenia and bipolar. I learned that you can never truly get away from mental health. You can only learn to manage and maintain."
He has been open about his mental health struggles since beating Klitschko, after which he took a near three-year hiatus from boxing, with his weight ballooning to 27 stone, while he contended with suicidal thoughts and alcohol addiction.
Fury admitted hearing "voices in my mind" following his win, adding: "Words can't explain the darkness I was in.
"I came from the living version of the pits of hellfire. And I 100% thought at times I'd end up in a padded room with the door locked, and that scared me so much."
The Fury v Joshua bout is set to take place at Cardiff's Principality Stadium in the early hours of 12 December in UK time, with the undercard beginning in the evening of the 11th.
