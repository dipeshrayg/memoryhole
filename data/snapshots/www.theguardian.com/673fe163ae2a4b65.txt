Nvidia's headhunters have reportedly approached the chief executive of the Cambridge-based chip company Arm, in its search for a new leader after Jensen Huang's dramatic exit.
Heidrick & Struggles, the recruiting firm hired by the company's board, has started contacting potential candidates, according to Bloomberg.
It reported that Rene Haas, who joined Arm in 2013 and became chief executive in 2022, is among those approached.
Nvidia is searching for a replacement for Huang, one of the firm's founders, who resigned last week after he admitted to an extramarital affair with a woman accused of spying for China.
Haas, 64, is viewed as the leading contender for the top job at Nvidia, having previously worked at the $3.6tn (£2.8tn) company for seven years as the general manager of its computing products.
Reports suggest that other potential candidates include: Ian Buck, an Nvidia vice-president; Lisa Su, AMD's chief executive; and Johny Srouji, Apple's chief hardware officer.
Arm, which is listed in New York but retains its global headquarters in Cambridge, has about 500 users of its chip designs worldwide, including Nvidia, Apple, Samsung and Qualcomm. The company employs more than 7,000 staff, including more than 3,000 in the UK. It is the biggest tech firm headquartered in Britain, now valued at $196bn.
Haas told a tech conference in Austin on Wednesday that Huang was "a legend". "What he built at Nvidia is historic," he said. "You don't have many champions like that."
Nvidia has sought to curb speculation that Huang could return to his role, after he said on Tuesday that he would be willing to come back.
He wrote in a statement: "If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO."
It came after US president, Donald Trump, and Silicon Valley insiders - including Elon Musk, prominent venture capitalists and the disgraced Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick - called for Huang's reinstatement.
However, the Nvidia chair, Harvey Jones, confirmed at the company's quarterly earnings results this week that the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO".
Jones said that "while this was an unexpected transition ... the board is proactively dealing with the consequences".
Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, was named interim CEO last week and presented the financial results on Tuesday, which showed that its quarterly revenue had doubled from the same period a year ago to $115.84bn.
Huang agreed on Tuesday to appear before the House of Representatives foreign affairs committee, the select committee on the Chinese Communist party and the intelligence committee for transcribed interviews in early December. Nvidia also faces an investigation by the US Securities and Exchange Commission over its business in China, the Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday.
Nvidia and Arm were approached for comment.
