SFO charges eight people over collapse of London Capital & Finance
Newsflash: The UK's Serious Fraud Office (SFO) has charged eight individuals in connection with its investigation into the collapse of London Capital & Finance Plc (LCF), one of Britain's biggest retail investment scandals.
The SFO says that Michael Andrew Thomson (53), Simon Hume-Kendall (73), Elten Barker (53), Paul Careless (51), Robert Sedgwick (78) and John Russell-Murphy (52) have each been charged with conspiracy to defraud. Helen Hume-Kendall (64) has been charged with concealing criminal property.
An eighth defendant has been charged with conspiracy to defraud and acting in contravention of a director disqualification undertaking.
The collapse of LCF, which promised high interest rates on "mini-bonds" between 2013 and 2018, cost about 11,600 investors more than £237m, leaving taxpayers to pay a £120m compensation bill.
US announces new rules to limit remote ccess to advanced AI chips
Newsflash: The US Commerce Department has issued a new rule to limit remote access to advanced artificial intelligence chips by certain third countries.
The interim final rule (IFR) is designed to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries, the US government says.
The IFR will require licenses to reexport advanced AI chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, or Brazil.
This move follows reports that Singapore-based Megaspeed, the company where suspected Chinese spy AliceHuang worked, had transferred advanced Nvidia chips to Chinese companies.
The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry & Security explains:
Rapid advancement in cloud computing and SI infrastructure have made it increasingly possible to access advanced American computing capabilities remotely, including through data centers located outside countries subject to U.S. export restrictions.
This IFR addresses this evolving technological landscape by further aligning protections governing remote access to advanced computing capabilities with existing controls on the physical shipment of sensitive American technology.
