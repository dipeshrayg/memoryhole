The number of young people across the UK not in education, employment or training (Neet) has decreased but remains close to a million as Andy Burnham's government pushes to tackle Britain's youth jobs crisis.
As ministers prepare to make sweeping changes in response to Alan Milburn's landmark report on young people and work, the Office for National Statistics said the number of Neets fell by 22,000 in the three months to September, to 959,000.
On an annual basis, the number of 16- to 24-year-olds not earning or learning was 17,000 higher than a year ago. Although down from a peak of just over a million in March, the latest figure remains close to the highest level in a decade.
The prime minister last week promised to create an entirely new welfare system for young people after Milburn said Britain risked "losing a generation of young people" without a radical change in course.
Milburn also called for a radical shake-up of employment support to guarantee young people would get help, alongside changes to education policy and healthcare.
A parallel review into disability benefits by the Labour minister Stephen Timms this week called for significant changes to a welfare system that was "no longer fit for purpose".
Pat McFadden, the work and pensions secretary, said young people deserved better. "That's why we're fundamentally reforming how we support young people to grow up in this country," he said. "As part of our response to the Milburn review, we're building a new settlement that invests in young people's success instead of paying for failure."
It is understood the government is preparing a full response to Milburn's recommendations spelling out the changes that will be made. Ministers will then publish a white paper next year on welfare reform.
Overhauling welfare will, however, stand as one of the biggest challenges for Burnham's government as he pushes to curb a rising welfare bill without triggering a repeat of the backbench rebellion faced by Keir Starmer.
Business leaders last week seized on elements of Milburn's report calling for tax cuts and for the government to take a more cautious approach on its plan to boost workers' rights, which he said could hit youth hiring. Bosses have blamed Labour for adding to employment costs, increasing the pressure on companies amid surging inflation triggered by the war in the Middle East.
Helen Whately, the shadow work and pensions secretary, said: "Since Labour took power they have killed jobs for young people, with their jobs tax and Employment Rights Act. Labour's solution of throwing taxpayers' money at subsidised work placements will do nothing to fix the underlying problems."
According to the latest snapshot from the ONS, one in eight young people in the UK were Neet in the three months to September - down slightly from the level in June but largely unchanged from a year earlier.
The drop in the three months to September was driven by a decline in the number of 18- to 24-year-old Neets to 867,000. However, the number of 16- and 17-year-olds finishing their education without finding work or training rose to 92,000 - the highest level since 2012.
Iona Ledwidge, the chief executive of the youth employment charity Spear, said: "While any decrease in these figures is welcome, they remain far too high. Rolling out the support that Alan Milburn recommended can't come soon enough. Even six months out of work damages a young person's wellbeing."
