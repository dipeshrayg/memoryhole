JonPullin, Cheltenham's clerk of the course, has been discussing the ground and slew of non-runners on Racing TV.
"We're comfortable that we did everything we could and got as much water on as possible ahead of the meeting," Pullin said.
"When we finished irrigating on Thursday afternoon ahead of Thursday night's frost we were comfortable there was enough moisture in the ground. We were hopeful that would hold for yesterday and today because we knew realistically we couldn't get anymore water on due to the frost.
"As it transpired we had a sharper frost than forecast and that sharpened it up on Thursday into Friday and the same last night so we've gone good, good to firm in places but the majority is good.
"With irrigation and the little rain we have had between the Showcase [meeting in October] and this one, we have put 400mm on, but we've only had 16mm of rain."

Hello and welcome to the Guardian's live-blog coverage of what should be one of the great days in the jumping calendar: Paddy Power Gold Cup day at Cheltenham's November meeting.
Unfortunately, though, "should be" is doing the kind of heavy lifting in that sentence that Red Rum achieved in his second Grand National victory in 1974, because the prevailing fast ground has already seen the card shorn of several star names, and around two dozen of the original declarations in all.
The original field of 15 for the Paddy Power Gold Cup, perhaps the biggest handicap chase in the first half of the winter campaign, is now down to 10 runners - July Flower is the latest absentee - while the "Big Two" in the Grade Two Arkle Trial - Kabral Du Mathan and Old Park Star, last year's Supreme Novice Hurdle winner, are scratches too.
It is disappointing and also deeply frustrating for the track, as it is difficult to know what else Jon Pullin, the clerk of the course, could have done. Sub-zero temperatures overnight meant that he was unable to switch on the taps on Thursday or Friday, and there has been so little rain for months, rather than just weeks, that even watered ground is unlikely to be any easier than good.
The 30k racegoers expected through the gates still have a busy afternoon to look forward to, though, kicking off with a handicap chase for amateur riders at 12.35 before the ITV Racing action begins with the Grade Two Triumph Hurdle Trial at 1.10. There are a couple of top-class prospects in that race - Atomic City, backed down to favourite this morning, and Charles Darnay - while the Paddy Power Gold Cup is due to be off and running at 2.20.
