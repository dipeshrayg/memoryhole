Ding ding goes the Wall Street opening bell, and trading is underway after the US stock market's most bruising day in 19 months yesterday.
And it's a mixed picture!
Tech stocks are rising, lifting the Nasdaq up by 1.2% in early trading. AI-linked stocks such as AMD (+8.1%), SanDisk (+8.3%) and Nvidia (+6.5%) are among the top risers.
But the much-storied DowJonesIndustrialAverage (DJIA) is moving the other way, down 1% at the open. It's being dragged down by old-economy stocks such as Nike (-3.5%), Walmart (-3.2%) and McDonalds (-3.1%).
Reminder: Yesterday the Nasdaq index slumped by 5.5%, while the DJIA dropped by 3.44%. So this is only a small recovery for the Nasdaq, and further pain for the Dow.

The NasdaqComposite index, which sank into 'correction territory' yesterday, is up 1.3% now.
Aerospace manufacturer and launch service provider RocketLab are the top riser, up 12.5%, followed by Airbnb (+11.7%), AppLovin (+10%), AdvancedMicroDevices (+9.3%) and Intel (+8.9%).

With European stock markets closed after a nervy day, and Wall Street calmer than yesterday, it's time to wrap up...
European stock markets fell sharply today as a rout in US tech stocks spread across the Atlantic, after the Democrats' midterms victory sparked fears of a slowdown in the AI industry.
A sell-off on Wall Street on Thursday was followed by sharp falls in Asian stocks markets overnight. The investor sentiment then reached London, where the market was, at one stage, poised for its worst day since March.
The FTSE100 index, which tracks blue-chip shares listed in London, was down as much as 254 points, or 2.35%, at 10,528 points during a wave of selling in morning trading. But by the close of the day it had recovered to a 1% fall, helped by a bounce in US stocks when Wall Street reopened.
Across Europe, there were similar losses: Germany's Dax index has fallen by 2%, France's Cac40 has lost 0.8%, and Spain's Ibex ended the day down 0.6%. Italy's FTSE Mib fell 0.9%.
In Asia-Pacific markets, Japan's Nikkei225 index sunk 6% and South Korea's Kospi index tumbled more than 6.5%.
"It's actually a bit of a disaster," said Christopher Forbes, the head of Asia and Middle East at CMC Markets.
"[Clients] were long index, long stocks, and they all got carried out. All night, all day - we had client margin calls, client complaints ... because it's a very emotional thing, right? People are wedded to their positions and views on a stock or outcome, and you're telling them: 'Look, you've just lost money.'"
Over in New York right now, the Nasdaq is up 1.5% (having fallen by 5.5% yesterday), and the S&P 500 is up 0.7%.

Hargreaves Lansdown: our clients are buying the dip
Trading platform Hargreaves Lansdown reports that its clients have been "buying the dip" - the practice where investors pile in when a stock falls to tempting levels.
Anna Macdonald, investment strategy director at Hargreaves Lansdown, suggests this week's tech selloff (which is already unwinding) is a buying opportunity:
Macdonald says:
"Trading interest in overseas AI and technology companies has been growing all week. We've seen some larger investors taking risk off the table, but the average trade has been a buy with Nvidia, SpaceX, Micron, AMD and Alphabet making up the top five. Nvidia and SpaceX are the headline names, while Micron and AMD give investors exposure to the hardware side of the story. Alphabet is interesting too, as its shares were marked down after disappointing numbers and clients have used that as an opportunity to buy.
Overall, many of our clients are taking a long-term view and see this as a moment to get back into some of these names at a cheaper entry point.
Yesterday's sell-off looks like an overreaction. We've seen these wobbles before and we'll see them again, but investors need to focus on the fundamentals. Demand for AI isn't going away, and we expect the newly elected US officials to take a pragmatic approach to the buildout.
For patient investors, this is an opportunity to invest in high-quality companies at valuations we haven't seen in a long time."
