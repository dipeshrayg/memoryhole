Newcastle United's owner, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF), is looking to sell up to 25% of the club by issuing new shares.
The New York-based bank JP Morgan, whose most recent involvement in a major football deal was Fifa's aborted attempt to sell off 20% of its commercial rights last summer, has been appointed to handle the sale, as first reported by the Athletic.
PIF is seeking investment partners to help fund major infrastructure projects, such as building a new stadium or redeveloping St James' Park, and would dilute its shareholding rather than profiting from the proceeds of the share issue.
The state-owned fund owns 84% of Newcastle after the departure of the minority shareholder Amanda Staveley two years ago, with the remaining 16% owned by the London-based Reuben brothers.
Newcastle are the second Premier League club to formally seek investment this season, after Everton's owners appointed the US bank Moelis & Company to handle their sale process last month. Last summer Liverpool's owner, Fenway Sports Group, sold 38% of the club to a consortium which includes the Amazon founder Jeff Bezos.
PIF sources insist it retains a long-term commitment to Newcastle, whose value has soared since it bought the club from Mike Ashley for £305m five years ago in a deal that has not proved as transformative for their fortunes as many expected.
Newcastle qualified for the Champions League twice under Eddie Howe, who also led the club to their first domestic trophy for 70 years by winning the Carabao Cup in 2025, but the Premier League's spending rules have prevented the owners from investing as heavily as the competition's other state-owned club, Manchester City.
PIF has also cut back its spending on sport generally, with the Newcastle share issue coming months after the fund withdrew its support for LIV Golf, which filed for bankruptcy this month.
PIF has also sold a stake in the Saudi Pro League club Al-Hilal, is open to selling other clubs, and has withdrawn from a three-year deal to host the Women's Tennis Association finals 12 months early.
Newcastle have been wrestling for several years with the issue of whether to expand St James' Park or build a new stadium on an adjacent site, but have made clear a project on that scale would require external funding.
The club declined to comment.
