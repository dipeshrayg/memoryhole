The new Belgium coach Mark van Bommel on named his first squad for their four Nations League matches, retaining the core of the World Cup selection.
Van Bommel, who has replaced Rudi Garcia, added eight players who did not take part in the tournament in Canada, Mexico and the US as he named a 28-man squad for matches against Italy, France (twice), and Turkey between 25 September and 5 October.
Belgium must do without first-choice goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois, who asked to be excused from the Nations League campaign to regulate his playing time at the age of 34. Jérémy Doku, who was seen as a key player for the World Cup campaign but had a disappointing tournament, misses out due to injury, as does Amadou Onana.
Attacker Alexis Saelemaekers and veteran defender Thomas Meunier have been dropped while Axel Witsel has retired from international football. The uncapped players in the squad include goalkeepers Jari De Busser, Mike Penders and Maarten Vandevoordt and the 21-year-old full-back Kyriani Sabbe.
At the World Cup, Belgium reached the quarter-finals, being eliminated after a 2-1 defeat to eventual champions Spain.Belgium were ultimately the only team to score a goal against Spain throughout the latter's victorious World Cup campaign.

De la Fuente announces squad from city at centre of migration crisis
Spain coach Luis de la Fuente announced his Nations League squad from Ceuta on Friday in a show of support for the Spanish city in northern Africa that has become the centre of a migration crisis.
For his first squad announcement since Spain won the World Cup , De la Fuente revealed his list in a video featuring him in Ceuta accompanied by residents of the Spanish exclave. In late July, over 70,000 people pushed across the border from Morocco, with more than 100 dying in the chaos. Most quickly returned, but a few thousand remain in the city of just over 80,000.
De la Fuente did not directly mention the border crossing or its impact, but he did say: "This is Ceuta, this is Spain." It echoed the rise in sentiment in Spain that Ceuta - a territory that until the mass crossing was mostly an afterthought for most mainland Spaniards - needs to be supported.
Spain open the Nations League against England at Wembley Stadium on 26 September, when its players will wear two stars on their shirts for the first time after winning a second world title.
After visiting London, Spain will host Croatia on 29 September and the Czech Republic on 3 October, before visiting Croatia three says later.
Del la Fuente kept his World Cup squad mostly intact. Inter Milan goalkeeper Josep Martínez replaced Barcelona's Joan García, who is out with a minor knee issue. Real Madrid defender Dean Huijsen is in for Marcos Llorente, who retired from international soccer.
Fermín López replaced fellow Barcelona midfielder Gavi, while Espanyol striker Roberto Fernández got his first call-up, replacing Borja Iglesias.
Ceuta is one of two Spanish territories on the north African coast along with Melilla. Ceuta has been in Spanish possession since 1580. Christians and Muslims, Spanish and Moroccan residents and day workers all live there in relative harmony behind a border fence that many migrants from across Africa attempt to cross or swim around for a better life in Europe. Tuchel: '99.9% of fans don't speak about substitutions'
A blunt question for Tuchel to field. He's asked what happened in his meetings with his bosses at the FA?
The German says with a glint in his eye: "I got told off, of course, for the substitutions that I made. Just joking." And then the full reveal...
We had several reviews, which is good to do, which is a bit unusual to do because you don't have the time in club football to do so many reviews, but it's a part of moving forward and it's a part to learn. It's sometimes painful, sometimes emotional, sometimes beautiful, because people speak up and they speak about situations, about moments that you had not so high on your priority list that meant the world to them, so it's beautiful.
I can just tell you very honestly that I felt the full support from all my bosses through the World Cup, through the whole tournament. There was a moment, a very special moment, before the Mexico game where we had a talk and my bosses told me, 'we know that anything can happen in a football match and we know that anything can happen in a match in Mexico, in a World Cup match, but don't worry, we see what's going on and we want to continue like this'.
This was a beautiful moment for me as a coach and I felt this kind of freedom to take my choices and to just coach freely and there was never a doubt in my mind that we will continue together and there was obviously never a doubt in their mind.
We have critical reviews that will stay, of course, behind closed doors. I am the biggest critic of myself and reflection is sometimes necessary to get better and experience is a crucial part of coaching, so this is where we are.
I tell you something, 99.9% of the fans don't speak about substitutions, tactical twists and tactical things. They speak about where they have watched the Mexico game, they speak about the second half, the energy of the Croatia match, they speak about how nerve-wracking it was to watch our match against Congo and where they celebrated. This is the feedback that I get and it's 99.9% and I think this is what we created and this is how it should be.
This is what a World Cup is for, to connect with the people and to give them experiences that they will not forget. This is the feedback that I get. It makes me proud, it makes me happy because we share, even if it's just in the cab or in the coffee place or in the supermarket, you share these little moments and you understand that it actually meant something and that what the players built was transmitted and this is what we want to build.
