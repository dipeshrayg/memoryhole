Going out: Cinema
Wicker
Out now
Anyone who has ever fantasised about having a husband made out of wicker (surely a universal dream) will want to get themselves along to this Olivia Colman romantasy about a loner fisher who does exactly that. With Alexander Skarsgård, Richard E Grant, Peter Dinklage and Elizabeth Debicki.
Nightborn
Out now
Seidi Haarla gives a wonderfully unhinged performance as a woman convinced there is something wrong with her newborn baby: the hellish crying, her bleeding nipples ... surely this child is a monster? All will be revealed in this tense but humorous supernatural drama, with Rupert Grint having fun as the sceptical new father.
Primetime
Out now
Robert Pattinson continues a recent hot streak with a characterful turn as Chris Hansen, the TV personality famous for fronting To Catch a Predator, the US hit that lured and publicly exposed men who were interested in having sex with teenagers. Directed by Lance Oppenheim (also on a hot streak with You Can See Everything).
I Love Boosters
Out now
Shoplifting, teleportation, the consequences of sweatshop labour, designer fashion, workers' rights and demons who suck out your soul while performing oral sex: here's a comedy from Boots Riley that has it all. Keke Palmer, Naomi Ackie and Taylour Paige star as Bay Area shoplifters the Velvet Gang, with Demi Moore as a powerful designer. Catherine Bray
Going out: Gigs
Pitchfork music festival London
Various venues, 2 to 9 November
The always divisive music website returns with another city festival lineup featuring the best in alternative soundscapes. Highlights include the Avalanches, Olof Dreijer, Jacob Alon and Keiyaa, plus Nigel Godrich's new project Dragonflies, while the best band name goes to Psychedelic Porn Crumpets. Michael Cragg
Papa Roach
1 to 9 November; tour starts Glasgow
Twenty-six years after their breakthrough with second album Infest, the perpetually angry nu-metallers are still going strong. Expect a heavy focus on that album - Last Resort is pretty undeniable in a live scenario - and follow-up Lovehatetragedy on this huge arena tour. MC
Guildhall School's Opera Double Bill
Silk Street theatre, London, 2 to 9 November
Students on one of the country's leading conservatoire opera courses perform two mid-century works: the UK premiere of Gian Carlo Menotti's comic one-acter Amelia Al Ballo and Bernstein's jazz-soaked modern classic Trouble in Tahiti. Expect high-energy professionalism and a chance to pick out the opera stars of the future. Flora Willson
Fergus McCreadie
The Glasshouse, Sage Two, Gateshead, 5 November
Since 2021, young pianist/composer Fergus McCreadie has grown in stature within the restless evolution of contemporary global jazz as a Mercury prize nominee, the traditional folk music of his Scottish homeland, and at the cutting edge of chamber music. The Glasshouse's Innovators programme and Chamber Music Scotland showcase this adventurous wildlife-inspired co‐commission. John Fordham
Going out: Art
Winston Taylor Photo Portrait prize
National Portrait Gallery, London, 5 November to 17 January
The biggest photographic portraiture prize in the country has rebranded - you might know it better as the Taylor Wessing - but is otherwise unchanged: expect the finest photography by established pros and amateurs alike, featuring work from James Clifford Kent's long-term project Yuma, including his portrait of aspiring pentathlete Neisser Yoel Gálvez Tabares.
Mad Bloomsbury
Charleston Firle, Lewes, 4 November to 11 April
Coinciding with the centenary of Virginia Woolf's essay On Being Ill, this show looks at how the Bloomsbury Group - the likes of Roger Fry, Vanessa Bell and Duncan Grant - dealt with ideas of madness, disability and neurodiversity.
Steve McQueen: Resistance
Leeds Art Gallery, 6 November to 25 April
Deeply moving documentation of how acts of resistance and rebellion have transformed the UK, all conceived by the brilliant Turner prize-winning artist and Oscar-winning director Steve McQueen.
The Nue Black Aesthetic
Design Museum, London, 6 November to 8 August
This display of more than 100 objects celebrates the black creatives who are shaping the future of design in Britain, including Taiba Akhuetie, Ini Archibong and Tejumola Adenuga. Eddy Frankel
Going out: Stage
Jordan Brookes
Touring to 27 November; starts Bristol
In 2019, the Surrey-raised standup expressed his long-held ambition to become "the existential Michael McIntyre". In his excellent new show, The Part of You That's Always Screaming, Brookes reaches those heights, with an unlikely combination of laugh-out-loud observational comedy and genuinely terrifying evocations of the abyss. Rachel Aroesti
Peanut Butter and Blueberries
Leeds Playhouse, to 7 November
After opening to strong reviews at Kiln theatre, Suhaiymah Manzoor-Khan's thoughtful romcom transfers to Leeds. It's about two university students and practising Muslims who move to London from the north, fall in love - but come up against harsh realities and rumbling racism. Miriam Gillinson
Hamlet Hail to the Thief
Barbican theatre, London, to 23 January
Steven Hoggett and Christine Jones's music- and movement-infused Hamlet lands in London. The backbone of the show is Radiohead's 2003 album Hail to the Thief, which frontman Thom Yorke has stripped back, rewired and threaded through the show with electrifying intent. MG
Luail: Nōs
The Mac, Belfast, to 7 November
Only formed two years ago, Luail is Ireland's all-island national dance company, spanning Northern Ireland and the Republic. Their triple bill includes works by Belfast's own Oona Doherty, Liz Roche and a classic from Akram Khan. Lyndsey Winship
Staying in: Streaming
Yaga
Sky Atlantic, 31 October, 9pm
Hudson Williams swaps romance for folklore fantasy as he follows his star-making turn in Heated Rivalry by playing two characters - the missing scion of a wealthy family and his odd housemate - in Kat Sandler's adaptation of her own play. Could a witchy professor be the key to it all?
The Rise of the Rockstar Chef
BBC Two/iPlayer, 5 November, 9pm
The collective yearning for 2000s monoculture continues to be stoked with this documentary series about three of the decade's rule-breaking, gamechanging chefs: Marco Pierre White, Heston Blumenthal and Gordon Ramsay (whose own production company is behind the show). Catnip for 00s nostalgists.
The Greatest
Prime Video, 4 November
Boxing dramas are a Hollywood mainstay, but this year the genre is migrating to the small screen with the likes of Fightland, A Thousand Blows and this new Muhammad Ali biopic, whose first season chronicles the vertiginous early 1960s rise of the then Cassius Clay (Jaalen Best).
Allies in Exile
BBC Four/iPlayer, 3 November, 10pm
BBC Four's Storyville strand has long brought the world's best feature-length documentaries to television. Now briefer films will be showcased too, starting with this one following the arduous journey of two Syrian film-makers through the UK's asylum system. An Unknown Substance, about the poisoning of a Russian politician, airs afterwards. RA
Staying in: Games
The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time
Switch 2; out 5 November
The only major game challenging GTA 6 this month is this lively, stylish remake of Nintendo's 1998 adventure classic, in which series hero Link becomes a time traveller to save the land of Hyrule from evil warlock Ganondorf. A nostalgic treat for the ages.
Where the Seeds Fall
PS5, Switch 2, PC; out 5 November
An intriguing action adventure which follows the human inhabitants of a vast tree as they set out to descend its branches and rediscover the land they were once forced to flee. Part mystical role-playing game, part climbing challenge. Keith Stuart
Staying in: Albums
Ex:Re - Save As
Out now
With her band Daughter currently between albums, Elena Tonra returns to her Ex:Re solo moniker via this second album of textural alt-rock. Songs such as Sleep creep along with a hint of menace, while opener Radio Station spins off into a trippy road trip anthem.
DIIV - Zirp!
Out now
American alt-rock curios DIIV return with this AG Cook-produced fifth album. While steeped in their typically opaque, shoegazey textures, songs such as the pop-adjacent Kid and chunky lead single The Fountain dial up the melodies and frontman Zachary Cole Smith's hushed vocals.
Chloe Qisha - Fantasize
Out now
After quitting a psychology degree for pop stardom, Malaysia-born, UK-based singer-songwriter Qisha found instant success with 2024's sultry I Lied, I'm Sorry. She increases the sex chat on this debut album, specifically on gloriously overblown banger YDH, AKA young, dumb and horny.
The Big Moon - Forever
Out now
London quartet the Big Moon return with a sunnier take on their brand of indie rock. Created as an imaginary utopia after a period of upheaval in frontwoman Juliette Jackson's life, songs such as the buoyant Three Things and Fun offer sweet solace. MC
Staying in: Brain food
Provocations
Podcast
For the numerically challenged, this new series hosted by Nihal Arthanayake is essential listening. Experts break down the big stories in finance, such as changes in lending policies and how technology can affect banking.
Interior Motives
YouTube
On the surface this video series looks like a flippant guessing game pairing pictures of domestic interiors with their owners, but it in fact uncovers fascinating truths about the ways our homes reveal our hidden selves.
Concorde: The Untold Story
PBS America, 2 November, 8.40pm
Encompassing everything from engineering breakthroughs to espionage and political hand-wringing, this film on the race to build the first supersonic airliner between Europe, the Soviet Union and the US makes for surprisingly engaging viewing. Ammar Kalia
