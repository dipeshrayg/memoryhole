Aston Villa were quite happy to sell Emiliano Martínez but many feared it would be difficult to find an upgrade on the World Cup-winning goalkeeper. They have seemingly done so in the form of the £30m signing Zion Suzuki, who was superb against Manchester City. Nicolas Jackson's goals stole the headlines but Suzuki's bounty of saves ensured that Villa were victorious, while also impressing with his distribution. "He's ambitious. I think it's very important, his mentality," Unai Emery said. "Of course, we need a keeper first to save us in the goal, but as well we need one keeper to try to help us in our buildup. And in buildup, he's a really fantastic player because he can play short, he can understand where is the superiority to break the opponent's press. He has qualities to do long ball, getting our players in attack. So, we are very, very impressed how he adapted so quick with us." WU

Mikel Arteta says he has been compiling a dossier of evidence of other managers straying outside the technical area after the Arsenal manager was shown a yellow card during the 2-0 win over Everton. It was his second of the season and leaves the Spaniard at risk of suspension if he gets one more, although Arteta suggested he is by no means the worst offender. "There are managers who have the record in the league, 36 times in 10 minutes [they] leave the touchline - no yellows," he said, naming no names. "I am clipping all of that so I now have a massive storage of images, so if somebody comes to me I can say: 'I am not in the record yet.' Somebody else has the record so let's see. Then it's 36 yellow cards in 10 minutes for the managers. I don't know what we are going to do - let's see." Ed Aarons
Nico González cost Manchester City £50m when they signed him early in 2025, as part of that January spree as Pep Guardiola tried to arrest the first real slump of his Premier League career. Although he started nine league games that season and 17 last, González made very little impact at the Etihad. He probably wasn't even the best Nico González in world football, given the Argentinian winger's performances for Juventus and Atlético. But the Spanish Nico González clearly is a player of substance, his calm in midfield one of the few undoubted positives for Newcastle so far this season. Only Lewis Hall has made more tackles per game than him, he set up Sven Botman's goal at Coventry, while he's made more key passes per game than anybody other than Harvey Barnes. At £48m, he may end up representing extremely good value. Jonathan Wilson

In spectacular style, Oscar Bobb's wait for a first Premier League goal for Fulham came to an end at Coventry. Bobb was signed in January, fitting the recruitment template that brought success and stability under Marco Silva, a manager adept at regenerating talent. Social media footage from the away end showed Fulham fans ask what Bobb, weaving and bobbing, was doing only for him to feint past two Coventry defenders and score. That broke the deadlock when Coventry appeared fully capable of claiming a point or better. It remains to be seen whether Álvaro Arbeloa possesses the some restorative powers as Silva. After two wins in succession, two clean sheets and a first win away from Craven Cottage since February, early-season panic has dissipated. For now. If survival is Fulham's first objective, then successive wins over the promoted teams Hull and Coventry were minimum requirements. Though should Fulham aim much higher? Silva departed because his ambitions were not met. John Brewin

With Nottingham Forest the Premier League's joint-lowest scorers, the pressure on Liam Delap is increasing. At Ipswich, where he was a rare bright spot with 12 goals in the relegation season of 2024-25, Delap was booed on every touch. The jeers got louder when each of his four attempts on goal missed their target. Only three of 18 Premier League shots in a Forest shirt have been on target. The raw materials that Chelsea moved for in summer 2025 remain present, the physical prowess and ability to find promising positions. The finishing touches are, at present, lacking, when Forest need someone to start scoring. Failing to take their chances at Portman Road left Forest in the relegation zone, endangering Oliver Glasner, when the ownership is trigger-happy with managers. Chris Wood, previously Forest's talisman, came on in the 89th minute for Delap, but has been no super-sub so far, without a goal or assist from seven cameo league appearances. JB
