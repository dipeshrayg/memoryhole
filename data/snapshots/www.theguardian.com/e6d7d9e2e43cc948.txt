Boom in weight-loss drugs could trigger drop in beer sales, Heineken says
Mark Sweney
Heineken has warned British bars and pubs that the boom in popularity of weight-loss drugs could lead to a fall in sales of beer and cider.
The world's second largest brewer sent a presentation to British hospitality customers earlier this month warning that GLP-1 use - led by Ozempic and Wegovy - can reduce the desire to drink alcohol, which could lead to "fullness and fewer serves" of pints in pubs and bars.
The document, revealed by Reuters, warned that the GLP-1 era could lead to the tradition of the multi-pint drinking session becoming a less frequent occurrence.
The Netherlands-headquartered company offered advice including promoting half pints, low- and zero-alcohol options and pairing drinks with small plates of food, or risk declines in customer visits and spending.
GLP-1 use has soared as consumers seek a quick, easy solution to rapid weight loss, with 10 million people turning to medication in the US last year, according to research by JPMorgan.
Heineken, the world's second largest brewer after ABInBev, said the presentation was shared with customers as part of its regular engagement with UK businesses to help them understand how consumer habits are evolving.

UK consumer confidence falls at fastest rate since Covid as cost of living bites
Richard Partington
Consumer confidence in the UK has fallen at the fastest rate outside the Covid pandemic since the 2016 Brexit vote as the escalating economic damage from the war in the Middle East hits Britain.
With less than a week before JohnHealey's first budget, the latest barometer from the data company GfK indicated confidence plunged by 11 percentage points in October to -24 amid pressure on consumers from the rising cost of living.
Highlighting the challenge for AndyBurnham and his chancellor before the statement on Wednesday, GfK said its consumer confidence index last fell by this much in a single month in July 2016, following the shock leave result in the EU referendum.
The index - which has been closely tracked by the government and the Bank of England for early warning signs from the economy since the early 1970s - recorded its largest ever fall, of 25 points, in March 2020 just after the start of the first UK lockdown.
NeilBellamy, consumer insights director at GfK, said:
Relentless cost-of-living pressures, against an international backdrop of unresolved regional conflicts that are themselves contributing to domestic inflation now at 3.5%, appear to have swept aside the nascent optimism we saw following the appointment of a new UK prime minister this summer."
Gloomy picture for retailers in Christmas quarter, economists warn
Economists are warning there could be more gloom ahead for retailers as they enter into the crucial final quarter of the year - which includes Christmas.
Paul Dales, chief UK economist at Capital Economics, said:
October's sharp fall in consumer confidence and the dip in retail sales volumes in September show that times are getting tougher for households just as the all-important festive period for retailers is just around the corner.
In itself, the 0.4% m/m fall in retail sales volumes in September (consensus -0.1%, CE 0.0%) isn't bad as it followed a 0.7% m/m rise in August, which was revised up from the first estimate of 0.5% m/m. Admittedly, some of it was due to a 2.2% m/m rise in fuel sales as households topped up their tanks before fuel prices rose even further. They are unlikely to do the same in October. But equally, part of the 0.9% m/m fall in sales at clothing stores was due to the unusually mild weather limiting sales of the new autumn/winter ranges. Soft clothing sales also contributed to the 0.4% m/m drop in sales at supermarkets. Both may perform better in October. Either way, total sales rose by a decent 0.7% q/q in Q3 as a whole.
That all said, the plunge in the GfK measure of consumer confidence, from -13 in September to a six-month low of -24 in October, shows that rising fuel and utility prices, higher borrowing costs and the fear of tax rises in the budget are taking a bigger toll on households. On the face of it, that's consistent with the annual growth rate of retail sales volumes slowing from 0.8% in September to well below 0%.
This supports our view that both real consumer spending and real GDP will grind to a halt in Q4 and Q1. And this fall in consumer confidence is also the kind of thing that makes the prime minister less likely to call an early election soon.
Jacqueline Windsor, head of retail at PwC UK, noted that fashion retailers had fared the worst in September, despite the warm weather.
Although all other retail categories saw sales rise year-on-year, rising petrol prices meant that consumers had to divert more of their spending on filling up their cars - over 30% more than last September - meaning less to spend on other things.
So, while retail prices, and grocery prices in particular, have remained subdued thanks to industry competition and the delayed pass-through of cost increases due to the conflict in the Middle East, the higher-than-expected inflation reported earlier this week will inevitably translate into less in shoppers' pockets in the critical run-up to Christmas.
With most retailers' sales coming in the last three months of the year, the upcoming peak season remains on a knife edge. This time last year, uncertainty surrounding the late budget at the end of November held back spending in the preceding two months. While that makes this year's comparisons easier, retailers will be hoping that anything announced by the Chancellor this year does not add further to their cost of doing business nor dent consumers' sentiment, which already seems to have fallen from the highs at the end of summer."
