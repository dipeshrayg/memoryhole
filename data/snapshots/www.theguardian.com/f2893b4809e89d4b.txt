A stadium selfie could soon cost fans of German giants Borussia Dortmund after the club announced plans to reward those who leave their phones alone during the game.
Dortmund are offering signed jerseys and gift cards for club merchandise to fans who use an app which measures how often they unlock their phones at its stadium. Arguing that "more and more fans are annoyed by selfies, streaming, and social media in the stands," Dortmund say supporters can opt to use a "fan mode" in the club's app, starting Saturday with a home game against Eintracht Frankfurt. The rewards scheme and app are entirely voluntary and there's no ban on fans using their phones.
Fan mode will measure how often the phone is unlocked during the game - not counting the halftime break - and award points to people based on how little they use the device, with prizes on offer for those with the most points. Each time the phone is unlocked, the user loses points.
"Over 81,000 people in the stadium, singing, cheering, and holding their breath together: this here and now is irreplaceable," said Dortmund chief executive Carsten Cramer.
The club's stance is part of a backlash in German football against attempts to turn its stadium atmosphere into social media content, especially by influencers using professional camera equipment.
Hertha Berlin said this month it was banning "commercially produced and monetized" video productions from its games, while allowing fans to take photos and videos for personal use.
Another club, Dynamo Dresden, said in August it would ban commercial filming for social media, saying it wanted to ensure fans felt comfortable at the game and to protect the club's TV rights deals. Agence France-Presse
The ongoing security situation in Saudi Arabia makes it difficult for the country to host next year's Asian Cup as scheduled, according to Tsuneyasu Miyamoto, president of the Japanese Football Association.
Several major sporting events in Saudi Arabia have been cancelled or postponed amid escalating attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis, raising doubts over whether the continental soccer tournament can take place as scheduled in January and February next year.
"Based on what I've learnt from the news, the situation is still unsafe, so at this point, I don't think it would be easy to hold the AFC (Asian) Cup there," Miyamoto told reporters at the Asian Football Confederation's (AFC) extraordinary congress in Tokyo.
"I just had a meeting with the Saudi side and they said that their top priority is the safety of the players, the fans, and everyone who would be attending the tournament.
"They said they would work with the AFC to decide whether the tournament can go ahead, so they did mention that it would take some time (to make that decision)."
AFC chief Salman bin Ibrahim Al-Khalifa said last week a postponement until 2028 remained a possibility if necessary, but the local organising committee later said it was sticking to the original schedule. Reuters

Manchester United are inviting fans to offer their views via a survey on the design of the proposed new 100,000-seat stadium.
Colette Roche, United's CEO New Stadium Development, said: "Our proposed new stadium is one of the most significant projects Manchester United will undertake in the next 100 years, and it is vital that we shape it together with our fans. Above all else, this is a football stadium built for them, so getting the details right matters."
Supporters have until 6 November to take the survey, with subjects including: "creating a world-class football stadium and match-day experience for all fans"; "heritage and legacy"; "atmosphere"; "the wider campus"; and "getting to and from the stadium."
The survey will be sent to season ticket holders, cup season ticket holders, executive club members, official members, supporters who have purchased hospitality at Old Trafford within the past three years and season ticket waiting list members, a press release stated.
