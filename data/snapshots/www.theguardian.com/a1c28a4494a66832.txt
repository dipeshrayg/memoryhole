Greg Fisilau will make his first England start against the All Blacks on Saturday with Steve Borthwick making five changes to his side for this weekend's rematch with New Zealand, recalling Freddie Steward at full-back.
Fisilau, 23, has impressed in England's three victories to date as a replacement and earns his first start in the No 7 jersey with Ben Curry dropping to the bench. Elsewhere, there are starts for Tommy Freeman on the wing as well Ellis Genge and Luke Cowan-Dickie in the front row.
Borthwick's decision to start Fisilau means Henry Pollock remains on the replacements bench but it is a richly deserved start for the Exeter back-rower. He was singled out by the head coach for his cameo against Japan and has the opportunity on Saturday to further advance his claim for a place in next year's World Cup squad.
Steward comes back into the side at full-back with George Furbank dropping out of the 23-man squad. The Leicester No 15 replaced Furbank against Japan because the Harlequins full-back was struggling with a groin problem but Borthwick has given Steward the nod for tactical reasons this weekend.
Up front, Borthwick has opted to start Genge and Cowan-Dickie with Fin Baxter and Jamie George dropping to the bench. Freeman's recall, meanwhile, was expected after he was a late withdrawal from the 37-12 victory over the All Blacks last weekend with a thigh injury.
The rest of the squad is unchanged with Joe Heyes continuing at tighthead prop and the captain Maro Itoje partnering Alex Coles in the second row. Ollie Chessum continues at blindside flanker while Ben Earl is again at No 8 after his man-of-the-match performance last weekend.
Jack van Poortvliet stays at scrum-half with Fin Smith wearing the No 10 jersey while Seb Atkinson and Henry Slade stay at centre. Will Joseph makes his third consecutive start on the wing. On the bench, Will Stuart joins Baxter and George as front-row cover while George Martin is one of six forwards among the replacements. Archie McParland and Marcus Smith are the backs.
Borthwick's side are chasing a record third straight victory over the All Blacks as they bid to finish third in the inaugural Nations Championship. "To have the opportunity to play New Zealand again is something the players are really looking forward to," said Borthwick. "We know the quality New Zealand have and the challenge they'll bring across the field. For us, it's about building on last Saturday's performance, maintaining the standards we set and finding that same level again."
Meanwhile, South Africa have refused to send a coach to the Nations Championship finals weekend media day at Twickenham with only the captain Siya Kolisi expected to attend. With Rassie Erasmus serving a six-match suspension for his remarkable rant, in which he questioned whether officials were "match-fixing" this month, the Boks are the only one of the 12 competing nations not represented by a coach at the midweek event.
All nations taking part were obliged to send their coach and captain for a series of media appearances. It is understood that the Springboks will not be fined for their coaches boycotting the event before their contest the final against France on Sunday.
This week, the South Africa assistant coach Mzwandile Stick conducted a press conference in his mother tongue of Xhosa to avoid being misinterpreted. When speaking the week before, Stick made comments about the decision by a disciplinary panel to clear Sam Prendergast to play for Ireland against South Africa which could yet land him in hot water.
The fly-half was cited for a hip-drop tackle against Fiji but later cleared. Stick said: "If that was a Fijian player, he would be sitting in Fiji now doing some extra lessons of how to tackle. But it's an Irish player, it's free to play."
Erasmus's ban came after he claimed officials had missed 17 knee-drop tackles committed against his side in their defeat by France, suggesting that incidents of foul play are not being picked up in what he described as a "manipulation of the game" and that officials are showing more leniency to northern hemisphere teams.
When serving the first match of his ban Erasmus set up an 18+ watchalong in which supporters were able to view the head coach sitting in a Dublin hotel room while he watched their narrow defeat by Ireland. Erasmus has already named South Africa's side to face France but will be forced into a change with Pieter-Steph du Toit ruled out through injury.
