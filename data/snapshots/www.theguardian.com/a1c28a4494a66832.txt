Steve Borthwick has admitted he considers Henry Pollock most dangerous in his role of supersub after again leaving him on the bench against New Zealand and instead handing Greg Fisilau a surprise first start.
The 23-year-old Fisilau came into the autumn uncapped but has impressed off the bench in all three of England's victories and is rewarded with his first start as Borthwick's side bid for a third straight win over New Zealand for the first time.
Borthwick revealed Fisilau has been selected due to his physicality at the breakdown, having highlighted the All Blacks' ability to slow England's ball down last weekend, with Ben Curry dropping to the bench as one of five changes made by the head coach. Elsewhere, Tommy Freeman comes back on to the wing, Freddie Steward replaces George Furbank at full-back while Ellis Genge and Luke Cowan-Dickie get the nod in the front row.
Pollock, meanwhile, remains on the bench. He will win his 17th cap but has made just two starts for England to date - the disastrous Six Nations defeat by Ireland and the emphatic win over Japan earlier this month. Against Japan, Pollock impressed defensively but all eight of his England tries have come as a replacement, including two when deployed on the wing against Australia this November.
Citing the examples of Marcus Smith, who has emerged as Borthwick's favourite for the No 23 jersey as part of a 6-2 bench, and George Martin, Borthwick revealed he favours unleashing Pollock in the latter stages of matches when explaining why Fisilau had seemingly leapfrogged the Northampton player in the pecking order.
"I enjoy what Henry has done off the bench," said Borthwick. "Another one, George Martin, a number of us could say, 'George Martin has had so much impact, he could start'. It's not just Henry, there's a number of people. Marcus - every time Marcus comes on, how well does he play? Every time. These guys are doing such a good job at the end of games. They do such a good job in that latter third of the game.
"There is always the question of balance. The balance of how many changes, the balance of what changes. I talked about the breakdown, which is significant, and suits Greg's skill set. I also like Henry coming on and playing against tired defenders with a bit more space."
The Exeter back-row Fisilau, whose father, Keni, played for Tonga, was first called into England camp in late 2024 but has had to bide his time for a first cap until this autumn. Handed the No 7 jersey, however, he now has the chance to further advance his claims for a place in Borthwick's World Cup squad next year.
"Greg has been with the squad for a number of campaigns and didn't get an opportunity in Test match 23," added Borthwick. "And I think he's then seized his chance. It's interesting how opportunities come around, where a couple of knocks and bangs to a couple of players, and he's into the matchday 23.
"Tom Curry in that week one, when his hip tied up, opportunity arises and he [Fisilau] has grabbed his chance and taken it with both hands. I'm delighted for him, delighted for his family. I think I said to you before that his dad always said, until he plays for England, his dad's the best rugby player in the family.
"Well, Greg's now the best rugby player in the family, and I think he's very proud of it. I'm delighted for him. I've got an email from [Exeter's director of rugby] Rob Baxter just now, who's said the same thing. I'm just absolutely delighted for what Greg's done."
Borthwick's decision to omit Furbank from the matchday 23 is a surprise, given how impressive he has been against Australia - on his first England appearance for two years - and in the record victory against the All Blacks last weekend. Steward shone when starting against Japan, however.
"George Furbank has played very well and I thought he was very good last weekend," said Borthwick. "And at the same point in time, as I said, I want to develop a squad as well as a team.
"Freddie Steward's been playing really well. And I thought against Japan, I thought he played to a really high standard, did exactly what the team needed of him. And he keeps adding dimensions to his game. And so I was delighted to name him in the starting team."
