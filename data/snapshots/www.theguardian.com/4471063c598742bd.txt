Google Cloud outage: problems fixed for 'most' customers, as regulators push for details - as it happened
Google apologises after power issue causes a major Cloud outage in its London region today that led to problems at banks, Revolut, and court video cameras
A power outage at a Google Cloud data centre near London has caused serious failures to services today, affecting operations for several UK banks.
Problems were first detected just after 7am this morning, disrupting "multiple services" in Google's europe-west2 region (London).
Google blamed the problem on a 'power issue' - just afternoon today it said this event had been mitigated. By 1pm UK time services were recovering, and at 3pm Google said services were back online for most customers, with only "a very small number" still affected.
But by that time, millions of customers had been left unable to access and send cash at banks including Lloyds, HSBC and Revolut.
Revolut said:
"We are currently experiencing a service disruption affecting multiple services. We can confirm that all our customers' money and data are safe. We've been working to fix the issue since it was identified this morning and apologise to anyone affected and for any inconvenience caused."
A spokesperson for Lloyds added:
"Our app is online and customers can log in normally, though we understand some are having issues with certain online services this morning. We are aware of similar issues affecting other organisations and are monitoring the situation closely."
Legal proceedings were disrupted, because the video system used by defendants, lawyers and other parties to attend court hearings remotely across England and Wales which failed to work throughout the morning.
The City regulator, the FinancialConductAuthority, confirmed it was in touch with Google and seeking more information about the outage.
This outage will be the first major test for the FCA and BankofEngland, since the City watchdogs were handed powers to regulate Google and other rival services that have been supplying "critical" cloud services to UK financial firms this summer.
Tech experts said the outage was another reminder of the dangers of relying on a small number of technology providers for cloud services, a year after problems at Amazon Cloud caused more widespread disruption.
Reynolds: Chinese EV decision will depend on UK interests
Lisa O'Carroll
Business secretary Johnny Reynolds has insisted any decision to impose tariffs on Chinese EVs will be about British, not EU interests.
He told the business select committee that some were under the misapprehension that the EU wanted the UK to put tariffs on EVs because they feared the Chinese could use Britain as a "backdoor" to the bloc, circumventing trade defence measures including tariffs on EVs and quota on electric hybrid cars.
He point out that this Chinese cars exported from the UK "would not qualify" for duty free exports to the EU under the rules of origin clauses in the Brexit trade and cooperation agreement".
He was also asked about last week's Franco-German paper proposing that the UK would be included in the upcoming Made in Europe legislation on condition London aligned with EU trade defence measures.
But he said the "detail" of that proposal, which has yet to be adopted by the EU in any new drafting of the Industrial Accelerator Act, "has not been surfaced to the degree to which we'd be able to definitively say that that is what the ask is of the UK."

UK regulators want answers from Google about Cloud outage
Kalyeena Makortoff
The Financial Conduct Authority and Bank of England are pushing Google for details of today's cloud services outage, as they face their first major test as new regulators of "critical" tech suppliers to UK banks.
Both the FCA and BankofEngland were handed powers in July to start directly overseeing the local arms of Google Cloud and three other Silicon Valley rivals - Amazon Web Services, Oracle and Microsoft - after being designated as critical third parties by the UK government, as their services become a crucial part of increasingly digital banking services.
A spokesperson for the FCA said they were in touch with Google and seeking more information about the outage. The Bank of England's Prudential Regulation Authority is also understood to be speaking with the tech firm about the service outage which began this morning (details start here).
The watchdogs are in charge of ensuring that the cloud providers are resilient and actively reducing the risk of cyber-attacks and major outages like the one experienced today, which has disrupted services for millions of people and businesses across the UK.
For its part, Google is meant to prove it is carrying out proper stress testing, including how it would respond to imagined emergency scenarios that would put their operations under severe strain. They're also meant to have reported any major incidents, including this power outages, to the two regulators (though it's unclear in what time frame).

Newsflash: Google says the 'power event' which affected its London-based cloud computing operating and caused widepread disruption to services in the UK has been 'mitigated'.
In a new statement on its Service Health page, Google reports seeing signs that services are being restored.
It says:
A power event caused an outage within europe-west2-b zone. The power event is mitigated. We are seeing the recovery of services and are prioritizing the recovery of existing workloads.Customers may not be able to yet create new workloads.
Full recovery of existing workloads should be completed, tentatively by Tuesday, 2026-10-20 06:00 [Pacific Daylight Time].
The Google Cloud outage could result in some companies losing sales, and they might struggle to get much compensation, suggests Ryan Gracey, partner at law firm Gordons.
"Many businesses will be discovering today that their cloud contracts protect the supplier far more than they protect the customer.
"When a provider goes down, the usual remedy is a modest credit against future fees. That is small consolation set against lost revenue, frustrated customers and damage to reputation.
"Liability for those wider losses is typically capped or excluded altogether. The real exposure sits in the gap between how heavily a business depends on its provider and how little its contract actually guarantees. For banks, the stakes are higher still. Regulators expect them to keep essential services running even when a key supplier fails.
"Outsourcing a service does not mean outsourcing responsibility for it. Boards should be asking, not just what their contracts promise, but whether the business could keep going if its provider went dark for a day."

PA: Court hearings delayed as nationwide video system goes down
The video system used by courts across England and Wales went down on Tuesday in an issue believed to be linked to the wider Google outage, the Press Association report.
The Cloud Video Platform (CVP), which is used by defendants, lawyers and other parties to attend court hearings remotely, was not functioning throughout the morning.
Users expecting to attend court on video link were met with a message saying there was an internal server error.
The issue is expected to have left many hearings across the country delayed or unable to go ahead as the system is often used for defendants who are in custody to attend court.
The judge in the Russell Brand trial, Mr Justice Bennathan, decided to press on with proceedings at Southwark Crown Court on Tuesday morning despite the link being broken.
Some journalists who would have been watching online were squeezed into extra seats in court, but many were not able to follow the proceedings as the fourth complainant started her evidence.
Staff at the court said there was a national outage and did not know when it would be resolved.
It is understood to be linked to an outage affecting Google's Cloud network, which affected businesses including online bank Revolut, delivery service DPD and Royal Mail.
The nature of cloud ecosystems mean that a problem in one region can ripple out and cause widesread disruption.
Dr Amro Al-Said Ahmad, lecturer in computer science at KeeleUniversity, explains:
"Another outage, another reminder of the fragility of cloud infrastructure. Google Cloud is currently experiencing a major incident affecting the europe-west2 (London) region, with elevated errors and intermittent network failures impacting more than 38 cloud-native services across key categories including Compute & Containers, Storage & Databases, Analytics & Data Processing, Networking & Connectivity, Messaging & Streaming, Caching & Memory Services, and AI/Platform Services.
"Google Engineers are actively investigating the issue and testing mitigations, including moving impacted workloads to alternative regions where feasible. The disruption has also affected several major organisations and financial institutions that rely on the affected region.
"More importantly, the incident highlights the dependency effect of modern cloud ecosystems. When a core cloud region experiences failures, the impact extends far beyond the directly affected services. Applications, APIs, databases, authentication systems, payment platforms, analytics pipelines, and third-party SaaS products that depend on those services can all experience cascading failures.
"A problem in a single cloud region can therefore propagate through an entire digital supply chain, affecting organisations that are several layers removed from the original outage. While this is a regional outage centred on europe-west2, the breadth of affected services highlights a key cloud engineering lesson: resilience is not achieved by simply moving to the cloud. True resilience requires designing for regional failures through multi-region deployments, workload portability, and tested disaster recovery procedures."
