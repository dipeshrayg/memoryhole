Newsflash: Google's cloud computing services have been hit by technical problems in Europe.
Google reports that "multiple Google Cloud services are experiencing elevated errors and intermittent network failures in europe-west2 region", and it does not currently have a work around.
Incident affecting BigQuery Data Transfer Service, Cloud Filestore, Cloud Run, Google App Engine, Google Cloud Dataflow, Google Cloud NetApp Volumes, Google Cloud Pub/Sub, Google Cloud Storage, Google Compute Engine, Google Kubernetes Engine, Looker (Google Cloud core), Managed Airflow, Managed Service for Apache Kafka, Persistent Disk, Storage Transfer Service
The problem is hurting some fintech companies, and banks.
The nature of cloud ecosystems mean that a problem in one region can ripple out and cause widesread disruption.
Dr Amro Al-Said Ahmad, lecturer in computer science at KeeleUniversity, explains:
"Another outage, another reminder of the fragility of cloud infrastructure. Google Cloud is currently experiencing a major incident affecting the europe-west2 (London) region, with elevated errors and intermittent network failures impacting more than 38 cloud-native services across key categories including Compute & Containers, Storage & Databases, Analytics & Data Processing, Networking & Connectivity, Messaging & Streaming, Caching & Memory Services, and AI/Platform Services.
"Google Engineers are actively investigating the issue and testing mitigations, including moving impacted workloads to alternative regions where feasible. The disruption has also affected several major organisations and financial institutions that rely on the affected region.
"More importantly, the incident highlights the dependency effect of modern cloud ecosystems. When a core cloud region experiences failures, the impact extends far beyond the directly affected services. Applications, APIs, databases, authentication systems, payment platforms, analytics pipelines, and third-party SaaS products that depend on those services can all experience cascading failures.
"A problem in a single cloud region can therefore propagate through an entire digital supply chain, affecting organisations that are several layers removed from the original outage. While this is a regional outage centred on europe-west2, the breadth of affected services highlights a key cloud engineering lesson: resilience is not achieved by simply moving to the cloud. True resilience requires designing for regional failures through multi-region deployments, workload portability, and tested disaster recovery procedures."
A Lloyds spokesperson has confirmed the bank (which includes Halifax) has experienced problems today:
"Our app is online and customers can log in normally, though we understand some are having issues with certain online services this morning. We are aware of similar issues affecting other organisations and are monitoring the situation closely."
Now, as then, it's a reminder of the perils of relying on a small number of companies for operating the global internet, especially as more and more services have been moved onto the cloud.
Jake Moore, Global Cybersecurity Adviser at ESET, Europe's leading cybersecurity company:
"Today's outages highlight just how much dependence we put on a handful of technology giants to keep our digital lives running. With only a small number of major cloud providers to choose from, the knock-on effects can be catastrophic if something goes wrong and can leave millions of people unable to use everyday services.
"Cloud services are a huge point of failure and will always be a potential weakness in digital infrastructures. They could even become a potential target on such services by cybercriminals. Businesses therefore need to plan for when these services go down and have strict contingencies in place for this unfortunate inevitable event."
