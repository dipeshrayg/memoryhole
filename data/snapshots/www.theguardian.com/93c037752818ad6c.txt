This week the world's top 400 rugby players will be assembling in the same city. You can only hope London's steak restaurants and cafes are braced for the simultaneous influx of an army of huge men with even larger appetites. If tall buildings start to sway in the Twickenham area, you will know the buildup to this weekend's Nations Championship finals is intensifying.
Which begs a timely question. Mirror, mirror on the wall, who's the most valuable of them all? With this week billed as a north v south duel - there is even a trophy on offer to the champion hemisphere - it is not a bad moment to sit down with pen and paper and play fantasy selector. Name two all-star XVs to represent north and south respectively. And which of them would win if they went head to head?
A subjective exercise, clearly, but an instructive one too. Particularly after the last couple of weekends when South Africa and New Zealand have found themselves under the cosh. If you were picking a northern hemisphere team based on this month there would be no shortage of Welsh, English and Irish representatives, albeit with a few French accents mixed in.
Assuming everyone is fit, who gets the nod? It is no easy task. Consider the competition on the right wing, for example. Louis Rees-Zammit, Manny Feyi-Waboso or Tommy Freeman? And that doesn't even include Ireland's Robert Baloucoune or France's Theo Attissogbe. Gone are the days when the Six Nations was perceived as lacking the same attacking firepower as the Rugby Championship.
It is a similar story at scrum-half. Yes, Antoine Dupont is freakishly good but what about Maxime Lucu, Baptiste Serin, Tomos Williams and Jamison Gibson-Park? While New Zealand's head coach, Dave Rennie, acknowledged his side were "miles off" during last weekend's record loss to England, he also stressed the rising quality of the opposition they are facing in Europe. As Rennie muttered: "I've often said there's a lot of good teams up here."
Similarly, the south is awash with choice in certain positions. Take loose forward where two of Pieter-Steph du Toit, Siya Kolisi, Jasper Wiese, Ardie Savea and Fraser McReight have to miss out. And that's before the claims of Argentina's Pablo Matera, Joaquín Oviedo and Marcos Kremer are considered, let alone Paul de Villiers, Cameron Hanekom, Australia's Rob Valetini and New Zealand's Peter Lakai.
All you can do is tick off the certainties. Will Jordan and Cam Roigard may have had a tough day at Twickenham on Saturday but class is permanent. Ditto the Springbok front row, regardless of whether they start or finish. Damian de Allende is not going to weave many pretty midfield patterns but he might just be among the most consistently important cogs, along with Marx and Du Toit, in Rassie Erasmus's World Cup-winning machine.
Cheslin Kolbe's remarkable record also demands respect but do Jordie Barrett, just shortlisted for World Rugby player of the year, and his brother Beauden still automatically make the cut? Not this time with Australia's Len Ikitau slotting in alongside De Allende and Argentina's playmaker, Tomás Albornoz, just nipping in ahead of Damian McKenzie. Hopefully there will be space for around 20 replacements each on the bench ...
The north XV also involves a raft of tough decisions. Judging by the weekend just gone, the entire English backline are suddenly contenders with George Furbank, Henry Slade and Seb Atkinson all further enhancing their reputations. How can Stuart McCloskey or Mack Hansen not be in there? Where are the brilliant Finn Russell and his fellow Scottish Lion, Huw Jones? On current form, Wales's Eddie James and Blair Murray would not look out of place either.
Only 15 individuals can start, though, and some of those selections are non-negotiable. Matthieu Jalibert has to be Dupont's half-back partner and Louis Bielle-Biarrey is another dead cert on the left wing. All three also make it into the Breakdown's combined World XV alongside their inventive compatriot Thomas Ramos, who also happens to be the planet's finest goalkicker.
Another area of northern strength is the second row; when it comes to lineout forwards they are especially well stocked. Maro Itoje is back at full throttle and Ollie Chessum, at his rip-snorting best, is also fast emerging as World XV material. Throw in the redoubtable Joe Heyes at tighthead and England's developing pack is further represented, with Tom Curry in reserve.
Plenty of other versatile forwards clearly merit consideration - Tadhg Beirne, Caelan Doris, Nick Timoney, Aaron Wainwright and Cameron Woki, to name but five - but Wales's Jac Morgan has to be in and so, too, at hooker does Dan Sheehan, just ahead of Dewi Lake. No 8? Charles Ollivon and Gregory Aldritt are both outstanding but Ben Earl's colossal effort against the All Blacks cannot be ignored.
Which leaves one last decision: who should be the head coaches? Let's assume Rassie Erasmus has served his latest ban, and invite him to show his knack of uniting South Africans behind the Springboks can be replicated elsewhere. And the north? It has to be Andy Farrell, who got his Ireland selections and tactics spot on for the South Africa showdown. As a series-winning British & Irish Lions coach, he is good in these situations. Could he be the catalyst for a northern triumph? Either way, it is a good way of passing the time while all those steaks are grilling.

World XV: Thomas Ramos (Fra); Will Jordan (NZ), Tommaso Menoncello (Ita), Damian de Allende (SA), Louis Bielle-Biarrey (Fra); Matthieu Jalibert (Fr), Antoine Dupont (Fra, capt); Ox Nché (SA), Malcolm Marx (SA), Wilco Louw (SA), Maro Itoje (Eng), Ollie Chessum (Eng), Pieter-Steph du Toit (SA), Siya Kolisi (SA, capt), Ardie Savea (NZ).
