Introduction: Nvidia, down $430bn, hires major US law firm to investigate alleged Chinese spy affair
Good morning, and welcome to our rolling coverage of business, the financial markets, and the world economy.
The crisis gripping Nvidia is gathering pace, with around $430bn wiped off the value of the world's most valuable chip company since it was hit by a spying scandal.
Overnight, Nvidia announced it has hired a former top federal prosecutor to help probe chief executive Jensen Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy.
The company's board have retained Joon H. Kim, the former acting U.S. Attorney of the Southern District of New York, and his firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct "a thorough and independent investigation".
Wachtell is a heavyweight corporate law firm. In 2022 it was hired by Twitter to force Elon Musk to complete his $44bn takeover, for example, and has a track record of advising major companies on issues such as mergers and corporate governance.
It will now look into the circumstances of Huang's affair - thought to be with AliceHuang, who US officials believe is a Chinese spy, and who worked for Nvidia customer Megaspeed.
Although Beijing has denied allegations that it operated an espionage operation targeting JensenHuang, Nvidia's value has been buffeted this week.
Yesterday, its shares fell by almost 5.5%, adding to the 4.27% drop on Monday. This has pulled the company's value down to $4.07tn, from $4.50tn before the story broke last weekend.

Taylor Wimpey, one of Britain's biggest housebuilders, has cut its outlook for home completions for a second time this year and blamed rising mortgage and living costs - another sign of a weakening housing market.
The government's 1.5m new homes target over five years looks even more out of reach, as the company joined others in warning of a "subdued" autumn selling season. It comes after Britain's biggest brickmaker, Ibstock, issued a profit warning last week.
Taylor Wimpey now expects to complete 10,500 homes this year, excluding joint ventures, below its previous forecast of 10,600 to 10,800 homes. Adjusted operating profit is expected to be at the lower end of its £262m to £299m range. The firm also reduced its outlook in July.
Its order book declined to 6,839 homes as of 8 November, versus 7,253 a year earlier. TaylorWimpey has also been hit by higher build cost inflation, running at 3% to 4%, due to knock-on effects from the Iran war.
JennieDaly, the chief executive, said:
"The autumn selling season has been more subdued, as higher mortgage rates and living costs continue to reduce customer affordability."
Its share price fell 4.5% in early trading and is now 3.5% lower, and dragged down rival Persimmon, which is trading 1% lower.
The Bank of England raised its base rate last week, from 3.75% to 4%, which hit mortgage borrowers but will benefit savers. The Bank's governor, Andrew Bailey, said another hike could be needed soon, but this (partly) depends on a resolution of the Iran war and the consequent impact on energy prices and inflation.
To entice buyers, TaylorWimpey is offering more incentives than usual, for example a deposit top-up and one-off mortgage contribution. And it has cut its selling prices by 2.5% year on year.
It said it is well positioned to participate in the government's new Your First Home scheme aimed at first-time buyers. But the scheme won't launch until April, and experts have warned that the market could remain sluggish until then.
