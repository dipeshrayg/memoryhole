Australia will come back tomorrow with a lead of 139 runs and with ten second innings wickets in hand. They hold all the cards in this Test match but they can't control when the sun sets.
We're off five minutes later than yesterday but it is indeed STUMPS now on day three in Durban.
"Hello James, sorry if you have answered my question many times before. I live in Maputo, Mozambique, about 450 km north of Durban. I reckon that light would be good enough for cricket until about 5pm here. Not long past the equinox, I would expect daylight to be moving in Durban's favour.
Please elucidate."
I can neither elucidate or illuminate Gary Moors. Here in the UK the light lasts longer the further north you go, something to do with the axial tilt? (Hark at me) What I know for certain is that we have now lost over 60 overs of this Test match to bad light.

Sure enough the sun dips behind a blanket of dark cloud and the umpires decide it is too dark to continue. That could well be it, another light curtailed day in Durban. Madness.

Just as the partnership was building into something significant Maharaj tosses it away with a lame plink to Cummins at mid off. He can't believe he has got out in such a fashion after grafting for over an hour. The deficit is 141 and out comes South Africa's last man - Anrich Nortje.
69th over: South Africa 247-8 (Harmer 17, Maharaj 32) Now then! Maharaj flicks Hazlewood off his hip for SIX! He timed the undercrackers off that one. 68th over: South Africa 238-8 (Harmer 17, Maharaj 23) Starc starts with a maiden from around the wicket. There's a hint of reverse swing as Harmer is beaten on the outside edge. Josh Hazlewood is back from the other end, the Labuschagne and Head experiments are over for now, If South Africa see them again in this innings then they'll have done mightily well.
Here come the players for the final session. Here comes John Starbuck with a pertinent question:
"Given that (probably) for the third day in a row they have to go off early because of bad light, why don 't the adminstators start the day's play much earlier to help make up time? It happens in other places, so why not Durban?"
I suspect it comes down to pre-arranged broadcast times/rights, thankfully is is brighter out there now than it was at this time yesterday and the day before.
The bowling quartet of Cummins, Hazlewood, Lyon and Starc has moved past 600 wickets today, now 602, the most by any quartet in all Tests, in the 38 Tests where all four have played. Second on the list is Moeen, Anderson, Broad and Stokes, 415 wickets in 31 Tests together."
That's why they call them the fab four. Toppermost of the poppermost.
