I don't think I'm being cheeky when I say that passing is the most exciting part of football. A solid hit is nice, and a big run can thrill you, but this is a quarterback-centric game. When a QB is bad, teams usually lose and fans get bored. So, Sunday's performance by Baker Mayfield and the Tampa Bay Buccaneers had me positively gobsmacked. I wouldn't say their 16-0 win over the Atlanta Falcons was thrill-a-minute, but it certainly was historic. By completing a grand total of zero passes out of a paltry three attempts, Baker Mayfield led the Bucs to the first game with no passing offense since the Buffalo Bills beat the New York Jets 16-12 on 29 September 1974. If you are miraculously old enough to remember the NFL before the advent of the forward pass in 1906, first off, congratulations on being over 120 years of age. Please email me your full diet and exercise regimen. You're probably on your feet (if standing is still something physically possible), cheering a bit of hard-nosed, classic football. The rest of us young whippersnappers still grinding through our first century are holding our noses.
Of course, Mayfield - a former No 1 overall pick - can throw. As recently as 2024 he threw for 41 touchdowns and 4,500 passing yards in a season. But Sunday's game was played in pouring rain that led to farcical moments like this, and Tampa Bay clearly decided that the ground game was the only way to go. That the Bucs could score at all, let alone 16 points, is a credit to Bucky Irving and Kenneth Gainwell running as though their lives depended on it. Irving finished the day with 170 rushing yards and one touchdown, while Gainwell had 140. Combined with Mayfield, they rushed 56 times for 324 yards - the most ever in a single game for the Bucs. They more than made up for Mayfield's borderline irrelevance in the pocket. Mayfield made himself useful blocking for his runners, but otherwise, I probably could have started at QB for the Buccaneers and the game would have been the same, other than the high likelihood of me suffering at least one gruesome injury.
"If I'm not going to throw the ball, I have to get active somehow," Mayfield said after the game. He certainly was more valuable for his team than Falcons quarterback Michael Penix Jr, who had a game that would be front-page terrible if not for the historic passing futility on the other side. Penix went 8-for-24 with four interceptions. The field was wet and as slippery as a greased-up ferret with a sugar rush. But even a wet playing surface wasn't enough to stop Tampa's run game, or kicker Chase McLaughlin's three field goals. Having said that, McLaughlin didn't even need to make one - Atlanta were just that bad. Next week, the Falcons face the Bengals in Spain. If Atlanta play like this in Madrid, they'll be the worst American export to Europe since David Hasselhoff's first album.
MVP of the week
Joe Burrow, QB, Cincinnati Bengals. We'll start with the positives: Burrow threw for five touchdowns, ran for another, racked up 462 passing yards and led his team back from 13 points down to a 43-36 overtime win on Sunday. On the downside, he threw three interceptions and all this came against the Tennessee Titans. But Burrow gets MVP this week for a refusal to do anything dull. And the Bengals are off to their best start since the 2021 season, when they made the Super Bowl.
Video of the week
The Chiefs-Broncos game was absorbing until the final moments. Kansas City, down 28-24, looked set to pull of a late victory when they had a first and goal on the Denver 4-yard line with 35 seconds left. But stout Broncos defending and a sloppy penalty from the Chiefs when they thought they had scored the winning touchdown meant they needed to score on the final play of the game. When Patrick Mahomes is under center in this type of situation, a Chiefs win is automatic. Not this time, though.
Stat of the week
259. That's the number of career touchdown passes across the regular season and playoffs by Josh Allen for the Bills after his pair during his team's victory over the Baltimore Ravens on Sunday. In reaching that milestone, he broke the record set by Buffalo legend Jim Kelly. The victory moved the Bills to 6-1 and they now have the best record in the AFC. Sadly, the game was also marked by a thumb injury to the Ravens' Lamar Jackson. It may end his season, a sad turn of events for one of the most thrilling players in the league.
