Roberto De Zerbi - who having picked up a booking today will be missing from the touchline for the visit of Ipswich in a fortnight's time - talks to TNT. "The first half, we played well ... the second half, we deserved to lose the game ... we lost all duels ... we didn't play like we can ... it's a journey for us ... we can learn from this game ... there was a way to play better ... to defend better ... I am sorry for the result but most of all I am sorry for the performance in the second half ... we didn't play like a top team ... we played without the right spirit, the right character, the right physicality in the duels ... it is tough to accept this defeat after those good games in a row ... but it is part of the journey ... the most important thing is to understand why we didn't play well in the second half ... it's just a mental aspect."
As for the Bogle tackle: "Pedro Porro was [booked for] a normal duel ... an incredible yellow card ... the first situation of Mathys Tel I didn't see so I can't say ... I have big confidence in the referee ... nothing changes if he goes to watch the clip or not ... there was the VAR ... if they didn't take the right decision, I don't know ... we have to keep our eyes on the performance, which can improve."
He also reports that James Maddison was not fit enough to play much more than 15 minutes, hence his remaining on the bench.
Daniel Farke speaks to TNT Sports. "We are very happy against an in-form Tottenham side ... the second half it was never in doubt ... we missed a few chances but we were relentless ... great commitment ... great unity ... fully deserved ... Noah Okafor was a week out ... so credit to the doctors ... they told me to stay disciplined and only put him on the pitch for 15, 20 minutes ... an ice-cold finish ... we need to stay cool in the head ... close the centre ... be technically spot on ... to be brave ... committed in the moments when we can press ... I think we found a good balance today ... it's good to have the winning feeling back in the dressing room, so credit to my boys."
His view on the Bogle tackle: "I haven't watched it back ... there were several doubtful situations ... a possible handball ... I just know Jayden Bogle does nothing with any bad intention ... it looked like a coming together ... an unlucky outcome that Mathys [Tel] is injured ... we wish him all the best ... the last thing Bogle is, is a nasty player ... he is probably the softest player in the whole league ... I always call him my butterfly! ... never he has bad intention, which I can guarantee, but of course I hope Mathys can recover quickly."

And here's how those points change everything, ahead of what remains of matchday 10, with Spurs remaining in tenth spot and Leeds leapfrogging them into eighth.
Leeds celebrate their big win as local lads Kaiser Chiefs predict a riot. They were much the better side in the second half, the only surprise being the deciding goal took so long to arrive. Its scorer Noah Okafor wasn't hanging around, mind: he'd only been on the pitch for 97 seconds when he found the net! On the balance of play, it's a richly deserved victory for Leeds, who might have had a penalty early doors - conflicting pictorial evidence exists of the ball hitting Mateus Fernandes on the arm/shoulder, so pick your poison and take your position - then completely dominated the second half. The one huge elephant in the room, however, is Jayden Bogle's crude connection with Mathys Tel's achilles, which should have resulted in a first-half red card, and would surely have changed the course of the game. But here we are. Premier League football, at its wild and unpredictable best, for all its faults.
