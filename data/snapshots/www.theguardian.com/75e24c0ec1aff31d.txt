Beckham takes £39m dividend as World Cup deals drive record profits
David Beckham has taken a $51m (£39m) dividend from his business empire fuelled by a series of deals with brands for the Fifa World Cup.
Beckham appeared in about a dozen ad campaigns across this summer's tournament in the US, working with brands including Lenovo, McDonald's, Bank of America, Verizon and PepsiCo's crisps brand Lay's.
The World Cup work helped to fuel a record year for DRJB Holdings, the commercial company for Beckham's brands business, where revenues surged by a fifth to $110.5m last year, from $92.3m in 2024.
Pre-tax profits also climbed to a record $65.7m, a 46% increase on 2024.
Havertz returns to Arsenal for treatment after suffering injnury on Germany duty
Kai Havertz will return to Arsenal for treatment after he sustained a "muscle sprain" while on Germany duty.
Havertz was forced off in the 30th minute of the nation's 1-1 draw with the Netherlands due to a hamstring problem.
The German FA confirmed a subsequent scan had identified that the 27-year-old had suffered a sprain and he has now left Jurgen Klopp's squad.
Havertz was sidelined for nearly five months with a knee injury last season, but he has started all five of Arsenal's Premier League games so far, preferred to Viktor Gyokeres by manager Mikel Arteta.
The Premier League champions are hopeful Havertz's latest setback is not serious.

US men's national team head coach Mauricio Pochettino says he regrets not being "more involved" in the situation surrounding US forward Folarin Balogun at this summer's World Cup.
Balogun was given a red card after a challenge against Bosnia and Herzegovina defender Tarik Muharemović, which by rule is accompanied with a one-game suspension. Balogun's ban for the next game against Belgium was suspended after the US Soccer Federation and US president Donald Trump lobbied Fifa, with Trump saying that he personally placed a call to Fifa president Gianni Infantino to ask the ruling be reviewed. Infantino maintains that the decision to reinstate Balogun was made independently by Fifa's disciplinary committee.
The entire affair cast a pall over what had been an otherwise positive tournament for the US to that point. Belgium's players and coaches said in the wake of it that it provided extra motivation ahead of the match. Balogun himself later spoke of having felt uncomfortable with the attention the situation placed upon him.

The Premier League, Bundesliga, La Liga and Serie A have issued a joint statement calling for reform at Fifa. In a rare coordinated move, the leagues have criticised the Fifa president, Gianni Infantino, making reference to his abandoned World Cup sell-off plan - the Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE) scheme - and the Folarin Balogun affair.
The four leagues have taken aim at Fifa's "relentless expansionism" and said leagues, together with national associations, clubs, and players, need a greater role in decision-making.
"Football's longstanding foundations have, in recent years, been threatened by a fundamental governance problem, whereby Fifa's president has actively promoted exploitative ideas rather than safeguarding against them," the statement said. "And while football's stakeholders ultimately forced the withdrawal of the Fifa Forward Enterprise scheme, the conditions that led to FFE remain.
"The Fifa president has now acknowledged the need for reform, but it is not enough for surface-level, process changes to be explored in the aftermath of FFE - fundamental governance reform is essential to restore clear constitutional boundaries within Fifa, including confidence that individual disciplinary matters remain demonstrably insulated from political interference."
