A Swiss tourist has been killed and British and American tourists have been injured when a group of hikers encountered a herd of elephants in Cameroon.
The group of six tourists, four British, one Swiss and one American, accompanied by local guides, were hiking near the village of Bakingili in the south of the Mount Cameroon national park on 19 November, on a path used by elephants to access a nearby lake, when they encountered the herd of about 15 animals, according to media reports.
The elephants attacked the group and Alain Throo, a Swiss insurance executive based in London, was reportedly gored and one of the guides tossed into a ditch on top of Throo, while the others hid in trees.
Throo had a black eye and was initially responsive and complaining about an injury to his arm, one of the British female hikers told Cameroonian media, which said they had feared he was bleeding internally.
"He said that the elephant attacked him," an unidentified Cameroonian man added, according to a video posted by CBS Radio Buea FM. "It smashed him."
Throo later died of his injuries and his body was carried out of the park by a combined team of park staff and the military to a mortuary in the town of Limbeon 21 November. The American hiker was treated at the same hospital and later discharged, according to local media.
One of the British tourists, who said he had hid in a tree stump for two hours, recalled the moment the elephants attacked their group. "I saw these elephants almost next to me. And I thought, if I go down this path as well, I'm going to get hit. So I just moved to the opposite direction. They ran down the path, I ran and hid in a bush. And I heard some screaming."
Sean McGovern, the UK chief executive of the insurer Axa XL, where Throo had worked for 13 years, confirmed the death, saying Throo was a "valued member of our team, respected colleague and friend whose contribution, professionalism and warmth touched many people".
Cameroonian media reported that clashes between elephants and local people had been happening for the past 10 years.
Mount Cameroon is the highest mountain in west and central Africa, at more than 4,000 metres. The African forest elephant was declared critically endangered by the International Union for Conservation of Nature in 2021. There are about 195,000 left in the wild, less than 15% of its population 50 years ago, according to the World Wildlife Fund.
Just 5% of forest elephants, which are smaller than their savannah-based counterparts, are found outside central Africa. In Cameroon, there are about 6,000 forest elephants, according to the NGO Global Conservation, which said the species was at risk from poaching syndicates.
A spokesperson for Britain's Foreign Office said: "We offered assistance after a number of British nationals were injured in Cameroon."
