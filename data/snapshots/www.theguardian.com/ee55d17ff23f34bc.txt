"Two proud Welshmen ticking a box for the England and Wales Cricket Board," is how a beaming Aneurin Donald jokingly put it. He and Phil Salt had just unleashed a brutal display of six-hitting on the Gold Coast to level this T20 series - the kind that would have had any low-flying Tiger Moths taking evasive action.
This was England's first outing at Carrara Oval since David Gower and John Morris famously took to the skies 35 years ago. While it may not furnish quite as many after-dinner speeches down the line, this was still a pretty emphatic response after Saturday's defeat by an Australian side light on star power.
Donald monstered nine sixes en route to 85 from 32 balls, while Salt raced to 58 from 30. By putting on 151 runs for the first wicket in just 10.1 overs, this Welsh-born opening pair - a first for England in any format - had helped vaporise a target of 185 with 41 balls to spare for a convincing eight-wicket margin.
Harry Brook described himself as "speechless" at the end, while Donald had plenty to say. With his parents in the crowd struggling to hold back tears, it was rich reward for a cricketer who has overcome two career-threatening knee injuries and since reinvented himself as a T20 hitter committed to all-out aggression.
"I realise I'm not great at batting, but I'm quite good at slogging," said Donald. "Luckily, there might be a career in it now. There probably wasn't a few years ago. It's just trusting that bat swing. I feel like I've been in a pretty good place with my game for a little while now.
"I'm not the most skilful. I'm probably not the most powerful. But my intent has probably separated me in the last few months. There's going to be periods where it's not quite working, and it's about staying true to that as much as I can."
After Australia scrambled to 184, Donald needed only 18 balls for his half-century and though he and Salt eventually perished, the match was long since settled. Tom Banton ransacked the bulk of the remaining runs to finish unbeaten on an eight-ball 26, sealing victory with England's 16th six.
Australia's earlier innings had been in keeping with the atmosphere in nearby Surfers Paradise this week. There were 11 sixes, 13 fours, and eight wickets, making it as chaotic and unpredictable as the hordes of high-school graduates who have been cutting loose and keeping the local authorities on their toes.
The opening power play set the tone. Mitch Marsh banged two unreal hits off Henry Crocombe before chipping one straight back to the Sussex man, while Jake Fraser-McGurk launched three monsters of his own before a top-edge off Sam Curran was gobbled up by Brook.
James Coles had sent down a tidy first over on his debut, suffered at the hands of Fraser-McGurk in his second, and then pulled off a diving direct hit from short fine leg to run out Sam Harper. When Tim David gave Coles his first catch off the final ball of the sixth over, Australia had somehow slipped to 58 for four.
Three or more wickets inside the powerplay tends to be curtains but the hosts rebuilt well in the middle overs. Nikhil Chaudhary rolled over his form from Saturday's opener with 45 from 35 balls, and while Adil Rashid tied Lachlan Shaw in knots for a tortured 17-ball 10, the all-rounder Joel Davies struck 39 off 25.
England's Crocombe and Jamie Overton were a bit guileless on this drop-in surface - albeit with three wickets between them - whereas Curran was all tricks and sleight of hand. With just three balls of the innings to go, the all-rounder's figures were a sublime two for 11 - only for Ben Dwarshuis to send all three over the rope.
A competitive score had seemingly become an imposing one. That was until Donald began flaying all-comers and Salt, happy to rotate the strike and handed a life on 10, lit the afterburners. Next stop the Gabba on Friday for round three in this series, albeit with Donald planning to hit one of the local golf courses first.
"My golf is exactly like my batting," Donald added. "Not very straight but occasionally very long."
