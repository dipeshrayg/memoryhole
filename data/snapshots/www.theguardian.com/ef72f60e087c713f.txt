A big week for...
Bayern Munich have hit a snagging point, with injuries wracking Vincent Kompany's squad just as the head coach returns to work following his own health scare. For Lille's winner, to exact Bayern's first loss of the season, Aleksandar Pavlović had an own goal for the ages, desperately chasing back to then somehow direct the ball into his own net. Bayern are 15th in the Champions League table, and with Harry Kane out until well into 2027 and 18-year-old Lennart Karl asked to lead the line, resources are stretched. Jamal Musiala, subject of club grandees Uli Hoeness and Karl-Heinz Rummenigge's gripes over his contract in light of his neurological condition, could also play only 15 minutes.
Praise instead is due to Lille, coached by Davide Ancelotti to their first win of the group stage. They are captained by 36-year-old Benjamin André, who played one of the best matches of his long career. "Underrated" in the view of Ancelotti, who worked at Bayern under his father, Carlo. "Tonight was something special, we played like a monster."
Soccer's world-leading content delivery machine continues to supply a high grade of entertainment. While critics carp against the group stage's baggy format, Manchester United have supplied the thrills, the spills, the pratfalls. Following the pathos of Atlético Madrid and Como, a comedy of errors at Sporting, with Senne Lammens saving a penalty from Luis Suárez, being unable to save Suárez's next spot-kick and then dropping the ball for the same player to equalise for a 2-2 scoreline that undersold the chaos. United's second goal, Bruno Fernandes's second, also came via farcical means, beating Kaique Pereira, the Sporting keeper, after a bobble off a divot in the Estádio José Alvalade's surface that Fernandes had himself created when trying to keep his footing. A word, too, for Szymon Marciniak, who gave a strange, overly fussy referee's performance, ruling out two seemingly legitimate United goals.
Were United improved? It was difficult to gauge. "With a performance like today it's the best medicine to prepare the next game," said Mauricio Pochettino, possibly still jet-lagged yet already deep in the United reeds. His team are 19th in both the Champions League and Premier League.
Kevin De Bruyne's return to the Etihad brought yearning from both player and former club. Manchester City's struggles continue and De Bruyne, now 35, is not performing as desired for Napoli, while those from within City continue to push the idea of business as usual amid the limbo of those guilty charges, two empty tiers of the renovated stadium cast telling shadows. Playing in an unfamiliar orange kit for reasons of partnership with a fashion firm best known for football casual "clobber" only added to the surrealism.
"When you don't win, you question everything," Erling Haaland said following a 2-2 draw rescued by some Rayan Cherki magic. Among those questions is how Enzo Maresca is dealing with the succession and legacy of Pep Guardiola. Recent signs are unpromising.
Player of the week
In late 2026, the year of Harry Kane, another Englishman is setting controls for the Ballon d'Or. Jude Bellingham has never played a Premier League match but who in the world's richest league could afford him? At half-time against PSV Eindhoven, Real Madrid were 1-0 down, and then José Mourinho threw on Bellingham. He scored only one of the five his team ran in, though it was spectacular; a volley bearing a light comparison to Madrid legend Zinedine Zidane's goal in 2002's final in Glasgow. "I think Zidane is much better than me, he's much more elegant and he's my idol," said a humble Bellingham, while also dismissing talk of inheriting Kane's crown.
Club in focus: Real Betis
Only their second season in the competition, their previous campaign coming in 2005-06, when Marcos Assunção, Ricardo Oliveira and Joaquín were the star names, Betis are sixth in the table with four wins from five. The knockouts beckon.
The 2026 vintage is full of experience, with Manuel Pellegrini, 73, coaching a team captained by Isco, 34, and still as inventive as ever. The star man in defeating Slovan Bratislava was the Colombian striker Cucho Hernández, previously of Columbus Crew. Two towering headers made sure Slovan remained bottom on zero points.
"I was convinced it was going to be a magical night for me personally," said Hernández, who was returning after over a month's absence. Troy Parrott faces a fight to get back in, having been the match-winner in Betis's opening defeat of Lille. Wednesday was another night where Antony, a Manchester United escapee flourishing elsewhere, was a major threat.
