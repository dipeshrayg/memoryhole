London Broncos are targeting Super League fixtures in new territories and new Australia-friendly kick-off times after returning to the top flight as part of their plan to grow rugby league, before campaigning to the NRL to be part of a unique double-header at Wembley in 2028 too.
London will have a Super League presence in 2027 after the Rugby Football League awarded the Broncos the 15th place in a newly expanded competition. They swept aside all competition in the Championship last year, losing just one league fixture and winning a domestic treble with a squad many believed was Super League-ready.
They will now get the chance to put that to the test, but London's ownership group of Grant Wechsel and Australian rugby league icon Darren Lockyer are intent on making a big impact in Super League on and off the field.
That includes initiatives to boost their own profile and that of Super League, including the prospect of some of London's home games being played in new areas for the sport. Wechsel has already floated the idea of playing a game against Wigan at Fulham's Craven Cottage, the first venue for professional rugby league in London in 1980.
But he now wants to go further with games in cities across the south, and potentially even Wales, to try to grow the game. "We will definitely be doing something," Wechsel told the Guardian. "I can't guarantee it would be Fulham because if you play at a football stadium, the pitch has to be renovated and this has happened quite late.
"But we will take at least one game somewhere to a southern city in England or perhaps into Wales. We've seen that Wigan and Warrington are taking a game to Dublin [in April] and we want to do the same, and try to grow rugby league as much as possible, both in London and farther afield."
Super League will align closely with the NRL in 2027 in a number of areas before an official partnership with the Australian governing body begins in 2028, which will see it take control of Super League. That includes spreading kick-off times to reduce clashes and put more eyeballs on the game, and Wechsel has volunteered London to take a new time-slot.
"Thursday nights in London will be great because everyone works from home on a Friday, so they could be tempted out on Thursdays," he said. "But I've thrown up the idea of playing at 12.30pm on a Saturday lunchtime, like in the Premier League. Connect the dots; that's 9.30pm on Saturday night in Australia. Both sides of the world can tune in."
The NRL is aiming to launch a global round in 2028, which will see games played in locations such as Hong Kong, Tokyo and London as well as Las Vegas. With the NRL adamant London is central to their vision to grow the sport in the UK, Wechsel has also proposed a unique double-header, with Wembley mooted as a possible venue for such an event.
"I'm very vocal here that the NRL send Brisbane Broncos to play in London in 2028 if they're going to do a game here," he said. "That way, it's a Broncos double-header with a London Super League game on the billing too."
