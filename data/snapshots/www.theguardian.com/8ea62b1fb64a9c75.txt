Anthropic's shares expected to pop when trading begins
There's just 90 minutes to go until the US stock market opens for trading.
Despite lifting the price of its IPO yesterday, to $83 a share, Anthropic is still expected to jump in value once its stock starts trading.
That means we should see the traditional 'pop' when a new company joins the stock market, and investors who missed out on the sale process try to buy in.
Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at investing and trading platform IG, has the details:
"IG's pre-IPO market for Anthropic has seen a notable bounce since Nvidia's results as sentiment towards tech and AI becomes more enthusiastic as the Jensen scandal settles down.
From a low of $97 earlier in the week IG clients expect the stock to easily top $100 in initial trading and continue to gain. With the price now trading at $105, the AI giant would be valued at $1.9 trillion, just shy of the magic $2 trillion mark that had initially been mooted when the IPO plans first became widespread news.
Markets needed reassurance that the AI trade still has plenty of life left in it, and it looks like the Anthropic IPO will do just that."
It's frustrating, but not at all unusual, to have to wait for a newly floated company to start trading.
Newly listed shares do not start publicly trading at the opening bell. Nasdaq and the lead investment banks first gather buy and sell orders, a process that can take several hours for a large listing, the AFP newswire points out.
The biggest IPO in world history is officially underway!
After a clock counted down to 9.30am in New York, Anthropic rang the opening bell, and the AI titan's top staff are applauding happily at Nasdaq Marketsite.
Now Dario Amodei takes the stage, saying he and his cofounders started Anthropic beause "we believe the most powerful technology of our time should be built for the benefit of humankind".
He explains that he and his sister Daniela lost their father to a disease that became curable just a few years later.
Dario Amodei argues that AI can cut through the complexity that scientists face when trying to cure diseases, and bring new treatments faster.
