Dionisi says report of Watford sacking 'fake news'
Despite reports suggesting he would be sacked if Watford lost at Cardiff on Tuesday, Alessio Dionisi remains in post. "There is good news, bad news or fake news probably," the Italian told the BBC yesterday. "In this case for the moment, it is fake news. But I don't decide."
The situation at the club is dire: a squad haphazardly thrown together, with no money spent on transfer fees in the summer, the division's lowest wage bill, few players arriving until the final days of the window, a miserable dearth of Championship experience (which the club leadership acknowledged and pledged to address at the end of last season, and for that matter before most recent transfer windows), a disjointed and lopsided squad notably bereft of midfield options, and an aloof and increasingly unpopular owner.
Since the start of March the club have played 21 league games and won only three, the worst record of any club which has been in the division throughout that time (indeed Coventry, Ipswich, West Ham and Oxford United all have more wins and more Championship points despite spending much of it in other divisions). Most recently they played Burnley at home when their opponents were bottom of the league and lost 4-0, and then went to 21st-placed Cardiff and lost 5-1. They are now 22nd, and both Elton John - who said he was "upset and disappointed at the decline" - and Troy Deeney have criticised the way they are playing and the way they are being run.
Today Gino Pozzo is meeting a representative of fans' group the 1881 Movement - who recently published an open letter stating their "complete loss of confidence" in Pozzo, and criticising "the endless cycle of appointments and dismissals [which] has reduced a once-proud club to a source of ridicule". Tomorrow they host 13th-placed but also troubled Charlton, who won three of their first four league games but have not secured a victory since August.
Word is that the club has already made contact with potential replacements for Dionisi, but that the level of vitriol coming from those fans still sufficiently motivated to turn up to games has stayed Pozzo's hand, for now. The club certainly have some players, the likes of Othmane Maamma and Myron Boadu, who are able to play at a much higher level to the one the team is currently showing, but do they have the motivation (and the teammates) to demonstrate that ability, or has the cycle of sackings drained the squad of all motivation and cohesion?
Coventry boss Frank Lampard is not prepared to relax after watching his side record back-to-back wins in the Premier League, writes the Press Association.
The Sky Blues looked to be in for a long season after losing their opening four games without scoring a goal. But they have kick-started their campaign with wins against Nottingham Forest and Newcastle and as he prepares to visit Tottenham on Monday Lampard wants to keep the foot on the gas.
"Keep driving it," he said. "It's very important to look at the the good things out of the game, also the things we can improve. But when we talk about the good things, I'm not overly patting them on the back because we have to work hard to get better.
"Every game brings different challenges, so I think the players understand me and my approach and the staff's approach here. We never want to rest. I don't. I'm always fearful of resting on something and thinking that you've made it. This league's too difficult for that.
"So one of my first jobs is to keep pushing from that front, and the players need to feel that."
Fulham fans fretting about life under Álvaro Arbeloa will not feel any better when they remember how things were under Marco Silva, who was one of the most effective managers in the Premier League when it came to making substitutions.
Silva's substitutes were involved in the fourth-highest goal contributions last season and the most in the 2024-25 season. Fulham knew how to dig in and salvage unlikely points. But Silva left last summer, joining Benfica after concluding he could achieve nothing more at Craven Cottage, and his replacement is already under pressure after some puzzling tactical choices.
The worrying shift under Arbeloa was evident during Fulham's collapse against Ipswich last weekend. The west Londoners were 1-0 up and looking good for their first league win of the season when Arbeloa weakened his midfield by replacing Shea Charles with Manuel Ángel in the 70th minute.
It was a strange way to shut up shop. Charles is physically imposing, has impressed since joining from Southampton and was doing well alongside Sander Berge. The 22-year-old Ángel is 5ft 7in, was making his Premier League debut and has little experience of senior football. Ipswich's comeback to win 2-1 was not a surprise, particularly when Arbeloa's other rejigs included bringing on Rodrigo Muniz and switching from a 4-2-3-1 system to a flat 4-4-2 in an attempt to defend the lead.

Newcastle head coach Matthias Jaissle says he is uninterested in dwelling on what might have been when highly-rated Aston Villa midfielder Johan Manzambi visits St James' Park this weekend, writes the Press Association.
Manzambi looked set for a switch to Tyneside over the summer but the deal was hijacked as Villa swooped in to sign the Switzerland international, who has flourished during his short time in the midlands.
The 21-year-old opened the scoring in Villa's 6-0 Champions League rout of Fenerbahce in midweek and he could rub salt into his former suitors' wounds when Newcastle welcome Unai Emery's side on Saturday.
But while Jaissle, who succeeded Eddie Howe after Manzambi snubbed a move to the north-east, is a fan of the youngster, the German side-stepped the question of whether he would have liked him in his ranks.
"He is a really good player," said Jaissle, who will be looking for a response after Monday's 4-1 loss at Coventry. "But I'm not here to talk now about players, about rumours, about potential signings. The pure focus is on our players. We have many things to do, many things to improve, and that's the approach to not get distracted by anything else."
