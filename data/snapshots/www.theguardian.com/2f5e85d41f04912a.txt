This was Hull last night:
"I've never been in that situation before, playing an innings like that. I haven't really had the opportunity, so it was all pretty new to me. At the start, the message was just about taking it spell by spell, just bat time, trying to get the ball a bit softer, get the bowlers tired.
"I never felt it got easier, to be honest. There were times when I was seeing the ball a bit better, getting used to the bowlers and I was, like, okay, this is quite fun. And when the second new ball came along, it was dark, under lights, and the ball was nipping and zooming around a lot and it was tough.
"Obviously, I'm a bit gutted not to get to the hundred, but it was something I never really thought about the whole way through. I never really thought I had it in me to play that kind of innings. When I got to 50, and I was like, oh, this is quite cool, I'm happy with that.
"The same coming back out at 60, and then it happened pretty quick, I was suddenly on 90. When I was about 95, I started to think it could happen. But I was trying to very much just think about the game still and what got me to that position. That was my process, just trying to just face as many balls as I could."
Round-up: Somerset's Championship dream alive as Surrey sneak thriller
Somerset's dreams of a first Championship pennant continue to bubble and squeak, for now sitting pretty at the top of the table with just three rounds to go. The innings victory over Glamorgan, while Nottinghamshire were hamstrung by the weather and a stoical Leicestershire, put the spring in a west country step.
There were three wickets each for the on-song Jack Leach, Craig Overton and Lewis Gregory, the main resistance coming from Asa Tribe and Sean Dickson, who both made forties, but it was all over before tea.
But Warwickshire were close on their tails after mopping up at Southampton. A match that had started grubbily for Hampshire with the signing and unsigning of Scott Kuggeleijn finished with them dismissed for 115 and 154 to lose by an innings, flailing below the pack at the bottom of Division One. Chris Woakes whipped out both openers cheaply in Hampshire's second innings and Manav Suthar pocketed four.
There was a high-wire finish at the Oval as Surrey snookered Yorkshire by one wicket and with it the spoils of 19 points to leapfrog them and out of the relegation zone. Surrey's pursuit of 185 was stop-start-stop and when Ollie Pope went for a 12 that contained only two scoring shots and Ben Foakes was done by a Ben Coad chestnut before lunch, Surrey looked shot. But while Josh Blake's stoical 43 off 183 balls blocked one end, the tail played shots at the other. With Surrey eight wickets down, and eight runs needed, Auqib Nabi trumpeted Coad out of the ground but was out next ball. Ten scoreless deliveries followed until Dan Worrall finally hoiked a boundary to win.
The wretched season at Old Trafford rolled on, as Tom Helm (four for 36) and co bowled Middlesex to victory. After a promising start in pursuit of 297, Lancashire crumpled, losing seven for 39. George Balderson had earlier grabbed five for 54 as Middlesex spiralled in a similar manner, buoyed up by a sing-song 37 from Toby Roland-Jones.
Nottinghamshire's campaign was held up by the unlikely figure of the Leicestershirefast bowler Josh Hull, who came in as nightwatcher at Trent Bridge on Thursday night, and had only reached double figures three times in 34 innings. He was finally out after more than five hours at the crease for 97.
