As per Cricket Australia's chief executive, Todd Greenberg, when announcing the creation of a "forum" for players to share memories of sandpaper use in South Africa eight years ago, two diametrically opposed versions of events can both be true.
By that logic, anyone recalling this deciding one-day international as a win for Australia years from now will not technically be wrong. It will simply be "their truth", to borrow one of the most overused phrases in this whole sorry saga.
Back in the real world, however, on the day when Cameron Bancroft's 4,000-word mea culpa implicated pretty much Australia's entire touring party back in the day, the opposite was very much the case. Driven by Ben Duckett's 115-ball 133, it was England who emerged victorious under lights by a huge margin of 97 runs.
First came a helter-skelter charge to 328 all out in 48.3 overs, with Duckett's flurry of 15 fours and three sixes augmented by a critical half-century from Will Jacks at the back end. Then came a charge with the ball similar to Adelaide, culminating in Australia's collapse to 231 all out.
A 2-1 series win for Harry Brook's tourists, therefore, and a low-key encouraging one, too. The hosts were without Pat Cummins, Josh Hazlewood and Mitchell Starc - a penny for their thoughts right now - but this was still the first time England had come from behind to claim an ODI series in Australia.
Off-field events dominated once again such that Mitch Marsh, Australia's white-ball captain and part of the Test side in 2018, ducked the post-match press conference. In came poor Jack Edwards, with the rookie all-rounder accidentally letting slip that all the focus on sandpaper "takes the heat off us to be honest".
Duckett wisely chose to shoulder arms to the subject, preferring to reflect on his seventh ODI century, his first in any format on Australian soil, and one that ignored wickets falling at the other end to knit together a match-winning score. "Going 1-0 down in Perth, we had to show a lot of fight," he said. "It's been awesome."
One of Duckett's observations was how his promising new alliance up top with Tom Banton has the same little-and-large, left-right chemistry as that with Zak Crawley in the Test side a while back. After his crisp 121 on Sunday, Banton's 35-ball 44 gave England a launchpad here of 74 runs inside the first nine overs.
It took a sparkling catch from Marsh to end it and Duckett was handed a life on 29 soon after when Alex Carey, recalled as a specialist batter, grassed a pull shot at square leg. Duckett was undeterred, enjoying an absence of the bounce that has previously been his undoing in this part of the world. After that struggle in the Ashes 12 months ago, this 94-ball hundred offered a little moment of catharsis.
Adam Zampa suffering an 11th-hour back spasm left Australia with just three frontline bowlers and, faced with a diet of part-time spin, England got a bit greedy. Matt Renshaw's serviceable off-breaks saw Joe Root bowled and Jordan Cox stumped, while Brook holed out for 17 off Cooper Connolly's left-arm tweakers.
There was a bit of funkiness from England, too. Jofra Archer was promoted to No 6 with the score 172 for four at the start of the 29th over and the experiment didn't work. At that stage, even with Duckett purring, England were in danger of squandering their position without someone else holding their hand up.
Enter Jacks, who has been one of the success stories of the Brook era by dutifully adapting to the role of limited-overs finisher. The vice-captain's 59 from 48 balls was a combination of clean hitting and a cool head - the last man out to give Edwards a fourth but having smartly marshalled the tail.
Another growing strength is the pace attack, with Gus Atkinson knocking over Travis Head for the third time in a row after the restart and Sonny Baker's zippy outswing teasing an early edge off Marsh. Had Baker then held a simple catch off Renshaw - palms skywards, Australian-style - it would have been 22 for three.
As it was, even with Baker then getting a nick off Josh Inglis for 14, Renshaw wriggled free to add 74 with Carey for the fourth wicket, hinting at a possible contest. That was until Liam Dawson held a sharp return catch off Renshaw, the first of three for the left-armer to finish with 19 for the year. No teammate has more in ODIs.
The most galling dismissal for Australia was Carey being run out following a mix-up with Cameron Green, who in turn suffered the most painful departure. Struck on the grille by a nasty short-ball from Atkinson, Green trudged off woozily towards the dugout - retired hurt on three - with blood pouring from his mouth.
As imagery went, it summed up Australia's day. And without him, a lower order already behind the required rate was bombarded by the quicks and folded. Baker finished with figures of four for 45, before one of Brook's wrong-footed dobbers to Spencer Johnson inflicted the final, ignominious blow.
