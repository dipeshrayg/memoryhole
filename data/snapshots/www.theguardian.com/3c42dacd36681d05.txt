The UK has been confirmed as hosts of the 2035 Women's World Cup, the first global competition to be staged in the country since England won the men's World Cup in 1966.
A joint bid from the Football Associations of England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales was approved by acclamation at an Extraordinary Fifa Congress conducted virtually, with another uncontested bid from the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica being awarded the 2031 tournament.
In a surprise move the UK bid was not presented by the FA or any of the home nations' chief executives despite them all travelling to Zurich, with the presentation instead led by the Sky Sports News presenter Jess Creighton and four schoolchildren, one from each country.
FA sources rejected suggestions this was a deliberate attempt to distance themselves from Fifa's president, Gianni Infantino, who presided over the congress. All four associations called on him to resign in the summer.
After the tournament had been formally awarded, the FA chair, Debbie Hewitt, took the floor and hugged Infantino before promising the UK would "celebrate the power of women's football" and deliver an "exceptional" World Cup.
The UK pledged to deliver the biggest single-sport event ever held in the country, setting targets for 4.5m ticket sales and a projected TV audience of 3.5bn. With 63 million people living within two hours of at least one proposed venue the bid team also claimed it would be the most accessible tournament ever staged in the UK.
"The world is coming to the United Kingdom," Andy Burnham said. "In 2035 the biggest tournament in women's sport will be played in cities across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, bringing visitors, investment and jobs to every part of the country.
"Our players have inspired a generation of girls. In 2035 that generation will watch the best in the world play on their doorstep - and some of them will be on the pitch. But the impact of this World Cup will go much further. It will reach into communities across the country, bring more people into the game and leave a legacy that lasts long after the final whistle."
The UK bid lists 22 stadiums as potential venues: 16 in England, three in Wales, two in Scotland and one in Northern Ireland. 15 host cities are in the running (the bid officially lists 16, counting Trafford as distinct from Manchester).
Wembley has been proposed for the final, having hosted it at the 2022 Women's European Championship, which was won by England.
Fifa has evaluated 19 stadiums, assessing them as being of "high quality". All were awarded a score of at least four out of five, except for Elland Road in Leeds (3.9), Easter Road in Edinburgh, Windsor Park in Belfast and Nottingham's City Ground (all 3.6). Old Trafford has been assessed by Fifa but the proposed new Manchester United stadium could play a significant role instead if built in time.
The three listed stadiums not assessed by Fifa are Birmingham City's proposed 62,000-seat Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham's stadium - which is due two stages of redevelopment - and Chelsea's stadium because only a redeveloped Stamford Bridge or a new club stadium would come into the picture.
The 22 stadiums will be whittled down to between 14 and 16. The tournament is scheduled to involve 104 matches played by 48 teams over 39 days. The dates are yet to be confirmed but indicative dates of 21 June to 29 July have been informally used as a template during the bidding process.
The tournament is due to be the second Women's World Cup to include 48 teams, after the 2031 event in North America, Mexico and the Caribbean, mirroring this year's men's tournament. There are to be 12 groups of four teams followed by knockouts starting with a last 32.
As revealed by the Guardian last week, Fifa is at loggerheads with the UK's government and four football associations over the world governing body's demands for tax-free status and exemptions from exchange-rate fees at the tournament.
England, Scotland and Wales are also due to co-host - along with the Republic of Ireland - the men's European Championship in 2028.
Northern Ireland was forced to drop out of hosting Euro 2028 after failing to secure the local political backing and funding to rebuild Windsor Park, which will host games in 2035.
