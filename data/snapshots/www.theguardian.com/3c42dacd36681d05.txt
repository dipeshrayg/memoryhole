The UK is to host the 2035 Women's World Cup, Fifa has confirmed. England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales will stage the first World Cup hosted in the country since the 1966 men's tournament in England.
The UK bid was the only one under consideration for 2035. The 2031 tournament has been awarded, also uncontested, to the United States, Mexico, Costa Rica and Jamaica. The decisions were formalised at a virtual meeting of Fifa's national associations on Monday.
Debbie Hewitt, the Football Association chair, said of the 2035 World Cup: "Together we will celebrate the power of women's football, inspire the next generation and unite fans from every corner of the world."
The UK bid lists 22 stadiums as potential venues. Sixteen are in England, three in Wales, two in Scotland and one is in Northern Ireland. Fifteen host cities are in the running (the bid officially lists 16, counting Trafford as distinct from Manchester).
Andy Burnham said the tournament would bring "visitors, investment and jobs to every part of the country". The prime minister also pointed to the sporting impact, saying it would "bring more people into the game and leave a legacy that lasts long after the final whistle".
Wembley has been proposed for the final, having hosted it at the 2022 Women's European Championship. Fifa has evaluated 19 stadiums, assessing them as being of "high quality". All were awarded an score of at least four out of five, except for Elland Road in Leeds (3.9), Easter Road in Edinburgh, Windsor Park in Belfast and Nottingham's City Ground (all 3.6). Old Trafford has been assessed by Fifa but the proposed new Manchester United stadium could play a significant role instead if built in time.
The three listed stadiums not assessed by Fifa are Birmingham City's proposed 62,000-seat Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham's stadium - which is due two stages of redevelopment - and Chelsea's stadium because only a redeveloped Stamford Bridge or a new club stadium would come into the picture.
The 22 stadiums will be whittled down to between 14 and 16. The tournament is scheduled to involve 104 matches played by 48 teams over 39 days. The dates are yet to be confirmed but indicative dates of 21 June to 29 July have been informally used as a template during the bidding process.
The tournament is due to be the second Women's World Cup to include 48 teams, after 2031's event, mirroring this year's men's tournament and making it the largest single-sport event staged in the UK. There are to be 12 groups of four teams followed by knockouts starting with a last 32.
As revealed by the Guardian last week, Fifa is at loggerheads with the UK's government and four football associations over the world governing body's demands for tax-free status and exemptions from exchange-rate fees at the tournament.
England, Scotland and Wales are also due to co-host - along with the Republic of Ireland - the men's European Championship in 2028.
