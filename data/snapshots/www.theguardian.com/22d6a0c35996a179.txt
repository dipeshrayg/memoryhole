33 mins: Rutter isn't a regular starter for Brighton, especially not in the No 9 position and I wonder whether the Seagulls are missing having an out-and-out striker. Rutter, Gomez, Gross and Ayari ... they all kind of prefer playing in the pocket. Maybe a focal point would benefit Brighton instead.
32 mins: Cho gets a rare opportunity to run at Costinha and gets in a cross with his left foot, which Matt Crooks tries to flick from behind him. Unfortunately for Hull, Crooks cannot get any power on the flick and it's straight at Verbruggen.
Tzolakis parries from Rutter at the other end, another smart save from the Hull stopper.
28 mins: A well co-ordinated press from Hull has Verbruggen and his defenders worrying for a moment, hurriedly squirrelling the ball into touch, which allows another long throw from the hosts. Mendy hooks it tamely at the Brighton goal, and Verbruggen collects.
23 mins: Some really strong and determined play from Iroegbunam earns his team a free-kick. The referee tried to play an advantage for Hull before bringing the play back.
Is this worth a shot? Slater and Belloumi stand over it.
21 mins: Another chance for Brighton, this time Ayari picking up the headed knockdown from Rutter and scuffing his effort, which means it's an easy save for Tzolakis.
20 mins: Brighton are starting to tick here. Their short passing, their movement, their invention ... it's all superb to watch. A fluid move ends with Costinha having a shot, but he guides it wide. That was another big chance.
18 mins: A deflection runs kindly for Gomez down the right and then Rutter gets it wrong in taking the return away from his teammate. Brighton keep the pressure on, before Costinha's cross is held by Tzolakis.
17 mins: Ironic cheers from the home fans as their team get a free-kick. Slater bundled over. Brighton have been fairly spiky in their tackling so far, and perhaps the Hull fans felt that free-kick was overdue.
A huge save from Tzolakis! That looked goal bound. It was De Cuyper whose low cross from the left was brilliantly precise, and Gomez should probably have finished it off. The Hull keeper made himself big to deny the Paraguayan at close range.
14 mins: Both keepers have had crosses to catch and errant through-balls to collect, but we haven't really had a shot in anger yet. Gomez drills in a powerful cross low from the right but nobody in a Brighton shirt is anywhere near it.
13 mins: Brighton are actually playing more on the counterattack at the moment, which isn't quite what I envisaged for this match, against the low-blockers of Hull City.
Boscagli's speculative cross does not find Rutter.
8 mins: Brighton are finally getting into it, pushing Hull back with a prolonged period of possession. The black and amber shirts are shuttling back and forth to keep the visitors chanceless, at the moment. Good disciplined defence.
6 mins: Hull's shape does resemble more of a 4-3-3, or 4-5-1, with Cho and Belloumi being asked to get forward into wide positions and support McBurnie. The home team have started confidently.
4 mins: Rutter and Gomez link up nicely down Brighton's inside-right channel but Tzolakis is there to catch the former's cutback. Nice pacy start to the game, this.
2 mins: Brighton's press baiting play deep in possession might not be so successful against a Hull team who are happy to sit in a low block, even at home. But the home side have come out quite aggressively, with Regan Slater barging into Diego Gomez to concede a free-kick.

Fabian Hürzeler sounded a little way of Hull in his pre-match press conference, citing the Tigers' narrow 1-0 defeat to table-topping (and possible title rivals) Arsenal as a template for how today's game might go.
"I think they do an impressive job," Hürzeler said. "They had a brilliant start in the Premier League. They are very structured. Against Arsenal, I think Arsenal suffered. They [Arsenal] didn't create a lot of big chances, and therefore we need to be patient because we also know that Hull are really good in transition.
"They also have individual quality in their team. Very well structured, very well organised, good in transition, good in set pieces, and I think it's not an easy place to go to."
Hull obviously beat Manchester United 2-0 in their opening home game and have drawn four successive matches at the MKM Stadium since, so that proves Hürzeler's point somewhat.

My personal two-pennies-worth is that Brighton will probably fall away from the title race at some stage after Christmas. Their fixture list in the short term is fairly inviting, so I see their lofty position being sustained for a little while.
But realistically, a top five finish and Champions League qualification should be their primary objective. That's really not impossible when you consider the struggles of Manchester United and Chelsea, and the possible fate of Manchester City if and when a verdict/punishment arrives re their 115s charges.
The fact Fabian Hürzeler could achieve that just after his 34th birthday would be an astonishing feat in itself.
The stats don't lie when it comes to Brighton's brilliant form. They are well clear as the Premier League's top scorers with 26, despite many of their rivals having played a game more. They are also level with Arsenal (7) in terms of goals conceded, again having played fewer games than the Gunners.
Pascal Gross also leads way in the league this season for chances created (29).
They don't have one outstanding goalscorer, but Gross is on four so far this season, with Yasin Ayari, Jack Hinshelwood and Charalampos Kostoulas all chipping in with three.

Hello and welcome to our live coverage of the solitary early kick-off in the Premier League today. Many will view Hull City versus Brighton as a mere hors d'oeuvres to the main dish of Liverpool against Manchester United later, but there is plenty riding on it.
Hull have not won a game in any competition since claiming back-to-back victories in a shock opening burst after winning promotion, while Brighton have lost just twice across 17 matches in all competitions. So this is a nailed-on Seagulls win, right?
Maybe, though Fabian Hürzeler will be guarding against complacency, especially because Hull have not lost by more than one goal in any game this season and have not conceded more than two. They are the proverbial hard nut to crack, albeit scoring goals is a problem for the Tigers - despite sitting 14th in the table, only two teams (Nottingham Forest and Sunderland) have scored fewer.
Team news has already dropped so we'll bring you that in the next post. Kick-off is at 2pm (GMT) so please do stay tuned. I'm predicting this will be a tighter game than many would imagine.
