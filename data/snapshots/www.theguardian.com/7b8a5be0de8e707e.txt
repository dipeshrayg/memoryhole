Prem Rugby has fired the starting gun on expanding to 12 teams by formally asking interested clubs to come forward by February. The chief executive Simon Massie-Taylor is currently in the United States attempting to woo investors with the Prem determined to add two more teams to its ranks for the 2029-30 competition.
Previous top flight sides Worcester Warriors, who won last season's rebranded Champ, and London Irish - who have signalled their intentions to return to the second tier as soon as next season - are among the clubs expecting to throw their hats into the ring while the Welsh Rugby Union has already sent a letter outlining a desire for clubs to join the Prem and create an Anglo-Welsh league.
In order to join the Prem, however, interested clubs would be required to buy a P share - valued at £20m - which explains Massie-Taylor's charm offensive in the US. Coventry, Ealing and Cornish Pirates, who have already secured significant investment, are among the Champ sides expected to come forward before the 1 February deadline to register interest, after which a tender process will begin.
Birmingham Moseley, currently playing in the third tier, National League 1, are an attractive proposition because Birmingham City FC's US investors Knighthead Capital Management are already said to have expressed an interest. Insiders see multi-sport investors as those to particularly target with Bournemouth FC's US owners Black Knight recently taking over Exeter Chiefs. With Red Bull also investing in Newcastle and James Dyson doing so at Bath, Prem executives are confident of a further flurry of interest.
As well as investment in existing clubs, Prem Rugby is also open to the creation of new franchises, particularly if it allows for a geographical spread. Yorkshire is considered a fertile ground to break while Prem officials are watching developments at Wasps closely. The club have secured a 10-year option on land near Sevenoaks in Kent, the county with the second-highest number of players behind Yorkshire.
Amid interest from Wales, Prem officials are understood to be committed to welcoming English clubs to its expanded league. They question whether Welsh clubs would be willing to spend at least a season in the Championship - one of the key stipulations for interested parties - and whether they could afford a P share.
Prem Rugby has previously used the services of Deloitte and Raine, the US bank that ran the Hundred sales process, while Massie-Taylor used to work in New York and is said to be a key player in attracting investment. Leicester, Harlequins and Gloucester are among the existing Prem clubs said to also be gaining interest.
"As we celebrate our 30th season the Prem is in a strong position to grow as we look to write a new chapter with new clubs and new stories," said Massie-Taylor, who was on Wednesday due to attend the Sports Business Journal's Dealmakers conference where the CVC chair Marc Allera was due to speak. "Expansion is a unique opportunity to welcome new audiences and clubs and we want to hear from anyone who shares our ambition to make the Gallagher Prem the best league in the world."
