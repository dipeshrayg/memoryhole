Carlos Alcaraz cruised to a second win at this year's ATP Finals, seeing off Daniil Medvedev 6-2, 6-4 despite more physical concerns.
Alcaraz went into the tournament on a nine-match winning streak after collecting titles in Vienna and Paris, and followed up an opening victory over Frances Tiafoe in Turin by defeating Medvedev. He is seeking his first title at the end-of-season event and looks in supreme form, but there are worries over his physical state.
The Spaniard arrived in Italy looking to protect a right-knee issue and took a medical timeout after three games of the second set to have his left hamstring taped. By that stage he was already a set and a break up on Medvedev and he appeared untroubled in wrapping up another rapid victory.
Alcaraz made certain of his place in the semi-finals for a second consecutive year when Frances Tiafoe beat Flavio Cobolli 7-6 (6), 6-0 in the later group match. One of the principal reasons we watch sport is because, unlike real life, we don't know, with crushing and tedious inevitability, what's going to happen. However that doesn't feel like the case in men's tennis at the moment: roughly, if Jannik Sinner and Carlos Alcaraz are healthy, one of them wins, but if they aren't and it's a tournament that really matters, Alexander Zverev wins.
And, if this week has shown us anything, it's that this is even more the case now tan before. Zverev is 2-0 but more telling than that is the the manner of of his wins, neither Arthur Fils nor Ben Shelton able to so much as threaten him - and he has levels in reserve he's not had to bother seeking.
Similarly, Carlos Alcaraz devastated Francis Tiafoe in his first match, the gulf in class and intensity chasmic. Tiafoe couldn't even rustle up the aggression to get down on himself because he knew he was helpless.
On the face of things, our match this afternoon feels like more of the same. Except Daniil Medvedev is the maverick's maverick, capable of the best and worst, in play and attitude - sometimes during the same rally. In typical style, he's been brutal in explaining how little he wants to be playing this tournament and yet, now that he is, he can't be discounted. He's already beaten Alcaraz on hards this season - albeit outdoors - and presents a unique challenge, able to stand way out of court because of how unusually his movement belies his height. If he's at it, he'll make this interesting and fun; if he isn't, it'll be over quicksmart - yet still fun, because Alcaraz can do no other, one aspect of overwhelming predictability we can all embrace.
