The 'nervous system changes' behind Kulusevski's comeback
Dejan Kulusevski returned to competitive action for Tottenham after a 17-month injury layoff to play the last few minutes in the club's 3-1 home victory against Coventry last week and followed that up with another substitute appearance in Saturday's win at Chelsea.
Kulusevski had multiple setbacks after knee surgery in May 2025. But a second procedure in March finally set him on the road to recovery.
Speaking ahead of Saturday's match against Crystal Palace, the 26-year-old Kulusveski told SpursPlay: "It had been like one year and a couple of months and I still can't walk on the stairs. I can't walk on the stairs like a normal guy. I have to play football so in my head I'm not going to give up, but I have to think that if this doesn't work out, what do I do?
"I went to a physio in Sweden. He was able to change my nervous system and then it felt different. When I did the exercises, I said, 'I don't have pain, like this hurt yesterday. Why is this not hurting today?'
He added: "There was still pain but it was different. Now I had control of my leg and then that is when the grind started that summer. I started to play football and it got better every day. I was limping and everything, but it was different now.
"I got much stronger in a short time and I went from 'this guy will not play football any more' to playing really good football in just a couple of weeks." AFP

Fifa to miss equal Women's World Cup prize money ambition set by Infantino
Tom Garry
Fifa is to fall short of gender equality with the prize money on offer at next year's Women's World Cup, despite making a record $15bn (£11.2bn) in revenue from this summer's men's tournament.
Gianni Infantino, the Fifa president, said in 2023 that the organisation's "ambition" was to have "equality" for the prize pot for 2027, saying: "Fifa is stepping up with actions, not just with words." There had, therefore, been widespread hope across the women's game that 2027's prize pot would match the men's on at least a per-team basis.

U-turn on Afcon as tournament reverts to two-year cycle
The Africa Cup of Nations (Afcon) will revert to being held every two years after the continent's ruling body reversed its decision taken last year to move to a four-year cycle.
The African Football Confederation (Caf) said in a statement that it had: "Unanimously decided that the CAF Africa Cup of Nations (Afcon) will return to taking place every two years."
Caf said part of the reasoning for performing a U-turn on the decision was the length of time it would take for Afcon's replacement competition, the Africa Nations League Pan-African Competition, to become financially viable.
"We were also concerned by the several years required before the proposed new Africa Nations League Pan-African competition, (which was supposed to be identical to the current Afcon and take place every two years), could make the substantial financial and football development contributions for African Football and Caf competitions to be globally competitive and self-supporting," Caf president Patrice Motsepe said in the statement.
Motsepe added that the decision was also taken in the interests "of African players employed by football clubs outside Africa". That may prove contentious with their clubs looking at losing key players every two years as opposed to four.
Next year's tournament is being co-hosted by Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda and CAF said the following three would take place in 2028, 2031 and 2033.
"The forthcoming Fifa World Cup will take place in 2030 and in accordance with the Fida tradition, the final tournament of the Afcon and other confederations national team competitions may not take place during the year of the Fifa World Cup," said Motsepe, explaining the three-year gap between the 2028 edition and the scheduled 2031 tournament.
