A match delayed by missile attack warnings is not what Cristiano Ronaldo and co signed up for but it's the new reality; the Saudi sporting juggernaut is stalling
Cristiano Ronaldo grabbed the scarf in the colours of the Saudi Arabian flag and waved it at the sparse but enthusiastic crowd, who responded with rapturous cheers. It was a gesture of defiance rather than a victory salute, encapsulating the Saudi equivalent of "Keep calm and carry on".
Monday night's Asian Champions League Elite game between Al-Nassr and the Uzbek side Neftchi Fergana had ended with a 3-1 win for the Saudi club coached by Ange Postecoglou, with Ronaldo, supported by Sadio Mané, scoring, the smattering of star names indicative of the lure of the Saudi Pro League. Yet this isn't quite how Ronaldo and co would have anticipated life in Riyadh. The meagre attendance aside, Monday's game was delayed by a threat of missile attacks from Yemen's Houthi rebels. A safety warning issued 15 minutes before kick-off came two days after 13 people died and more than 300 were wounded by Houthi strikes on Riyadh's King Khalid international airport.

Real Madrid have again demanded the resignation of the president of La Liga after he compared the club's efforts to discredit referees with the propaganda techniques of Nazi Germany.
Javier Tebas lashed out at Madrid on Friday on social media with a long post that criticised how its club television channel says referees hurt its chances of winning. The channel regularly runs reels of what it considers gross errors by match officials who are set to work upcoming games for the 15-time European champions.
Tebas compared the tactic with those used by Joseph Goebbels, the minister of propaganda in Nazi Germany during the second world war. "The executive of Real Madrid has an orchestra of mouthpieces and Real Madrid leads the way," Tebas wrote. "Its script is always the same: select plays that favor it, repeat the alleged affront, increase suspicion and name enemies. It reminds one of the propaganda techniques that Goebbels used."
Tebas added: "The comparison is with the techniques, not with the ideology or, of course, with Madrid fans."

Leeds' Farke jumps to defence of under-fire Carrick
Daniel Farke has jumped to the defence of Michael Carrick, claiming the under-fire Manchester United boss has done "an amazing job".
Carrick delivered Champions League football via a third-placed finish last season after replacing Ruben Amorim on an interim basis in January and was rewarded in May with a permanent two-year contract. However, the former Middlesbrough manager has come under mounting pressure following United's poor start to the season and Farke said it is undeserved.
The German, whose side face the Reds at Elland Road on Sunday, said: "We don't have too many young British managers in the Premier League, and right now we have someone, a really promising young manager, who has done for me an excellent job at Middlesbrough, and he came in (at United) last season.
"[He] has helped a lot to stabilise the club, more or less like to steady the ship, and has done amazing job and right now, after six gamedays ... the criticism starts already. I don't like it. If we have young, promising managers, come on, let's look after them a bit, especially if they have a British passport."
Farke, who jokingly described himself as "half-British", added: "When you keep in mind what he has done for the club for many years as a player, what he has done last season, where many people would have been hesitant ... he has done a tremendous job.
"I like it when young British managers come through and for that, I don't see why there's so much negativity. I think we should be happy that we have someone with so much potential and don't question them too early." Press Association
