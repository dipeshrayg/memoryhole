Sir Jim Ratcliffe's Ineos Group has reached an out-of-court settlement with Sir Ben Ainslie over the £180m yacht their companies built for the 2024 America's Cup.
Ratcliffe and Ainslie worked closely together during two cycles of the America's Cup, culminating in Ineos Britannia becoming the first British crew to win the challenger series two years ago. But they fell out over plans for a third bid, with Ineos announcing last year that it had "reluctantly withdrawn its challenge" for the 2027 event in Naples.
Ineos launched a legal action in March after expressing "surprise" that Ainslie's Athena Racing had opted to keep using Britannia, which was beaten 7-2 by Emirates Team New Zealand in 2024, in the 2027 challenge.
Court documents filed this year showed Ineos was claiming more than £10m for Athena's alleged refusal to return the boat. The dispute had been set for a trial in January 2028 in the admiralty division of the court of England and Wales, but the claim has been withdrawn.
"Ineos has reached a settlement with Athena Racing and considers the matter now closed," the statement read. No further details, such as whether the yacht has been returned, were provided.
In a statement issued to members of Ainslie's club, Royal Yacht Squadron Ltd, its chair, Michael Grade, said: "Members will no doubt be aware of the ongoing commercial dispute between Ineos and Athena Racing/GB1.
"I am delighted to announce that this dispute has now been settled, with any and all claims between the parties now withdrawn. This is excellent news indeed as GB1 prepares to challenge for the 38th America's Cup in Naples next year. We continue to offer Sir Ben Ainslie and his team our full support as they look to build on their great success in Barcelona in 2024."
Ineos had claimed when withdrawing from the partnership last year that delays in reaching an agreement with Athena Racing had "undermined its ability to prepare" for the event, adding that it had spent £180m on Britannia on top of £170m on the boat used in the 2021 America's Cup campaign. In response, Athena claimed that they had always owned the assets and intellectual property from the two campaigns and that Ineos had been a sponsor.
In a defence filing in June, Athena alleged that Ainslie was warned on the eve of the 2024 America's Cup by Rob Nevin, Ineos Sport's chair, that Ratcliffe would "burn his house down" if he failed to give the billionaire what he wanted.
"We have a phrase at Ineos: 'scorched earth'," Ainslie claimed Nevin warned him in the defence filing. "It means that if you don't give Jim what he wants, he will burn your house down."
An Athena Racing/GB1 spokesperson told the Guardian: "Athena Racing/GB1 have always been, and remain, the owners of the assets, including the AC75 race yacht."
Ineos has had a chequered record since Ratcliffe took the decision for the petrochemical giant to invest in sport in 2019. Since buying the dominant cycling outfit Team Sky, who had won eight Grand Tour titles in eight years including six at the Tour de France, Team Ineos has added only the 2019 Tour de France and 2021 Giro d'Italia to the tally in seven years.
A sponsorship deal with the All Blacks also ended in an acrimonious dispute, with New Zealand Rugby taking legal action last year accusing Ineos of failing to pay the first instalment of a six-year deal, which was settled out of court.
Ineos also reached a settlement with Tottenham, who had alleged they were owed money after the early termination of a sponsorship agreement last year.
Ineos's current sporting investments are Nice, which has been up for sale for more than a year, and Manchester United, who are in the Premier League's relegation zone and last week appointed Mauricio Pochettino as their third permanent manager in less than three years since the company bought a 29% stake in the club.
Ineos declined to comment.
