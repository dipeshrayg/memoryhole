After a toe-curling, stonewalling press conference before the recent one-day series against England, Australia's usually happy-go-lucky white-ball captain, Mitch Marsh, opted out of a repeat before the Twenty20s that start here on Saturday.
In his place came Victoria's Sam Harper to talk about his looming debut at the MCG. A nice local angle, for sure, with the 29-year-old wicketkeeper having overcome some troubling concussions in the past. But also a touch optimistic if intended to switch the focus in Australia away from the sandpaper skip fire.
As per reports this week, Marsh nearly pulled out of the third one-day international in Hobart in anticipation of Cameron Bancroft, his Western Australia teammate, delivering that incendiary 4,000-word statement. In the end he played, pulling off a dazzling catch but then nicking off early during the series-deciding 97-run defeat.
Marsh gets a couple mentions in Bancroft's detailed account of events eight years ago, as one of the four seamers offering him feedback when sandpaper was first being trialled during the second Test in Port Elizabeth (now Gqeberha). When it helped produce a burst of four for 28 before stumps on the second day, Marsh claimed two of them. According to Bancroft, he was "joking about how much the ball was swinging and how he never used to swing it like that".
None of which is damning for Marsh, the only current squad member with links to that ill-fated tour. The 35-year-old did not sign the bowlers' statement in 2021 that denied knowledge of sandpaper use in Cape Town - a denial that Bancroft says he "struggled to reconcile ... with my memories".
Although as Bancroft carefully noted more broadly in that statement: "There is a distinction between knowing a ball is reversing, suspecting it has been altered and knowing specifically that sandpaper has been used."
Amid an increasingly febrile atmosphere in the country, Marsh is not expected to attend any press conferences during this five-match T20 series. Any interviews during the live broadcast on Fox Cricket, such as at the toss or the post-match presentations, are likely to stick to the cricket at hand.
England are back here in March for the 150th anniversary Test match, by which time Cricket Australia will pray the storm has passed. Given the scale of the occasion - the first three days already sold out, around 250 past and current players coming, and the hope of a royal attendee - the alternative would be a nightmare.
This was a point made by Greg Chappell in his column for the Nine newspapers on Friday, with the former Australia captain suggesting an independent inquiry be set up. Cummins should not be sacked from his Test role, he added, unless there is compelling evidence he knew sandpaper was being used.
"The mistake of 2018 was punishing people before the facts were known, and I don't want to see it repeated," wrote Chappell. "Believing others knew isn't the same as proving it. No man should lose his job based on a disputed account, no matter which side it comes from."
For now, at the scene of last year's solitary win during the Ashes, it is Twenty20 cricket that awaits England. On arrival for training at the MCG on Friday, Sam Curran was apparently quick to remind his teammates that Australia's mighty coliseum is where Jos Buttler's class of 2022 lifted the T20 World Cup.
Harry Brook was a rookie back then but now leads a side that have been flying in the format, with 23 wins from their last 26 completed matches. Brook, still to fire with the bat on tour, set a goal for the team of regaining their No 1 ranking - not realising they have recently returned there by dint of India's recent results.
Both sides are using this series as a proving ground for the next cabs off the rank, albeit England are blending this with more big guns. Among them is Phil Salt, back after missing the end of the home summer with injury, and it will make for a rare Welsh-born opening pair when he and Aneurin Donald walk out to the middle.
Brook, who will play the first four games before leaving for the Tests in South Africa, was again asked about Australia's off-field woes and specifically his thoughts on David Saker, the bowling coach who Bancroft claims encouraged him to use sandpaper and was with England in Pakistan last month on a short-term deal.
The England captain declined to comment here but the team's spokesperson later confirmed Saker will not be back due to the fact that two full-time bowling coaches - Tim Southee for white-ball cricket, Eric Simons for Tests - are now on board.
Saker is yet to respond to Bancroft's statement.
