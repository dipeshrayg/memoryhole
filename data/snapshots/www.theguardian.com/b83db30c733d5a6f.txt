Real Madrid coach José Mourinho is up to his old mind game tricks ahead of Sunday's clash with Barcelona. Can poor little Madrid pull off a shock?
"I think the people that thought a week ago Barca would thrash us will keep thinking the same thing. I don't think just for the Paris game, or Madrid's [4-1 win against] Leipzig, they will change their way of thinking," said Mourinho, keen to point out he's been in charge for only a few months whereas that Hansi Flick fella has been at Barca since 2024.
"People look at us like a finished product, but finished products in football are difficult because they are never finished... but they look at us like we're in an advanced phase of the process," continued Mourinho, really warming to the task now.
"We've been working for three months, out of that you can take pre-season when the players were arriving [back from the World Cup], and the recent international break, then we've been together a month-and-a-half, two months...
"This is the start of a product, we're in an initial phase where the coach is learning, analysing, bit by bit defining paths, the players are learning about the coach... comparing a team in this state [to more established sides], I think is unfair."
But now some defiance when asked about Barcelona winger Lamine Yamal. The 19-year-old Spain international is in sublime form and hopes to win the Ballon d'Or award on Monday.
"He's a great player and great players you respect," added Mourinho. One thing is respecting, another thing is to change plans, structures, forgetting your own essence. That we will not do, neither for him or any other player of great quality we face."
