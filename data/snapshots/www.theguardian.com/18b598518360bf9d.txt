Here's the view from DominicSchnider, head global FX & commodity, and GiovanniStaunovo of UBS Global Wealth Management:
The sharp price decline warrants revisiting the drivers of last year's gold rally into 1Q26. Rising expectations of Fed rate cuts, tariff concerns, questions about Fed independence, and perceptions that the US administration favoured a structurally weaker currency created a supportive backdrop.
Higher inflation has since shifted rate expectations toward hikes. Concerns about Fed independence have eased as the Fed has raised rates without material intervention from the US administration. Meanwhile, an AI-driven investment boom has supported US economic activity.
Taken together, these developments could be interpreted as driving a full retracement of gold's rally toward USD 3,500/oz. While this interpretation has some merit, we believe the market is overly optimistic about US economic resilience and may overestimate both the level and duration of higher interest rates.
We expect inflation to level off and US activity to moderate slightly in 2027, allowing rate expectations to shift from further hikes to a pause, followed by cuts later in the year. Beyond these cyclical factors, structural headwinds for the USD remain a topic, notably the US fiscal deficit trajectory.

Retail sales in Great Britain surged last month as the public rushed to buy products including tinned food and long-life milk, after the government issued a warning to stock up in case of a disaster.
