The chief executive of the troubled outsourcing group Capita has abruptly departed as he admitted it has "not delivered" for retired civil servants left waiting for pension payments.
Adolfo Hernandez has agreed to step down with "immediate effect", the company said on Monday. He is to be replaced by the chief financial officer, Pablo Andres.
Shares in Capita plunged by 70% to as low as 72p, Capita's lowest share price since October 1991, on Monday as it cut profit forecasts and proposed raising £190m in extra capital.
Capita said that adjusted operating profit in 2027 was expected to be £85m to £95m lower than previously expected, due to extra costs in relation to the UK Civil Service Pension Scheme (CSPS) contract, and "short-term contract headwinds".
Pensioners have reported being forced to borrow money from family to pay for food and heating, with some saying they feared losing their homes because they could not afford their rent or mortgage.
The reshuffle comes after Capita got into hot water for delays in administering the 1.7 million-member pension scheme, leaving thousands of civil servants waiting for payments and retirement quotes.
Capita, which has government contracts across education and defence, and collects the TV licence fee, agreed the pension contract with the former Conservative government in 2023. It came into force in December 2025.
The company has taken a hit to its finances as a result of the issues delivering the scheme and the implementation of a recovery plan. It also faced a grilling by MPs in parliament last month, with one Cabinet Office minister labelling the company's progress in improving how it runs the scheme as "abysmal".
Hernandez said he recognised the shortcomings that had affected members of the pension scheme.
He said: "It has been a privilege to lead Capita through this phase of its transformation. Three years ago we set out to turn Capita into a technology led, AI-enabled business services company. I am proud of the groundwork we have laid and the progress we have made under my leadership.
"Of course, within that I recognise that we have not delivered the service that members of the civil service pension scheme deserve."
The Guardian first reported on concerns over the pension scheme last year, and earlier this year the government admitted retired civil servants have been failed by the decision to outsource their pension scheme.
Multiple members of the CSPS scheme then came forward to say they were unable to afford rent and have been forced to use food banks after being left without an income. In July, the Cabinet Office confirmed it is hoping to take the scheme back in-house after "unacceptable" service levels.
Last week, Capita said: "We know that some members have been experiencing challenges with the [CSPS]. We're very sorry for any delays and the frustration this is causing."
MPs have blamed the problems on operational failures including overwhelmed revision teams, poor technology and incorrect pension quotes.
Mike Clancy, general secretary of the Prospect union which represents UK civil servants, said: "The running of the Civil Service Pension Scheme has been nothing short of a disaster and caused severe financial hardship to many.
"Adolfo Hernandez stepping down is the right decision but it will be small comfort to those affected. The problems did not start with Capita but their inaction and incompetence have compounded the problems and delayed rectification.
"It is hard to see how change at the top will have a lasting impact. This has been going on for over a year now and started with the previous administrators MyCSP. The only long-term solution that will work for scheme members, and taxpayers, is bring the contract in-house at the first suitable opportunity."
The company plans to raise the £190m in the first half of next year, "to reduce leverage and to allow the business to move forward from a more stable foundation". The decision was supported by its biggest shareholder, the activist investor Oasis Management Company, it said.
