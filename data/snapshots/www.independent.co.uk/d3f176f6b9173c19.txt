Kyiv air raid alerts hit record high - report
Air raid alerts in the Ukrainian capital of Kyiv are at their highest levels of the war so far, as Russia ramps up attacks on the Ukrainian capital, according to a report.
Moscow's new strategy of increasing daytime attacks on the city has forced it to drive up the frequency of its daily air raid alerts, BBC Verify reported in an analysis of Ukrainian government figures.
Residents have been forced to head to the shelters as often as 13 times a day, the analysis said.
'Overnight we all became war correspondents': Ukraine's local newspapers face battle to stay alive
In the weeks leading up to Vladimir Putin's invasion of Ukraine, the headlines in a Kyiv newspaper looked much like they would anywhere else.
There was some debate over whether Russia would actually attack its neighbour. But then, outside of politics, there was everything else: the tourists stuck abroad, changes to school meals and the spread of the Omicron variant of Covid.
On 24 February 2022, everything changed as Russian tanks rolled across the border. Businesses closed or relocated, martial law saw hundreds of thousands of working-age men recruited for military service, and around one million people fled the country in the first week.
A brave few stayed behind to keep a record of what happened as the missiles rained down. Local journalists observed and asked questions as their communities were torn apart by the war.

Eleven dead and six missing after avalanche in Russian mountains
The avalanche hit a group of climbers in the Kabardino-Balkaria region in the North Caucasus on Sunday.
It swept down Mount Mizhirgi, one of the area's highest peaks, around 5pm local time (2pm GMT), hitting a group of 33 climbers, emergency services said.
Ten climbers had made their own way down the mountain as of 9am local time on Monday.

Russia and North Korea open new bridge over Tumen river
North Korea and Russia opened their first road bridge on Monday, Russia's TASS state news agency reported.
The structure spans the Tumen river and is located near the "Friendship Bridge", a rail bridge commissioned in 1959 after the Korean war.
There are already direct air and rail links between Moscow and Pyongyang, who have described the new bridge as yet another milestone in their growing strategic partnership.
Russian prime minister Mikhail Mishustin said the bridge should spur development between the two countries in various spheres - including tourism - and create new jobs.
"I am convinced that the expansion of cross-border transport infrastructure will provide a powerful impetus for the further development of trade, economic, scientific, technological and cultural cooperation," TASS cited Mishustin as saying at the bridge's opening ceremony via video link.
Construction of the new bridge, which had been under discussion for years, began in April last year after its construction was agreed during a visit by President Vladimir Putin to North Korea in 2024.
The two countries' ties have grown stronger since the start of the war in Ukraine in 2022 with Pyongyang sending thousands of troops to Russia's Kursk region to help repel a 2024 Ukrainian incursion there.
