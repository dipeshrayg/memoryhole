Andy Burnham told Britain's young people "this deal is for you" as he landed a landmark agreement with the EU which will bring about the long awaited youth mobility scheme.
The prime minister also overcame a last minute row on tuition fees to get agreement on an end to food and drink checks at the border worth 0.5 per cent or £17bn for the British economy.
European Commission president Ursula von der Leyen described it as a "turning point" in UK-EU relations as the deal agreed includes a carbon sharing agreement on energy, as well as data sharing in tackling criminal gangs behind human trafficking and illegal migration.
But it was the deal on youth mobility which will allow 100,000 UK under-31s to live and work in the EU for up to three years and 100,000 EU under-30s to do the same in the UK that the prime minister took most pride in.
He said: "For young people out there who are wondering how this deal today affects them and their lives, I would say. this deal is for you.
This deal really is about lifting your life chances, and I think we're all conscious that the last decade has been hard for many people, but hard is most of all for the young people of Britain and the young people of the EU.
"I can say to the young people of Britain, you will have, as a result of this deal, a new freedom for anyone under 31. You will have much greater ease and ability to travel to any of the wonderful 27 member states of the European Union to live, to work, to study.
"Hope is the word, and it starts now."
He described the SPS deal on ending checks on food and drink as "the single biggest growth measure we have delivered."
Ms von der Leyen also hailed the agreement on youth mobility, stating: "These opportunities open horizons, and they create friendships that will last a lifetime. So it's a very emotional moment because these are the young people that will shape our future."
She noted that British students will now be able to rejoin the Erasmus+ scheme saying "we have missed you".
But the two sides were clear that the deal is a precursor to the British people making a decision on whether to rejoin the EU or not beyond the next election.
Mr Burnham repeated that "the British people will be the boss in this" but added that "the status quo is not working".
In his opening remarks European Council president António Costa said: "Prime minister, you are always welcome in the EU" adding that Britain had shown "with its leadership on Ukraine" that it was a trusted and valued partner.
Ms von der Leyen said the decision was up to the UK but added: "I have no doubt that the British can make up their minds."
Mr Burnham arrived in Brussels with his most senior ministers confident of landing an agreement but was hit by a final demand by the EU to reduce tuition fees for students from Europe which threatened to derail the talks.
The UK PM made it clear that legislation on the deal will be laid before parliament next week insisting he did not want "any delay" and it is expected it will be in place within six months.
Senior backbench MP Stella Creasy, chair Labour Movement for Europe welcomed the deal but insisted it must only be a "first step".
She told The Independent: "This summit is a first step towards escaping the madness of hard Brexit - the idea that we should make it much harder to trade with our largest market, and cut our security links with a union of 450 million people on our doorstep. ."
Former Labour leader and European commissioner Lord Kinnock told The Independent: "The basic truth always is that if you are not at the table, you are on the menu. We put ourselves on the menu by leaving the EU table. It's a fundamental reason for getting back around the table as a member state and, on many issues - including security, research, and the Single Market - being at the head of it as a major influence.
"Being outside the EU and the Single Market means that our economy loses about (estimates vary) 4 to 8 per cent of National Income,£40-80 billions in tax revenues.
"That's what we could recover in return for membership payments into the EU which would be a small fraction of those sums. It would restore UK significance inside the world's biggest market and strengthen our real terms power over our future - our meaningful sovereignty - in place of isolation. It's practical British patriotism."
Business leaders also called for the deal to be a "springboard".
Shevaun Haviland, director general of the British Chambers of Commerce (BCC), said: "These three deals are a big win for businesses and show what can be achieved when the UK and EU focus on practical solutions.
"But today should be a springboard, not the finish line. The world has changed dramatically since our initial trading relationship was agreed, and Europe is reshaping its support for industries and supply chains. "
Naomi Smith, chief executive of Best for Britain, which campaigns for the UK to restore its EU membership, said: "This marks one small step in our economic fortunes but one giant leap for young people's opportunities, and British businesses who for too long have had their horizons narrowed and wings clipped by needless Brexit red tape.
"Having drawn a line under a decade of drift and division, the Prime Minister must now build on the bold leadership he has shown so far, and take the essential and laudable step towards restoring our EU membership, as the majority of voters the length and breadth of Great Britain now demand."
Emma Knaggs, deputy chief executive of the European Movement UK, has welcomed Andy Burnham's deal with the EU but insisted he must be "clear eyed" that much more needs to be done.
She said: "We welcome today's deal. It starts to dismantle the trade barriers we put up ourselves when we left the single market. British exporters have paid for those barriers in lost sales, and families have paid for them at the supermarket checkout.
"The prime minister says the deal could add up to 0.5 per cent to GDP. We must, though remain clear eyed, that full EU membership is the real prize on offer. Goldman Sachs estimates that joining the EU could make our economy up to 6 per cent larger in the long run. That's billions extra every week."
The travel industry welcomed UK-EU Youth Experience Scheme and offers to help deliver it quickly Seasonal Businesses in Travel (SBiT) urged government to work with industry so young people do not have to wait until 2030.
Charles Owen, managing director of SBiT, said: "This is a great day for young people on both sides of the Channel. For nine years we have argued that the chance to work a season in Europe should never have been lost. Today the UK and the EU have given it back. Three in four people in this country wanted this, including most Leave voters, and the government has listened."
Critics though claimed that Mr Burnham had been "naive" and allowed himself to be strong-armed into making a concession on tuition fees he had wanted to avoid.
Nigel Farage, who was a member of the European Parliament for almost 21 years with Ukip and the Brexit Party, was in Brussels to campaign against the prime minister's deal and launch his own stay out campaign.
The Reform UK leader claimed that Labour had broken its election promise of not rejoining the EU single market by essentially agreeing to join a significant part of it.
He posted on X: "Naive Andy Burnham has gone to Brussels and been hit by the stark reality I've warned about for decades. Flexibility is a fantasy.
"They've used this moment to strong-arm the British prime minister over tuition fee cuts for hundreds of thousands of EU students, and have absolutely no concern for the impact it will have on Britain.
"It's the same old game, and he's fallen for it out of sheer inexperience."
But business and trade secretary Jonathan Reynolds retorted: "If Nigel Farage wants to campaign for more expensive food and drink then he is welcome."
Oliver Lewis, chairman of Stay Out, said: "This whole thing has been a humiliating spectacle for the prime minister. Burnham has been played like a fiddle by Brussels, which took one look at how badly he wants a deal and decided British taxpayers should foot the bill for cheaper fees for EU students before it will sign anything."
