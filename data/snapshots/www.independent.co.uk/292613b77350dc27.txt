Scotland football's ramped-up second-half performance offered some cause for optimism as Sebastien Pocognoli's tenure as head coach began with a goalless draw with Slovenia in Ljubljana.
The 39-year-old Belgian head coach, appointed in August as Steve Clarke's successor, refreshed the side for the Nations League Group B1 opener in the Stozice Stadium, but they were fortunate on a couple of occasions at least not to fall behind before rallying before the break.
Scotland had been insipid and uninspiring for most of the first half but after the interval they played with pace and purpose as Slovenia captain and goalkeeper Jan Oblak became a key figure, with Oli McBurnie and Kieron Bowie both squandering good chances.
However, the goal that would have made the former Union Saint-Gilloise and Monaco manager's debut as an international boss more memorable ultimately failed to arrive.

McBurnie admits 'I should have scored'
Oli McBurnie chats to the BBC after that draw and acknowledged that he should have scored with one of his chances.
McBurnie said: "It took us a bit of getting used to in the first half, the way the gaffer wants to play.
"We created enough chances to win the game. I should have scored.
"I was asked to play a different position and I enjoyed it. I'd rather be getting in the positions than not getting chances.
"Second half was a lot more of what the manager wanted from us. It was waves of pressure and the only thing missing was a goal or two."
