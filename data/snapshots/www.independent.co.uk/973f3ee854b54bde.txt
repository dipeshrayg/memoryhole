We've been fostering cats for a local charity for more than 10 years and, while it's an incredibly rewarding thing to do, our poor ancient vacuum cleaner has really been put through its paces over the years. So, who better than me to test the latest pet vacuums to see which ones are the best for dealing with the seemingly constant moulting of the cats?
You might think that any vacuum will deal with pet hair efficiently but in fact, your pet's hair is notoriously tricky to vacuum up, as Georgina Shepherd, cleaning expert at Housekeep, explains: "Pet hair isn't like normal dust or dirt. Individual hairs are very light, so they can become trapped in carpet fibres, upholstery and rugs rather than sitting on the surface. Long pet hair can wrap itself around carpet fibres and vacuum brush rolls, while shorter hairs can work their way deeper into fabric. And finally, static electricity is another challenge. Hair can cling to fabrics and soft furnishings, making it harder for a vacuum to lift it away."
All this means that I put a great deal of vacuum cleaners through their paces to find the best options for your home. From cordless vacuums to cylinder vacuums, not forgetting the automation of a robot vacuum cleaner, there are options here to suit all homes.

Shark vacuums are well known for the brand's excellent anti-hair wrap heads, and the pet pro is no exception. In a house frequently covered with clumps of cat fur and strands of my own long hair, the Shark breezed through carpets and hard floors alike, pulling up every bit of hair in its path. This is down to the impressive suction, which pulls hair and debris towards its head, rather than just pushing it along until it gets sucked up. LED lights on the front also help to make it easier to avoid missing any debris at all.
This is a cordless model, but you'll get around 60 minutes run time in the eco mode, which I found more than powerful enough for everything except the most stubborn debris. The other big selling point of the Shark is that it comes with a docking station containing a 2l bin that Shark says will store up to 45 days of debris (don't worry, there's also an odour cartridge to prevent nasty niffs!). The downside is that this means you only have a 0.47L bin on board - I found this large enough to vacuum my entire two bedroom house, but larger homes might find it a bore to have to keep emptying it. You'll also only get two attachments apart from the main head, with a motorised pet tool and crevice tool with duster included. Honestly though, I found these handled everything I needed them to and overall, the Shark is a fantastic choice for homes with pets.

The word 'budget' is a bit of an oxymoron when it comes to specialist pet vacuums, which normally command a slightly higher fee due to specialist attachments. I was particularly impressed with Hoover's anti-twist brush bar, which did a fantastic job in our cat fur test. It vacuumed up all the hair without fuss, although if I was being ultra picky, I would point out that a couple of my long hairs did catch round the roller and had to be pulled out. There's a clever rocker switch to change the HL2 from carpet to hard floor mode, and I also really liked the fact that you can carry accessories on board the vacuum. LED lights also do a fantastic job of picking out dust and debris. The only thing I would note is that the Hoover doesn't feel quite as substantial as some of its more expensive rivals in this list but then, of course, you are paying considerably less.

Dyson's V16 finally embraces just using one head that will adjust to all floor types. And if you're a pet owner, what a head it is! The anti-hair wrap technology on the V16 is the best I've ever come across, with the head cleanly sucking up hair before bundling it up to the sides and then shooting it up the extension wand to the bin. The laser is a useful feature, brilliantly highlighting every last bit of debris and I particularly enjoyed the 'cleancompaktor', which is simply a lever on the side of the bin that allows you to fit more dirt into it by sliding down an internal plate to compress the debris. One thing I did notice is that the vacuum can struggle with finer particles such as flour, as this can clog up the motor and cause it to register an error. It's also pretty heavy at the handheld vacuum end, which could be an issue for some users.

Robot vacuums seem like a dream idea but they're often not a great fit for pet owners, with the rollers easily getting tangled up with pet hair. The Mova Z60 gets round this with a dual rubber brush / rubber-bristle brush combo that does an excellent job of scooping hair up without tangling the roller. I also liked the fact that it automatically extends when it detects corners.
The Z60 is also a combined vacuum and mop, so it will clean paw prints off your hard surfaces as well as cleaning up cat hair. It does a good job of detecting between hard and carpeted areas as well, so no danger of ending up with soggy carpets. However, it did struggle a little with large clumps of pet hair, occasionally just running straight over them rather than vacuuming up. This was usually rectified on another pass though.
There's lots of flexibility on the app, allowing you to either custom tune each room cleaning or allow the AI system to make recommendations. However, I did find that the app could be a bit glitchy at times and did sometimes get confused about which room it was in.
