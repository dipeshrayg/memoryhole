SpaceX has launched the world's most powerful rocket into orbit for the first ever time.
Elon Musk's private space company is conducting its 14th test flight of Starship, and the first to try and reach orbit.
The mission launched at 7.48am local central time, or 1.48pm in the UK, lifting off from SpaceX's "Starbase" facility in Texas.
The spacecraft suffered an engine failure, putting its trip into orbit in doubt, but SpaceX continued on its plan to deploy 26 Starlink space internet satellites. After orbiting the planet six times, Starship is then set to splashdown in the Pacific Ocean, roughly 10 hours after launch.
It is seen as a major test both of SpaceX's future plans and its role in helping Nasa return to the Moon and then onto Mars.
The whole launch is available to watch online and covered in detail on The Independent. Follow below for all the latest on the launch - and what it means for our plans to head into space.

First Starlink satellite deploys
Starship has deployed the first of 26 Starlink V3 satellites. The next 25 should pop out over the next 30 minutes.
These are the first operational Starlink satellites to make it to orbit aboard a Starship rocket, and will help rapidly expand the space internet network.

Starship is orbital
Starship has made it to orbit! The engine burn was successful, marking a huge milestone for SpaceX. It is the first time the world's biggest and most powerful rocket has made it into orbit.
Next step will be getting 26 Starlink V3 satellites into orbit.
'We are go for orbit'
SpaceX has changed its mind - it is now going to attempt to send Starship to orbit, even if one of its engines has failed.
"We are go for orbit," SpaceX says.
The news gets big cheers from the SpaceX ground crew.
Starship is going to coast through space for a few more minutes before the orbital burn is attempted. If successful, the next big step will be deploying the Starlink satellites into orbit.
SpaceX to make 'go/ no go' decision for orbital mission
The engine failure might not prevent Starship from reaching orbit. We're just waiting to hear the final decision from SpaceX...
BREAKING: Starship not heading to orbit
Due to that engine going out, SpaceX is no longer planning to send Starship to orbit.
"We are not going to do the additional burn that will send us into orbit," SpaceX says.
Super Heavy booster splashes down
The Super Heavy booster has splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico after separating from the upper stage Starship rocket.
It was a "fairly soft" splashdown, according to the SpaceX team, meaning it didn't explode as soon as it hit the water.
One of the Starship engines has failed, though it's continuing its mission to orbit. "We need to check in and see what exactly that means for the rest of the mission here," SpaceX says.
Stage separation complete
The upper stage has successfully separated from the Super Heavy booster.
