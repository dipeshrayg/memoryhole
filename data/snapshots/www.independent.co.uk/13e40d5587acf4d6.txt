Comment: What is the real reason the CIA boss John Ratcliffe made a secret trip to Moscow?
Peter Frankopan writes:
Earlier this week, a C17 Globemaster descended into Moscow's Vnukovo airport. Although it was hopping in from Riga, Latvia, the plane had originated at Andrews Air Force Base, close to Washington DC. On board was John Ratcliffe, the director of the CIA. After being whisked into the Russian capital in a motorcade, a few hours later, Ratcliffe was up in the air again and on his way home.
Who Ratcliffe met was not clear. The visit had not been announced in advance in the US, while Kremlin spokesman Dimitry Peskov claimed to have no knowledge or information about the arrival of a US Air Force plane.
Not surprisingly, there has been much speculation as to why the most senior figure in US intelligence had gone to Moscow in person. After all, if Ratcliffe had an important message to share or communicate, there were other, easier and safer ways to do so than spending hours in a plane travelling to Russia.
Some speculated that Ratcliffe's mission involved Iran, perhaps a request for Moscow's help in focusing Tehran's mind about a workable settlement that might lead to the full re-opening of the Strait of Hormuz, or perhaps a warning that Putin's government should be careful in its dealings with a country that is the focus not only of US military but economic pressure: ties between Russia and Iran have been strong for years, with the latter a source of prodigious numbers of Shahed drones that have been used in the war against Ukraine.
