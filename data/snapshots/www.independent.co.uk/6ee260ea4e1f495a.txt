The Indian government shored up security and deployed riot-control vehicles in the capital, Delhi, as prime minister Narendra Modi's rivals held a mega rally demanding the resignation of the country's election chief.
Thousands of people took to the streets of Delhi on Sunday to join the rally led by Congress party leader Rahul Gandhi.
Protesters carried musical instruments and waved various flags as they chanted slogans against the policies of the central government. Several opposition parties of the alliance, formed in 2023 to unseat Mr Modi, mobilised their cadres from different states for the protest, which comes weeks after a police crackdown on protesters in Delhi.
Earlier, the bloc of opposition parties said the number of people joining the "Delhi Chalo" (come to Delhi) march from the central parts of the capital city to the designated protest site of Ram Leela Maidan could reach a million.
Opposition parties, student organisations, and the youth-led Cockroach Janta Party (CJP) have held near-daily demonstrations since last month demanding that chief election commissioner Gyanesh Kumar step down over the voter list revision that critics say favours Mr Modi's ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP).
The state responded to the protest by turning the capital into a fortress with hundreds of barricades, detaining students, protest leaders, politicians and journalists.
Observers say the youth protests since summer and the growing support for the opposition have shaped into the worst crisis for Mr Modi since he came to power in 2014. But his government remains stable, and the next general election is not due until 2029.
The protesters gathered at the sprawling public square after police last month withdrew Jantar Mantar from the list of officially designated venues for demonstrations. The historic observatory had long served as the capital's main protest site.
The protest march follows a symbolic "public impeachment" the opposition parties held on 31 October, where six charges were leveled against Mr Kumar.
The protests demanding his resignation erupted after the Indian Express newspaper reported in September that his two deputies had raised several concerns internally about voter list revisions, registration procedures, and digital systems.
The change, which began last year, removed over 130 million names from voter lists, including 28 million who died, 63 million who relocated, and 30 million who could not be traced.
Opposition parties have alleged that exclusion of voters through the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) exercise could benefit Mr Modi's party by widening the gap between the ruling party and the opposition. The critics claimed the deletions disproportionately affect Muslim voters and other minorities, who are unlikely to support the Hindu nationalist ruling party.
The CJP, which forced the resignation of the country's education minister, Dharmendra Pradhan, with sustained month-long protests, labelled Mr Kumar a "threat to Indian democracy".
India's election commission said the SIR was aimed at updating the voters' list by purging names of dead people and those who have moved away.
The BJP has defended the poll panel and said opposition parties had launched a smear campaign because they were regularly losing elections.
Riot control vehicles were stationed in parts of the city while dozens of security personnel armed with batons and shields outside the rally venue were parked.
Delhi Traffic Police said "a large gathering" was expected at the venue, and traffic regulations and diversions would be implemented "as required".
"People have started coming to participate in this rally...we have ensured proper arrangements at the venue, and arrangements are also planned on the route of the march that will happen in a few hours from now," senior police officer Rohit Rajbir told ANI.
The opposition bloc has accused the government of trying to prevent people from travelling to the capital from rest of the country to join the protest.
"Don't worry about the police, don't worry about lathis [batons] - the BJP will not have the guts to try any of their antics," wrote Mr Gandhi on X.
Federal minister JP Nadda has called the rally a "foolish and orchestrated drama" aimed at destabiling the government "at the behest of foreign powers".
Human Rights Watch on Thursday said the accusations over the voter roll revision raise "serious questions about the fairness of future Indian elections."
"The authorities should immediately scrap the voter roll revision process that has denied India's most marginalized people their right to vote and other government services," said Elaine Pearson, the group's Asia director, in a statement.
