Donald Trump has said he will soon declare the Strait of Hormuz a US territory.
The US president escalated his rhetoric over the vital waterway that carries a significant share of global oil supplies, as he spoke to the media on Friday.
He also told Fox News that the US will hit Iran hard economically as Washington and Tehran struggle to reach a deal to end a nearly six-month conflict.
His comments come as the US prepares to deploy the USS George Washington to the Middle East to relieve military personnel stationed on the USS Abraham Lincoln for more than 200 days amid growing concerns about living conditions on the aircraft carrier, according to reports.
The Lincoln has been deployed for more than 250 days and has not made a port call in 200 days, setting a record for consecutive days at sea, according to US senator Richard Blumenthal, who wrote to Pentagon chief Pete Hegseth about concerns for those on board.
Blumenthal cited reports of "shortages of basic supplies, water contamination, plumbing issues, deteriorating mental health, deck safety concerns, and disruptions in the mail system".
Trump officials have dismissed concerns about the conditions of the USS Lincoln.
US seeking mediation from more European countries - report
The Trump administration appears to be seeking support from more countries to help pressure Iran to stop the war, reports suggest.
Earlier this week, US secretary of state Marco Rubio met with the foreign minister of Austria in Washington and held a call with the foreign minister of Greece, AP News reported.
While official readouts of the discussions did not mention Iran by name, both foreign ministers reportedly spoke with Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi later in the week.
The diplomatic move comes as both the US and Iran are pushing for control of the Strait of Hormuz, and negotiations between countries including Pakistan and Oman with Iran have progressed at an uneven pace.

Poll: Just 29% of expected midterm voters say they approve of Trump's handling of Iran
A cable TV polling expert claims that numbers from two surveys conducted two years apart show just how deeply US President Donald Trump has disappointed America's voters when it comes to Iran.
CNN chief data analyst Harry Enten said Tuesday that a July 2024 poll of people who were extremely motivated to vote in that year's presidential election found that 53 percent were confident Trump would be able to handle the situation in Iran, according to a video clip posted online.
But a July poll of people extremely motivated to vote in November's midterm elections shows that just 29 percent now approve of how Trump is actually handling Iran, Enten said.
"We're talking about a 24-point fall in just two years", he said. "What was an advantage for President Trump going into the 2024 election amongst those who really wanted to turn out and vote has turned into a tremendous disadvantage this time around".

Father of USS Lincoln sailor said he's heard 'positive feedback,' but admits mental toll
The father of a sailor aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln has said he's heard "pretty positive feedback" about the aircraft carrier's conditions, but admits to the mental toll deployment has taken on the service members.
"As far as my particular child, I've heard pretty positive feedback about the conditions there", he told CNN. "And we understand that they're under a lot of mental stress and have nothing but sympathy and respect for the hardships and the stress that are inherent to the operation".
It follows reports of poor maintenance, food supply issues and sailors struggling with their mental health on the aircraft carrier deployed to the Middle East for more than 200 days.

Israeli officials shocked by the speed of Iran's military recovery after war, report says
Israeli military and intelligence officials were stunned by the rate of Iran's military recovery after the first weeks of the 2026 war, according to a report.
According to the Jerusalem Post, the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) was taken aback by how fast the Islamic Republic was able to reconstitute its forces after the main phase of fighting, including the redevelopment of its ballistic missile threat.
Iran launched missiles at Israel at the beginning of June and rose to an escalation in early July with renewed strikes on Arab states in the region. They were also accused of striking ships in the Strait of Hormuz, maintaining a hold over the vital waterway.
