Andy Burnham has proposed removing standing charges from energy bills as he faces a grilling at prime minister's questions.
His government's decision to approve drilling in the Jackdaw gas field in the North Sea has drawn criticism from Labour MPs, as well as climate activists, who are now pressuring the prime minister to reject the Rosebank oil and gas field project.
Zack Polanski described Wednesday's move as "a betrayal of all of our futures" and said Labour had "abandoned all credibility in the fight against climate change."
It comes after the prime minister said it would "protect hundreds of jobs and help keep millions of homes warm", but stressed it would not hinder the government's targets on renewable energy.
Energy secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh has told Britons not to expect lower energy bills this winter, telling BBC that the "key consideration in coming to this decision was at a time when we are in an energy crisis triggered by the conflict in the Middle East.
"Energy security is really important and this will contribute to the homegrown sources of energy that we have," she added.
Burnham suggests government could scrap standing charges from gas bills
Whitehall editor Kate Devlin writes:
The PM made the announcement as he came under pressure from the Tories over today's Jackdaw go-ahead.In a surprise move, he told MPs the decision would allow ministers "to do more to help people with bills".
He added: "I can tell the House today that I have asked the chancellor and the energy secretary to look at whether we can remove standing charges from gas bills and to report back to this House at the earliest opportunity."
Standing charges are a fixed daily fee added to energy bills and paid regardless of how much energy a household uses.
Ofgem estimates the average gas daily standing charge for those who pay by direct debit is currently 29.68p.
Burnham proposes removing standing charges from energy bills
Andy Burnham has said he is considering removing standard charges from energy bills as he faces scrutiny over the government's approval of the Jackdaw project.
He told the Commons that he had asked the chancellor and energy secretary to look at removing standing charges from gas bills.
Analysis: Burnham makes it clear that the door is open for Rosebank
Our political editor David Maddox reports:
Andy Burnham has upset the left of his party by giving the green light for drilling in the Jackdaw oil and gas field in the North Sea.
But his answer to Tory leader Kemi Badenoch has made clear that he is planning to go further.He blames the last Tory government for not doing the legal process properly in 2022 for the delay in Jackdaw being drilled for gas.
But he went on to note that the energy secretary Miata Fahnbulleh had "looked at the decision in relation to our climate obligations" and "will be doing the same with similar decisions going forward".
This is in reference to the Rosebank oil and gas field in the North Sea. It is clear he wants drilling to go on there.
This is not least because he agrees with Ms Badenoch that every barrel of oil the UK gets from the North Sea is one less Europe does not need to buy from Vladimir Putin.
Despite a jibe from Ms Badenoch that she thought she was listening to Mr Burnham's predecessor, we are already seeing some changes with the Starmer government in the confidence Mr Burnham is approaching making big decisions.
Burnham marks 60th anniversary of Aberfan disaster
Andy Burnham opened PMQs by marking the 60th anniversary of the Aberfan disaster, paying tribute to the "strength and spirit of a community that has stood together through adversity all of these years".
An entire generation was almost wiped out when 150,000 tonnes of coal waste slid down the hillside before engulfing Pantglas Junior School in Wales on October 21 1966, killing 116 children and 28 adults.
Addressing the Commons, the prime minister said: "Today also marks the 60th anniversary of the Aberfan disaster and I'm sure the thoughts of the whole house will be with the community today and with the people of Wales.
"I visited on Monday and heard how the injustices the people suffered at the time and in the aftermath of the tragedy at the hands of the state still hurt.
"This is a day to recognise that, say sorry for it, but also celebrate the strength and spirit of a community that has stood together through adversity all of these years."
Badenoch takes swipe at Labour's 'dithering' over Jackdaw
Kemi Badenoch has hit out at Labour's "dithering" over the Jackdaw project.
"The Conservatives first approved Jackdaw in 2022. Rather than fighting the leftie activists that tried to fight it, Labour appointed one of them as energy secretary," she told the Commons.
"So many jobs lost, so much time and money wasted.
"Britain cannot be a serious player on the world stage if the government dithers over approving a single license."
Government has been clear that Jackdaw approval won't lower bills - energy minister
The government has been "very clear" that new oil and gas will not take a penny of bills, energy minister has said.
Michael Shanks told MPs that the project will "do other things" for the public.
"I think we need to be really clear. The vast majority of British homes are heated by gas and so while we have to electrify the economy and move away from gas as home heating - that is not going to happen overnight.
"And so our supply does matter. And if we can get it from the continental shelf, that's important. That's the judgment the energy secretary has made on this particular project.
"It's also important for jobs and it's also important for a source of revenue that we're going to fund into the transition."
Dale Vince defends Labour over Jackdaw approval
Labour donor and green industrialist Dale Vince has rejected climate concerns over the government's approval of drilling at Jackdaw gasfields.
Defending the move, the founder of Ecotricity said on Wednesday: "The fuss made by environmentalists is inversely proportional to the impact of this. It's a tiny gas field that already had a licence.
"It will lower our carbon footprint as a country - not by much, because it is tiny. It won't bring bills down; the government said this today - at last, some honesty on this topic.
"It won't add to energy security by any significant degree, because it is tiny - holding just six months of annual UK consumption in total. It won't bring lots of new jobs - just 27 - again, government honesty, but Jackdaw will support a couple of hundred existing ones. It's really a lot of fuss about nothing.
"This is a pragmatic decision; it's the right one - this project had a licence from the Tories. When new governments tear up existing contracts, that's bad for business confidence, and it's also not moral. Giving this overheated project the go-ahead ought to quiet the increasingly shrill voices on the right, and voices on the left or green side of life just need to see it in perspective.
"A tiny project, consented to by the Tories, won't change much of anything. On emissions, it will lower them (but not by much). The most important thing is that we get on with the green transition - £2 billion more announced for that today."
Andy Burnham cheerleader disappointed by Jackdaw decision
Richard Burgon was one of the biggest cheerleaders for Andy Burnham replacing Keir Starmer as Labour leader and prime minister.
But the Leeds MP who leads the Socialist Campaign Group has not had a job offered to him and now has been left disappointed by the decision to allow oil and gas drilling at the Jackdaw site in the North Sea.
He posted on X: "An absolutely terrible decision on Jackdaw.
"It will do nothing to lower people's energy bills or improve our energy security.
"Investing in renewable energy is the way to do that.
"The government has got this badly wrong. It must not repeat the same mistake with Rosebank."
He and his allies are finding out that not much is changing with a Burnham government from a Starmer one.
Burnham to be grilled at PMQ's over Jackdaw approval
Andy Burnham is set to be grilled at prime minister's questions after the government approved drilling at Jackdaw gasfields this morning.
He has faced sharp criticism from MPs within his party, as well as climate activists, who are now pressuring him to reject the Rosebank oil and gas field project.
At the same time, the prime minister is also being urged to approve the project at the largest untapped oil field in the North Sea.
