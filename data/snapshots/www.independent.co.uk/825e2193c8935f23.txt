The United States is preparing to send another aircraft carrier to the Middle East after Donald Trump vowed to continue the war with Iran "for as long as necessary" and without a timetable to resume peace talks.

Iran facing 'full-scale economic war,' but will win, says Tehran
Iran's Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf has said the country is in "a full-scale economic war."
Speaking to the state's IRNA news agency, Ghalibaf assured the public that in spite of the country's financial spiral, Iran "will defeat the enemy in this field as well."
Iran is facing extreme pressures on its domestic economy after the US announced a barrage of unilateral sanctions against the state on Monday.
Nuclear watchdog dismisses US claims of Iranian nuclear weapon
The United Nations' nuclear watchdog chief, Rafael Grossi, has said there is no "solid evidence" to suggest that Iran is rebuilding its nuclear facilities following US and Israeli attacks.
In an interview published by Argentinian newspaper Clarin, Grossi said that talks on Tehran's nuclear programme remain stalled because of the war.
Both the US and Israel's leaders have previously stressed that preventing the potential development of Iran's nuclear programme remains a political priority.

Foreign ministry spokesperson, Taher Andarab has said that Washington's sanctions on Iran do not adhere to international law, and that issues should be resolved through dialogue rather than an "economic D-day".
Addressing reporters in Islamabad on Thursday, Andarab stressed that diplomacy is the pathway to ending the US-Iran conflict.
Following the outbreak of the war, Pakistan has acted as the primary mediator between both states.

No military vessels to be allowed in Hormuz, Iran says
Under the Iran-Oman deal agreed on Wednesday, no military vessels will be allowed to enter the Strait of Homuz, Tehran has said.
Speaking to state media, Iran's foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi said: "If the agreement becomes binding, no military vessel will be allowed through the Strait of Hormuz ... No military vessels whatsoever."
The deal is currently subject to US approval, and uncertainty continues to dominate the future of the waterway.
Tehran slams Trump's policies as 'pure parody'
Iranian foreign ministry spokesperson, Esmaeil Baqaei has ridiculed Washington's latest economic pressure campaign, describing US policy toward Tehran as having "reached pure parody."
In a statement posted on X on Thursday, Baqaei criticised the Trump administration for boasting about allied support from Bahrain, noting that the Gulf state has "virtually no meaningful economic exchange with Iran" to demonstrate that its coalition is working.
Accusing Trump of dressing up symbolic policy moves, Baqaei added that additional US measures such as halting Iranian sports and academic exchanges targets programs that have already been defunct for years.
Pakistan says Iran talks focused on de-escalation
Pakistan's foreign ministry has said that talks held between military chief, Syed Asim Munir and Tehran focused on regional de-escalation.
A spokesperson for the ministry shared: "The discussions during the visit focused on regional de-escalation, breaking the military stalemate and exploring a negotiated settlement, restoring regional peace and stability."
Pakistan continues to be a primary contributor to mediation efforts in the region, where the state hosted the landmark Islamabad Peace Talks in April, facilitating the first direct, high-level, in-person negotiations between Washington and Tehran since the 1979 revolution.
Greek Patriot battery intercepts drones over Saudi oil hub
A Greek-operated Patriot air defence battery in Saudi Arabia has intercepted a drone swarm over its Yanbu region on Thursday, Greek security sources told Reuters.
The Greek system, which was deployed to safeguard major energy infrastructure, fired three interceptor missiles in coordination with Saudi forces.
While nobody has claimed responsibility for the drones, security context indicates that Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi movement may be the source of the swarm.
The Houthis have repeatedly targeted Yanbu -Saudi Arabia's crucial Red Sea oil export terminal - using asymmetric drone and missile strikes to disrupt bypass routes around the Strait of Hormuz.

CIA chief pressed Russia to reduce support for Iran during Moscow trip
CIA director John Ratcliffe used a rare, unannounced visit to Moscow on Tuesday to press Russian officials to reduce their military and economic support for Iran, Politico reported, citing a person familiar with the trip and a UK official with knowledge of the visit.
Ratcliffe also warned Russia against escalating conflict with US Nato allies, particularly Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, amid concerns over Moscow's growing threats to alliance members supporting Ukraine.
The trip was the first known visit to Moscow by a CIA director under president Donald Trump and was not publicly acknowledged by the administration until Wednesday.
Trump described the visit as "semi-routine" and said it was aimed at helping bring the war in Ukraine to an end.
