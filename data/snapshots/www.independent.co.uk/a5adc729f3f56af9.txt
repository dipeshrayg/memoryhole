Older people are being urged to receive flu vaccinations after modelling indicated nearly 4,000 deaths in England were caused by flu during last winter.
Age UK called on older people to "not get complacent" and ensure they get all the vaccinations they are eligible for.
Figures from the UK Health Security Agency's (UKHSA) annual report indicate excess deaths from flu reached 3,798 last winter.
Caroline Abrahams, charity director at Age UK, said: "Nearly 4,000 deaths linked to flu in a single winter is a stark reminder that these seasonal viruses should never be dismissed as just a bad cold, particularly in later life.
"The latest evidence shows that vaccination prevented thousands of hospital admissions last winter and helped reduce the pressure on NHS services at one of the busiest times of the year.
"If you are offered a flu, Covid-19 or RSV vaccination this autumn and winter, we strongly encourage you to take up the opportunity."
The report also found that England's vaccination rollout prevented an estimated 104,000 hospital admissions along with 7,100 deaths.
This occurred even though overall vaccine efficacy among adults dropped from the previous winter down to between 21% and 34%.
Among children, who often transmit flu to older groups and can fall severely ill, vaccine effectiveness was recorded at 51% to 68%.
UKHSA data also showed the RSV vaccine was about 82% effective at preventing hospitalisations for adults aged 75 to 79.
RSV is a common respiratory virus causing typically mild, cold-like symptoms, though it can ultimately prove fatal to older people.
Although flu cases are currently low, hospital admissions in September have been higher than in the same period over the last four years, according to the UKHSA's first weekly winter respiratory virus surveillance report.
Ms Abrahams added: "These vaccines can reduce your risk of becoming seriously ill and help you stay well, independent and active throughout the colder months.
"Many older people are already facing challenges this winter, including concerns about energy costs, keeping warm and managing existing health conditions.
"Having the vaccinations you are entitled to is one practical step you can take to protect your health.
"I would also encourage friends and family members to check that older relatives know which vaccinations they are eligible for and help them to arrange appointments if needed."
Commenting on the UKHSA data, Andrew McCracken, director of External Affairs at Asthma + Lung UK, said the sooner people can get your flu jab, the better.
"The flu season has started and the sooner you get your vaccine, the sooner you will be protected. That's especially true for older people with chronic lung conditions, who are particularly vulnerable," he said.
"Catching flu doesn't just mean a few days in bed. If you live with an existing lung condition, flu can trigger life-threatening flare ups leading to emergency hospitalisation.
"The latest evidence shows that people living with chronic lung conditions are seven times more likely to die from flu.
"Everyone with a chronic respiratory condition in England is now eligible for a free flu jab and taking it up as soon as possible is the best way to keep yourself safe and well."
Separate UKHSA figures published on Thursday suggest flu is currently circulating at low levels, having previously shown signs of an early rise in infections.
Hospital admissions for flu in England currently stand at 1.9 per 100,000 people, unchanged from a week ago and roughly the same rate as this point in 2025.
Flu levels last year went on to peak at the start of December, which was earlier than usual.
Dr Jamie Lopez-Bernal of the UKHSA said the agency would be "closely tracking" all the major respiratory viruses throughout the winter, including both flu and Covid-19, to "spot trends early and keep the public informed".
He added: "While currently these virus levels are low, we can't easily predict what may happen over the coming weeks and vaccination remains our best defence.
"If eligible, book your free NHS flu, Covid-19 or RSV vaccine or ensure your child receives their easy and painless nose spray flu vaccine as soon as possible, before viruses start circulating more widely this winter."
Health minister Dame Diana Johnson said: "During the winter months, dangerous viruses spread more easily, posing an increased risk to older people and those with existing health conditions, who are more vulnerable.
"I am proud to support Age UK's campaign and encourage everyone who has not yet come forward to book life-saving vaccinations against flu, Covid-19 and RSV as soon as possible."
Dr Amanda Doyle from NHS England said that more than seven million people have already had their flu vaccine, adding: "If you're eligible for a free NHS flu vaccination, including if you're pregnant or have a young child who is offered the vaccine, please do come forward as soon as possible to help protect yourself and those around you."
