Thanksgiving is just around the corner, and cost-conscious shoppers have reason to give thanks.
Grocery stores nationwide have launched their annual Thanksgiving meal deals, with several selling bundles for under $40. That's roughly $15 below the national average for a 10-person meal if items are purchased separately, according to agriculture advocate Farm Bureau.
The meals typically include a turkey, sides and a dessert and can feed anywhere from six to 10 people, enough food for a smallish, extended family gathering or Friendsgiving.
"These bundles are great value for this tough year that most households are experiencing since inflation, higher gas prices, and higher agricultural inputs are pushing up food prices," Bryan Quoc Le, owner of food science consultancy Mendocino Food Consulting, told The Independent in an email.
"These bundles give relief for families that are in need of a nice meal at an affordable price point for everyone."
Grocers aren't just in it to win points with shoppers, though - they're hoping for something more, Le said.
"These deals are a great way for these big box stores to bring in customers on some of the busiest grocery-shopping days of the year," Le said. "If they can get them in the door, it's a guarantee that customers will purchase higher-margin items such as wine or bulk items for the holidays."
Cost plays a central role not only in consumers' finances but in their attitudes too, according to a Walmart report released in October. Some 60 percent of shoppers say holiday costs shape their mood, the report found.
"For many shoppers, the price tag sets the tone," the company said.
The Independent chose the bundles below based on their per-person cost or overall cost. Eligible meal deals offered a whole turkey, sides and a dessert.
Leading the pack
Lidl, a German-based grocery store, has the cheapest Thanksgiving deal available this season: $34.40 for a meal that feeds up to 10 people, or $3.40 per person.
The bundle includes ingredients for the following main course, sides and dessert:
- 14-pound turkey, pumpkin pie filling, pie crust dough, 3 pounds of yellow onions, 5 pounds of Russet potatoes, 3 pounds of sweet potatoes, fresh celery, chicken broth, 2 cans of condensed mushroom soup, 2 cans of green beans, evaporated milk, French-fried onions, whipped topping, 2 packets of gravy mix, 2 cans of jellied cranberry sauce, mini marshmallows, Hawaiian sweet rolls, poultry seasoning, 2 boxes of stuffing mix, 2 pounds of baby carrots and 2 boxes of macaroni and cheese.
The Lidl bundle makes one turkey, stuffing, mashed potatoes, sweet potato casserole, green bean casserole, mac and cheese, rolls and a pumpkin pie.
Lidl requires customers to sign up for its free "Lidl Plus" loyalty program to get the $34.40 bundle price. Experts say that there should be enough turkey for 1 to 1.25 pounds of uncooked meat per person. Lidl's Thanksgiving meal offer runs through November 25.
Long-lasting bargain
Walmart, the nation's biggest brick-and-mortar retailer, provides a $35 Thanksgiving meal for 10 people, or $3.50 per person. The meal's price is based on a 13.5-pound turkey - a bigger turkey will increase the deal's price.
The bundle includes 26 ingredients that make a turkey, stuffing, cranberry sauce, mashed potatoes and gravy, green bean casserole, sweet potato casserole, corn casserole and pumpkin pie:
- 10- to 16-pound turkey, 2 boxes of corn muffin mix, 3 cans of corn, 3 cans of green beans, one can of condensed cream of mushroom soup, French-fried onions, 5 pounds of Russet potatoes, 2 packets of gravy mix, pie crusts, whipped topping, pumpkin filling, evaporated milk, rolls, 2 boxes of stuffing, fresh cranberries, mini marshmallows and 3 sweet potatoes.
Walmart's Thanksgiving meal has 18 percent more items than last year but costs 12 percent less, according to the company. The bundle's standout feature is that it's available through December 24, while Lidl, Aldi and Amazon's meal-deal pricing ends on or before Thanksgiving.
Walmart's two-month meal deal not only makes the affordable meal available for Thanksgiving and Christmas, but also helps Walmart better plan for the holiday shopping season, Chad Brinton, a general manager at supply-chain software firm Atomic, said in an email to The Independent.
"By running the deal over a few weeks, grocers spread the demand out so every shopper isn't hitting the store the same weekend," Brinton said. "This tactic takes pressure off the supply chain and cuts the risk of empty shelves."
The biggest bird
Aldi, a sister store to Lidl, sells a Thanksgiving meal for $38.74 that feeds 10 people, or $3.87 per person. The bundle includes the biggest turkey out of Aldi, Amazon and Walmart - 16-pounds - and the following ingredients for sides:
- 16-pound turkey, 3 boxes of stuffing mix, baby carrots, celery, 12 ounces of fresh cranberries, 3 pounds of sweet potatoes, Hawaiian sweet rolls, 2 bags of steamable frozen peas, pumpkin pie mix, pie crusts, whole milk and whipping cream.
Shoppers don't have to sign up for a loyalty program to buy the meal. The Aldi bundle's 16-pound turkey is the biggest of the top Thanksgiving meal deals. Aldi's offer is good through November 25.
An affordable small meal
Online retail giant Amazon launched a $25 Thanksgiving meal for six in early November. The retailer's meal is cheapest for groups of six, coming in at $4.17 per person.
The bundle includes enough ingredients to make a whole turkey with gravy, mashed potatoes, stuffing, macaroni and cheese, green bean casserole, rolls, corn, carrots, and a pumpkin pie:
- 8-pound frozen turkey, one can of jellied cranberry sauce, 2 turkey gravy mix packets, 2 boxes of mac and cheese, turkey stuffing, one can of cream of mushroom soup, crispy fried onions, and one can of evaporated milk, Russet potatoes, 2 cans of corn, whole carrots, 2 cans of cut green beans, crescent rolls, pie crusts, and organic pumpkin puree.
Amazon's bundle includes a mix of name-brand and Amazon-brand products. The company's Thanksgiving meal is cheaper per person than last year's deal of $25 for five people. The online retailer's meal deal is good through November 26.
Grocers can offer the same price for a meal that feeds more people, in part, because they're better at preventing food spoilage, Brinton said.
"[Grocers'] ability to more accurately predict demand and accurately purchase inventory against that demand to reduce their spoilage expense helps to keep prices down for consumers," he said.
