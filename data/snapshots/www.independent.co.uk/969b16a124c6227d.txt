MPs have been told that plans for Heathrow's third runway fail to detail how the M25 and M4 will be saved from being turned into "Europe's largest car park".
Ruth Cadbury, who chairs the Commons Transport Committee, warned there are "no clear plans" to assist nearby motorways in managing extra passengers.
Her panel examined the government's draft Heathrow expansion national policy statement (Henps), setting out standards a formal planning submission must satisfy for approval.
Heathrow bosses are consulting on their plans before seeking permission to move forward.
The proposal would increase annual air transport movements - take-offs and landings - from around 475,000 to up to 756,000.
The current M25 "would be realigned horizontally and vertically" close to the airport, running under the proposed new runway in a tunnel.
The proposals would "support and safeguard the introduction of" future rail links into the airport complex from the west and south.
Last year, Transport Secretary Heidi Alexander told the Commons that if a third runway is built at the London airport, "extra staff and passengers must be able to get to and from the airport without turning the M4 and the M25 into Europe's largest car park".
But after her committee's examination of Henps, Ms Cadbury said: "There are no clear plans in place for how millions of extra passengers and staff will travel by public transport to an expanded Heathrow and how these plans - particularly the new rail lines - will be funded.
"Again and again, we were told the government should have set out an overarching strategy for aviation because Heathrow's role can only be properly assessed in the context of the UK's aviation system as a whole.
"The picture we've been left with is incomplete.
"These concerns need to be addressed before the government brings forward a final Henps for designation."
A Department for Transport spokesperson previously said: "The government has been clear that Heathrow expansion can only proceed if it meets our four tests on economic growth, climate, noise and air quality.
"We note the findings from the committee's report and will consider these as we continue to review responses to our consultation on the policy framework underpinning expansion."
