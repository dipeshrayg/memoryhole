Amnesty says Gaza mass funeral highlights 'chilling' destruction of entire families
Amnesty International says a mass funeral for 112 members of the al-Hasayna and Abu Sharia families in Gaza City underscores a pattern of Israeli attacks that have wiped out entire multi-generational families.
Writing on X, Erika Guevara Rosas, Amnesty International's senior director for research, advocacy, policy and campaigns, said the victims' remains were recovered months after an Israeli strike on 23 November 2023 that reportedly killed more than 300 Palestinians.
She said recovery efforts had been delayed by Israeli restrictions on the entry of heavy equipment needed to clear rubble, with thousands of bodies still believed to be trapped beneath collapsed buildings.
"Israel must urgently allow access to Gaza for UN bodies and independent human rights investigators to preserve evidence, in line with the ICJ's orders," she added, using the acronym for the International Court of Justice.

Saudi Arabia expects imminent attacks from north and south, senior official says
Saudi Arabia expects imminent coordinated attacks from the north and south by Iraqi militias and Yemen's Houthis under the supervision of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a senior Saudi official said on Thursday.
The official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said intelligence reports from Saudi Arabia, the United States and other regional countries indicated civilian and economic sites could be targeted, including energy infrastructure, ports and airports.
Saudi Arabia had also observed drones and missiles being moved, suggesting coordinated operations from both directions, the official said.
The official said the reported threats were particularly alarming as Riyadh continued to pursue de-escalation and a negotiated settlement, adding that contacts with all parties, including Iran, and mediation efforts appeared to be moving "in the right direction." The official said the planned attacks could be intended to disrupt those diplomatic efforts.
The official said Saudi-US cooperation remained "very high" at all levels, including operationally with US Central Command, and said Saudi Arabia was prepared to take all necessary measures to respond to any aggression.

Trump denies shortage of key munitions in war with Iran
Donald Trump has denied reports that the United States is running low on key munitions amid its war with Iran, insisting the country has "massive amounts" of ammunition and threatening lengthy prison sentences for officials who leak information about US weapons stockpiles.
In a post on Truth Social on Thursday, Trump said: "Defence companies are building the largest number of plants and factories in our country's history."
The president claimed more weapons were being manufactured and shipped to the region, though he did not provide evidence or specify which munitions he was referring to.
His comments followed reports that he confronted defence secretary Pete Hegseth during a Cabinet meeting at Camp David last week over alleged shortages of critical weapons.
According to the Washington Post, citing two unnamed sources familiar with the discussion, Trump told Hegseth he felt misled because he believed the issue "had been fixed".
The newspaper reported that Hegseth defended his handling of the matter and sought to place responsibility on his deputy, Stephen Feinberg.
Trump later lashed out at the reports, accusing the media of publishing "treasonous statements"."The 'leakers' of these treasonous statements are being hunted down," he wrote. "Long term jail sentences will be sought!"
