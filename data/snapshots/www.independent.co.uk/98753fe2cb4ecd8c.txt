A woman who was sexually abused by four men after being advertised for sex by her "vile monster" husband said the ordeal has "cost me my life as I knew it".
Joanne Young, the former spouse of Philip Young, who previously pleaded guilty to multiple charges of abuse against her, added: "The person I trusted most in the world betrayed me in the most horrific way".
Ms Young, who waived her right to anonymity, also urged the government to look at the law of consent "as a priority" so that "women are properly protected in the future", adding her abuse case raised "serious questions".
Norman Macksoni, 48, Richard Wilkins, 62, Connor Sanderson-Doyle, 32, and Mohammed Hassan, 38, who were all recruited by Mr Young to abuse his wife at their home in Swindon, were convicted at Winchester Crown Court.
Speaking outside Winchester Crown Court, Ms Young said: "The guilty result today is a relief, and it's a relief not just for me and my family, but a relief for what this means for other women in the future. It means that those who find themselves facing what I have are more likely to come forward, and more likely to have faith in the criminal justice system.
"More broadly, for women and girls who have been abused by men, it sends the message that you can be believed and understood. Knowing what has happened to me without my knowledge or consent has been incredibly difficult to process, and this abuse has cost me my life as I knew it. The person I trusted most in the world betrayed me in the most horrific way.
"This has resulted in a devastating impact on the lives of myself and my children. What happened to me was not the act of one man alone. Others were invited to take part, and they chose to. That choice makes them complicit in what was done to me, and today's verdicts recognise that."
Ms Young added: "This case also raises serious questions about how the law deals with consent. Consent has to come from the person themselves, no one should be able to give it on your behalf, and no one should be able to rely on someone else's word that you agreed.
"At no point did anyone ask me for my consent. I urge the government to look at this as a priority, so that women are properly protected in the future. I live with a pain that's hard to put into words, and this is something that will affect our lives forever. It is terrifying that this could happen to anyone.
"What I have been through means I now never feel completely safe, and while it can now be hard for me to leave the house, this horrific abuse showed me that home offered no protection either. Even though at the time I believed I was safe, what happened to me destroys trust, and I'm working hard to rebuild my life."
Macksoni, 48, of Sharnbrook, Bedfordshire, has been found guilty of one charge of rape.
Wilkins, 62, of Toothill, Swindon, has been found guilty of one count of rape and one of assault by penetration.
Sanderson-Doyle, 32, of Swindon, has been convicted of sexual assault by penetration and sexual touching and Hassan, 38, of Swindon, has been found guilty of one count of sexual touching.
Young has pleaded guilty to a total 56 charges made up of 14 counts of voyeurism, 11 of rape, 11 of administering a substance with intent, seven of assault by penetration, four of sexual assault and one of publishing obscene articles in relation to his wife.
The other charges are three charges of making indecent images of children, one of possessing prohibited images of children in July 2024 and four counts of possessing an extreme pornographic image between January 2019 and January 2024.
A sixth defendant, Dean Hamilton, 47, of Street, Somerset, has admitted one charge of assault by penetration, two of sexual assault and one of rape.
During a 13-year period, Young, who served as a Conservative Swindon borough councillor between 2007 and 2010, drugged and raped his wife and invited other men on websites and apps such as Craigslist, Grindr and Fab Swingers using the username London Monkey to have sex with her without her knowledge.
Police discovered that 50-year-old Young, whose most recent address was in Enfield, north London, had taken 480 intimate images and videos of his wife on 200 occasions and posted images online on at least 500 occasions - with the vast majority of these taken at the family home.
The crimes of the former Tory councillor emerged after the highly publicised trial of Dominique Pelicot, who had drugged and raped his wife Gisele and invited 50 other men to abuse her and was jailed for 20 years in France in 2024.
Police and prosecutors building the case against Young sought the expertise of the French investigation team to assist them in bringing him to justice.
This included a Teams meeting between the detectives at Wiltshire Police and their French counterparts.
And the CPS engaged the same expert used in the French case who analysed hair samples from Joanne Young to show signs of the two sedative drugs used by her husband to enable his offending.
As the verdicts were read out, Mrs Young sat with her hands clasped together to her face.
As the judge left the court, she stood composed and smiled with her hands together in front of her.
The four defendants, dressed in suits, all looked down in front of them as the verdicts were read out.
Remanding them in custody until sentencing, Mr Justice Saini said: "Each of the defendants have been convicted of what are very serious sexual offences.
"I have heard persuasive applications on their behalf for bail, I will need pre sentence reports for each of them.
"Given my current view they are facing significant prison sentences, I will not be granting them bail."
The judge also praised the victim, saying: "Mrs Young has shown amazing bravery throughout these proceedings."
James Foster, specialist prosecutor at the Crown Prosecution Service, said: "These men chose to commit unspeakable acts of abuse against Joanne Young without bothering to check if she was consenting.
"By building the case with a suspect-centric approach, we could prove to a jury that Philip Young could not provide consent on the victim's behalf and each defendant played a part in this horrific sexual abuse.
"During the trial - Macksoni, Wilkins, Sanderson-Doyle and Hassan hid behind the claim that they believed she was freely entering into a sexual relationship with them - despite never seeking reassurances that this was in fact the case.
"They are now rightfully facing sentence for these cowardly and egregious crimes.
"Our thoughts today remain with Joanne Young, whose courageous testimony helped bring this case to justice."
Siobhan Blake, the CPS national lead for rape and serious sexual offences (Rasso), said: "Organised drug-facilitated sexual assault is some of the worst offending I have seen in my career. It is vital that we stand with our justice partners against these abhorrent crimes.
"These cases predominantly involve the use of sedatives - often within the context of intimate or long-term relationships - and sharing the media online. Many victims have no memory of the abuse.
"Our prosecutors are seeing an increasing number of these cases - and the CPS will continue to work with partners including police, the National Crime Agency and the National Centre for Violence Against Women and Girls and Public Protection, to bring more criminals, like Philip Young and his accomplices, to justice.
"If you think you have been a victim of similar crimes of this nature, even if you aren't sure, we urge you to report the incident to the police - safe in the knowledge that you will be heard."
The sentencing for the four defendants will take place over December 21 and 22.
