Michael Carrick's future as Manchester United manager is in severe doubt after overseeing the club's worst start to the Premier League season in 40 years and with his side in the relegation zone.
A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, might not be enough to save Carrick's job and he is at risk of being sacked after just 10 games of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.
United ended a nine-game winless run when they came from two goals down to beat Roma in the Champions League last week but they have not won in eight attempts in the Premier League and have just one top-flight win this campaign.
Carrick survived crisis talks with the United hierarchy after defeats to Como and Bournemouth last month and was given time to turn results around, but speculation over a change begun the day after Sunday's chaotic draw and The Independent understands contact was made with United States boss Mauricio Pochettino over succeeding Carrick.
The Old Trafford hierarchy, including Jason Wilcox, Omar Berrarda and Sir Jim Ratcliffe, are set to meet in Monaco today to discuss Carrick's future as the club are reportedly close to agreeing a deal with Pochettino should they decide to move on from Carrick.

Carrick back players despite struggles
There are many problems at Manchester United and one of the perceived issues, certainly with fans, is that the club have brought in players who are not good enough to excel at the club.
Diogo Dalot was the most recent recipent of the supporters' ire following the 4-4 draw with Aston Villa.
Dalot was substituted during the game and the decision to remove him was cheered.
"We've got to be careful with that, I think," Carrick said after the game.
"I understand the frustration. I understand the disappointment of the supporters, so I get it. We're all frustrated. We're all disappointed.
"The players are as much as anyone, I guarantee you that. Diogo gives absolutely everything. He's played in different positions. He's played left-back. He's played really well at times.
"He gives a penalty away. He knows that, maybe he can do a little bit better. Alright, that's part of it, he's made a mistake.
"But he cares an awful lot, and he's given everything, so I like what he brings to the team. I like what he brings to the group."
Carrick carrying on as normal
Michael Carrick spoke about Man Utd's tough run of results ahead of this current international break.
Following United's 4-4 draw against Aston Villa on Sunday, Carrick said: "Yeah, it's been a right mixed week or two or three, really, if you're looking at the events.
"But I'm pretty clear where we need to go and how we need to get there.
"Obviously some of the boys will be flying off around the world and different things, and the group will be split for 10 days or so.
"But as a normal international week is, you know, we'll have some training. We'll have one or two days off, but not too much. But we'll train, and we'll get ready for the next game."

The United States head coach has a contract until the 2030 World Cup but the former Tottenham and Chelsea boss could emerge as a candidate to succeed Michael Carrick with United lodged inside the relegation zone.
More candidates to possibly replace Michael Carrick
Antonio Conte
The appeal of the combustible Italian only has grown while United have been accused of becoming too passive and pedestrian on the pitch. Conte is one of the most demanding coaches in the game and would instantly bring a level of intensity and urgency that has been lacking this season, while his CV - with Serie A titles at Juventus, Inter Milan and Napoli and a Premier League crown with Chelsea - commands respect.
Conte also brought an immediate improvement when he took charge of a floundering Tottenham five years ago and led them back into the Champions League, which now has to be United's biggest concern. So the 57-year-old offers impact, but then what?
Conte has a sharp tongue and is not afraid to criticise his bosses if he feels they are failing to support him. His exit from Tottenham in March 2023 followed a stunning evisceration of the club in a now legendary press conference at Southampton.
United would be playing with fire but options of Conte's calibre are few and far between. Conte has also distanced himself from the Manchester United job while branding reports that he is in "advanced negotiations" with any club as "speculation".
More candidates to possibly replace Michael Carrick
Unai Emery
The Spaniard has done a superb job at Aston Villa by securing Champions League qualification and ending the club's trophy drought with victory in the Europa League.
But Aston Villa will be even more determined to keep Emery given the club are only in the early stages of their rebuild under him, having completely overhauled the spine of their team over the summer.
As it is, Emery enjoys complete control over his project at Villa Park, a reality he is unlikely to find at Old Trafford, while he appears enthused by the challenge of constructing a second competitive team in the Premier League.
With Emery, who is under contract until 2029, United would also need to pay Villa compensation, and that may place him above their budget.

Candidates to possibly replace Michael Carrick
Julian Nagelsmann
The former wunderkind of German coaching was reportedly on United's radar when they sacked Ruben Amorim at the start of the year, but as an option for the summer following the World Cup.
Well, that was a bit of a disaster for the former Bayern Munich and RB Leipzig coach, as Germany were knocked out in the last-32 by Paraguay.
Nagelsmann, 39, would naturally be interested in managing United and the club's director of recruitment, Christopher Vivell, previously worked with him at Red Bull.
Candidates to possibly replace Michael Carrick
Fabian Hurzeler
Brighton have been the surprise package in the Premier League this season and the 33-year-old Hurzeler has drawn praise for turning the Seagulls into a side capable of competing with the country's biggest clubs.
Brighton's 3-0 victory over champions Arsenal earlier this season was a masterclass in modern coaching while Hurzeler's side also knocked United out of the Carabao Cup with a brilliant 3-2 win at Old Trafford.
The Independent's Chief Football Writer Miguel Delaney has reported that Hurzeler is admired at Old Trafford but Brighton would demand significant compensation for the highly-rated German and United may not have the cash.
At Brighton, Hurzeler has also enjoyed an environment where the club have made all the right decisions off the pitch. At United, he would arguably be walking into the opposite.
Candidates to possibly replace Michael Carrick
Eddie Howe
Howe told the club he needed a break from management but the chance to take charge at Old Trafford, three months later, would surely be too good to turn down if an offer arrives.
The 48-year-old remains highly regarded in English football after taking over a Newcastle side in the relegation zone and leading them to the club's highest Premier League finish in two decades, as well as their first domestic silverware in 70 years.
Howe twice took Newcastle into the Champions League playing an energetic, high-intensity style of football that would be popular at Old Trafford.
