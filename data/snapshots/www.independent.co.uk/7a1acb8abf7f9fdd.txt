Mauricio Pochettino has agreed to become the new Manchester United manager and will face the media for the first time since Michael Carrick's sacking as he concludes his time as United States boss.
A second Old Trafford comeback of the week, this time battling from two goals down twice to rescue a 4-4 draw with Aston Villa on Sunday, was not enough to save Carrick's job and the club announced on Thursday that it had parted ways with him after just 10 games of the season and with his side 18th during the international break.
The Independent reported that contact was made with Pochettino over succeeding Carrick on Monday and the Argentinian is now on the verge of returning to the Premier League after reaching a reported agreement with the club.
But with USMNT having two Nations League matches with Haiti in the docket this international break, Pochettino could still be set to lead his team for these upcoming fixtures before reporting to Carrington shortly afterwards, something he may address at today's press conference.

The ultimate Michael Carrick mistake that left his Man United dream doomed
When Manchester United finally decided to push ahead with plans to appoint Mauricio Pochettino after a decade of interest, two considerations were key. One was "experience" of a club of this size. Another was "something extra".
That latter idea had previously formed one of the concerns that some of the United hierarchy had about Carrick on appointing him for the full-time job back in spring. The thinking at the time was that the former midfielder would offer the stability that everyone was talking about, surely returning United to the Champions League that season, but they wondered whether he had the "x factor" for a proper title push. Many felt there was opportunity, because of how many big clubs were going through periods of upheaval, with Pep Guardiola's exit at Man City, while Xabi Alonso and Andoni Iraola begun adventures at Chelsea and Liverpool respectively.
They just didn't expect United to be one of them. The speed of the collapse has shocked the club leadership.

Mauricio Pochettino's credentials
Mauricio Pochettino began his managerial career at Espanyol before moving to Southampton for a 16-month spell in charge.
The Argentine was then appointed as manager of Tottenham in the summer of 2014 and established them as a top four club, taking them to the Champions League final in 2019 before being dismissed less than six months afterwards.
He won three trophies as manager of Paris Saint-Germain including the 2021/22 Ligue 1 title.
In 2023/24 he spent a season as Chelsea boss before being fired in the summer.
Pochettino then replaced Gregg Berhalter as United States Men's National Team head coach in 2024, following the side's group-stage exit from the Copa America. He has won 21 of his 35 matches in charge and took the nation to the last-16 of the World Cup this summer.
Sir Alex Ferguson's endorsement of Mauricio Pochettino
Sir Alex Ferguson is already a known fan of Mauricio Pochettino.
"Well the manager has been fantastic," the Scot said back in 2016, with Pochettino then linked with the job several times since 2018.
"They have a bad record of managers, there is no doubt about that. They have had so many over the years, but this lad has composure.
"You never see him ruffled and the way his team play is a testimony to the work he is doing.
"The most important thing for English football is how many English and young players are in his team.
"He understands the advantage of playing young people. They won't let you down and they have been fantastic."
How Michael Carrick's fate was sealed
Michael Carrick has been at serious peril of the sack for nearly three weeks now.
Back-to-back humiliations against Como and Bournemouth, two defeats by three goals, left the Manchester United hierarchy discussing next steps in crisis talks at Carrington on Monday 26 October.
Carrick, who was at the United training ground that day watching the U21s, survived those talks and was kept on the manage the trip to Chelsea, which ended in a 2-0 defeat despite signs of improvement.
He clung on by the skin of his teeth in the 3-2 comeback win against Roma in midweek, which could have easily been Carrick's final game in charge if not for Matheus Cunha's late heroics.
And while United left it late again on the weekend, scoring two goals in stoppage time to salvage a 4-4 draw against Villa, the resumption of crisis talks in Monaco this week spelled the end for Carrick, with Jim Ratcliffe and the United upper brass calling time on his reign.
Gary Neville explains why Mauricio Pochettino is the right manager for Man United
Gary Neville has spoken on the news that Mauricio Pochettino will become the new manager of Manchester United following the sacking of Michael Carrick on Thursday.
"I've always, and I think this is public knowledge... I've always wanted Mauricio Pochettino as manager at [Manchester] United," said Neville.
"I've said that numerous times, back when Jose Mourinho was maybe coming to Manchester United, I think maybe when Erik ten Hag came to Manchester United.
"I think he's a brilliant person, I think he'll handle the media, I think he understands the size of the club but it's a monumental task to win everybody else over and get everybody on side and make sure he has a team that can compete.
"But he's a brilliant coach with great experience and he's a good man. And I think he also believes in young players and bringing players through and developing them and coaching them, which is what he's going to have to do at Manchester United.
"I've always liked Mauricio Pochettino, whether this is the right point for him to take the Manchester United job, who knows?
"We'll find out in the next year or two but I'm hoping that he's made some pretty strong demands on the way in and he's got control of the club."

Man Utd's worst start
Manchester United have made their worst start to a season in 40 years under Michael Carrick this year.
They've won just once in the Premier League, against Ipswich Town, and have gained only eight points from the first 10 matches.
Pressure has mounted increasingly on Carrick over recent weeks though United are unbeaten in their last two games with a 3-2 win over Roma in the Champions League followed by a 4-4 draw against Aston Villa on Sunday.
That win over Roma is United's only victory, across all competitions, in their last 11 games.
