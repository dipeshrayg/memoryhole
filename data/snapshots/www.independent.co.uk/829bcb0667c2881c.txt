A loyalist Orange Order march is underway on a Catholic street in Northern Ireland for the first time in nearly 30 years.
A late-night legal ruling by the Parades Commission authorizing the parade along Garvaghy Road in Portadown during an "unprecedented" late-night High Court session in Belfast.
Large groups of protesters, many wearing balaclavas, gathered along the road on Sunday morning-a location renowned as a major Troubles flashpoint in the 1990s.
Loud cheers erupted after they were informed the Orange Order had left Drumcree, which were soon followed by some chants of "Bring Stormont down".
Speaking from the area on Sunday morning, Sinn Féin president Mary Lou McDonald said the people of Garvaghy Road would "not be drawn back to the past".
"This is madness. This, in fact, is primitive treatment of any community in the year 2026. It is intolerable, and it will not be tolerated."
The stand-off at Drumcree made headlines in the 1990s, with nationalist residents of the Garvaghy Road opposed to the parade passing through the area, leading to violent clashes for several summers and political tension necessitating a major security operation.
Orange Order march given go-ahead after 29 year ban
A controversial Orange Order march has departed Drumcree in Northern Ireland following a late-night legal ruling.
The decision by the Parades Commission authorizing the parade along Garvaghy Road in Portadown was upheld during an "unprecedented" late-night High Court session in Belfast, concluding only a few hours before the march began.
Large groups of protesters, many wearing balaclavas, gathered along the road on Sunday morning-a location renowned as a major Troubles flashpoint in the 1990s.
A substantial display of fireworks was set off nearby roughly five minutes ahead of its scheduled 8am start, while a loudspeaker announcement warned demonstrators that they faced potential arrest for taking part in an unnoticed procession.
Loud cheers broke out after crowd members were informed that the Orange Order had departed Drumcree, followed shortly by chants calling to "Bring Stormont down".
