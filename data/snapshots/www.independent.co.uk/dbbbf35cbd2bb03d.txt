Arsenal recorded a first Premier League win at Anfield in 16 years but needed some help from Liverpool goalkeeper Alisson Becker, whose first-half error led to Kai Havertz's solitary goal in the 1-0 win.
The Gunners pounced on Alisson's loose pass on 16 minutes and ruthlessly capitalised as Declan Rice slid a sublime cross to the back post where Havertz slid home to give the visitors a lead to cling on to.
Not since manager Mikel Arteta was running the Arsenal midfield in 2012 had they tasted victory in the red half of Merseyside and they were tested to the limit in ending end that run.
However, if it is one thing Arteta's side are well-drilled at it is seeing out games having maximised their opportunities and the celebrations in front of the visiting fans having moved top of the table showed how significant they felt this result was.

Was win a defining moment?
"Today is too early," Arteta says of such big talk.
"Today we just live in the present. Let's enjoy this incredible victory with our players, the staff and our people.
"And tomorrow we start to prepare another big, big, big fight in Prague."
Iraola takes positives despite clear room for improvement
"We still have a lot of things to improve," he told Sky.
"I think we have things that I love in the team, in terms of the willingness and the spirit. I think there is something that I like in this team.
"But today we messed up a goal kick, we concede from there, things that we definitely need to improve."
On the Isak penalty shout, he added: "The Alex Isak one is a clear penalty. You hold the t-shirt; we can all see that he holds the t-shirt. The ball just goes one centimetre in front of Alex. Alex is going to hit the ball in front of the goal."
Rice explains Arteta demands
"The manager had a really honest conversation with me at the start of the season and still thinks that I can do more in this shirt and for this club," he tells Sky.
"And this season so far I've been feeling really good. We spoke about overlaps before the game.
"I spoke to this guy [Havertz] about attacking the box with real aggression. He was there, and he nearly could have had another one with the same action.
"So I'm so happy for him because he deserved that goal today."
'Crazy' stats that defined Arsenal's statement win
There was a time when Mikel Arteta resorted to gimmicks in his bid to win at Anfield. He has tried blasting out You'll Never Walk Alone at Arsenal's training ground. That didn't work.
Assembling this redoubtable, forceful side did. Five days after the worst result of Arteta's reign came one of the best. "The defeat against Fleetwood looks the end of the world," said Arteta. In contrast, this made him feel on top of the world.
Anfield may not quite be the final frontier for the Spaniard - perhaps winning at the Etihad Stadium is or, more importantly, winning the Champions League is - but another ambition has been realised.
