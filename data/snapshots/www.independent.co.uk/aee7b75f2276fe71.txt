The father of a missing three-year-old boy is urging volunteers to widen their search, saying he "cannot go another night knowing he's out there alone".
Police and local residents are desperately trying to locate Noah Woods, who went missing near a playground in Brantham on Tuesday afternoon.
Rhys Woods wrote on Facebook: "Please don't give up on him guys," adding: "He is out there somewhere and I appreciate every single one of you."
Hundreds of people are reported to have joined the search for the child, who was last seen around 3pm yesterday, when he became separated from a relative after visiting the playground in Merriam Close, Suffolk Police said.
Noah, who is mildly autistic and has partial hearing loss, is reported to have run off down the alleyway beside the playground towards the new build housing estate.
He is white with curly ginger hair and was last seen wearing a white t-shirt with the TV character Bluey on, shorts and black Vans trainers with a white stripe.
Police said specially trained officers and dog units have been searching the village, and have been supported by helicopters.
Hundreds of locals come together to 'do whatever we can to find him'
Hundreds of local residents have poured onto the streets to look for three-year-old Noah as the search enters its second day.
Search parties began coming together on Tuesday afternoon, just shortly after he disappeared, and now include residents in neighbouring areas who wanted to help.
Suffolk Police said they were "incredibly grateful" for the public's help and support and have asked them to work alongside the force.
Melissa Dark, a local mother of two who is on maternity leave from her work in major incidents for the police, told BBC: "I just saw the news and needed to come down and help," she said.
"I've bought my six-month-old baby here. We're all just pulling together and doing whatever we can to find him."
Sarah Keys, the clerk of Brantham Parish Council, has been at Brantham Village Hall since 4pm yesterday to help co-ordinate the search efforts.
She told the broadcaster that the community had been "flooded" with people wanting to help.
"Every parent in the country is thinking that could have been my child," Ms Keys added.
"I hope that what Noah's parents will see is a community that are really there for them. We are really pushing to try and find their little boy and I hope they are feeling supported and loved by that response.
"We are hopeful today is the day that Noah is going to get returned home safely."
Noah's father Rhys Woods wrote: "I cannot go another night knowing he's out there alone, please don't give up on him guys. He is out there somewhere and I appreciate every single one of you."
Volunteers search farmland in desperate hunt for Noah
Lowland search and rescue volunteers in white helmets are searching along a ditch between farm fields and checking in undergrowths as efforts to find three-year-old Noah continue.
Members of the public, many of them in orange Hi Vis jackets, spread out across the area as they tried to find him.
Some used sticks to help them peer into foliage.
Two men in Environment Agency jackets, one of them carrying binoculars, were also among the searchers.
Police urge for people with information or sightings to come forward
Officers have asked anyone who believes they have seen Noah to contact Suffolk police on 999, quoting CAD 203 of 15 September.
People can also use the live chat service by clicking the icon at the bottom right corner of the force's website here.
In the event of an emergency you should always call 999, Suffolk Police added.

Police urge locals to check gardens, sheds, outbuildings, and CCTV
Suffolk Police have told local residents to thoroughly check their gardens, sheds and outbuildings, or any potential areas where a child might hide away.
The force has asked for anyone with private CCTV or doorbell cameras in the village to review the footage from 3pm yesterday onwards for any possible sightings of Noah.
The same is also asked of anyone driving through Brantham with a dashcam fitted in their vehicle.
"We know that people want to help and so we are urging local residents to carefully read our requests to check their gardens and outbuildings and review any CCTV or dashcam footage as a priority.
Superintendent Pete Partridge said in a statement: "Anyone who believes they have seen Noah is asked to contact us immediately."

Volunteers told 'not to grab Noah' if they see him
Volunteers have been told "not to grab" or approach Noah if they find him as it might scare him.
The three year old is partially deaf, non-verbal and mildly autistic.
Sarah Keys, from Brantham Parish Council, told people searching for him: "If you see Noah, we need a gentle approach," BBC reported.
"Do not grab him, it might scare him. Please call 999 and tell them where you are."
Volunteers have been asked to download location system What Three Words to pinpoint their exact location.
"Please stay safe and work at least in pairs if not in groups. Do not put yourself at any risk, no heroics. We don't want to be rescuing anyone else today," Ms Keys added.
"We need to focus on finding Noah."
What should you do if a child is missing - and what will happen next?
A child is reported missing every two and a half minutes in the UK. In the critical moments after a child disappears, knowing what to do - and acting quickly - can make all the difference.
Paul Joseph, the head of helplines at charity Missing People, previously spoke to The Independent, saying the first step should always be to contact the police.
To report someone missing, you can call 101 or submit a report through your local police website. If it is urgent - or if a child has disappeared - call 999 immediately.
When filing a report, provide as much detail as possible: where the child was last seen, what they were wearing, what belongings they had, and places they often visit. A physical description, including any scars or birthmarks, is also vital.
If there is anything that makes them particularly vulnerable - such as a medical condition, mental health issue or risk from specific people - tell police immediately.
After filing a report, the police will aim to respond within an hour, assessing the level of risk before deciding on their next steps.
Depending on the level of concern, officers may search the home, check local hospitals, review CCTV, or knock on doors near where the child was last seen. They might also look at mobile or computer activity.
If you need help speaking to the police, or are seeking emotional support or advice, Missing People can help at any time of day.
If you are a child in need of help, call Childline on 0800 1111. If you are an adult worried about a child, call the NSPCC on 0808 800 5000. If you are struggling or in distress, contact Samaritans on 116 123 (UK and ROI).
Father tells volunteers to avoid searching near pond
The father of Noah Woods has asked volunteers to avoid searching near the pond close to the playground that he went missing from yesterday.
Rhys Woods said the decoy pond had been searched "thoroughly" and the amount of people in the way was "making it hard to pick up the infrared signal we need".
He said: "We still have nothing, all the emergency services and all of you have shown how strong this community is, with people coming from far and wide also, the emergency services are very confident that the high risk areas (Decoy pond, railway lines) are all clear.
"Thank you again for all your help, and I'm praying we find him," he added.
