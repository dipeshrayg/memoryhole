England open their November campaign under huge pressure as they face Australia in the Nations Championship.
England's senior players were forced to issue a strong defence of head coach Steve Borthwick this week after a report suggested that they had lost faith in their boss amid a toxic atmosphere in camp during the summer. Captain Maro Itoje and deputy Jamie George were among those to come out firing for Borthwick, promising to produce a performance that showed their unity and spirit.
It heightens the tension for a visit from a Wallabies team full of threat. Australia may have been well beaten by New Zealand in two Bledisloe Cup clashes last month but have already achieved a success over South Africa in the short tenure of Les Kiss, and will surely spy an opportunity against a home side facing a few tough questions.

Jamie George issues furious response to claim England have lost faith in Steve Borthwick
The England hooker Jamie George has hit back at claims of a divided dressing room and toxic camp in a staunch defence of Steve Borthwick's management ahead of their clash against Australia.
A report in The Times on Friday claimed that members of the England squad had lost faith in Borthwick during the first phase of the Nations Championship in July, and disagreed with the direction set by the head coach.
That followed the side's worst-ever Six Nations campaign and first defeat to Italy earlier in the year, though George - who captained in the stead of the rested Maro Itoje in the summer - insisted that much of the reporting was wide of the mark.
