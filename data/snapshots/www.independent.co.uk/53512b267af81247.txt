England delivered the backlash promised in support of their under-fire head coach Steve Borthwick as Henry Pollock starred in a record 45-22 rout of Australia at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham.
Reeling from a report published on Thursday claiming that Borthwick presided over a toxic environment during the July tour, seven tries were engineered in a show of unity from his players.
One of the best attacking displays of the Borthwick era was lit up by electric performances from Pollock and Immanuel Feyi-Waboso, who crossed three times between them.
The blow of seeing the explosive Feyi-Waboso limp off with a leg injury at half-time having repeatedly punched holes in Australia's defence was softened by his replacement Pollock shining on his debut on the wing. A back row who Borthwick has identified as wing cover, the headline-grabbing 21-year-old adapted to his new position in style.

England lift the Ella-Mobbs trophy
England have earned a trophy for their troubles today... They go home with the incredibly prestigious Ella-Mobbs Cup.
Formerly the Cook Cup, its named was changed and a new trophy was commissioned in 2022 and is awarded to the winners of the England-Australia fixture whenever it's played.
It is named after Indigenous 25-cap Wallabies great Mark Ella and English war hero Edgar Mobbs, who played nine Tests for England before being killed on the World War I western front in 1917.
Feyi-Waboso and Atkinson injury worries
It wasn't all good news for England. Immanuel Feyi-Waboso went off injured at half-time and was spotted in a walking boot after the game.
The winger had looked brilliant in the first half but that walking boot suggests a potentially serious issue.
Impressive inside centre Seb Atkinson also limped off very, very gingerly during the second 40 minutes - an injury for him would be another big blow for England.

'Our defence was not physical enoguh'
A disappointing day for Australia and skipper Harry Wilson admitted after the match that they weren't physical enough.
Wilson told ITV: "We started the game and we didn't get much ball and didn't get many A-zone entries. England were the better team today.
"They fronted up physically and put us under the pump. Our defence was not physical enough. Credit to England.
"A lot of the issues might be our mindset but we have seven days to take feedback and make sure we are a lot better.
"We know when we hold the ball for long phases how much we can cut things up but we couldn't be doing in the last 15 minutes, it has to be in minute one."
Henry Slade: 'Putting points on the board is really promising'
Henry Slade was named man of the match today after an impressive display in the centres. He spoke to ITV after the game today.
"I am really happy," said Slade. "After just a short week together we did really well to come together and put in a great performance. What a crowd to play in front of - it's amazing to play in front of them."
On a record-breaking win: "That's really pleasing. It's always nice for us to put some points on the board and to do it so early in the campaign is really, really promising.
"You always want to get off to a good start. Thankfully, we did that today. We've got another big challenge coming next week and the week after so we're just excited about whatever's put in front of us. We're going to go and take it with both hands."
REPORT: England send message with emphatic Australia victory to show faith in Steve Borthwick
England's players had promised a performance that showed their faith in Steve Borthwick; consider that message of unity emphatically sent. Crisis? What crisis?
If last November's victory over New Zealand remains the high watermark of the Borthwick era given the opposition, here, at least, was a performance that rose close to those standards, an answering of critics and call to arms that they needed at the start of this potentially fractious campaign. There was daring, there was dynamism, and there was more than a hint of indignation after a week of questioning over their spirit.
A pot still to boil or a flash in a pan? The next three weeks will reveal plenty about what Borthwick is really cooking but they have, at least, backed up strong words with equally forceful actions.
