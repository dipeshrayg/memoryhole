Wales face up to the daunting challenge of New Zealand as they again seek to end their seven-decade wait for success against the All Blacks.
Steve Tandy's side made a winning, if unconvincing, start to their November with Nations Championship success against Japan last week, but will know they must lift their level to stay with their vaunted visitors. Aaron Wainwright's injury absence is a blow to the hosts, who have not beaten New Zealand since 1953.
This looks a touring team in increasingly fine fettle, too, though Dave Rennie takes the opportunity to look at the strength in the depth of his squad. Beauden Barrett captains the All Blacks for the first time as the full-back closes in on a century and a half of international caps, while Richie Mo'unga is a rather handy third-string fly half to bring in.

Team news - New Zealand
New Zealand ring the changes from the win over Scotland as Beauden Barrett captains the All Blacks for the first time on the occasion of his 149th cap. Richie Mo'unga comes in as his playmaking partner at fly half, while Fehi Fineanganofo and Anton Segner start for the first time at flanker and on the wing respectively.
There are six forwards on the bench, in a sense: Leicester Fainga'anuku has featured regularly in the back row for the Crusaders this year having forged his reputation as a hard-running outside back.
New Zealand XV: 1 George Bower, 2 Asafo Aumua, 3 Fletcher Newell; 4 Scott Barrett, 5 Josh Lord; 6 Anton Segner, 7 Peter Lakai, 8 Wallace Sititi; 9 Cortez Ratima, 10 Richie Mo'unga; 11 Josh Moorby, 12 Anton Lienert-Brown, 13 Quinn Tupaea, 14 Fehi Fineanganofo; 15 Beauden Barrett (capt.).
Replacements: 16 George Bell, 17 Xavier Numia, 18 Pasilio Tosi, 19 Jamie Hannah, 20 Ethan Blackadder, 21 Leicester Fainga'anuku; 22 Kyle Preston, 23 Rieko Ioane.
Team news - Wales
Wales are without No 8 Aaron Wainwright, which leads to a bit of a back row reshuffle from Steve Tandy. Taine Basham makes his first Wales appearance in nearly three years at the base of the scrum with James Botham brought in on the flank, while Adam Beard's experience and lineout nous are favoured in the second row.
Josh Adams is rested, which allows Tandy to reward Gabriel Hamer-Webb for a good start to the season at Leicester.

Wales vs New Zealand
New Zealand have tallied 54, 55 and 52 points on their last three visits to the Principality Stadium, setting a pretty consistent benchmark against with Wales can measure themselves.
You may wonder why Wales' record against the All Blacks is quite so poor given some of the sides they've fielded over the decades. It did not help that they only played New Zealand twice during the 1970s glory years, while their best crop under Warren Gatland rather coincided with an era-defining, dominant All Blacks team.
Familiar faces
There's a fair bit of familiarity between the two coaching staffs. Dave Rennie's three-year stint in Scotland with Glasgow crossed over with Steve Tandy and Danny Wilson's time as assistants to Gregor Townsend, while Wales defence coach Peter Murchie was part of Rennie's backroom team with the Kobelco Kobe Steelers in Japan before making the switch to Cardiff earlier this year.
"I know them well and they are very good, smart coaches," said All Blacks boss Rennie.
"Pete did a phenomenal job defensively with us at Kobe, and I can see the impact he's having already. And likewise with Steve.
"It's going to take time to turn things around, but I can definitely see the impact they're having."
