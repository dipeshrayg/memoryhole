Wales pulled off one of the biggest shocks in rugby history with a stunning, historic 35-28 win over New Zealand after a brilliant, brave performance in Cardiff in the Nations Championship.
Despite entering the match as huge underdogs, following three years in the relative rugby doldrums and amid constant off-field turmoil, Steve Tandy's side somehow ended their seven-decade wait for success against the All Blacks by earning a first win over rugby's most iconic side since 1953.
Wales flew out of the blocks at the Principality Stadium with two tries inside the first six minutes from Tomos Williams and Dewi Lake but the All Blacks hung in there and appeared to turn the match their way with a try on the stroke of half-time from Fehi Fineanganofo to level things at 21-21.
Instead, Wales started the second-half strongly and rebuilt their 14-point cushion through Eddie James and Louis Rees-Zammit tries before the impressive Peter Lakai pulled New Zealand within seven thanks to a 54th-minute score. The hosts then produced one of the greatest defensive stands of all-time, spending almost 20 straight minutes in their own half at one point but repelled wave after wave of All Black attack to hang on for the most incredible of victories to blow this Nations Championship wide open.

An extraordinary day in Cardiff.
All Blacks coach credits Wales for deserved victory
All Blacks head coach Dave Rennie said: "We are massively disappointed. We started both halves poorly and conceded 14 points quickly. We had plenty of territory, and we need to be a lot better than that.
"Credit to Wales. They were really clinical when they got their chances, and they defended for massive amounts and were the better side. We got a bit one-dimensional, and that's decision-making under pressure. They ran hard on to the ball, and we struggled to slow it down."
Wales 'felt like a brick wall at times'
Three Wales players - Dafydd Jenkins, Jac Morgan and James Botham - each made more than 30 tackles, and New Zealand skipper Beauden Barrett conceded how tough it had been to break Wales down in the second half.
"It is a tough one to take, particularly when we were building a lot of pressure but couldn't convert into points," Barrett said. "It felt like a brick wall at times, so you have to give Wales credit. We were a little bit indecisive, we had three hold-ups over the line, and we have got be better at converting those opportunities."
Steve Tandy proud of 'phenomenal' Wales players after famous win over All Blacks
Wales head coach Steve Tandy hailed his "phenomenal" players after they delivered a stunning performance to sink New Zealand 35-28 in Cardiff.
It was the first time since 1953 that Wales could celebrate beating the All Blacks, ending a run of 34 successive defeats in one of world rugby's historically most one-sided fixtures.
On three separate occasions Wales led by 14 points, and for the first time they scored five tries against New Zealand, but ultimately it was heroic defensive work that denied the visitors.
"I feel pride for the players, and to see what it means to the supporters," Tandy said. "We have been wanting some positivity, and I think the players delivered that today."
Steve Tandy celebrates his history boys
"The effort that the players and staff have put in over the last 15 months or so, they got what they deserved today. It was an unbelievable performance from the boys.
"We spoke about attacking, building the performance and being brave and I thought the boys were, probably over brave before half-time, but they had intent to go after the game.
"And in fairness, when New Zealand were coming back at half-time it would be easy to doubt yourselves, but I thought the way the boys attacked it, the bench coming on as well, and in the last couple of minutes I thought they looked really composed in fairness to the group.
"The boys have created history, they've got to celebrate and enjoy that moment, we want to back up performances, but we have to celebrate these moments."
Dewi Lake on a day of history
"We came under deserved scrutiny last week, and we had some hard discussions," the Wales captain tells S4C.
"I'm so proud of how we responded to the questions that were asked of us.
"I believe that the hard times drive you to get more, it's taught us that we can be on top or behind, and still find out how to win this game.
"There's a lot of pride and trust in our team and we have to be consistent from now on.
"This group can be so good, a lot have grown into the shirt after coming in come in at hard times we've been through a lot."
Wales beat the All Blacks
Wales have to go again in seven days' time against Australia - what sort of state will they be in after a game like that? Cardiff could be carnage this evening.
For what it is worth, those two losing bonus points could be key for New Zealand in the chase for top spot in their Nations Championship pool after South Africa's defeat last night. Not that it will be at the immediate forefront of their minds.
Player of the match James Botham reacts to Wales' win
"I honestly can't describe the feeling," Botham says to S4C.
"I imagine everyone was writing us off, obviously the last win was 1953, so to get that historic win now just shows how far we've come and how much we're gelling as a team.
"I can't wait for the next game already."
