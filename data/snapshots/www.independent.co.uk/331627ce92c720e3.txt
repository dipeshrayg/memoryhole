Scientists in Switzerland have invented a device that allows wearers to silently communicate with artificial intelligence.
Described as a "gadget straight out of James Bond film", a team from ETH Zurich developed the InvisiSpeech to give people a private, hands-free way to talk with AI assistants without speaking out loud or moving their lips.
Placed between a back molar and the cheek, it works by picking up the tiny vibrations made when a person forms words with their tongue.
The signals are then transmitted to a smartphone, where an AI model developed by the researchers decodes them and carries out the commands given.
"The device itself is so small that it can be worn comfortably in your mouth," said Yijing Jiang, a doctoral student who worked on the project.
Unlike brain-computer interfaces that allow people to communicate with computers with their thoughts, the InvisiSpeech requires no invasive surgeries to insert or remove.
If combined with an earpiece, the device could support two-way communication without anyone around the wearer overhearing or even realising they are using an AI assistant.
"InvisiSpeech enables us to interact in fundamentally new ways: the invisible input of commands to an AI via natural language," said Christian Holz, a professor of computer science at ETH Zurich who led the research.
A study involving 15 people found that InvisiSpeech correctly recognised 90 per cent of 50 predefined commands, though the device needed to be calibrated for each different user.
The device could be used to control AI glasses, or simply used in environments like offices, meetings, or out in public to avoid disturbing people.
It has already been tested for its ability to retrieve information during a meeting, as well as using voice commands to switch devices on and off.
The researchers hope that further development will make natural conversations with AI possible.
ETH Zurich is now working with Aarhus University in Denmark and Penn State University in the US to continue developing the InvisiSpeech device.
