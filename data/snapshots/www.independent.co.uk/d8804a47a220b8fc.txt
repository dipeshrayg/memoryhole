A major internet outage has taken down Revolut, the online banking app, and many of the UK's biggest websites.
The problems appeared to be at least partly linked to technical issues at Google Cloud, the search giant's web services division. That provides internet infrastructure to a wide array of websites - meaning that any issue at the company can become clear across the web.
The problems at Revolut were particularly troublesome, leaving customers not only unable to open the app but also meaning that they were unable to pay in stores using their cards.
But the problem hit online services from Rightmove to UK courts, where an outage in the video system used for trials forced hearings to be delayed.
Google also said that it was aware of the issues and that it's engineers were working on fixing them. But it did not give any indication of when it might be fixed.
DPD says it is aware of problems with its website
On its Irish social media pages, delivery company DPD says it is aware that its website is not working properly.
"We are aware of current connection issues affecting our website," it said. "Our team is working to get this resolved as quickly as possible. We apologise for any inconvenience caused."
Court hearings delayed as video system goes down
The video system used by courts across England and Wales went down on Tuesday in an issue believed to be linked to a wider Google outage.
The Cloud Video Platform (CVP), which is used by defendants, lawyers and other parties to attend court hearings remotely, was not functioning throughout the morning.
Users expecting to attend court on video link were met with a message saying there was an internal server error.
The issue is expected to have left many hearings across the country delayed or unable to go ahead as the system is often used for defendants who are in custody to attend court.
The judge in the Russell Brand trial, Mr Justice Bennathan, decided to press on with proceedings at Southwark Crown Court on Tuesday morning despite the link being broken.
Some journalists who would have been watching online were squeezed into extra seats in court, but many were not able to follow the proceedings as the fourth complainant started her evidence.
Staff at the court said there was a national outage and did not know when it would be resolved.
Google updates its customers - to say the problem is ongoing
Google had promised that another update on its services would arrive at 4am local pacific time, or midday in the UK. And it has now arrived.
But it is identical to the previous update, posted more than 90 minutes ago. It says that it is aware that "multiple services are experiencing issues" in Western Europe, that its "engineering team continues to investigate the issue", that it is sorry, and that the only workaround is to move work to other regions, which might not be feasible.
It commits to share another update within the hour.
Outage shows 'how much we depend on infrastructure we never see', expert warns
Marijus Briedis, chief technology officer at NordVPN, says that the outage means that customers might be hit by problems that they never realised could happen in this way. And - as below - he warns that the response is not so much about avoiding such outages as companies ensuring that they can continue to work when they happen.
"When a cloud outage leaves people unable to make payments or access everyday services, it exposes how much we depend on infrastructure we never see," he said.
"As Google is one of the world's biggest technology companies, disruption to its cloud services can also affect businesses whose customers may have no idea they depend on Google at all.
"For everyday Google users, this dependence can mean several services they rely on suddenly being unavailable at the same time. Someone unable to pay through one banking app may find their alternative is affected too. Keeping a second payment option can help, although using a different bank does not guarantee it relies on different technology.
"Banks, retailers and delivery companies may look like separate businesses, but behind the scenes many rely on the same handful of technology providers. A problem at one supplier can quickly spread beyond its own customers.
"For businesses, the lesson is not that the cloud is unreliable, but that using a major provider does not remove the responsibility to prepare for disruption. Critical services need tested arrangements to switch to another system when something goes wrong.
"A backup is only useful if it can operate independently of whatever has failed. Businesses need to check whether their main service and their fallback rely on the same underlying systems, and test how quickly they can switch between them. Having copies of your data does not, on its own, mean customers can carry on making payments or accessing their accounts."
Problem is not the outage but companies failing to prepare, analyst says
Simon Baxter, research director at analyst firm TechMarketView, says that the trouble with today's outage is not that services have gone down - but that companies were not properly prepared for it.
"This latest disruption is another reminder of how heavily large enterprises now rely on hyperscale infrastructure. But the bigger issue is resilience, which is increasingly driving customer conversations. Whether it's a cloud outage, a cyber incident, loss of access to an AI model or any other event outside their control, organisations need to be better prepared for disruption," he said.
"Some disruption is unavoidable, but businesses should be planning for a wider range of scenarios and putting measures in place to limit the impact where they can.
"It's also why we're seeing growing calls for sovereign infrastructure and services. That conversation is not just about UK data residency though. It's increasingly about control and visibility over the infrastructure, applications, data, AI and support services organisations consume.
"Many firms have made some progress on understanding what they have, where it's run and managed, and what governance, security and resilience controls are in place, but few have the full picture. Fewer still have proactively prepared for when one of those dependencies fails."
Halifax says it is also having problems
UK bank Halifax is also being hit by an outage, it has said.
"We're aware some customers are having issues with our app and online," it wrote on X. "We're sorry about this and are working hard to fix it. If you need to you can still call us.
"We'll let you know as soon as it's back to normal."
It did not link the problems specifically to Google Cloud.
Outage highlights major problem with today's internet, expert says
Jake Moore, global cybersecurity adviser at ESET, says that today's outage shows just how dependent the web is on a very small number of companies. And they could come with even bigger dangers that a morning of outages, he warns.
"Today's outages highlight just how much dependence we put on a handful of technology giants to keep our digital lives running. With only a small number of major cloud providers to choose from, the knock-on effects can be catastrophic if something goes wrong and can leave millions of people unable to use everyday services," he said.
"Cloud services are a huge point of failure and will always be a potential weakness in digital infrastructures. They could even become a potential target on such services by cybercriminals. Businesses therefore need to plan for when these services go down and have strict contingencies in place for this unfortunate inevitable event."
Budgeting app Emma also hit by outage
Emma, which allows users to pull together information from across their bank accounts to build a budget, says it is also hit by the problems.
"Emma is currently down due to a Google Cloud outage affecting services in London since 7am," it wrote on X. "This is a technical outage, not a security issue.
"We're monitoring closely and will update here as soon as the app is back. Sorry for the disruption"
Has this happened before?
In short, yes. And not just with Google Cloud.
In 2025, for instance, Cloudflare - which offers similar services to Google Cloud - suffered its own technical problems. That took a whole host of websites down, many of them much larger and more global than the ones affected by this outage, including ChatGPT and X.
At the time, that led to all sorts of conversations about whether the internet is too dependent on a small number of service providers. That's likely to happen again this time around - though there are plenty of good reasons that it has happened.
Revolut's status page confirms everything is broken
Revolut said in its statement that the problems are affecting "multiple services". But its official "system status" page confirms that covers everything it does.
That's not just the app itself, but ATM withdrawals and card payments.
All of those services have the same message appearing alongside them.
"We are currently experiencing an incident affecting this service," it reads. "Our team is investigating and working on a fix. We apologise for the inconvenience and will update the status page as soon as the issue is resolved."
