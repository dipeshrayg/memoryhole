The polls have opened for Tuesday's crucial midterm elections, during which voters across the country will decide whether Democrats or Republicans will control the U.S. House and Senate.
Democrats are looking to take control of Congress to rebuff President Donald Trump's agenda during the last two years of his term. They will need to flip four seats in the Senate and three seats in the House to receive a majority.
Trump's poor approval rating and Americans' concerns over the high cost of living and war in Iran have been key campaign issues for Democrats.
Many states that Trump previously won are at play in the Senate race, including Alaska, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, Texas, Kansas and Iowa.
Meanwhile, in the House, Republicans are mostly on the defense, looking to hold on to battleground districts won by Trump in 2024.
The midterms are widely seen as a referendum on Trump's second term. The president made his last-ditch effort to appeal to voters Monday night, calling in to Fox News to urge conservatives to "pretend I'm on the ballot."
Millions will vote in new congressional districts
Millions of Americans are set to vote in new congressional districts this Election Day.
The change comes as a result of redistricting, which has changed the boundaries of about one-third of congressional districts, meaning the congressional map for 2026 looks different than the one used in 2024.
Voting machines down at most polling sites in Westchester County, suburbs north of Manhattan
Voting machines at most polling sites in the suburbs just north of Manhattan were down Tuesday morning due to an issue, officials said.
Scanners at most polling places in Westchester County, New York, began experiencing issues when polls opened at 6 a.m.
Voters are being instructed to stay in line and fill out a paper ballot, which will go into a secure box.
Republican Mike Lawler, who is up for reelection in New York's 17th Congressional District, said every scanner in the county was not working.
His Democratic challenger Cait Conley also posted about the issue, telling voters "don't be dissuaded and have your voice heard."
New York Attorney General Letitia James said her office is looking into the issue.

Hillary Clinton says Trump has 'truly lost the plot'
Hillary Clinton had some choice words to share, speaking about Donald Trump during an interview Monday night with CNN's Anderson Cooper.
"He's totally unfettered and unhinged now," Clinton said, referring to how Trump was when he was first elected president in 2016.
"For him to go to the reflecting pool, one of the great failures of his efforts to redo Washington, and stand there as though it was a huge success and go on and on about all his projects, ripping up Washington, destroying the East Wing, building an arch that will interfere with air traffic control navigation in and out of Washington," Clinton said.
"You can't make this up! He is so focused on himself," Clinton exclaimed, later adding, "I mean he's truly lost the plot of being president for the American people, of winning a midterm election."

Polls open on East Coast for crucial midterm elections as 56m vote early
The polls opened in Vermont at 5am this morning and have just opened in New Jersey, Virginia, Connecticut and Kentucky too.
According to CNN, around 56 million Americans had already voted by mail before Election Day even dawned.
Early turnout has outpaced the last midterm cycle in 2022 in each of the major Senate battlegrounds, The New York Times notes.
In Texas, for instance, where James Talarico is taking on Ken Paxton, an estimated 7.7 million people have already voted, compared to 5.5 million four years ago, a 41 percent increase.
Early voting is also well up in South Carolina, North Carolina, Michigan, Iowa, Maine, Alaska, Ohio, Georgia and Kansas.
