President Donald Trump has announced that chief of staff Susie Wiles is out and Stephen Miller is taking her place in the White House.
Wiles, who has been rumored to be leaving the Trump administration, becomes the highest-profile figure to lose her role after the Republican Party's disastrous midterm results. In her place, Miller will bring his hardline immigration reform.
The poor showing in the midterms has also left some Republicans questioning the future and looking for someone to blame.
Republican John Sununu lost in a Senate race and noted that voters said in 2024 Trump ran on a campaign of no new wars and draining the swamp. But, Trump went to war with Iran and there are allegations of his family benefiting from his time in office.
"And those two things, I think, caused people to say, 'You know, I just can't vote for a Republican while he's still in office,'" Sununu said.
The Republican's comments come as some others in the party worry about the 2028 electoral map, given the party lost races in key battleground states. Or ones that used to be near-locks such as Texas.
Republicans get a rare flip in the midterms
Republicans are getting a rare flip.
Democratic candidate Matt Dunlap has conceded the race for Maine's 2nd congressional district to former Gov. Paul LePage.
Not seeing a pathway to victory as the vote count moves to ranked-choice voting tabulation, Dunlap said in a statement: "This isn't the result we were hoping for, and I'm disappointed that we came short."
"Congratulations to Paul LePage, I wish him the best," Dunlap said. "I want to thank every volunteer who knocked doors, made calls, and made their voices heard."
Tucker Carlson says Trump era is over - and with it Fox News
Tucker Carlson declared the era of President Donald Trump - and Fox News - officially over, following the midterm elections which saw Democrats reclaim control of both the House and the Senate.
The former Trump ally and ex-Fox News host made the remarks on his podcast Wednesday, a day after Democrats romped to victory in a string of red states, ushering in an era of divided government.
Carlson called the election "the biggest blowout of our lifetimes," "a collective howl of pain," and a disaster on par with the sinking of the Lusitania.

Republican lawmaker says 'there should be some heads rolling' after midterm losses
Rep. Marlin Stutzman, an Indiana Republican, said the Trump administration should enact staffing changes as a result of the party's resounding defeat in the midterm elections.
"I think there should be some heads rolling from the White House right now," Stutzman told Newsmax on Thursday.
Democrats have now flipped at least 500 state legislative seats
Democrats have now flipped at least 500 state legislative seats, including picking up Democratic majorities in at least 12 chambers and breaking at least 11 GOP supermajorities.
"Donald Trump's failed legacy for Republicans now includes putting Democrats in their strongest position in the states in nearly two decades," according to Democratic Legislative Campaign Committee president Heather Williams.
"Statehouse elections will continue to have tremendous impacts on the future of our nation, and defending our new seats will be crucial to ensuring Democrats hold power over redistricting, voting rights, and protecting our elections," she added.

Trump defends Pete Hegseth
During a Thursday Oval Office appearance, President Donald Trump defended Pete Hegseth.
"Pete Hegseth has done a fantastic job," the president said.
Some have called for Hegseth to lose his job in the aftermath of the midterm elections, where many voters cited displeasure with the Iran war and higher prices as reasons they voted Democrat.
