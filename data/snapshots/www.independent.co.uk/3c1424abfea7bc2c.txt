There's been a revolution in the mattress world over the past 20 years, with the advent of mattresses in a box and a multitude of companies producing cheaper options that still rival some of the best mattresses on the market. But when it comes to finding a decent mattress that costs under £500 (for a UK double), are you going to be out of luck? The happy answer to this question is no, and you'll be pleased to hear that there are comfortable and supportive options available for a price that is, in mattress terms, a complete steal.
Budget mattresses have seriously upped their game in the last few years, and by combining them with comfortable duvets and pillows, you'll be able to create a bedroom that's comfortable and inviting to sleep in.
However, you do need to be realistic, as Tristine Hargreaves, executive director of the National Bed Federation (NBF) & Bed Advice UK, explains: "Cheap mattresses tend to have basic constructions such as containing open coil/open spring units, which are more affordable than their pocket spring counterparts. Don't expect layers of cashmere, wool, deeper layers of memory foam or thousands of pocket springs in a double-size mattress that costs £300 or less. If you do see a mattress that is advertised as supposedly containing these more luxurious fillings, think twice. It may be too good to be true, and the product description may not match what is inside."
I've been testing mattresses for the last six years, and I've tried out everything from mattresses costing £200 all the way up to mattresses costing over £4,000. From memory foam to hybrid and pocket sprung, there isn't any type of mattress I haven't tried out, so I'm confident that these mattresses are a real bargain. By the way, do note that prices are all for a UK double size. For context, I'm of a lightweight build and am 5ft 9in, while my husband is 5ft 11in and of a lightweight/average build. I tested 12 budget mattresses for this guide, and these are my top seven that I felt deserved inclusion.

The REM-Fit 1000 has recently been rejigged, taking its height up from 20cm to 25cm and adding a top comfort layer of open-cell memory foam for improved breathability. That's all very well and good, but what made the REM-Fit my top choice?

However, for the price of this mattress I think these are acceptable trade-offs and overall I was hugely impressed by how supported I felt, with aches and pains being nicely soothed.

How I tested cheap mattresses
My husband and I slept on each mattress, and I also asked friends of various shapes and sizes to come and lie on the mattress to give me their opinions. I rated each mattress on the following:
- Comfort and support: It's no good having one without the other, so I looked at how well these mattresses cushioned the pressure points around the shoulders, hips and knees while also assessing how supportive they were and if they did a good job of keeping the spine aligned.
- Motion isolation: You don't want a mattress that allows you to feel every movement a partner makes, particularly if you're a light sleeper. So I looked for a mattress that helped to isolate movement and didn't leave me wide awake when my husband did his impression of a walrus leaving the sea as he threw himself from side to side.
- Edge support: If, like me, you share your bed with pets (and, in the past, small children) you'll want a mattress that you can spread out to the very edge of. It's also useful to be able to sit on a bed without the danger of sliding onto the floor, so I looked at how good edge support was on each mattress.
- Temperature regulation: My husband sleeps hot, so he assesses each mattress for its temperature regulation and its ability to keep him comfortable overnight. I assess each mattress on whether it keeps me from feeling too cold at night!
- Extras: Do you get a sleep trial and guarantee with the mattress? And is delivery free or do you need to pay? I check all these so you can weigh up which mattresses have the best options.
Your questions about cheap mattresses answered
What are the pros and cons of buying a cheap mattress?
"Pros: it's purse-friendly," says Tristine Hargreaves. "But what is 'cheap' to one person isn't to another. Generally, a double-size mattress that costs under £300 will be made of a very basic construction, such as an open coil or open spring unit. We slept on these types of mattresses for decades before pocket springs came along and survived, so while there is nothing necessarily wrong with them, they may not offer the same comfort as a pocket sprung mattress or a mattress with deep layers of fillings such as wool, cotton, memory foam, etc."
"Additionally, if you buy a mattress that costs less than £300 but advertises as containing these fillings, please be wary as it is probably too good to be true. If they are potentially untruthful about the fillings and construction, can you trust that the mattress meets the UK's strict health and safety regulations?"
Is buying a cheap mattress a good idea?
"Obviously, everyone's financial circumstances are different, and not everyone can afford to spend £1,000 on a new mattress," says Hargreaves. "Mattresses range from under £100 to several thousand, so spend as much as you can afford. We always encourage consumers to choose the best value mattress, not the lowest price. Of course, there are some perfectly acceptable, budget mattresses available (better than an unhygienic second-hand bed). However, the better the construction and quality of fillings, the better the support and comfort and the longer the bed will last."
"Never buy from the back of a van and avoid online/social media marketplaces if you can. There is pretty much no traceability and no real way of knowing the mattress is everything the seller claims it is. Always buy from a genuine, reputable bed retailer or e-tailer."
What should you look for in a cheap mattress to ensure you'll get a good night's sleep?
"Every mattress sold in the UK, no matter its cost, must display a blue BS 7177 fire safety label that demonstrates it complies with the BS 7177 standard (British Standard which specifies requirements for the resistance to ignition of mattresses, mattress pads, divans and bed bases)," explains Hargreaves. "Ideally, buy from a National Bed Federation-approved brand. These UK and Irish brands are members of the National Bed Federation (NBF) and have chosen to be rigorously and independently audited to check that they comply with the UK's strict regulations around fire-safety, cleanliness of mattress fillings and genuine product descriptions."
"Be wary of unusual brands and 'too good to be true' deals. We're not saying every brand you've never heard of is bad, but there's a reason why some names only ever crop up in a sale or deal. Bargain or not, you still want a bed that's safe, clean and is what it claims it is.
Save money by avoiding luxurious features such as hand-stitched side panels, decorative tufting, or special technologies such as moisture-wicking or temperature-controlling fabrics. They're nice additions but not essential."
