Gianni Infantino has officially scrapped his plan to sell-off stakes in the World Cup but Uefa has come out and declared it has lost confidence in his Fifa presidency after the controversial proposals.
"The current Fifa leadership has not only lost Uefa's confidence but also that of many other members of the football family," said Uefa in a statement on Saturday morning and claimed "the shabby, back room, opaque deal he hatched and tried to force through were anything but transparent" in a damning missive.
Infantino is now is under mounting pressure from football federations as well as from within Fifa, with his long-term adviser Carlos Cordeiro choosing to quit in protest over Infantino's initial proposals, while Fifa's chief operating officer has said that Infantino "deceived" staff over his plans. Questions are now arising as to whether his presidency remains tenable.
Donald Trump meanwhile told reporters on Friday that he had not spoken to Infantino about the plans despite their close relationship and the fact that Josh Kushner, the brother of Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, was expected to lead the proposed investor group.

Uefa leads push to force Gianni Infantino resignation after World Cup plan collapses
Uefa is leading a multi-front strategy to force Gianni Infantino's resignation as Fifa president after the humiliating collapse of his plan to privatise the World Cup.
With Infantino's support otherwise frittering away, with Wales and Finland starting the process of countries withdrawing endorsement letters for the Swiss official to run for another term from 2027 to 2031, there is now hope from his critics that he doesn't even get to another election, which is due to be staged in March 2027.
Uefa and Concacaf are seeking to cut off a usual vote-winning strategy of Infantino promising more money to individual associations by insisting that should be coming to them anyway, after such a lucrative World Cup, and all of this is then complemented by the threat of a legal case over the proposals.

Trust in Infantino 'badly shaken' says Uefa vice president
Uefa vice president Laura McAllister has hit out at Gianni Infantino for his World Cup sell-off plan and says trust in the Fifa boss has been "badly shaken".
Speaking on BBC Radio 4's Today programme, McAllister said: "It's not for me to speak for for Uefa at this moment in time, but I think trust in the president of Fifa has been badly shaken this week, without a doubt, and I think quite rightly, people will ask questions about whether he is the leader to take us forward as a global football organisation.
"Because we have to stick to the ethical principles of sustainability, fair play, and accessibility to football that really motivate us all. So I think those are the questions that all of us involved in football will be asking now."
Asked if she could envision circumstances in which Uefa might find a challenger to him to stand against him, she said: "I think all of the confederations would be considering options now for sure, and we'll certainly be doing that in Uefa.
"But you know, it's a democratic election. If people want to stand, they will, and there will be moves, I'm sure, to encourage people to stand. But I think it's early days yet."
She later added: "Interestingly, I don't think the sport of football is damaged by what's happened this week. I think Fifa as an organisation, is, and I think the president is."

Infantino's senior adviser quits in protest at World Cup plan
Gianni Infantino's senior adviser Carlos Cordeiro quit on Friday, only adding to the pressure on the Fifa president.
Cordeiro, in announcing his departure, described the initiative to create Fifa Forward Enterprise as a "bad deal for football".
He elaborated in a statement: "Football has been central to my life and after more than 35 years in banking, I understand both the value of this asset and the consequences of giving part of it away. That is why this proposal should be rejected."
Gianni Infantino's statement in full
Here's Gianni Infantino's full statement backing down from his World Cup sell-off plan:
"The Fifa Forward Enterprise project was intended to provide a basis for further strengthening our Fifa Member Associations and our sport worldwide, especially in those countries where support is most needed. And more so, as we said from the outset, to do this only if a majority of the Fifa Member Associations were in support and always subject to a consultation process with them, the FIFA Council, the Confederations and wider stakeholders.
"Having listened carefully to all the views, it has become clear that the project has created divisions of a nature that, regardless of the level of support, are no longer in the interest of the objective set out in the first place.
"Our purpose has always been - and will always be - to unite and improve. As a result, this proposal will not proceed.
"Moving forward, my intent is to bring all interested parties back together in the coming days and weeks in the spirit of shared interest in our game, and with the objective to continue growing football everywhere, particularly in those countries that mostly need our support."
