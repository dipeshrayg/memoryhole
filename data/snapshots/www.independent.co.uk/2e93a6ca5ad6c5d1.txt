No 10 has described the glorification of dangerous driving on social media as "utterly disgraceful" as the government ordered TikTok to take down videos "celebrating" a trend to drive in the wrong direction.
The intervention comes after two police officers - PC Matthew Blades, 37, and PC Tom Clough, 38 - died when the vehicle they were driving was struck head-on by a Volkswagen Passat travelling on the wrong side of the A66 in Teesside on Saturday night.
Five young men travelling in the Passat - Cole Robert Worthy, 17, Theo Rae, 17, Makai Saddington, 18, and 23-year-olds Michael Robert Cahill and Jacob Matusiak - were pronounced dead at the scene. One of the teenagers killed was involved in filming dangerous driving for TikTok before the fatal incident took place.
On Monday, Andy Burnham criticised people who have posted videos of themselves driving recklessly on social media, telling reporters during a visit to Kyiv on Monday: "Anybody who glorifies dangerous driving is doing something that's truly reprehensible."
No 10 also condemned the practice, hitting out at those posting such videos "for clips and entertainment". It is understood the government has contacted TikTok, which is already under investigation by Ofcom as part of a probe into whether the platform's age checks are effective in preventing children from seeing harmful content.
Cleveland police has since said that there is currently no evidence to indicate that any filming of activities that took place on Friday night and Saturday morning has taken place for the purposes of TikTok. Cleveland Police said 12 other people have been arrested over alleged criminality leading up to the collision.
A Downing Street spokeswoman said: "The glorification of dangerous driving for clips and entertainment is utterly disgraceful and that is why we have raised the issue of this appalling content with social media companies since the tragedy, and the Secretary of State for Culture, Media and Sport (Lisa Nandy) is clear that it should be removed in line with their legal obligations."
Earlier on Monday, defence minister Luke Pollard said social media platforms should act to remove posts showing people driving the wrong way down roads, saying they would need to "step up".
"There's lots of content online on social media platforms of people celebrating dangerous driving, celebrating driving inappropriately on our roads down the wrong side of the roads, behaving incredibly badly," he told BBC Breakfast.
"Now that's a decision that social media companies have now. They've argued for many years they don't need to be regulated, that they can police themselves. So this is a challenge for them. Remove that content.
"They know exactly what content is on their site. They know how their algorithm works. They also know that people post content to get views.
"The more views you can get, sometimes you get money for it. So it's a financial incentive for people to post outrageous content that gets more people watching it."
He also told BBC Radio 5 Live that the government will be using powers in the Online Safety Act to compel platforms to take dangerous driving content down.
"But if the social media platforms were to regulate themselves and remove it in the first place, not allow their algorithm to share this type of content, because they know exactly what type of content that algorithm is designed to share, then I think we'd be in a better place," he said.
The online safety regulator has said social media platforms are required under the Online Safety Act to minimise children's exposure to content that encourages dangerous stunts and challenges.
An Ofcom spokesperson said: "Under the Online Safety Act, platforms must minimise children's exposure to violent material and content promoting dangerous challenges.
"In July, we launched an investigation into TikTok's compliance with its child safety duties, and we've shown we'll take action if evidence suggests companies are falling short."
TikTok has said its policies strictly prohibit content that could cause significant physical harm, including dangerous driving, and that it actively removes any videos and accounts that break its rules.
Meanwhile, court records show that Saddington was accused of assaulting a police officer and of causing racially aggravated alarm or distress to another in Middlesbrough on 9 June.
He had appeared before Teesside Magistrates' Court on 17 July charged with the offences and was awaiting trial. Four of the men killed had 37 previous convictions between them.
Cleveland Police had launched a pursuit after spotting the VW acting "suspiciously", the force said in the aftermath of the fatal crash.
Officers began following the car in South Bank, near Middlesbrough in Teesside, before it joined the A66 in the wrong direction.
Officers ended their pursuit, but just 10 seconds later the VW collided with an oncoming police armed response unit at 3.39am. The police car, which was travelling in the right direction, was not part of the pursuit.
In tributes released by Cleveland Police, the two officers' families said they had died "in the line of duty" and that their lives will "never be the same".
"He was our hero with a fantastic sense of humour," PC Clough's family said. "Jumped into action whenever needed, and willing to help anyone for any reason. Proudly fought for his country to make the world a better place. Always on public duty throughout his short life."
In a separate tribute, PC Blades' wife said he was a "devoted" father and her "best friend".
On Monday, police said twelve people have been arrested on offences including being involved with organised crime by detectives investigating the lead up to the A66 crash.
Donations to a fundraiser set up by Cleveland Police to support the families of the two police officers have now reached more than £250,000. Organisers said funds raised would be split equally between the two officers' families and the federation would continue to provide support.
