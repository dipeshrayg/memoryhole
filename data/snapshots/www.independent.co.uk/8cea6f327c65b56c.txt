Anthropic is rolling out its text watermarking more broadly, meaning that text will carry an invisible trail to show that it was written with Claude.
In August, Anthropic announced that it would integrate a text watermark into its models, which includes an invisible pattern inside the words that can be spotted by an automated system. It was launched in response to EU regulation that requires such technology across generative AI tools.
But initially the change was only made to Anthropic's newest models. Now, the company has said that it will be added to older systems - such as Mythos 5, Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6, and Sonnet 4.6 - as well.
As when the change was initially added, there is no way to opt out and it will cover all users globally, despite being a response to European regulation. What's more, the markings are entirely visible and there is no way for normal users to check whether their writing would be flagged by it.
At the moment, only eligible organisations as defined under EU law - such as regulators, law enforcement, journalists and researchers - are able to actually detect the watermark. It is also available to companies that have to verify watermarking under the EU's AI Act.
The announcement of the change in August brought a range of criticism from users who said that it was unfair. There were a whole host of different criticisms, including from users who said that it was not right that even proofreading or fact-checking text with Claude could trigger the detection, and that bad actors would simply be able to re-phrase their work with another AI system to hide the watermark.
Anthropic has accepted that there are some weaknesses in the implementation, including the fact that shorter text might not include enough of the pattern to allow it to be spotted. But it has pushed back against other criticisms, including from users who suggested that the watermarking system would degrade the quality of the outputs.
There does appear to still be a way for users to escape the watermark on some of its tools. On two older models - Opus 4.6 and Claude Sonnet 4.6 - Anthropic says that changing the settings that decide how the system chooses its words, which can be done through the API, will remove the watermark.
Anthropic also allows users to choose even older models, which have not yet been updated to include the watermarking changes. In Claude, users can choose models all the way back to Opus 3 - first released in 2023 - which is not yet covered by the changes.
Other companies have indicated that they will comply with the EU rules, though Anthropic is unusual in rolling it out even in countries that are not covered by them. OpenAI, for instance, has added a feature called textGrain to ChatGPT - but it will only be added automatically in Europe.
