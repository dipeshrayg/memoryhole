Michael Carrick said he still expects to be working with Manchester United over the international break and insisted he will not walk away after his players scored two stoppage-time goals to rescue an extraordinary 4-4 draw against Aston Villa in the Premier League.
The visitors appeared on course for a comfortable win after restoring their two-goal advantage in a crazy game Old Trafford, but late goals from Joshua Zirkzee and Patrick Dorgu saw United pull off their second great escape of the week after Tuesday's incredible comeback victory against Roma.
The result still leaves United in the Premier League's bottom three, however, and they will spend the international break 18th in the table before visiting rivals Liverpool. United have won just once in their first 10 in the Premier League, but Carrick was still applauded off the pitch by the United fans.
"We haven't won enough games, but I expect to move forward and keep working on things that we've been working on and showing a lot of the good in the game today," Carrick said. "We need to tighten up on a few mistakes and win football matches."
Villa boss Unai Emery was hugely frustrated to see his side twice throw away a two-goal lead, and stormed down the tunnel before full-time after Dorgu's equaliser. Villa were unhappy that United goalkeeper Senne Lammens appeared to prevent Zion Suzuki from catching the ball before Dorgu headed in.

Michael Carrick has painfully awkward exchange with interviewer over Man United future
Michael Carrick endured an awkward end to a post-match interview as he had an excruciating exchange about his future as Manchester United manager.
His long-term future appears to very much still be up in the air and his post-match interview on Sky Sports with reporter Patrick Davison was an awkward watch.
Carrick firstly bristled slightly when asked if United are looking at a relegation fight, saying: "No, we're in November. I don't like this situation we're in, I don't like the results we've had but we can definitely do something about it."
From there, things got worse, as Davison asked a lengthy question suggesting there were clearly going to be conversations about his future during the international break over the next couple of weeks, to which Carrick simply responded: "Is there?"
When it was suggested that one would assume so, Carrick tersely said: "Would you?" and when he was pushed for what he thought about his situation, the Man United boss said to Davison: "You're telling me, what do you think".
Aston Villa boss Unai Emery storms down the tunnel after last-gasp Man United equaliser
Aston Villa boss Unai Emery appeared to storm off down the tunnel after Manchester United grabbed a last-gasp equaliser against his side to seal a remarkable 4-4 draw in one of the most chaotic Premier League games of the season.
In his post-match interview with Sky Sports, Emery suggested he simply left because United scored so late that he thought the match was essentially over.
Emery had already watched his side squander a 2-0 lead after the perfect start for the visiting Villans, with Alysson and Nicolas Jackson both finding the net inside the first five minutes.
'That's not going to happen': Michael Carrick will not walk away from Man Utd job
Michael Carrick said he still expects to be working with Manchester United over the international break and insisted he will not walk away after his players scored two stoppage-time goals to rescue an extraordinary 4-4 draw against Aston Villa in the Premier League.
The point means United sit 18th in the table, doing little to ease the intense scrutiny on Carrick given they are now eight league games without a win - a club record in the Premier League era - with their only victory of the season back in August against newly-promoted Ipswich.
Asked on Sky Sports if he would quit if he felt it was the right thing to do for the club, Carrick said: "That's not going to happen."
Then, speaking in his post-match press conference, Carrick expressed confidence his side could still turn things around this season.
"One hundred per cent," he said. "I think there's more than enough. Goals change games and we've got to deal with it and it's something we have to improve on drastically. But there's more than enough, we're just making it too difficult for ourselves at the moment in certain times...
"I'm quite clear with what direction the club needs to go in. We've just got to win games."
Man United's unique comeback leaves no one knowing how to feel
As the Manchester United players who had been told they were not fit to wear the shirt, in perhaps the earliest ever airing of that chant in footballing history, celebrated a second ridiculous comeback in five days, the Stretford End launched into a different chorus.
They had told the team they were unworthy of representing them after a mere five minutes, had jeered in damning fashion when the dreadful Diogo Dalot was substituted, had been vitriolic about the Glazer family, Sir Jim Ratcliffe and Jason Wilcox. But United left the pitch in the relegation zone while their supporters serenaded Michael Carrick.
"It's hard to believe it's not Scholes," they sang. In one respect, it was hard to believe it was not Sir Alex Ferguson, plucking results from nowhere. In another, it was hard to believe this was not the prelude to a sacking. It may still be.
Man Utd 4-4 Aston Villa: Late goals complete another comeback
A huge cheer greeted Diogo Dalot's substitution and Old Trafford would empty as the clock wound down, only for those that stayed to be rewarded with a dramatic conclusion.
Mason Mount hit the crossbar and fellow sub Joshua Zirkzee pulled one back in stoppage time when turning home a Dorgu cross.Those remaining roared on the team and the sixth minute of stoppage time brought bedlam.
Dorgu rose to head home a Fernandes corner, with Suzuki's appeals he was upended trying to meet the cross ignored.
Under-fire Carrick left applauding fans chanting his name at the end of a crazy game, while Villa boss Emery was not there for the conclusion having stormed down the touchline after the leveller.
