England thrashed New Zealand by a record margin in a remarkable performance at Twickenham to establish themselves as World Cup contenders.
The hosts ran rampant on their way to a 37-12 victory, beating the All Blacks in back-to-back meetings for just the second time in their history. New Zealand had promised a response following a shock defeat to Wales but were not allowed to find their flow, with England on top in just about every facet in a complete team performance.
Maro Itoje's men had been inspired by the presence of Lewis Moody, a 2003 World Cup winner, who joined the squad to stare down the haka ahead of kick off. The English players wore personalised boots in support of the former captain's battle against motor neurone disease, and produced a performance of which Moody could be most proud.

The Lewis Moody message that inspired England to record win over New Zealand
England captain Maro Itoje revealed an inspirational speech from predecessor Lewis Moody inspired his side to a record win over New Zealand at Twickenham.
Moody, a 2003 World Cup winner who was diagnosed with motor neurone disease last year, joined Itoje and his side to stare down the All Blacks' haka in an emotional pre-match answer to the traditional Maori challenge.
The 48-year-old had presented Steve Borthwick's squad with their match shirts on Friday evening and addressed the side, setting the tone for a 37-12 success - their largest over the All Blacks.

REPORT: England summon spirit of World Cup winners in remarkable All Blacks thrashing
The last England side to conquer the All Blacks in back-to-back meetings went on to rule the world and on the evidence of this fantastical, fanciful afternoon, Steve Borthwick's men may yet have hope of emulating them.
Twickenham has known a few remarkable rugby days but seldom anything quite like this; not even in 2012, when England ran riot, has this mighty New Zealand team been reduced to rubble quite so spectacularly.
It was an English performance of the heady halcyon days, summoning the spirit of 2003 with a World Cup winner setting the tone.
Perhaps we should have known what was to come when Lewis Moody strode out to join Maro Itoje's men to stare down the haka - the former captain wore white again in a show of unity as he faces a battle beyond rugby; it proved a spell-binding start to a quite extraordinary afternoon.
