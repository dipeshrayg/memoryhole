Two people were killed after Ukrainian forces fired more than 1,000 drones at Russia overnight.
Moscow's mayor described the wave of drones as the "largest ever" attack on the Russian capital, setting alight an oil refinery and a residential building.
Across the wider Moscow region, the attack killed two people and wounded 20, local Governor Andrei Vorobyov said. The dead were a 74-year-old man and a 44-year-old woman, he said.
A video posted on social media by Volodoymr Zelensky showed footage of flames tearing across buildings and large plumes of black smoke rising from the blazing oil refinery.
The Ukrainian president said: "Our long-range responses had a very significant impact in the Moscow region last night. One of Russia's key oil industry facilities and the aggressor's logistics facility were hit."
The attack came as the Kremlin was wrapping up the third and last day of its parliamentary elections.
Ukraine denounced the election as illegal because it included residents of regions Moscow has illegally annexed from Ukraine to tighten president Vladimir Putin's 26-year grip on power.
Most anti-war candidates were barred from the ballot.

Slovak PM Fico says some in West seek war between Russia and Nato
Some Western politicians are seeking conflict between Nato and Russia after a "failed" strategy to defeat Moscow in its war with Ukraine, Slovak prime minister Robert Fico said on Saturday.
Longtime Slovak leader Fico, who has kept up warm relations with Russia and refuses military aid for Ukraine, said this week that Europe was closer than ever to a large-scale war erupting from the Ukraine war.
At the same time, some European leaders, such as those in France and Poland, have warned of Russian hybrid attacks.
In his video message, Fico said, "We face a mortal danger" while watching the West "pour fuel on the fire" with its support for Ukraine, which he said had been to no avail.
"The failure of the strategy to destroy Russia through the war in Ukraine has led some Western politicians to believe that if the goal cannot be achieved through the Ukrainian army and full support for it, then every effort must be made to trigger a conflict between Nato and Russia," Fico said in the video posted on social media.
He said the war in Ukraine was not Slovakia's nor Nato's war but said, "Certain war hawks within Nato are trying, at any cost, to turn this war into a conflict between Russia and Nato."
Earlier this week, Fico warned a drone incursion could spark the activation of Nato's collective defence clause and he wanted to prevent Slovakia - which neighbours Ukraine and is a member of the Nato military alliance - from becoming part of an armed conflict.
He said on Saturday that creating a pretext to start a war was easy and reiterated Slovakia would not send soldiers to fight under a fabricated pretext.
Ukraine denounces Russia's inclusion of annexed regions in parliamentary elections
Ukraine has denounced Moscow for including residents of regions it illegally annexed from Ukraine in Russia's parliamentary elections for the first time since the start of the war.
Kyiv officials called the voting illegal, while a human rights activist said residents in the territories were being coerced into participating in the poll, which forms part of Kremlin efforts to consolidate its gains in Ukraine.
Voting across Russia began on Friday and runs until Sunday. However, balloting in the annexed Ukrainian territories and some areas on the Russian side of the border has been under way since the end of last month, with Russian officials citing security concerns for the extended voting period.
Russian authorities say the Donetsk, Luhansk, Kherson and Zaporizhzhia regions - annexed by Russia in September 2022 in a move not recognised internationally - have more than 3.5 million eligible voters.
Kyiv authorities have called the voting in those territories illegal and urged foreign governments and international organisations not to recognise the election.
They also warned Ukrainians that taking part in organising the vote for the State Duma, Russia's lower house of parliament, could carry criminal liability.
Russian president Vladimir Putin has emphasised the importance of voting in the territories in an election intended to demonstrate public support for the war. The ruling pro-Putin United Russia party is widely expected to retain its dominance.
Two killed in Moscow region after attack with hundreds of drones, mayor says
Hundreds of drones targeted Moscow and the surrounding region on Sunday, killing at least two people and damaging a major oil refinery as Russia's three-day parliamentary election drew to a close, authorities claimed.
Moscow mayor Sergei Sobyanin said more than 1,600 drones had been intercepted and shot down, including 450 targeting the capital.
Sobyanin accused Ukraine of attempting to disrupt the tightly controlled election, saying the "unprecedented attack" had been planned to interfere with the vote.
"The enemy did not succeed," he said on Telegram.
Moscow region governor Andrey Vorobyov said two people - a 44-year-old woman and an elderly man - were killed at separate locations.
A drone crash also caused a fire at a 21-storey building, prompting the evacuation of about 400 people, Vorobyov said.
A facility at the Moscow oil refinery was damaged, according to Sobyanin. No casualties were reported there.
Ukraine has not commented on the attacks.
