Pierre Gasly delivered the shock of the season by taking the first pole position of his career for Sunday's Italian Grand Prix.
Gasly's improbable triumph arrived a day on from his Alpine boss Flavio Briatore accusing an FIA judge of having a "working relationship" with McLaren after the British team's appeal saw the Frenchman stripped of his podium from June's Monaco Grand Prix.
George Russell, widely expected to claim a much-needed pole in his world championship quest, had to settle for second, pipped to top spot by Gasly by just 0.060 seconds.
McLaren's Oscar Piastri finished third, with Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton fourth and fifth respectively for Ferrari. Championship leader Kimi Antonelli took seventh but will start his home race from the back of the grid as he serves an engine penalty.

'It's very emotional': Michael Schumacher tribute paid by brother Ralf ahead of Italian Grand Prix
Ralf Schumacher paid a glowing tribute to his brother Michael on an Italian Grand Prix weekend where Ferrari are honouring their most successful Formula 1 driver.
This season represents 30 years since Michael, six-and-a-half years older than fellow F1 driver and brother Ralf, made his debut for the Scuderia, a team he went on to win five consecutive world championships with.
Both Lewis Hamilton's and Charles Leclerc's cars will adorn Schumacher's logo and seven stars on the chassis to represent Schumacher's title tally - a feat only matched by Hamilton. On Sunday, Sebastian Vettel will also drive a "celebratory" lap in Schumacher's title-winning 2002 car.

Lando Norris teams up with Prime Video for 2027 docu-series
F1 world champion Lando Norris will be subject to a new Prime Video behind-the-scenes docu-series next year.
The show, which has a working title of Lando, will air in 2027 and will offer "unprecedented access" to a "more personal side of his life", as well as "what matters to him beyond the racetrack."
The show will be produced by Box to Box Films, which produces Netflix's hit show Formula 1: Drive to Survive.

F1 heat hazard declared at Italian Grand Prix amid Milan heatwave
This weekend's Italian Grand Prix has been declared a "heat-hazard" race - giving drivers the option to use a cooling system in the cockpit.
Temperatures in Monza, just north-east of Milan, are expected to be higher than 31C - the barometer for a "heat hazard" race - throughout the weekend. On Wednesday, it hit 36C in Milan.
The ruling allows drivers to use a system that cools liquid in their cockpit, using pipes underneath a fireproof top worn under their racing suit.
