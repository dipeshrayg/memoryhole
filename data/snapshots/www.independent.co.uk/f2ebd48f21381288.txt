Experts explain likelihood of Ebola ever spreading to the UK
- A severe Ebola epidemic caused by the Bundibugyo virus in the Democratic Republic of the Congo has resulted in over 10,000 cases and 5,000 deaths, with infections spreading to neighbouring nations.
- Travel-associated cases have already emerged outside Africa, including a returning humanitarian doctor in France, raising questions about international transmission.
- Although Britain has recorded four non-fatal Ebola cases since 1976, health experts emphasise that a major domestic outbreak remains unlikely.
- Ebola only spreads through direct contact with bodily fluids of symptomatic individuals, allowing health authorities to trace and isolate contacts far more effectively than during Covid-19.
- The UK Health Security Agency assesses the overall risk to the British public as low, supported by established NHS procedures for managing potential imported cases.
