Michael van Gerwen and Tom Sykes both threw nine-dart finishes in the first six legs of a pulsating Grand Slam of Darts quarter-final, which saw the Dutchman advance to the last four.
Van Gerwen threw perfection in the opening leg in Wolverhampton to record his first televised nine-darter on Sky Sports in 14 years, with Sykes hitting back soon after for his first ever on a big stage.
It set the tone for an incredible match, which the Dutchman came through 16-11 to end a brilliant breakthrough tournament for Sykes.
The 32-year-old, who came into the tournament ranked 81st in the world, beat Luke Humphries on the way to a first major quarter-final and he was in a good position to take another huge scalp when he led 8-6.
But Van Gerwen proved he is still a force to be reckoned with, winning eight of the next nine legs to take control of the match.
He is into a first major semi-final in over a year and with Luke Littler and Humphries already at home, he will be looking to get his hands on some silverware this week.
"It's just a leg, I know that," Van Gerwen said on stage. "They all witnessed it, I never started a game with a nine-darter before in my life, so that's a good thing.
"Everyone's doubting me. I'm still here. Don't worry. I'm never going to run away. Of course, I had tough times.
"It's been, it's been hard. It's been tough. I've not been producing what I'm capable of.
"But today, I think I played a fantastic game, and then to play against also our top side because it was amazing."
It could not have started much better for him as he hit perfection in the first leg.
The Dutchman threw back-to-back 180s, before taking out 141 with a treble 20, treble 19 and double 12 to win the leg against the throw.
Sykes could have crumbled under the pressure against Van Gerwen, but he hit back with a perfect nine darts of his own in the sixth leg, taking the same route for his first televised nine-darter.
The drama did not finish there as Sykes then reeled in a big fish - a 170 checkout - to take a lead at the midway point of the contest.
He eventually ran out of steam in the longest match he had ever played, but leaves Wolverhampton with his head held high.
Gian van Veen set up an all-Dutch semi-final with Van Gerwen as he won a bruising encounter against Nathan Aspinall.
Van Veen made the early running and eyed a more comfortable victory as he led 8-4, but renowned fighter Aspinall clawed it back and they entered the final stages locked at 14-14.
He regained his scoring form to win successive legs and claim a 16-14 victory.
"Nathan and I, we always have great battles," Van Veen said. "It's always going to be a close game.
"It was close tonight, but I'm really happy with this win, of course.
"I think I will need to play a lot better than I did if Michael plays like he did."
