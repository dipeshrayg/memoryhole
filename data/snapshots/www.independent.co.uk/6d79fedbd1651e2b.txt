Seven Uzbek citizens killed in drone attack on Russia's Tatarstan region, consulate says
Seven Uzbek citizens were killed in a drone attack in Russia's Tatarstan region on Monday, the Uzbek consulate in Kazan said.
The consulate did not immediately provide further details about the victims.
They were among 13 people, including a child, killed in a Ukrainian drone attack on industrial and civilian targets in the city of Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan region, some 680 miles from the border with Ukraine.
It was one of Ukraine's deadliest and deepest strikes inside Russia.
Between two and three million citizens of Uzbekistan, Central Asia's most populous country, work in Russia, with the Uzbek economy partly based on remittances from migrants.
Uzbekistan has struck a diplomatic balance on the war in Ukraine, maintaining close ties with Russia while also stressing support for Ukraine's territorial integrity.
Drone attack kills one in Russia's Voronezh region
A drone attack killed one person and injured two others in Russia's Voronezh region overnight, while falling debris triggered fires at warehouse facilities, Alexander Gusev, the regional governor, said on Tuesday.
Russia's air defence units destroyed 15 drones over the city of Voronezh and five districts of the region, Gusev said on Telegram.
"A drone crash caused fires at two warehouses of a large company. At one facility, the fire has been contained to an area of 16,000 square meters, while at the other, a blaze covering 20,000 square meters is being extinguished. Emergency services are on scene," the governor said.
He did not identify the company whose warehouses were affected.
Earlier on Tuesday, Russian online retailer Wildberries said its logistics facilities in the Voronezh region had been evacuated in accordance with safety requirements.

Ukrainian MiG-29 aircraft lost after catching fire
A Ukrainian MiG-29 fighter jet caught fire and was lost during a combat mission, with the pilot ejecting safely, the Ukrainian Air Force said on Monday.
A statement on the Telegram messaging app said the pilot was targeting Russian positions while in the southern region of Odesa.
"According to preliminary data, during the launch of a missile, an emergency situation occurred, causing the plane to catch fire and the pilot to lose control, even though he tried to save the aircraft," the statement said.
The Air Force said the pilot ejected safely and was transported to a hospital. The cause of the crash was under investigation.

Swedish security service says it broke up Russian spy plot
Sweden's security service says it shut down a Russian spying operation that had tried to influence decision-making there and discredit the Nordic country and its EU and NATO allies.
The operation had been planned and directed by Russia's SVR foreign intelligence service and involved three people working under the cover of diplomatic immunity, Sweden's SAPO service said on Monday.
"This is consistent with the threat we see Russia posing," a SAPO spokesperson told Reuters.
They said the individuals allegedly involved "are no longer in Sweden" and that they have "been able to break up this operation".
The spokesperson declined to give any further information about the operation or the actions taken by the security service.
Russia blocks last anti-war party from upcoming parliamentary election
Russia has effectively shut down a key avenue for voting against the war in the upcoming elections, as most of Vladimir Putin's opponents remain jailed or in exile.
The supreme court upheld a case brought by the small pro-Kremlin nationalist party Rodina to strip voters opposed to the war of their last chance to vote for Yabloko, the country's last remaining anti-war party.
The court barred Yabloko's entire slate of federal election candidates from running for seats in Russia's State Duma.
Following the ruling, hundreds of Yabloko supporters gathered outside the courthouse to protest, as security forces prepared to disperse the crowd. Protesters chanted "Shame! Shame!" as about 500 people gathered outside the court.
Addressing the protesters, Yabloko leader Nikolay Rybakov said the party would appeal the decision.
Yabloko is the only liberal party still operating in Russia, while most of Putin's opponents are jailed, exiled or dead.
Alternative voting options to Putin's de facto party, the ruling United Russia, are tolerated only when they are too weak to win, support the Kremlin or act as "spoilers" by drawing votes away from candidates who could pose a threat to the regime.
The party told AFP over the weekend that 40 of its activists were facing prosecution for opposing the war.
Its deputy leader, Maxim Kruglov, was jailed for seven months in June over social media posts, while Rybakov was fined and barred from elections after posting a picture with Alexei Navalny.

Russia to hand over control of two key Mediterranean bases to Syria
Russian forces will relinquish control of two strategically vital Mediterranean coastal bases, Syria's foreign ministry says, and they will be transformed into joint training centres under a new bilateral agreement.
The development signals a significant recalibration of Moscow's military footprint in the region.
The Hmeimem air base and the Tartus naval base served as crucial Russian military outposts in the eastern Mediterranean throughout Syria's civil war.
Russia was the primary political and military ally of former president Bashar al-Assad, who was ousted in December 2024 by Isis-affiliated insurgents led by now-leader Ahmad al-Sharaa, known by the nom de guerre Abu Mohammed al-Jolani, and subsequently sought exile in Russia.
