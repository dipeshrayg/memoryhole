At least three people, including a child, were among those killed in Russian strikes in the region surrounding capital Kyiv in the early hours today, officials said.
The Russian strikes were recorded at three locations in the Boryspil district, near the capital. Three more people were injured in the attack, said Tymur Tkachenko, head of the Kyiv military administration.
Fires and powerful explosions that shook the centre of the city were reported in two districts, mayor Vitali Klitschko said.
The attack comes at a time Ukraine is evidently chronically short of air defences capable of downing Russia's ballistic missiles.
Yesterday, a Russian strike hit a football stadium in Odesa, injuring at least one person.
The strike hit the 34,000-seater Chornomorets Stadium. The shelling destroyed the roof, windows, and stands, according to Ukraine's State Emergency Service.
A 29-year-old woman was injured with shrapnel wounds, Odesa's regional governor Oleh Kiper said.
The attack came a day before a Ukrainian Premier League game between Chornomorets and Kolos. No official announcement has been made yet over whether the match will be postponed or cancelled.
Zelensky wants 'concrete results' in developing anti-ballistic missile defence system
Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine aims to achieve "concrete results" by the end of this year in developing a European anti-ballistic missile defence system under the Freyja programme.
"Ukraine is capable of producing its missile and launcher, while our partners will provide the necessary components and critical elements - radars, sensors, and everything else required ... "We are creating all the necessary conditions to make that happen."
Ukraine are hitting deep inside Russia - but don't expect Putin to capitulate yet
Russia's war on Ukraine has entered its latest phase, with each side making use of increasing numbers of long-range munitions to strike deep into the other's territory.
In the early summer of this year, there was a surge of optimism across the media when it became clear that Ukraine was stepping up its strikes at enormous ranges into Russia, and there was little that Moscow could do about it. But that optimism needs to be balanced against the new reality that with anti-missile interceptor systems exhausted, there is not much Ukraine itself can do to blunt Russian missile attacks. For now, those attacks sow misery and death by blasting residential areas with little warning: but next winter, they could once again target heating and power infrastructure and have an even more devastating effect on Ukraine's ability to keep its people alive than last year.
Russia's campaign against Ukrainian civilian infrastructure is not new: it began almost as soon as it became clear that Russia had failed in its initial objective of swiftly overrunning the country in February 2022. But its new character today results both from massive increases in the numbers of missiles and drones available to Russia, and the way they have outstripped Ukraine's air defences.
The reluctance of allies to provide the weapons needed to take the fight to Moscow has seen the Ukrainians carry out their own campaign against their enemies' infrastructure - but, Keir Giles writes, we know by now that Russia's leader is happy to let his country burn.
Zelensky arrives in Serbia for first visit
Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky arrived in Serbia on Friday for his first visit to the Balkan country expected to focus on economic cooperation and bids by both countries to secure European Union membership.
Zelensky posted a video on Telegram showing his arrival ahead of talks with populist president Aleksandar Vucic and other officials.
"We will be speaking about expanding economic ties between our countries, relations with the European Union, and other things that can work for our peoples, as well as security issues," Zelensky wrote.
Zelenskiy's visit will likely sour ties between Serbia and Russia, its long-time ally and main natural gas supplier.
On Thursday, Vucic said the two leaders would discuss European Union accession bids as well as economic and energy cooperation.

Russian attacks kill two in Ukraine's southeastern Dnipropetrovsk Region
Russian strikes on Ukraine's southeastern Dnipropetrovsk Region on Friday killed two people and injured six, the regional governor said.
Oleksandr Hanzha said Russian forces had launched nearly 50 attacks on five districts in the region, deploying drones, artillery and aerial bombs.
Two people were killed near Nikopol, a frequent Russian target on the western bank of the Dnipro River, opposite the Russian-held Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant.
One of the wounded was in a serious condition, Ganzha said.

Russia dismisses German drone incident as 'fabricated provocation'
The Russian embassy in Berlin dismissed a suspected drone attack at a major German air freight hub this week as "fabricated provocation" on Friday, saying it was another example of accusations against Russia without evidence.
"The embassy is expressing its concern about a new wave of anti-Russian hysteria in Germany," said a statement published on its website in German and Russian.
German federal prosecutors launched a probe this week into what they described as a serious attack that could have impacted foreign and domestic security.
"It is obvious that this hastily fabricated provocation only serves the interests of Kyiv and the militarist wing of the European political class," the Russian embassy said in its statement.
