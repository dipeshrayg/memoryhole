Firefighters in France and Spain face a race against time to bring their raging wildfires under control before heatwave conditions return to the two countries, officials said.
On Tuesday, French meteorological agency Meteo-France placed Gironde and Landes under a yellow heatwave warning starting at noon today. The alert affects more than a dozen departments in the east of France.
Spain's interior minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska called it an "absolutely crucial" period, with weather forecasters in both countries warning temperatures could reach 40C again in some parts later today.
Spain's state meteorological agency says the new heatwave will then last until "at least" Sunday, "making firefighting efforts more difficult due to high temperatures [and] locally gusty southerly winds".
Emmanuel Macron said France was facing its most difficult situation since the Second World War, urging fire crews to "stay strong" as they try to bring the "unprecedented" blaze under control.
Authorities warned that the fires raging just 15km outside the city of Bordeaux could last for months, with crews from all over the country forced to rotate in to extinguish the blaze.
More than 360,000 people have been evacuated across France and Spain due to fires as Europe bakes in one of the hottest summers on record.

Full story: Huge wildfires rage in France and Spain as firefighters race against time before next 40C heatwave
Emergency services tackling massive wildfires in Spain and France are in a race against time to get the blazes under control before another 40C heatwave hits Europe this week.
In France, flames have burned through an area roughly four times the size of Paris and are now just nine miles outside the country's sixth-largest city of Bordeaux, with no sign of slowing down. In central Spain, three separate fronts remained active in the mountains northwest of Madrid in the provinces of Madrid, Toledo and Avila.
The crisis is expected to intensify further with temperatures set to hit 40C in Spain and France within days, according to the meteorological agencies of both countries. The soaring temperatures come amid warnings the wildfires in France could burn for weeks, if not months.
France warns wildfires could burn for 'several months'
French officials warned the raging wildfires could burn for weeks if not months before they are fully extinguished.
Lieutenant Colonel David Annotel from the National Federation of Firefighters of France told RTL that it would take "many weeks, probably several months" before authorities could declare the fire in the south-west completely extinguished.
Emmanuel Macron has pledged the government's full support in responding to one of the largest disasters faced by France since the Second World War.
"We will be there, and together we are going to win this battle, and we will also be there over the long term," he said.
He added that when the blaze is extinguished "we'll have to replant and rebuild a different forest because this forest is not the same".
