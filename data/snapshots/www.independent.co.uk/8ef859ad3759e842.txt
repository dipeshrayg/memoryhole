A 10-tonne electric commercial aircraft has completed its first crewed flight in China.
According to the Commercial Aircraft Corporation of China (COMAC), aircraft CE-25A operated a 17-minute test flight in Binzhou, in east China's Shandong Province, on Saturday.
On board the CE-25A, Comac's first electric commercial aircraft, were two pilots and one flight-test engineer.
Comac said that the "aircraft operated normally, and all performance indicators met expectations."
The aircraft is designed for intercity commercial flights with a capacity for 19 passengers and a maximum takeoff weight of 10 tonnes.
CE-25A has a cruising speed of 300 km per hour, a cruising altitude of 2,400 met and a range of 400 km, said Comac.
In February, the aircraft developed by Comac, Chinese battery maker CATL and Shanghai Jiao Tong University completed its first unmanned autonomous flight.
It's not the only electric plane set to take off with passengers. Loganair will operate a fleet of at least five electric planes from 2029. The planes will support passenger and cargo operations, the regional airline said.
It will be the first commercial airline in Europe to operate an electric aircraft fleet, it added, following the successful trial of a plane earlier in 2026.
The Scottish airline agreed to purchase the Beta Alia CX300 all-electric planes, with an option of a further five, on the first day of the Farnborough Airshow in July.
The electric planes will operate from existing runways and can recharge in 20 to 40 minutes.
Loganair chief executive Luke Farajallah said: "Our demonstration programme earlier this year proved that electric aviation is no longer a future concept; it is a viable commercial opportunity.
"The aircraft demonstrated the potential to reduce operating costs by up to 80 per cent while maintaining the reliable regional connectivity our customers and communities depend on."
