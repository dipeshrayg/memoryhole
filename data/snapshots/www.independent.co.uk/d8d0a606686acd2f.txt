Tory leader Kemi Badenoch has confirmed that every Conservative candidate at the next general election will have to sign a document committing them to two flagship policies: leaving the European Convention on Human Rights and scrapping the net zero target.
The two positions prospective candidates must endorse are quitting the convention and abandoning the goal of reaching net zero emissions by 2050.
Mrs Badenoch set out the requirement at the party's regional East Midlands conference.
She told the event: "All Conservative candidates at the next election will be required to support the party's official policy that Britain will leave the European Convention on Human Rights.
"Let me be really clear: if any deal is signed with the European Union between now and then that prevents us from leaving the ECHR, we will reverse that deal too."
The Tory leader said the so-called "candidate covenant" sets out "basic Conservative ideas based on our party's principles".
Her position on net zero marks a significant shift in Conservative policy from the era when the party was in government and made the commitment.
She reiterated that Conservatives would need to back her plans to repeal the Climate Change Act of 2008 and the net zero target.
"It doesn't matter if you are a member of my shadow cabinet, or someone who has been an MP for decades, or someone standing for our party for the first time - signing this covenant will be a mandatory condition to stand as a concerted candidate at the next election," she said.
She insisted it is not a "purity test" and said while she hopes her parliamentary colleagues will sign, it is "entirely up to them".
She added: "The Conservative Party has never been as united as it is now, and I would hate to lose any of my brilliant team."
Former Conservative MP and veterans minister Tobias Ellwood has already said the move bars him from standing for the party.
Mr Ellwood, another ex-minister who lost his seat at the last election, said he had applied to stand again - but was told he did not make the cut.
"I did not pass the ... purity tests to be able to be considered as a candidate at the next general election ... that is quite a sad place to be right now, so yes, I'm just reflecting on that," he told BBC Radio 5Live.
He said it was opportunity, enterprise and responsibility that "binds all Conservatives".
He added: "When you sign up to individual policies of the day, can you imagine if ... prior to the war, you had to sign up to Chamberlain's 'Peace in our time' speech?
"Churchill wouldn't have been allowed to stand, or if you had to sign up to Suez.
"I mean, there must be times where you can have different views - and different views within a party are actually helpful."
Former Tory whip Sir Mark Spencer took to the stage to become the first to sign the covenant, sitting at a table beside Mrs Badenoch and then posing with the documents in hand.
The former minister, who lost his parliamentary seat in 2024, introduced Mrs Badenoch for her speech.
