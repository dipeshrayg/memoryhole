Donald Trump said on Saturday that it was time for new leadership in Ukraine, after president Volodymyr Zelensky lashed out at the controversial new US-Russian deal on diesel.
The US president told reporters at the White House that Zelensky "wants to make problems for the world" by authorising attacks on Russian refineries.
"He better damn well stop," Trump said, adding: "I suggest they get a new leader who can make a deal."
Russia and Ukraine have ramped up attacks on each other's energy infrastructure in recent months, raising the global price of diesel and affecting farmers in the US.
Ukraine had shown willing to engage in a so-called 'energy ceasefire' with Russia, but Moscow signalled no public commitment and reportedly sought sanctions relief in private.
Trump on Friday announced a deal to lift sanctions and Moscow to supply some 300,000 metric tons of diesel to the US and global markets. Zelensky told Axios the deal was "absolutely terrible" and looked like a birthday present for Putin.

Zelensky says Ukraine will halt refinery strikes if Russia stops energy attacks
Ukraine is ready to halt attacks on Russian oil refineries if Moscow stops striking Ukrainian energy infrastructure, president Volodymyr Zelensky said on Sunday.
Any agreement would require "real guarantees", Zelensky said, adding: "It's possible to stop strikes on energy infrastructure as early as today, but Putin does not want to do so."
Trump on Saturday blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries for pushing up US diesel prices, saying Zelensky had "better d**n well stop".
He also suggested Ukraine "get a new leader who can make a deal" with Moscow.
Zelensky said he would continue discussions with European leaders on Sunday. Ukraine's foreign minister, Andrii Sybiha, is due to attend an EU foreign affairs meeting on Monday, where ministers are expected to discuss further sanctions on Russia and Ukraine's EU accession.
Germany says support for Ukraine unchanged after Trump's Russia deal
Germany said on Saturday it would maintain sanctions against Russia over its war in Ukraine, after US president Donald Trump's decision to temporarily ease restrictions on Russian fuel exports.
"The federal government has taken note of the US decision relating to Russian diesel deliveries," government spokesperson Stefan Kornelius said.
"The federal government remains committed to the decisions on sanctions against Russia and is working with its European partners on further sanctions," Kornelius said, adding that Germany's support for Ukraine remained unchanged and diplomatic efforts to end the war would continue.
Trump announced on Friday that Russia had agreed to supply diesel to global markets in an effort to bring down fuel prices. His administration temporarily lifted US sanctions on Russian fuel, which were designed to deprive Moscow of revenue for its war effort.
Germany is Ukraine's largest European donor. Chancellor Friedrich Merz visited Kyiv last weekend and has repeatedly pledged to maintain Berlin's support.
Miami talks end without breakthrough as Ukraine delegation heads home
A delegation of Ukrainian officials headed home on Saturday after talks with US and European representatives in Miami aimed at developing proposals to end Russia's war in Ukraine ended earlier than planned.
The discussions on Friday brought together Ukraine's negotiating team, led by presidential office chief Kyrylo Budanov, US special envoy Steve Witkoff and Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, and European representatives.
US envoy Witkoff described the meeting as "constructive", but no breakthrough or ceasefire agreement was announced. The talks covered security guarantees for Ukraine, post-war reconstruction, territorial questions and measures to protect energy infrastructure ahead of winter.
On Friday, Trump announced a deal with Vladimir Putin to ease restrictions on Russian diesel exports.
Ukraine's presidential adviser Dmytro Lytvyn said the first day had run longer than expected and had taken on "a slightly different flavour". The team was expected to brief president Volodymyr Zelensky in Kyiv, while he continued consultations with European leaders.

UK PM pledges stronger sanctions on Russia after call with Zelensky
British prime minister Andy Burnham has pledged to strengthen sanctions on Russia after telling Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky that the UK was in "complete solidarity" with Ukraine, following Donald Trump's call for Kyiv to replace its leader.
Burnham told Zelensky he recognised that "the past 24 hours had been difficult for Ukraine", according to a Downing Street readout of their call on Saturday.
The two leaders discussed "the importance of maintaining pressure on Russia", with Burnham making clear that the UK "would continue to strengthen its sanctions on Russia and to take further action to constrain the Russian war machine".
They agreed to meet in person in the coming days, Downing Street said.
Trump has blamed Ukrainian attacks on Russian oil refineries for pushing up global diesel prices and demanded that Zelensky stop striking them.

Ukraine's foreign minister defends Zelensky after Trump calls for new president
Ukraine's foreign minister Andrii Sybiha has defended President Volodymyr Zelensky after Donald Trump called for Ukraine to elect a new leader.
"President Zelensky leads our nation in a time of war - a war for independence, freedom, and dignity," Sybiha wrote on X on Saturday. "He has the support of the Ukrainian people, and they have the right to decide. Statements from abroad only confirm that he is doing his job well. He deserves support."
Trump called for Zelensky to be replaced on Saturday, accusing the Ukrainian president of failing to reach a deal to end the war with Russia.
Suspected drone attack disrupts third Yandex data centre in four days
Russian technology company Yandex has shut down a third data centre in four days after a suspected drone attack, with outages reported across its taxi-booking and other digital services, according to The Kyiv Independent.
Yandex Cloud reported power disruptions at its facility in Vladimir Oblast on Sunday, while Russian state news agency TASS reported that the data centre had been completely shut down.
The reported incident follows attacks on Yandex data centres in Ryazan Oblast on Thursday and Kaluga Oblast on Friday. Yandex confirmed damage at both facilities, with the first forced to suspend operations entirely and several modules disabled at the second.
Users across Russia and several other countries reported problems with Yandex Go, the company's taxi-booking app, as well as its services for drivers, couriers and smart-home devices.
Zelensky says Russia 'thanked' Trump for diesel sanctions relief with deadly attack
Russian glide bombs killed 20 people, including three children, and wounded 18 in the southern Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia on Saturday.
Ukrainian officials said Russian forces dropped five guided aerial bombs on residential buildings, reducing part of an apartment block to rubble. A six-year-old girl was among those killed.
President Volodymyr Zelensky condemned the attack a day after Donald Trump announced a deal to ease sanctions on Russian diesel exports.
"Today, Russia said thank you for the lifting of sanctions on diesel exports by dropping five glide bombs on residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia," Zelensky said.
He warned that Moscow interpreted any easing of pressure as a signal it could continue attacking Ukrainian cities.
