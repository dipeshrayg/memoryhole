Nato's top commander bluntly warned Russia on Monday that his forces would destroy "anything and everything" involved in any attack if Moscow is "foolish enough" to launch an assault.
US air force general Alexus Grynkewich, outlining his 'theory of victory' at a security conference in Warsaw, said the defensive alliance would block, blunt and break any attack on any member.
"If someone is foolish enough to attack us, we will fight," he said. "We will stop the attack and deny them the ability to achieve their objectives, whatever those might be."
"We will, in short, ensure any attack, against any ally, proves utterly futile."
European governments have accused Moscow of using sabotage, drone incursions and cyberattacks against them and Russia warned last month it would use nuclear weapons if Nato attempted to cut off its exclave of Kaliningrad.
Meanwhile Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky demanded his US counterpart Donald Trump pressure Russia to honour an energy ceasefire after alleging Moscow attacked Ukrainian infrastructure overnight.
Zelensky said such strikes continued hours after Trump announced that Russia had agreed to a limited ceasefire on energy targets. The Kremlin has yet to confirm or deny those claims.
Ukraine hits back over Russia's claims it 'doesn't deserve a ceasefire'
Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha, writing on social media, pushes back on his Russian counterpart's assertion that Volodymyr Zelensky "has not earned a respite on the battlefield".
"Lavrov, however, has already earned a front-row seat at the Special Tribunal for the Crime of Aggression," Sybiha writes. "The more irrelevant and cynical comments - the more evidence for the Tribunal."
Sergei Lavrov had suggested the Ukrainian president was undeserving of an energy ceasefire as announced by US president Donald Trump.
Trump defends diesel deal with Russia amid backlash
Donald Trump appeared to defend Friday's announcement of a controversial diesel deal with Russia.
Writing on Truth Social on Monday, he said: "The European Union paid Russia 250 Billion Dollars for Energy, and the Fake News Media doesn't want to get near that story. I made the right choice for the American People - ALWAYS, AMERICA FIRST! President DONALD J. TRUMP"
The Helsinki-based think tank Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA) estimates that the EU has paid €238bn ($266bn) to import Russian fossil fuels since the beginning of the full-scale invasion in 2022.
The latest: Zelensky demands Trump pressure on Putin after 'energy ceasefire breached'
Volodymyr Zelensky has demanded that US president Donald Trump pressure Russia to honour the ceasefire after alleging that Moscow's overnight attack targeted Ukrainian energy infrastructure.
"Americans must make sure Russians respect the energy ceasefire," he said. "We need American pressure on Russia, not sanctions relief so we can achieve an energy ceasefire," Zelensky said.
Zelensky said such strikes continued hours after Trump announced that Russia had agreed to a limited ceasefire on energy targets. The Kremlin has yet to confirm or deny those claims.
Power outages were reported overnight in parts of Ukraine following ongoing Russian strikes on energy infrastructure, including a power plant. Zelensky on Monday said Kyiv was willing to honour the energy ceasefire if Russia agrees to it as well. "We agree if they agree," he said. "We have already shown that we mean it. Last night Russian oil refineries were not attacked," he added.
Meanwhile, the Financial Times reported that the US is threatening to stop the procurement system, Purl, that provides Ukraine with weapons in order to press Kyiv to stop its deep strikes on Russian energy infrastructure.
Germany's Merz says Russia not serious about peace, calls for tougher sanctions
Russia has shown that it is not serious about peace talks by responding to the US proposal for an energy ceasefire with fresh attacks on Ukraine's civilian energy infrastructure, said German Chancellor Friedrich Merz in Berlin on Monday.
"Russia will only negotiate seriously under pressure, which is why we are sticking to our sanctions against Russia," said Merz. "Following Moscow's ruthless attacks on Ukraine's civilian energy infrastructure, we must actually tighten them further."
People are rejoicing about the removal of Russian bots from social media - but the truth is far more complicated
Social media is busy with claims that something dramatic just happened to the online environment.
Huge numbers of posts claim that Russian bot traffic has fallen dramatically across the internet. And, with that, so has the number of posts and replies from automated accounts on sites such as X, formerly Twitter, and Facebook, the posts claim.
It has led to something of a trend on X, in which users post what they call a "bot test": an opinion or claim of the kind that would ordinarily lead to an influx of automated responses. Some users do indeed claim that they have seen a substantial reduction in the number of replies of a certain kind, and use that to suggest both that the previous responses were from automated accounts and that those accounts have reduced in number.

Europe should impose more sanctions on Russia, says Poland's Tusk
European countries should increase sanctions on Russia and all Western nations should show their solidarity with Ukraine, Polish prime minister Donald Tusk said.
"Russia is not interested in a ceasefire, de-escalation, or peace... that is why I will go to Brussels to urge all European partners to consider increasing sanctions," Tusk told the Warsaw Security Forum.
"Ukraine cannot be broken or intimidated. Poland cannot be intimidated. The world cannot be broken or intimidated. That signal must go out today from every capital, from Warsaw to Washington."
EU foreign policy chief doubts Trump's 'energy ceasefire'
The European Union's top diplomat on Monday welcomed the prospect of a pause in energy strikes in Ukraine, but said she sees no sign that Russia would be willing to respect it.
"We haven't yet seen that also Russia will stick to it," EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said at a meeting of the bloc's foreign ministers in Luxembourg. "Personally, I would also welcome that the ceasefire would include people not being killed, not only energy."
Kallas also pushed back on Trump's comments on Saturday that it was time for Ukraine to get a new president and that Zelensky has not done enough to end the war. She said that now is not the time to hold elections in Ukraine, at least not without a ceasefire.
On Monday, Zelensky said holding elections in Ukraine was impossible during war time, especially with new Russian tactics involving attacks during both the day and night.
He added, however, that he would be ready to work towards doing so if Ukraine secured a full ceasefire lasting at least 60 days.
Kremlin offers one-day ceasefire for Ukraine to hold election
Russian president Vladimir Putin would be ready to order his forces not to attack Ukraine during a presidential election - but only on the day of the vote itself, according to Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov.
The Kremlin earlier rejected a 60-day ceasefire idea proposed by Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky to allow his country to hold presidential elections, claiming it wants an overall peace deal instead.
Zelensky backs 'energy ceasefire', but no confirmation from Putin
Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine will support a bilateral "energy ceasefire" with Russia, after being caught off guard by Donald Trump's shock announcement that a deal had already been agreed.
In a post on Truth Social on Sunday, the US president said: "Effective immediately, there is an ENERGY CEASEFIRE in the War between Russia and Ukraine. Both parties have agreed. Do not break it!"
The Ukrainian president said he had not heard of such a deal, but would agree to it if Russia did, according to Axios. Kyiv had publicly backed a truce on striking energy infrastructure before Trump announced on Friday plans to lift sanctions on Moscow to supply diesel to the US and global markets - much to Zelensky's chagrin.
The Kremlin has yet to acknowledge Trump's claim to have established a ceasefire between the warring sides. Russia had reportedly sought sanctions relief as a concession for its adherence to a ceasefire earlier in the week.
The controversial diesel deal with Russia has proven controversial even within Trump's own base as the president looks to bring down energy prices in the US ahead of the November midterm elections.
Trump hit out at Zelensky on Saturday, saying it was time for Ukraine to find a new leader after Kyiv struck Russian oil refineries. Zelensky said on Sunday he would hold new elections if Putin agrees to a 60-day "total ceasefire".
Zelensky says EU to provide €3bn in military funding this week
The EU will disburse almost €3bn (£2.5bn) in defence support to Ukraine this week as part of its €90bn (£76bn) support loan, Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky said.
"We deeply appreciate every concrete step taken to support our defense and our people," Zelensky said.
Since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, EU countries have provided €200bn (£169 bn) in economic, financial, and military support to Ukraine and approved €90bn (£76bn) more over the next two years as an EU support loan.
