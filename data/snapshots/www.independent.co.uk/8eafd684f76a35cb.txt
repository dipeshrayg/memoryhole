Volodymyr Zelensky praised "real leaders" for backing his country in an apparent swipe at US president Donald Trump.
The Ukrainian president said reporters it was "crucial" to count on support from allies and praised "real leaders" for backing Kyiv. "I am thankful the EU stands with us," he said.
Zelensky's comments came after Trump said Ukraine's resumption of strikes on Russian oil refineries was a "disgrace" that would keep global energy prices elevated.
"I'm not a big fan of Zelensky," he told Newsmax. "I think they actually both would like to see it end, but I think I find Zelensky to be more difficult to deal with than Putin."
Kyiv's forces struck Russian oil refineries on Thursday, defying Trump and his "energy ceasefire".
After Russian president Vladimir Putin vowed to retaliate, Zelensky said Ukraine needed to prepare for the winter in terms of energy and air defence as Moscow had shown no sign it was ready to end the war.
Following Trump's surprise announcement on Sunday that Moscow and Kyiv had agreed to halt attacks on energy infrastructure, Kyiv suspended strikes on Russian refineries. But in the days that followed, Russia was accused of launching missile and drone strikes on energy targets such as power stations.
EU leaders push back on a major budget proposal, putting a deal by end of the year in doubt
European Union leaders voiced strong opposition to a new proposal for the bloc's long-term budget, raising serious doubt about whether the key spending package will be agreed by the end of the year.
"This is far too big, far too expensive," Swedish prime minister Ulf Kristersson told reporters at an EU summit focused, in part, on trying to narrow down agreement on the seven-year budget, known as the multiannual financial framework.
"It would increase the Swedish net contribution (by) almost 60 per cent. That is simply totally unacceptable," Kristersson said. Sweden is one of about 10 net contributing countries paying more into the EU budget than they get back in allocations.

How Ukrainian strikes on data centres are upending daily life in Russia
After battering Russia's version of Amazon, Ukraine has set its sights on data centres - dealing an unlikely blow to Moscow's dating scene.
A barrage of attacks on data centres and telecommunications infrastructure has wreaked havoc on Russian daily life, including three strikes in the past week on buildings owned by Yandex - the Russian equivalent of Google.
Yandex said its infrastructure had been damaged, causing the complete shutdown of its data centre in the Vladimir region on Sunday. On Wednesday, Kyiv's drones struck a data centre owned by Sberbank, the country's largest bank.

How the relationship between Trump and Zelensky soured
Relations between Kyiv and Washington have deteriorated in recent days following a meeting in Miami on 9 October that convened representatives from both countries as well as Europe and the World Bank.
President Donald Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, the US administration's Middle East envoy, struck a tough tone with Ukraine in the meeting, according to a person familiar with the matter.
They told Ukrainian officials that the US was frustrated with Kyiv's continued strikes on Russian refineries despite repeated requests from Washington to stop.
The US delegation also explained Washington's decision to strike a diesel deal with Russia, saying the White House hoped it would soften Moscow's stance on peace talks.
That deal, under which Russia will supply more than 2.25 million barrels of diesel to US and global markets, has triggered a backlash from some US lawmakers, who say it will give Moscow cash to pursue its war without forcing president Vladimir Putin to make concessions to end the conflict.
In the days that followed, Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky and Trump criticised each other, with Zelensky calling the US decision on the Russian diesel deal "weak".
Trump in turn called for elections in Ukraine and Zelensky's ouster.
AfD leader threatens to pull German funding for Ukraine
The head of Germany's far-right party said that Berlin should end its support for Ukraine as the country is too small to defend itself against the invading Russian forces.
Alice Weidel, co-leader of the Alternative for Germany party (AfD), told The Economist that continuing the war is "not in our interest" and that it would be "wrong" to continue financing what she called "an already lost war".
"Ukraine is too small. Ukraine cannot win against Russia. It is impossible," she said, adding: "We are about to spend over 100 billion. No other country has spent that much, and we are highly indebted. I have the interest of my country first; it's Germany first and this war is not in our interest."

Ukraine testing new 'evacuation drone' that rescues wounded soldiers
Six propellers whir to life, lifting a huge drone above a field in Ukraine. A weighted stretcher secured to its frame simulates a wounded soldier in body armour.
Far from the front line, the demonstration offers a glimpse of a far more dangerous mission: flying into a battlefield where enemy drones and artillery can leave wounded troops stranded for weeks or months, then carrying them to safety without an evacuation crew.
The aerial medical evacuation drone was developed over the past year by Ukrainian defence technology company SkyFall.

Trump says Ukraine's resumption of strikes on Russian refineries is 'disgrace'
Ukraine's resumption of strikes on Russian refineries is a 'disgrace' that keeps global energy prices elevated, US president Donald Trump has said.
Ukraine struck a Russian oil refinery after president Volodymyr Zelensky declared that Kyiv was abandoning a ceasefire covering energy sites that Trump had announced, and criticised his willingness to strike deals with Moscow.

Trump says he is talking to Russia and China about denuclearization
US president Donald Trump said Washington was holding talks with Moscow and Beijing aimed at denuclearizing the countries with the biggest stockpiles of nuclear weapons.
Russia has the most stored nuclear warheads, followed by the United States and China, according to the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute. The US outpaces Russia on its number of deployed warheads.
During an interview with Newsmax, Trump said he expects China to "catch up" with the US within the next five years.
"The whole thing is a shame, because I'd love to denuclearize if possible, but we're actually talking to Russia about that, and we're talking to China about that denuclearization," Trump told the outlet.
Order to limit Starshield for Ukraine came from inside White House - report
The order to limit a satellite system used by Ukraine for battlefield communications and drone operations came from inside the White House, two US officials and a Western official told Reuters.
The officials said leaders at the defence department received a message that the SpaceX Starshield service for the Ukrainian military needed to be slowed.
The officials did not specify who in the White House made the decision or who was responsible for implementing the order. The Pentagon is the biggest customer of Starshield, which is the military-grade version of the Starlink service.
Ukrainian officials said this week that military-grade Starlink receptors stopped working in Ukraine when mounted on fast-moving objects, interfering with the performance of Ukrainian drones that rely on them for onboard guidance systems over Ukrainian territory.
