Zelensky to meet Witkoff in Oslo
Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelensky will fly to Oslo to meet US special envoy Steve Witkoff on Tuesday, trying to repair ties with Washington that have deteriorated sharply over the past two weeks as president Donald Trump has increasingly courted Moscow.
Trump, who has waived sanctions on Russia in what he says is an effort to lower US fuel prices, has repeatedly rebuked Zelensky. Kyiv says Washington has lately failed to provide early warning notification of Russian missile launches, and has throttled SpaceX satellite links that guide Ukrainian drones.
On Monday, Trump said "Russia, frankly, has been fine on Ukraine" and "Zelensky is not helping matters much". He called on Kyiv to find "somebody else to negotiate".
Zelensky said he had spoken with secretary of state Marco Rubio on issues including ballistic missile interceptors, early warning of Russian missile attacks and "the situation with Starlink/Starshield, which needs to be restored", referring to the SpaceX satellite system.
"Of course, all of this comes against the backdrop of our diplomatic efforts to end this horrific war and the constant killing of people," Zelensky said in a post on X.
South Korea to withdraw some embassy staff from Ukraine
South Korea on Tuesday said it will withdraw some staff from its embassy in Kyiv, deepening a diplomatic row over Ukraine's announcement last month of a transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war that Seoul says was meant to be secret.
The diplomatic dispute between the countries began after Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky disclosed the prisoners' transfer during a 23 September speech at the United Nations. South Korea has accused Ukraine of violating a nondisclosure agreement, but Ukraine denies the countries reached such a deal, further angering Seoul.
South Korea recalled its ambassador to Ukraine earlier this month in protest. Ukrainian foreign ministry spokesperson Heorhii Tykhyi told local media at the time that Kyiv remains open to "constructive dialogue" and that "diplomacy must prevail over emotions".
South Korean foreign ministry spokesperson Park Doosoon said that the withdrawal of additional embassy staff was due to Ukraine's "lack of constructive steps" to address Seoul's grievances as well as concerns about safety amid intensifying Russian attacks.

Russia says it hit cargo vessels and logistics hubs in Ukraine
The Russian military struck three cargo vessels in Ukraine's port of Reni and another cargo vessel en route to Odesa, the Russian defence ministry said on Telegram.
It also said it hit two logistics hubs in the Volyn region and in the city of Khmelnitskiy.
The Russian defence ministry claimed all the vessels and the logistics hubs were being used for military purposes.
Zelensky travels to Norway for talks on war and security
Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelensky is arriving in Oslo on Tuesday for talks on the war in Ukraine and European security, the Norwegian government and co-host Finland said.
Reuters reported on Monday that US president Donald Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff will travel to Norway this week for a round of negotiations on ending the war in Ukraine, and that Zelensky and European representatives were expected to attend.
"President Zelensky has asked Norway and Finland to arrange a meeting as part of ongoing diplomatic efforts, at a critical time for Ukraine," Norwegian prime minister Jonas Gahr Stoere said in a statement.
Finland's president Alexander Stubb said he will attend meetings with Zelensky and Stoere to discuss "Russia's illegal war of aggression against Ukraine and the overall security situation in Europe".
Zelensky, Stubb and Stoere will hold a press conference later on Tuesday, Norway said.
The statements from Norway and Finland made no mention of any US or European delegations.

Belarus bars entry to some goods from Latvia
Belarus, one of Russia's closest allies, said on Monday it was barring the entry of a number of goods from neighbouring Latvia because of the Baltic state's "unfriendly" relations.
Belarus's government had slapped the ban on a range of products including canned fish and various industrial goods, state news agency Belta said.
"This decision was taken in connection with the Latvian side maintaining an unconstructive approach and unfriendly rhetoric in relation to Belarus," Belta quoted the government decision as saying.
Latvia closed its border with Belarus in July.
Like Belarus's other neighbours, Latvia has encountered difficulties with Minsk over mass attempts by migrants to cross the Belarusian border into the European Union.
Belarus denies colluding or encouraging migrants from other countries to attempt to cross its borders into the EU.
Led by president Alexander Lukashenko since 1994, Belarus has long been accused of human rights abuses subject to Western sanctions. Lukashenko allowed Russia to use Belarusian territory to help launch the 2022 invasion of Ukraine but has declined to commit his country's forces to the conflict.

US officials plan to travel to Norway, Russia for Ukraine talks, say sources
US president Donald Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff is set to travel to Norway this week for the next round of negotiations on ending the war in Ukraine, according to three people familiar with the matter.
Two of the people said they expected Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky to attend the meetings. Another person familiar with the matter said Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, who was expected to take part in the talks, was sick and would attend virtually.
The Oslo discussions are expected to include Zelensky, Norwegian prime minister Jonas Gahr Store, representatives of the E4 - made up of Britain, France, Germany, and Poland - the European Union, and Nato.
Witkoff is also set to travel to Russia to meet president Vladimir Putin, two of the people familiar with the matter said. The Russian conversations are going to focus on a range of topics related to Ukraine as well as the release of American citizen Stephen Hubbard, who was jailed in Russia in 2022.
Senators hold call with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky
Senators who organised a call with Volodymyr Zelensky said he confirmed that critical Starlink services remain degraded, impeding Ukraine's ability to intercept Russian drones.
Republican senator Thom Tillis of North Carolina and Democratic senator Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut said in a statement that Zelensky also reported disruptions to US intelligence sharing concerning early warning of Russian ballistic missile launches.
"As a result of these disruptions, civilians - children - are dying," the senators said.
The senators participated in the call after the Trump administration waived sanctions against Russia approved recently by Congress.
The senators said that Democrats and Republicans understand what will happen if Russian president Vladimir Putin faces no consequences, and they remain committed to Ukraine and European allies trying to end "the slaughter in Russia's war of aggression".
"Zelensky reiterated his willingness to halt strikes on Russian energy facilities as President Trump has proposed, but only if Putin agrees to the same - a fair request," they said.
