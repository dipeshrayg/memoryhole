Donald Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff is facing mounting criticism for describing Russian president Vladimir Putin as a "humanitarian" during their talks in St Petersburg.
Volodymyr Zelensky said a massive Russian aerial attack overnight, which involved at least 21 ballistic missiles and killed two people, showed the US-Russia talks had resulted in "no real change in [Moscow's] attitude".
Without naming Witkoff directly, Ukraine's foreign minister Andrii Sybiha said the Kyiv strikes were "what Russian 'humanism' truly sounds like", adding: "Appeasement, servility, and flattery will not stop this war. Only strength will."
There was also growing criticism of Witkoff's remark, which came following Moscow's release of 74-year-old American citizen Stephen Hubbard, back home in Washington. Republican senator Thom Tillis responded with a post on X reading: "WTF, Steve? Look up the definition of 'humanitarian'."
Russian ballistic missiles struck several parts of Kyiv overnight into Thursday, damaging residential buildings, a car service station, a medical facility, a shopping centre and a restaurant. At least 13 explosions were reported in less than eight minutes during one of the three waves of strikes at around 3am local time, according to The Kyiv Independent.
Downing Street hits back at Steve Witkoff over claim Putin is a 'humanitarian'
The UK government has hit back at US special envoy Steve Witkoff's claim that Vladimir Putin is a "humanitarian", saying: "We do not recognise that characterisation."
Speaking to reporters, the prime minister's official spokesperson added: "President Putin has killed thousands of people both innocent Ukrainians and the Russian people who he has sent to their deaths on the front line.
"Ukraine's foreign minister summed it up well this morning when he said that last night the people of Kyiv heard what Russian humanitarianism truly sounds like."
The spokesperson also repeatedly refused to say whether the prime minister thinks President Trump is a humanitarian, instead saying: "We fully support President Trump in his efforts to secure a just and lasting peace in Ukraine."

German military classifies threat of Russian sabotage as 'significant' for first time - reports
Germany's military has classified the threat of Russian sabotage against its troops and facilities as "significant" for the first time, making it the second-highest threat level, German broadcasters WDR and the Sueddeutsche Zeitung reported.
This comes after the discovery of a drone carrying explosives near Wunstorf air base in September and reported sabotage incidents involving naval vessels.
"We are noting that we face a significant sabotage threat overall for [Germany's armed forces]," a defence ministry spokesperson said, according to Reuters.
Germany had also accused Moscow of attempting a drone attack at Leipzig/Halle Airport in August.
The move comes amid growing concerns across Europe about suspected Russian sabotage, cyberattacks and other forms of disruption.
Germany's foreign intelligence chief Martin Jaeger warned earlier this month that the country was in a "shadow war" with Russia and risked being drawn into a violent conflict with Moscow.
Lithuania and Denmark have also reported suspected Russian-linked efforts to target facilities or companies connected to support for Ukraine.
Trump claims Ukraine negotiations are 'closest we've been' to peace
Donald Trump has said negotiations to end the war in Ukraine are "the closest we've been", even as the Kremlin questioned the latest proposals put forward by Kyiv and European leaders.
Trump's comments come as US special envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg.
Witkoff had discussed the proposals with Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky and European leaders in Oslo before travelling to Russia.
Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Moscow was "in principle, ready to resume" trilateral negotiations in mid-November, but criticised the proposals, saying "there wasn't really much substance in them".
Zelensky has said Ukraine supports restarting the three-way talks as soon as possible.
Witkoff said discussions would continue at a technical level between Washington, Kyiv and Moscow.
Two dead in massive Russian missile attack as Putin talks 'make no difference'
At least two people have died in Russian aerial strikes on Kyiv overnight that involved at least 21 ballistic missiles, Ukrainian officials said.
Three waves of strikes damaged homes and civilian infrastructure including a shopping centre and medical facility, with at least six others reported injured.
President Volodymyr Zelensky said the attack showed Trump envoy Steve Witkoff's meeting with Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg a day earlier had made no difference.
"Almost immediately after another round of talks, Russia launched a pointed attack," Zelensky said.
"This means there has been no real change in its attitude toward everything that is happening.
"It is important to keep trying to find the right solutions to end this war."
Starlink access for Ukraine rests with SpaceX, Rubio says
US secretary of state Marco Rubio has said decisions over Ukraine's access to Starlink satellite communications are a matter for SpaceX and its owner, Elon Musk, rather than the White House.
His remarks followed a Wall Street Journal report that Musk had ordered restrictions on a military-grade version of Starlink used by Ukraine to defend against Russian missile attacks.
Meanwhile, Volodymyr Zelensky earlier said that Washington had resumed sharing intelligence with Kyiv on Russian missile strikes following his meeting with US special envoy Steve Witkoff and European leaders in Norway.
The developments come as the United States continues diplomatic engagement with both Ukraine and Russia over a possible end to the war.

Ukrainian foreign minister slams Witkoff's 'appeasement' of Putin
Ukraine's foreign minister Andrii Sybiha has become the latest figure to hit out at Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff for calling Vladimir Putin a "humanitarian", contrasting the statement with fresh Russian strikes on Kyiv overnight.
Sybiha did not name Witkoff directly, but wrote on X: "Overnight, Kyiv residents heard what Russian 'humanism' truly sounds like: a brutal attack with dozens of ballistic missiles striking energy infrastructure, medical facilities, shopping centres, and residential areas.
"Other cities across Ukraine came under attack as well," he wrote.
"Appeasement, servility, and flattery will not stop this war. Only strength will."
