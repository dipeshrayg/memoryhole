Student protests have gripped France as over 80,000 people have taken to the streets in violent demonstrations today.
Teachers, students and parents are marching in cities across France on Tuesday as protesters blockade hundreds of schools ,as they unite together against staff shortages and dilapidated school buildings.
Videos on social media show fires ablaze on the streets of Lyon, as protesters throw multiple projectiles and mortars towards the police.
Police in riot gear are seen pushing back and tackling crowds of young people to the ground in Strasbourg, after protestors charge towards police with dustbins and smoke flares.
A total of 891 high schools out of the 3,700 across France saw their lessons completely or partially suspended, according to the Ministry of Education.
In one violent protest on Monday, a 15-year-old student had their hand blown off after picking up a stun grenade being used by police, local authorities said.

Riot police use tear gas and streets set on fire
Protestors and police are in the middle of an "extremely tense standoff" in the streets of Lyon, a French journalist has reported.
Julien Damboise posted a video on social media which shows fires ablaze on the streets, as protestors throw multiple projectiles and mortars towards the police.
Riot police are responding by using tear gas on the crowds, he reported.
He wrote: "An extremely tense standoff is unfolding in the streets near where the procession is set to arrive.
"Barricades are ablaze, and local construction sites are providing rioters with an endless supply of projectiles. Police are responding with tear gas."
French students disrupt online learning with 'cyber blockade'
Distance learning programs set up in closed high schools due to protests are now experiencing disruptions due to "cyber blockades".
Young activists have been sharing tips on social media explaining how to disrupt virtual lessons, protest monitor Contre Attack reported.
One video which has been viewed hundreds of thousands of times, shows a high school student explaining the concept of an "e-blockade" and why it will be effective.

Protesting pupils blockade French high schools again
Protesting pupils blockaded their high schools again Tuesday, a day of planned demonstrations that will test the resilience and energy of a weeks-old nationwide movement demanding more resources for the school system.
At the Hélène Boucher lycée, or high school, in Paris' 20th district, protesters laid trash cans and bicycles on their sides to create a makeshift barricade in front of the school.
Like hundreds of others around the country, the school was closed.
Authorities began shutting schools last week to take some steam out of the demonstrations after widespread damage to schools and mounting injuries among staff, students and police.
French media also reported protests in other cities and towns on Tuesday morning, as labor unions called for workers to demonstrate later Tuesday in support of the high school movement that is rattling the government of President Emmanuel Macron.
