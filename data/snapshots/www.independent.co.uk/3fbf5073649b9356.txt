Iran has launched retaliatory strikes targeting US military bases in Jordan and the United Arab Emirates after American strikes on Iran's Larak Island, marking the first American strikes on the Islamic Republic since late July.
The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has confirmed it fired ballistic missiles at the King Hussein and Al-Azraq airbases in Jordan and launched dozens of attack drones toward Al-Minhad Air Base in the UAE, alleging the facilities were used to support the US attack on Iranian territory.
The small Iranian island of Larak in the Strait of Hormuz near the strategic port of Bandar Abbas overlooks shipping lanes at the mouth of the Gulf, giving it a key role in tension over Iran, maritime security and global energy supplies.
Iranian state media has reported that the US strikes resulted in the "martyrdom and injury" of several IRGC personnel and civilians, prompting Tehran to vow "response and punishment" against American forces.
The US military maintained it took "limited, precise" action against minelaying forces of the Guards that posed an "imminent threat" in the strait.
Despite the renewed strikes, Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian has emphasised that Tehran is still seeking a negotiated solution to its conflict with the United States, maintaining that dialogue and cooperation remains the only way to resolve the conflict.

US official clarifies strikes targeted Larak Island, Not Kharg Island
A US defence official has told Reuters that overnight American strikes exclusively targeted IRGC positions on Larak Island did not hit Kharg Island, Iran's primary oil export hub.
The clarification follows a post by President Donald Trump claiming Kharg Island was being "blown to smithereens," accompanied by a video clip that AI detection software and media analysis confirmed was synthetically generated.
Iranian energy officials dismissed claims of an attack on Kharg as "laughable," confirming that operations at the terminal remain intact.
US Central Command has reiterated that its overnight action was a "limited, precise" strike targeting IRGC forces preparing to deploy sea mines into Strait of Hormuz shipping lanes from Larak Island, located over 600 kilometers east of Kharg.

Oil prices climb above $90 after renewed US-Iran exchanges
Global oil prices have risen again after the US and Iran renewed strikes on Monday for the first time in more than a month.
Brent crude oil, the international standard, climbed above $91 per barrel, while US oil was trading at $86.26, up 3.44 per cent.
Since the beginning of the war, Brent crude oil is about about 25 per cent, presenting a growing problem for Donald Trump ahead of the midterm elections.
School starts soon in Lebanon but thousands still shelter in them during war
Eleven-year-old Fatima Khashab helps her mother tidy the small area where their family sleeps, in a row of makeshift living quarters in a school gymnasium.
About 200 families are sheltering in the school in Lebanon's southern coastal city of Tyre. Most are from villages farther south that have been destroyed in the latest war between Israel and the Iran-backed Lebanese militant group Hezbollah or are occupied by Israeli forces, like Fatima's hometown of Mansouri.
The school year will start on Sept. 15, but thousands of displaced people still shelter in public schools. With negotiations between Israel and Lebanon - and between the U.S. and Iran - stalled, there is no political solution in sight that would allow them to go home.
Education officials say they have a plan to reopen the schools while maintaining shelters for families with nowhere to go. But anxiety is high.
"We don't know if we will be able to go back to our village or if the crisis will get even worse. Nobody except for God knows what's going to happen," said Fatima's mother, Randa al-Taki, 51.

Iran will be looking to negotiate with the US from the stronger position it is in and is unlikely to surrender, a military analyst has said.

"I think for the US, for Donald Trump, it's a lot more difficult to see what his objective is here.
He said President Trump probably wanted to go back to war because he "started this whole conflict with a very strong hand, he now has a really weak hand.

Iranian oil executive calls Trump's Kharg Island claims 'laughable', says oil production continuing
A top Iranian oil executive has taken a swing at Donald Trump, insisting production was still happening on Kharg Island despite the president's claims.
It comes after Donald Trump posted an AI-generated video clip that he said showed Iran's oil hub being blown to "smithereens".
Hamid Bovard, the chief executive of government-run National Iranian Oil Co, said the claims were "laughable".
He told Iranian media: "Trump's tweets are laughable and conditions in Kharg are calm and appropriate."
Mr Bovard added that oil operations had not stopped and workers were busy repairing previous damage and modernising facilities.
"As I speak to you, several contractors are carrying out operations to rebuild and renovate storage tanks," he said.
Bessent heads to the G20 to rally allies on Iran as tariffs strain ties
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants to rally the world's largest economies this week at a time when the United States has disrupted the international order with aggressive tariffs on major allies and an unpopular war on Iran is keeping gas prices high for consumers.
As Bessent hosts meetings of the Group of 20 finance ministers in Asheville, North Carolina, on Monday and Tuesday, he intends to put the focus on a range of issues intended to draw uncontroversial support, from economic growth to addressing mounting debt.
However, the event comes shortly after hostilities renewed between the U.S. and Iran, and as trade friction with Canada spiked following a breakdown in negotiations that led to escalating tariffs.
Daniel Fried, a former assistant secretary of state for European affairs in the George W. Bush administration, said the U.S. may "find it difficult to rally its usual friends and allies" under the circumstances.
If Bessent and other administration officials want international support, he said, they will need to "take greater care in consulting with allies and partners than the U.S. has shown in recent months."
