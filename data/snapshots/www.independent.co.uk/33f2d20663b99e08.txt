US president Donald Trump has met with Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping in China ahead of their unprecedented three-way summit on Thursday.
China's presidentpersonally welcomed Trump upon his arrival at an airport in Shenzhen on Wednesday, a rare honour for a visiting foreign leader.
The US commander-in-chief was greeted with a hug from his usually-reserved Chinese counterpart after he descended from Air Force One. The pair later welcomed the Russian president with handshakes and a group photo ahead of an evening banquet.
Xi pledged to stand up against trade protectionism while Putin criticised Western sanctions as the stage was set for the high-stakes trilateral.
The Russian president, who took the stage after Xi, described Western powers as "former hegemons", claiming Europe was in "humiliating decline". It is the latest attack by the Kremlin, which has repeatedly sought to test the Nato alliance in recent months.
The three men will meet on the sidelines on Thursday and potentially discuss Russia's war with Ukraine and the US war in Iran, as well as other flashpoints such as trade, AI and nuclear weapons.
The gathering has drawn comparisons with the 1945 Yalta Conference between the US, Britain and the Soviet Union that formalised spheres of influence, set the stage for the post-World War Two order and became shorthand for great-power deals imposed on smaller countries.
But it has also unnerved leaders of smaller and medium economies, especially in Asia and Europe, who fear the three world powers will disregard or undermine their interests.
The warm reception for the Russian leader will be of particular concern for Ukraine, with Kyiv urging Trump to pressure Putin to end the near five year war rather than trying to appease him.
On the eve of the summit and sidelined by the trilateral, Ukraine delivered a symbolic blow to the Kremlin with two strikes on Putin's prized Crimea Bridge this week. The back and forth strikes between Moscow and Kyiv continue unabated, with four people killed in an overnight Russian attack on the port city of Odesa.
In his opening address, the Chinese president called for "free and open trade", as he attempted to projected the country as a stable partner on a global stage.
"We must firmly uphold the rules-based multilateral trading system with the World Trade Organization at its core, support free and open trade and investment, maintain the stability and smooth operation of global industrial and supply chains," Xi told an audience of business executives.
The remarks came after Washington announced restrictions on China's access to advanced AI chips from companies including Nvidia, position there trade policies in contrast to Trump's tariffs, and the US administration's adoption of a muscular foreign policy.
Chastising the West for the sanctions imposed on Russia following the invasion of Ukraine, Putin added: "Experience has shown that the traditional tools of economic dominance and de facto coercion have largely run their course, having lost much of their intimidating power."
"While sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions may still exert a certain deterrent effect, they are incapable of halting growth-let alone crushing the economies of competitors."
He also praised close partnership with China, pledging more measures for a "multipolar world".
The Kremlin this week dismissed comparisons with the Yalta Conference as "games of political scientists", but said the meeting will have a major impact on global affairs. Beijing said the trilateral will promote world peace.
Putin described the world as "turbulent" but said disagreements can be addressed by "sitting at the table".
"I therefore expect big potential benefit from the upcoming trilateral meeting," he said.
Xi's red carpet reception mirrored Trump's warm welcome of the Chinese premier when he arrived for their three-day summit in Washington earlier in September. The president had greeted Xi's entourage when it arrived at the Joint Base Andrews in Maryland.
Trump's trip to Asia is his first since his Republican Party's defeat in midterm elections, while allegations involving Nvidia founder Jensen Huang and a suspected Chinese spy have added to the tension.
