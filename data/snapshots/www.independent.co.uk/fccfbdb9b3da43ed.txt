Michael Carrick is once again fighting for his Manchester United future as his side travel to Chelsea in the Premier League.
Carrick appeared on the brink following the 3-0 home defeat to Bournemouth last week but survived crisis talks with the United hierarchy and remains in charge for the trip to Stamford Bridge, despite the club sounding out Eddie Howe and Julian Nagelsmann as his potential replacement.
An eight-game winless run in all competitions has heightened the pressure on Carrick, who has recalled Bruno Fernandes after holding clear-the-air talks with his captain. Fernandes was left on the bench last weekend after criticism of Carrick's tactics but starts and captains United at Stamford Bridge.
There is some pressure on Chelsea boss Xabi Alonso, too, following the home defeat to rivals Tottenham last weekend and their exit from the Carabao Cup against Liverpool in midweek. Joao Pedro remains sidelined due to injury, although Cole Palmer returns from suspension.

A run to forget
Michael Carrick's side are winless in their last eight games in all competitions overall (three draws and five defeats) since beating Sabah in the Champions League on 10 September.
They have not gone more matches without victory since a nine-game stretch in 1988.

Manchester United's recent outings
The pressure on Michael Carrick ramped up following Manchester United's two most recent results.
They were hammered 4-1 by Como in the Champions League before being heavily beaten 3-0 by Bournemouth last time out in the Premier League.
United have not lost three straight games in all competitions by three or more goals since doing so across November and December 1961.

Is there any way back for Michael Carrick?
Despite fair criticism of Michael Carrick as a coach, he has been left in a more challenging situation than any of his modern predecessors.
And that's inevitably left him facing a familiar crisis that submerged all of them, too.
David Moyes had it almost immediately. Louis van Gaal had it in the winter of 2015. Jose Mourinho endured it from early 2018, Ole Gunnar Solskjaer in mid-2021. Erik ten Hag had it from the start of the 2023-24 season and Carrick from the start of this one.
None ever fully recovered.
That is when a manager looks to have just "lost" a team, when the hold over them is "gone".
And that's when its centre can't hold, and we see collapses like those against Como and Bournemouth.
As numerous sources suggest, the connection that was there last season has gone. It's as if he just can't get a reaction out of the squad any more - just like many of his predecessors eventually found.
