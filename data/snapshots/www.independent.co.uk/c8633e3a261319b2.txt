Russian president Vladimir Putin has said all proposals for a settlement to the war in Ukraine remain "on the table," but has warned Kyiv to prepare for a sustained wave of retaliatory strikes.
Addressing journalists in St. Petersburg on Friday, the Russian leader claimed Moscow was ready to resume peace negotiations after his party secured a record supermajority Russia's parliamentary elections this week.
However, Putin accused Ukrainian forces of intentionally derailing potential diplomatic channels with long-range drone strikes on polling stations during the voting period.
Dismissing Kyiv's recent military operations, Putin said: "They are the ones who started it... Everywhere, on every front. Are these playthings, child's play?
"[Ukraine] should therefore be made to feel Russia's retaliatory strikes and understand that no provocative actions ... will work. They're only making things worse for themselves."
While insisting that ceasefire frameworks and diplomatic solutions remain possible, Putin stressed that Moscow will first carefully assess what best serves its own national interests before agreeing to any terms.
The comments come as Ukraine's president Volodymyr Zelensky confirmed in Washington that he asked US president Donald Trump to press Chinese President Xi Jinping to use Beijing's leverage over Moscow.
Four dead in Russian strikes across Ukraine
Russian attacks killed four people across Ukraine on Saturday, officials said, as the aerial war between Moscow and Kyiv intensified.
Russia has hammered Ukraine with cruise and ballistic missiles, powerful glide bombs and jet-powered drones in recent weeks while front line progress has slowed.
Two women in Zaporizhzhia died when they were trapped beneath the rubble of their destroyed home, Zaporizhzhia's regional military administration said. A separate Russian attack on the city of Sumy also killed two people, local officials said.
Elsewhere in Ukraine, two people were injured in the Kyiv region overnight after a Russian drone attack caused a fire at a warehouse in the Boryspil district. Russian drones also hit several districts in the Ukrainian capital itself, injuring four and damaging residential and office buildings, officials said.
Russia's defence ministry said on Saturday that its attack on Kyiv had damaged a data centre that supported both cellphone internet services and Starlink satellite communication systems.
Putin discusses energy cooperation with Serbian president
Russian president Vladimir Putin discussed the Ukraine war and energy cooperation with Serbian president Aleksandar Vucic during a phone call, the Kremlin said on Saturday.
Vucic has described Serbia as "neutral" when it comes to the Ukraine conflict. The country has long held close relations with Russia but has also supported UN resolutions condemning Putin's aggression.
Three killed in Ukrainian attack on Krasnodar region, says Russia
Three people were killed and two injured in a Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's Krasnodar region, local authorities there have said.
A local task force said fires at industrial sites that had broken out as a result of the attack had been extinguished.
Zelensky says he got Trump's OK for Patriot licences
The Ukrainian leader Volodymyr Zelensky says Donald Trump confirmed this week that he had made a final decision to allow Kyiv to get licences to produce Patriot air defence systems.
Ukraine is desperate for more of the American-made air defence systems that are needed to stop Russia's ballistic missiles, but they have been in short supply since the war in Iran started on 28 February.
Trump had said in July that he would allow Ukraine to make its own Patriots but later appeared to be reconsidering the move.
Zelensky urges Trump to get China to help end Russia's invasion
Ukrainian president Volodymyr Zelensky says he asked Donald Trump to press China's Xi Jinping to use Beijing's influence over Moscow and end the war on his country.
Zelensky told reporters late on Friday that he has not yet received feedback from the White House about Trump's talks with Xi, but expects to learn the outcome.
Nato countries have called China a "decisive enabler" of Russian president Vladimir Putin's invasion.
Zelensky was also in the US this week on a trip coinciding with the UN General Assembly. He met with Trump on Tuesday.
Zelensky met with US senators and Congress members during his trip and said he thanked them for their bipartisan support in passing a powerful sanctions bill aimed at Moscow last month. Trump signed the bill into law last week but hasn't enacted the measures yet.

Little movement on Ukraine peace after turbulent week of UN diplomacy
The UN General Assembly concluded on Friday after a week of speeches, meetings and hallway diplomacy.
While they reached no breakthroughs onthe world's most intractable conflicts, talks on the Iran and Ukraine wars inched forward.
US secretary of state Marco Rubio held talks with Russia's foreign minister Sergei Lavrov, after which he said Ukraine and Russia had both voiced an interest in a limited ceasefire involving grain and energy-related targets.
Ukraine and its allies are racing to secure these limited agreements before winter, fearing renewed Russian strikes on power and heating facilities.
But hours after Rubio spoke on Wednesday, Lavrov addressed the Security Council meeting on Ukraine, showing no indication Russia was prepared to engage on any subject. "Europe, whatever it thinks, just wants to get a pause, and it wants to use it to pump the Kyiv regime with weapons," Lavrov said.
UN refugee chief urges Security Council to do more to end wars
The UN's refugee chief used his first speech at the Security Council yesterday to call for a renewed focus on ending conflicts and crises, so that more than 117 million people forced to flee their homes can return and don't spend generations displaced in a foreign country.
The Ukraine war was just one of the conflicts cited by Barham Salih, who told the Council's 15 members that they have "a unique responsibility to help make peace possible."
He said the conflict started by Vladimir Putin's invasion in 2022 had displaced millions inside the country and sent millions more fleeing to other countries.
"For the millions of people forced from their homes - and the millions more who should never have to flee - I urge you to use that responsibility," the former refugee, who was president of Iraq from 2018 to 2022, told the meeting on the sidelines of the annual UN gathering of world leaders.
Drone hits Russia's Ilsky oil refinery
Ukrainian drones reportedly targeted the Ilsky oil refinery in Russia's Krasnodar Krai, causing a fire at the facility.
Local governor Veniamin Kondratiev said that the Ukrainian drones targeted Seversky district, which houses one of the largest oil-processing facilities in southern Russia.
The extent of the damage to the refinery and casualties were not immediately known.
Government warned Homes for Ukraine funding cut may 'send wrong message to Putin'
Planned government cuts to the Homes for Ukraine Scheme could "send the wrong message to Putin" by giving the impression that Western support for Kyiv is faltering, a Conservative former defence secretary has warned.
Starting from 1 January, monthly "thank-you" payments for households hosting Ukrainian refugees are set to drop from £350 to £100.
Local authority funding to support the initiative will also be scaled back on the same date, falling from £5,900 per guest to £3,300 per arrival.
