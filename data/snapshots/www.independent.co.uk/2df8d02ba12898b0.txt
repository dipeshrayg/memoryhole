The chief of the Metropolitan Police has said he would not be surprised if "many more" accusers of Russell Brand came forward following the comedian's conviction for rape and sexual assault.
Brand, 51, was found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault following a four-week trial at Southwark Crown Court on Thursday. The jury cleared him of two other counts of rape and one attempted indecent assault.
Following his conviction, police revealed Brand is under investigation for two more alleged rapes, including of a 16-year-old schoolgirl. Speaking to BBC Breakfast, Sir Mark Rowley said: "There are two more cases we're investigating at the moment. I clearly wouldn't be surprised if many more came forward."
He added that high-profile cases often lead to an "upsurge" in reporting because it gives victims confidence to speak up about attackers in their cases. He said: "It does make a difference because people see it is possible, and you will be believed."
At pre-trial court hearings, it was revealed that one of the complaints came from a woman who dated Brand when he was 30 and she was a 16-year-old schoolgirl.
Brand's victims came to court to reveal how he had forced them into sexual activity, sometimes within seconds of them first meeting, using his fame to determinedly pursue a goal of sex with 5,000 women.
Meanwhile, Sir Mark said some of what he heard reported from Brand's trial was "getting very close to victim blaming".
It was put to Sir Mark on BBC Radio 4's Today programme that Brand's defence lawyer Ian Winter KC told jurors that "none of the complainants had, in his words, resisted or screamed or even tried to leave".
Asked if he would support victims' commissioner Claire Waxman, who wants a legal duty in the Courts and Tribunals Bill that would require judges to issue directions to juries on rape myths and misconceptions in every rape trial, Sir Mark said: "I strongly support Claire.
"I think what I've heard reported heads towards, it's getting very close to victim blaming, which is something we've thought very hard about in policing and make sure we don't do.
"People fall into myths about (how) a rape victim must behave in a certain way, must react in a certain way.
"The evidence is really clear. Some people will fight. Some people will freeze. Some people will tell people straight away. Some people bottle it up for years. There isn't one way of reacting.
"We understand that it shouldn't be possible for inaccurate statements to be made to a jury to suggest that because a rape victim didn't behave in one way, that somehow that undermines their case, when the evidence is very clear. We all [have a] very different sort of psychological and personal make-up, and in the most extraordinary horrific moments, of course, people react very differently."
Sir Mark added on Times Radio: "The wider point I would make: this looks at celebrity, but most sex offending is where someone of power exploits someone who is less powerful themselves.
"It might be an uncle, it might be a teacher, it might be a vicar. Could be a whole range of different situations. And what cases like this show is, it doesn't matter how powerful the person is, we will support you, and we will try and bring down the person who did something ghastly to you."
The shamed comedian-turned-conspiracy theorist will spend the weekend in custody before he returns to the same court on Monday to learn his prison sentence.
His wife Laura Gallacher, who attended much of the trial, was seen hunched over in the public gallery as Mr Justice Bennathan told Brand that he now faces a lengthy jail term.
A couple of months after he started hosting Big Brother's Big Mouth in 2004, Brand sexually assaulted a woman in the Nordic bar in Soho, central London, by grabbing her breasts in the middle of the bar during a party of his former MTV colleagues.
She accused him of dragging her to the men's toilets and orally raping her, but the jury found Brand not guilty on that count.
A worker at Capital Radio said Brand forced himself on her during a visit to the station in 2005, pinning her to a wall while he groped and "sloppily" kissed her.
Brand was found guilty of sexually assaulting her.
The victim told jurors that members of his entourage laughed as they saw the attack happen.
He was also convicted of anally raping a 21-year-old fan in his £2.3m Hampstead townhouse in 2009, days after meeting her at one of his Hammersmith Apollo shows.
The same year, during a recording session at Channel 4, Brand sexually assaulted another woman by thrusting his head into her groin after she was told to give him a massage.
In September 2023, an investigation by journalists from The Sunday Times, The Times and Channel 4 Dispatches published accusations from women who said they had been raped, sexually abused and degraded by Brand.
The revelations prompted four women to come forward to accuse Brand of criminal offences, while another two women came forward once Brand had been charged.
