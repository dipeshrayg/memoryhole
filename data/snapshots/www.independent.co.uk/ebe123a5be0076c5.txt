The United Arab Emirates has launched an investigation into whether the flydubai flight hijacking was linked to terrorism, after the failed attempted to crash the plane on Wednesday morning.
The Flydubai Flight FZ1073 from Dubai to Israel dramatically descended approximately 13,000 feet in 30 seconds during the chaos on Wednesday morning.
UAE investigators will now determine "whether the incident was linked to any terrorist activity or purpose, or involved prior planning or direction", according to the UAE's state news agency.
Abu Dhabi authorities have jurisdiction over the incident because the aircraft is registered in the UAE and flies under its flag, the statement said.
It comes after Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu has said it is "too early to say" who was behind the incident, as he hailed the actions of the flight's captain.
Netanyahu blamed "fanaticism and hatred of an incalculable level" during a Fox News interview but said he has no confirmation yet of the motive for the attack. The attacker was arrested in Saudi Arabia.
He also named the Indian pilot who was stabbed during the attempted hijacking, calling him a "true hero".
Trump calls co-pilot who tried to hijack Israel flight 'a terrorist or a whack job'
President Donald Trump has said a co-pilot who stabbed his colleague during a flight to Israel was "a terrorist or a whack-job."
The commander-in-chief made the remarks Wednesday, after the attempted hijacking of a Flydubai flight headed to Tel Aviv. The co-pilot, who was arrested when the plane landed, was subdued by passengers who described what happened during the terrifying ordeal as the bloody scene unfolded.
Speaking in the Oval Office, Trump told reporters he had spoken about the incident with Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu.
"I did speak to Bibi Netanyahu at length about this event, and it seems as though the co-pilot was perhaps a terrorist or perhaps a whack-job, and he stabbed the pilot," Trump remarked.
The president was also asked whether he knew if the co-pilot had wanted to crash the jet.
'The plane felt like a rollercoaster as it nosedived'
We've just heard from another one of the passengers on the flydubai plane, Almog Italy, who revealed his dramatic experience to Sky News.
He said the plane felt like a "rollercoaster" as it initially dropped by around 13,000 feet in just 30 seconds.
"In the beginning, we didn't understand what was going on because we saw people rushing and also the crew members rushing to the front of the plane," he told the broadcaster.
"After three, four minutes, we understood that there was a kind of attack."
He said word soon spread about a stabbing in the cockpit, with the plane's passengers "afraid" but remaining calm.
Saudi Arabia refused Israeli requests to send planes to pick up passengers
Saudi Arabia turned down requests by Israel to send its own planes to pick up stranded passengers on Wednesday, a source has told AFP news agency.
Israeli passengers on the diverted flydubai flight were stranded in Tabuk, Saudi Arabia.
FlyDubai: The airline that made an emergency landing after a captain was stabbed
A copilot on a flight to Israel stabbed a captain on Wednesday before passengers pushed their way into the cockpit and wrestled with the attacker.
The FlyDubai plane fell rapidly, with a passenger saying he grabbed the controls and pulled upward, similar to how he had seen on television.
Other crew members soon took over, and the jetliner made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia.

UAE orders investigation into flydubai incident
The UAE prosecutor general has ordered an investigation into the flydubai incident, the state news agency reported on Thursday.
The investigation will examine the circumstances and motives, including whether it was linked to any militant activity or involved prior planning or direction.
UAE judicial authorities have jurisdiction over the incident because the aircraft is registered in the UAE and flies under its flag, the statement said.
It added that UAE law applies to crimes committed on board "even if they occur outside the country's territory".
