Iran committed crimes against humanity during protest crackdown, UN finds
The same UN report also found that Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during their deadly crackdown on anti-government protests.
The probe found the Iranian authorities carried out a widespread and systematic attack against civilians during protests that erupted in late December last year, involving unlawful killings, torture, arbitrary detention, enforced disappearances and severe restrictions on freedom of expression.
Rights groups say bystanders were among those killed during the biggest crackdown since Shi'ite Muslim clerics took power in the 1979 revolution.
Tehran blamed "terrorists and rioters" backed by exiled opponents and foreign foes the US and Israel.
The probe said it could not independently verify the death toll, but that it believed the number of dead and injured was likely far higher than official figures which say 3,038 people were killed, and 25,000 people injured.
The government's response to the protests - including violence and killings, cutting off the internet and use of the death penalty, marked a significant escalation from previous patterns of suppressing dissent, the report said.

South Korea says no decision made on military deployment to Strait of Hormuz
South Korean foreign minister Cho Hyun said on Thursday that no decision had been made on any military deployment to the Strait of Hormuz.
Cho was speaking to reporters before departing for Washington, where he is due to hold talks with US secretary of state Marco Rubio.
Earlier this month, it was reported that South Korea was considering deploying military assets to the Strait of Hormuz as it faced growing pressure from the US to do more in the Middle East.
Later, Seoul said that the military deployment was an option but clarified that nothing had been decided yet.
China calls on Iran and US to revive diplomatic process
Chinese foreign minister Wang Yi has called on Iran and the US to "rebuild the negotiation mechanism based on the consensus reached so far" and revive their stalled diplomatic process.
"We urge all parties to take effective measures to reopen the Strait of Hormuz at an early date" to safeguard the stability of international energy transport and supply chains Wang said, according to China's foreign ministry, adding that China "does not want to see regional tensions to spill over further into Yemen and the Red Sea".
Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araqchi, who was on a one-day visit to Beijing, said that Tehran was "fully prepared to powerfully defend its national sovereignty and territorial integrity and safeguard its security... against aggressors" and that it "welcomes diplomatic solutions that ensure the rights of the Iranian nation".
Wang said: "The current situation once again demonstrates that the existing security architecture in the Middle East is inadequate and needs to be reformed and strengthened."
US military helping to keep Strait of Hormuz open to commercial shipping, Centcom says
Despite an ongoing blockade of Iranian maritime trade and attacks by Iran's military on commercial ships, the US military has said its forces have kept the Strait of Hormuz open to commercial shipping.
The US also claimed that they are helping facilitate the movement of hundreds of millions of barrels of oil through the strategic waterway.
On Wednesday, US Navy Captain Tim Hawkins, a spokesperson for US Central Command, said traffic through the strait "continues to flow", rejecting suggestions that Iran controls the waterway.
"Since early May, we have assisted commercial vessels moving more than 900 million barrels of crude oil through the Strait of Hormuz to the global energy market," Hawkins told Al Jazeera.
The US and Iran have continued to make competing claims over who has greater control of the critical Strait of Hormuz in the Gulf.

US House approves bill imposing sanctions on Iran and Russia
The US House of Representatives has passed a bill named after the late Senator Lindsey Graham, voting 262-159 to impose sweeping sanctions and tariffs aimed at increasing economic pressure on Iran and Russia, Reuters reported.
The Lindsey O Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, introduced by Graham himself, was approved by the Senate last month in an 86-11 vote.
While the legislation primarily targets Russia, it also extends the Iran Sanctions Act of 1996, signed by then-president Bill Clinton.
The 1996 law was due to expire in 2026 but has now been extended for another five years.
Dozens of Democratic representatives joined most Republicans in backing the bill, sending it to president Donald Trump to be signed into law.
