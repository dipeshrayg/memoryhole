The US House of Representatives voted to end the war in Iran for the third time late on Tuesday, approving a war powers resolution that would halt president Donald Trump's ability to continue military action without congressional approval.
The result - 220 votes to 204 - was similar to earlier efforts this summer, with a few more Republicans joining most Democrats voting to bring an end to the conflict.
None of the resolutions have made it to the president's desk, where president Trump would almost certainly veto the measure.
The vote comes as a defence department report revealed that Trump's "Operation Epic Fury" assault on Iran cost $38bn in its first four months and has resulted in a munitions shortfall.
Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia said its air defences destroyed a Houthi drone south of Mecca before it entered prohibited airspace over the holy city.
The coalition claimed that the drone intercepted on Tuesday evening was the second Houthi attempt to target Mecca after a ballistic missile launch in July 2017.
The Iran-backed group strongly denied targeting "Mecca or any other holy sites", calling it a "worn-out lie that has been used before".

Yemen's Houthis claim downing of Saudi fighter jet
The Houthis claim to have shot down a Saudi F-15 fighter jet operating in Marib province, Yemen.
Spokesperson Mohammed Abdulsalam said Houthi forces shot down the jet with a "locally made surface-to-air missile", claiming that they had also confronted Eurofighter Typhoons in the region.

How destroyed jets and drones caused cost of Iran war to reach $33.4bn
A U.S. government watchdog released its first report on the impact of the Iran war on Monday, acknowledging the military's advanced weapons shortfalls and offering the first public look at the damage to American aircraft, bases and diplomatic outposts in the Middle East.
Much of the information has been previously reported over the past six months, but the Pentagon inspector general's report offered the fullest official accounting so far of the conflict's costs in American lives, taxpayer dollars and physical damage.
It included information from the watchdogs for the Defense and State departments as well as the U.S. Agency for International Development.
The report, whose publication was first reported by NBC News, noted that the U.S. war with Iran "has resulted in strategic inventory shortfalls and revealed industrial base bottlenecks for munitions resupply" in regard to advanced weapons.
Experts have previously said it will take about three years for military contractors to replenish advanced missiles and defensive missile interceptors to prewar levels.

Trump's Iran war cost hits $38bn
The six-month-old US war against Iran has cost $38bn so far and that amount is projected to rise by $3bn a month, Congress' nonpartisan bookkeeper reported, as the Donald Trump administration searches for ways to end the conflict and reopen the vital Strait of Hormuz.
The war is expected to increase inflation by 0.5 per cent in the first three months of 2027, according to the Congressional Budget Office accounting, which totaled expenses through 1 August.
The mounting costs have strained US munitions stockpiles and driven up energy prices, raising concerns about the country's ability to respond to other potential conflicts and adding pressure on an already stretched federal budget.
The cost was close to the $37.5bn that defence secretary Pete Hegseth reported at a Senate hearing on 21 July.
Children among 11 killed in Gaza building collapse
At least 11 Palestinians, including children, were killed after a war-damaged building collapsed in Gaza, local officials said.
The building was damaged by Israeli shelling and strikes during the conflict, officials said. It housed 70 people.
Rescuers pulled 12 people alive out of the debris, according to reports.
China defence minister urges an end to 'provocative acts'
Chinese defence minister Dong Jun urged nations to avoid provocative acts and curb the resurgence of regressive historical trends.
Dong's comments at the Beijing Xiangshan Forum on security come at a time of roiling regional tension in Asia since the US and Israel launched a war on Iran.
Warning that the world had arrived at a "fragile moment", Dong called for a fresher, more equal approach to resolving international tension, but offered few specifics or direct criticism of rivals.
"We must bear in mind the lessons of history and remain highly vigilant against all latent manifestations of hegemony, militarism and historical revisionism," he said in an opening speech.
Some 2,000 military officers, diplomats and academics from 100 countries are attending the three-day event set to run until Thursday, with delegates including some regional defence ministers, a senior Pentagon official, Russian academics and Taliban representatives.
