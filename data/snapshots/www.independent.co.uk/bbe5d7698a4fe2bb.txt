Donald Trump has said he faces a "big decision" on how to proceed with the Iran war, in his first remarks since a UN report said two US strikes in February may have amounted to war crimes.
The president has yet to respond directly to the UN report, but in an interview with Axios he said: "I have a big decision coming up. Do I want to go in and annihilate them [the Iranian regime] or do I not? It's a big decision. Anything could happen with me."
Earlier, the UN fact-finding mission to Iran said the US struck a school in Iran "while being aware of a substantial risk of striking a civilian object".
One of the strikes targeted a primary school in Minab, where more than 150 people were reportedly killed, including about 120 children, and another hit a sports centre in Lamerd that killed or injured 22 civilians.
The report, which also found that Iranian authorities committed crimes against humanity during their deadly crackdown on anti-government protests, will be submitted to the UN Human Rights Council in Geneva.

Barclays predicts November interest rate hike from Bank of England, warns of more amid Middle East conflict
Barclays expects the Bank of England to raise interest rates by 25 basis points in November, citing a "dramatically changed" medium-term energy outlook, and warned that a prolonged Middle East conflict could bring further tightening.
Barclays joins JP Morgan, which also expects hikes in November and February 2027 and has similarly warned the Middle East crisis could push rates higher. JP Morgan had previously expected one hike in November followed by two cuts in 2027.
The Bank kept interest rates on hold at 3.75 per cent on Thursday, as expected, but forecast inflation could top 4 per cent early next year.
Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis exchanged fresh strikes across their border yesterday, widening the Middle East conflict, while the US and Iran have held no peace talks since an interim agreement collapsed weeks after being reached in June.

US approves visas for Iranian leaders to attend UN meeting despite war stalemate
The Trump administration has granted entry visas to key Iranian figures, including the country's president and foreign minister, enabling them to attend next week's UN General Assembly high-level sessions in New York despite an ongoing conflict between the two nations.
On Thursday, the State Department confirmed that visas had been issued for Iran's core delegation and essential support staff. According to US officials speaking on condition of anonymity due to visa privacy laws, the main delegation features President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi.
The decision comes amidst an extraordinary backdrop of a military stalemate lasting over six months, with no immediate indications of progress toward reopening the Strait of Hormuz or resuming diplomatic talks regarding Iran's nuclear program.
