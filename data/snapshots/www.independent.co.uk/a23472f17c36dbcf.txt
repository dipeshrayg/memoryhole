At least eight migrants have been found dead after a boat carrying around 100 people was rescued near Spain's Canary Islands, the maritime rescue service reported on Friday, as a surge in arrivals continues to overwhelm regional authorities.
The deaths highlight the severe perils of a notorious migration route just days ahead of Sunday's general election in Spain, where immigration has become a central issue in the campaign.
Rescuers discovered between 8 and 10 bodies on board, while six people were airlifted to hospital in a serious condition. Found 39 miles (72 km) south of El Hierro, the vessel was being towed to the port of La Estaca.
A separate boat brought 84 survivors to the island's La Restinga port, home to a reception centre and field hospital responsible for processing most migrant vessels reaching El Hierro.
Arrivals across the Canary Islands have accelerated since October, reaching a single-day record of 2,100 people on 25 November alone, with most heading to El Hierro, which has a population of 11,400.
Demanding action from the Spanish government and European Commission, regional leader Fernando Clavijo posted on X expressing "A lot of pain and feeling of impotence over this new tragedy."
"The humanitarian emergency doesn't start on El Hierro, but on the shores of Senegal and Gambia, where we have to act," he wrote.
Earlier this week, Clavijo had also spoken out when nearly 2,000 migrants arrived on the Canary Islands on 24 November.
"Sadly, this is a historic day," he said in an interview with Spanish radio station Cadena Ser.
Around 100 minors were among Tuesday's arrivals, including a four-day-old baby born during the ocean journey, Clavijo said.
Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez is among Europe's strongest advocates for the economic benefits of immigration, while opposition right-wing parties leading opinion polls favour much tighter restrictions.
Every year, thousands of migrants attempt the Atlantic crossing in overcrowded craft to reach Spanish territory despite rough seas and strong currents. Crossings usually increase during the autumn when sea conditions are generally more favourable.
