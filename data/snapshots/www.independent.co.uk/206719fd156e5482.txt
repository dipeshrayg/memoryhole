A pilot on a flight from Dubai to Israel allegedly stabbed his co-pilot in an attempted hijacking in midair, terrified passengers have said.
The passengers have recalled the horrors of the Wednesday morning incident, when Flydubai Flight FZ1073 descended approximately 13,000 feet in 30 seconds as it veered off course on Wednesday morning, FlightRadar data shows.
Moments after the mid-air fight, one passenger with blood on his face posted to social media: "We're here during the hijacking of an airplane. At this point we've subdued the terrorists, we're approaching landing. Whoever is hearing me, we need help."
Another, called Meital, told Channel 12: "There were stabbings and a lot of blood. We're very emotional and under terrible stress, it's very difficult for me to talk,"
The Boeing 737 plane, which Israeli media said had around 150 Israeli passengers on board, landed at Tabuk airport in northwestern Saudi Arabia.
A witness told the Jerusalem Post that passengers near the front of the plane forced the attacker out of the cockpit. Two other pilots who were onboard at the back of the plane helped land the flight.
An Israeli official told the outlet that the IDF, Mossad and Shin Bet believe the incident may have been intended as an act of terror.
Ben Gvir says 'attack' shows Israel must intensify military action in the region
Far-right national security minister Itamar Ben Gvir has said that the hijacking of a FlyDubai plane on Wednesday demonstrates the need for Israel to justify its military campaign in the region.
"Thank you to the Creator of the Universe and to the heroic passengers, but Israel's security doctrine must go up a level!" he wrote in a post on X.
"Israel has done a wonderful job in the last two and a half years, but we must not stop and must continue until achieving total victory in Iran, Lebanon, Gaza, and Judea and Samaria.
"The naive notion that our enemies value life and that we should have mercy on them received another reminder this morning in the form of a terrorist who planned to crash a plane and murder the 180 Israelis on board.
"Terrorism is a devil with which no agreement should ever be reached except for its defeat and erasure from the face of the earth."
Passengers being treated 'well' in Saudi Arabia
Passengers who have landed in Saudi Arabia after a plane was diverted away from Tel Aviv, say they are being treated well.
"They're performing in the best way," Almog Italie told Channel 12 news. "They're taking care of us. At the moment, we're relying on them."
"In Saudi Arabia we trust!" he added.
All passengers safe and accounted for, says airline
An airline spokesperson said the FlyDubai flight experienced an "incident" en route to Tel Aviv and diverted to Tabuk, where it landed safely. All passengers were safe and accounted for, the spokesperson said, without providing any further details.
Lapid says Netanyahu warned of attacks ahead of elections but nothing 'remotely resembling' hijacking
Israeli opposition leader Yair Lapid has said that nothing to indicate the alleged hijacking of a FlyDubai plane was mentioned at a security briefing last night.
"Nothing even remotely resembling this morning's plane incident came up in the security briefing with the prime minister last night. Nothing similar, nothing reminiscent of it, not even a hint," he said.
"The whole spin that the mouthpieces are pushing now only proves what was already clear yesterday - that once again they are trying to play cheap politics at the expense of Israel's security. An irresponsible bunch."
Israeli government believes incident was 'terror attack', say sources
The Israeli government believes the incident on board a flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv was an attempted "terror attack", two Israeli officials told Reuters.
They said that the co-pilot is believed to have stabbed the captain before cabin crew entered the cockpit and took control.
An Israeli official had earlier told Reuters that the plan transmitted emergency signals after a physical altercation between two pilots.

Netanyahu office says situation is 'under control'
Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu was briefed over the incident, which his office said in a statement was "under control," adding that all efforts were being made to bring the Israeli passengers back to Israel.
