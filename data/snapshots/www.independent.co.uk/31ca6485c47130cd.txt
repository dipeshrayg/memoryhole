Morocco's federation submitted a last-minute challenge to the Court of Arbitration for Sport (Cas) against a member of the judicial panel before Senegal saw their Africa Cup of Nations appeal upheld, restoring them as 2025 champions.
Senegal contested the Confederation of African Football's (Caf) decision to declare Morocco champions after the team staged an injury-time walk-off in protest to a penalty awarded against them, and were successful in their appeal as Cas, sport's highest court, found that referee Jean-Jacques Ndala "did not commit any error of law" when he decided to resume the match.
The Royal Moroccan Football Federation (FRMF) responded to Tuesday's verdict by questioning "the integrity and credibility of the arbitration process", a complaint surrounding an alleged phone call involving Senegalese Football Federation (FSF) president Abdoulaye Fall and arbitrator Ulrich Haas.
The Independent understands that Cas received a challenge against Haas by the FRMF within one hour prior to the decision being announced. This formally came several hours after media reports stated it had already been filed.
The Icas Challenge Commission reviewed the FRMF's challenge but found there was not enough evidence at the time to delay the verdict's announcement.
Sources indicate that Haas strongly denies any direct contact at any moment with the Senegalese federation or any parties involved throughout the arbitration process, while the FSF also vehemently refuted the allegations as "fake news".
The FRMF has vowed to pursue further legal action to appeal the verdict.
A statement read: "The Royal Moroccan Football Federation expresses its surprise and protest at the failure to take into account its demand to arrange the necessary legal consequences for practices that would compromise the integrity and credibility of the arbitration procedure, especially what has been confirmed regarding communication between the German referee Ulrich Haas and the president of the Senegalese Football Federation, 48 hours before the decision was issued.
"The Royal Moroccan Football Federation also notes that the decision did not take into account the approach recently adopted by both the International Football Association Board (Ifab) and Fifa, regarding players leaving the field of play without the referee's permission, and the resulting disciplinary responsibilities and penalties.
"Accordingly, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation confirms its intention to pursue all available legal avenues to appeal the decision of the Court of Arbitration for Sport."
