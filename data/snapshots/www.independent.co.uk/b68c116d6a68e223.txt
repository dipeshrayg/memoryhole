At least three people have been killed and 21 injured after a woman brought an improvised explosive device (IED) near a central Moscow restaurant, according to Russian officials.
The explosion occurred at 8:10pm local time (6:10pm UK time), the Tass news agency said, citing an interior ministry official.
Following reports that the explosion was caused by a gas canister, the National Anti-Terrorist Committee confirmed that a woman had brought an IED to the eatery.
Those killed included "a security guard who prevented an unknown woman suspected of trying to carry the explosive device into the restaurant, the unknown woman and one of the visitors".
There is not yet any suggestion that the explosion is linked to the war in Ukraine.
It came after Kyiv struck three Russian oil refineries nearly 1,600 kilometres beyond enemy lines, Volodymyr Zelensky said on Saturday.
The attacks deal a fresh blow to Vladimir Putin as repeated strikes on Russia's energy infrastructure inflict damage on Russia's economy and oil industry.
Russia pounded Kyiv with ballistic missiles, killing at least 10 people and injuring 28 others in the second such intense barrage in a week.

Recap: Trump casts doubt on allowing Ukraine Patriot missile production
US president Donald Trump said he was "not sure" he would let Ukraine build Patriot surface-to-air interceptor missiles.
"It's a very extraordinary weapon, and we have to be a little bit careful of who we licence to," the president said in his comments to FT yesterday. This comes weeks after offering to share the license of the coveted air defence systems with the war-hit nation.
On 10 July at the Nato summit in Ankara, Turkey, Trump said: "We'll give them the right to make Patriots. We'll show them how to do it," Trump said. "I think they can produce them pretty quickly."
Ukraine has been requesting the US for Patriot missiles to shoot down incoming Russian missiles, drones and bombs.
Russia lost more than 42,000 troops last month 2026 record, claims Kyiv
The Ukrainian military has claimed Russia lost more troops in July than any other month so far this year, with 42,860 either wounded or killed.
In a post on Facebook, Kyiv's General Staff listed Moscow's troop casualties as follows:
- • January: 31,710
- • February: 26,090
- • March: 31,960
- • April: 32,980
- • May: 33,760
- • June: 39,290
- • July: 42,860 (record number for 2026)
Russia's total personnel losses over the first seven months of the year totals at 238,650, the General Staff said.
IAEA says Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant has lost off-site power
The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) said on Saturday that Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant had lost off-site power for the 23rd time since the military conflict began.
The cause of the latest off-site power loss, which lasted nearly two hours, is not yet known, the IAEA said.
Kyiv strikes three oil refineries 1,600km deep inside Russia - Zelensky
Ukraine hit the infrastructure of three Russian oil refineries in the Bashkortostan region, nearly 1,600 kilometres from the border, president Volodymyr Zelensky said on Saturday.
Mr Zelensky said on the Telegram app that his troops also sank a sanctioned Russian container ship, Yanina, sailing under the Russian flag and with a capacity of more than 100,000 tons, and hit other targets in the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov.
"Our targets are consistently defined facilities that sustain the war effort," Mr Zelensky said.
