The Prince of Wales has attended his first public engagement since a suspected break-in at his country estate in Norfolk.
Mirgen Doku, 34, of Courtfield Gardens in London, and Arilen Ndoci, 32, of no fixed address, appeared at Norwich Magistrates' Court on Wednesday over the alleged incident at Anmer Hall on Monday evening. They were remanded in custody to appear at the magistrates' court on Monday.
Meanwhile, William was celebrating the 25th anniversary of the Royal College of Paramedics, meeting frontline paramedics at their ambulance station in Slough and then hosting a champagne reception at Windsor Castle.
He joked and laughed at the Windsor reception with Carl Smith, an advanced paramedic who flew with William when the prince piloted helicopters for the East Anglian Air Ambulance.
William sat down with paramedics at the South Central Ambulance Resource Centre at Slough's Wexham Park Hospital, where he heard how a manager was able to return to work after receiving trauma therapy she affectionately dubbed "witchcraft".
While the prince joined Prime Minister Andy Burnham at Downing Street for a private meeting on Tuesday, his visit to the busy ambulance centre covering Windsor Castle and the wider area is considered his first public engagement since the alleged burglary.
Hannah Flower, a clinical operations manager, described how last year she received "fantastic" help for "burnout and complex PTSD" after her daughter was treated for leukaemia, and following issues at work.
She told William: "It worked, it definitely worked - I call it witchcraft.
"I don't know how it worked fully still, but it definitely worked and it changed things - I'm a big advocate."
In another room, William met the team behind a new project that received £250,000 of funding earlier this year to introduce a mental health programme initially supporting ambulance control centre staff at Frimley Health NHS Foundation Trust.
The issue resonated with William, who became patron of the Royal College of Paramedics last year, and he told the group: "If you're stressed and in the control room every day, there's no allowance for getting outside, having a walk, having some space for that.
"Is the service getting better at allowing space or allowing time to decompress, to process?"
Later, the prince hosted paramedics, board members and other staff from the royal college in Windsor Castle's green and crimson drawing rooms.
William served as a helicopter pilot with the East Anglian Air Ambulance, flying missions for two years before stepping down in July 2017 to focus on his royal duties.
He flew his last call-out with a crew that included Carl Smith, who still works as an advanced paramedic but is also the Royal College's head of clinical development for emergency and critical care.
When the prince saw his ex-colleague at the end of a line of guests, he laughed as he said: "Oh God, last but not least," and then said later: "I take the mickey out of him, but he's actually quite good."
Mr Smith said after the reception that William was "very passionate" about the mental health aspects of the paramedic's job.
He added: "He has been on some really serious jobs with us, very traumatic.
"He probably doesn't talk much of them, he has been involved in some very serious jobs that have certainly impacted him and the crews, over the time he's been with the air ambulance.
"He will talk about that privately - he has seen a lot of nasty stuff."
