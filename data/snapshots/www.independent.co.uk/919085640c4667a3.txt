Dennis Hastert, a long-serving Republican U.S. House of Representatives speaker whose legacy was ruined by admissions of past sexual abuse against teenage boys, has died at the age of 84, according to Illinois officials on Monday.
Jacquie Purcell, the Kendall County Coroner, confirmed to CBS News that Hastert passed away from natural causes at his residence over the weekend.
In April 2016, he received a 15-month prison sentence for banking violations tied to attempts to conceal hush-money payments intended to hide his history of sexual abuse.
Nicknamed "Denny," he rose to become one of the most prominent political leaders in the nation, presiding over the lower chamber of Congress between January 1999 and January 2007 across the presidencies of Democrat Bill Clinton and Republican George W. Bush.
While viewed by many as an affable Midwestern conservative, his governing style fueled deeper partisan divides within Capitol Hill.
Eight years after stepping down from Congress, his standing crumbled when details surfaced regarding his abuse of young boys while employed as a civics teacher and wrestling coach at Yorkville High School outside Chicago between the 1960s and early 1980s.
During his sentencing at age 74, U.S. District Judge Thomas Durkin delivered a harsh rebuke, telling the court, "The defendant is a serial child molester."
The judge added, "Some actions can obliterate a lifetime of good works."
Because the statute of limitations had lapsed, Hastert could not be prosecuted directly for the sexual assaults.
Instead, he admitted guilt to violating federal banking statutes by structuring cash withdrawals specifically to bypass reporting rules while attempting to keep payments to a former victim undisclosed.
Prosecutors outlined misconduct involving five teenagers as young as 14, alleging that Hastert abused his position as wrestling coach to commit offenses including "intentional touching of minors' groin area and genitals or oral sex with a minor."
According to federal authorities, Hastert invited one boy to share his hotel room during a wrestling trip and subsequently abused him.
Decades later, the victim challenged Hastert over the assault, resulting in an undisclosed agreement in which Hastert pledged $3.5 million to silence him and compensate for the trauma.
Federal agents eventually detected irregular banking activity tied to those hush payments.
Hastert initially brushed off inquiries as routine financial matters before his legal team falsely claimed to investigators that he was being extorted by an individual making baseless allegations.
Attending his sentencing in a wheelchair following a stroke, Hastert addressed the courtroom, stating, "I want to apologize to the boys I mistreated when I was their coach."
During the hearing, a victim's sister confronted him directly, urging, "Don't be a coward, Mr. Hastert." She continued, "Tell the truth. What you did wasn't misconduct. It was sexual abuse of a minor."
Alongside the prison term, the judge mandated that Hastert complete a sex offender rehabilitation program and contribute $250,000 to a victims' assistance fund.
In the wake of his conviction, officials removed Hastert's official portrait from the U.S. Capitol, and Wheaton College, his alma mater, erased his name from a public policy center.
Born on January 2, 1942, in Aurora, Illinois, John Dennis Hastert was raised on a farm before entering public service.
Following six years in the state legislature, he won election to the House in 1986, representing a suburban Chicago district for two decades.
His rise to the top post in the House came amid political turmoil.
As Republicans moved to impeach Clinton over his relationship with White House intern Monica Lewinsky, then-speaker Newt Gingrich resigned shortly after the 1998 midterms.
His expected replacement, Bob Livingston, then quickly withdrew after admitting to extramarital affairs.
Searching for stability, party members selected Hastert, a born-again Christian viewed as a clean figure who had publicly denounced Clinton's conduct as immoral.
He held the office of Speaker for eight years, placing him second in line to the presidency.
By 2006, Hastert had surpassed Joseph Cannon as the longest-serving Republican House speaker.
He stepped down from Congress in November 2007, after Democrats took control of the House in the 2006 elections and ended his speakership.
Throughout his tenure, Hastert deepened the political friction in Washington by keeping his party unified around conservative policy goals while engaging in minimal collaboration with Democrats.
He established what became known as the "Hastert Rule," an informal practice requiring that any legislation brought to the floor have backing from "the majority of the majority." Under this approach, significant legislation was routinely set aside rather than passed using predominantly Democratic votes.
In 2006, the House ethics committee determined that Hastert and other Republican leaders acted negligently by failing to protect teenage congressional pages from improper sexual advances by Florida Representative Mark Foley.
Investigators noted that leadership's failure to act may have stemmed from an effort to protect Foley from political fallout, a controversy that contributed significantly to the party's loss of the House majority.
Following his departure from office, Hastert worked as a lucrative lobbyist.
He married Jean in 1973, with whom he had two sons.
