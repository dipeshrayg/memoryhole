Trump's aides push to prevent Iran war from escalating - Reuters
Senior aides to Donald Trump are pushing to prevent the Iran war from escalating, after the US ramped up the intensity of its attacks on Wednesday.
The aides want to prevent the war escalating before November's midterm elections to hinder Republican electoral losses, four sources told the Reuters news agency.
Officials will consider once again ramping up military action after the vote in November, but a return to the full-scale conflict is not a fiven, they said.
They said that for now, the administration is focused on tightening the economic pressure on Iran in an effort to extract concessions in any future negotiation that months of military action failed to secure.
"We are keeping the pressure on Iran," one White House official said. "But November is a priority."
Keeping the war mostly contained until then would help prevent the unpopular conflict from dominating the news as Trump's Republicans campaign to defend thin majorities in the Senate and House of Representatives, said the sources, three of whom are White House officials.
Exhausted and demoralised? How US sailors are really coping after six months at sea
While President Trump and his self-styled secretary of state for "War", Pete Hegseth, dismissed it as 'fake news', by all accounts, life for the 5,000 men and women on board the USS Abraham Lincoln had become a living nightmare after more than 260 days at sea without respite.
The nuclear-powered warship left San Diego in November and has, until recently, been stationed in the Middle East to support the U.S. war against Iran. While the Lincoln left the Middle East on August 22 on its way home, with a planned Thailand stop, she has spent a record amount of time at sea without a port visit. Stories abound about poor food, bad hygiene, blocked toilets, intermittent showers, plummeting morale and shredded mental health. Oil tankers hit sea mines while transiting Strait of Hormuz, says Tehran
Two oil tankers hit sea mines and were disabled while attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Wednesday.

Moscow helping Iran produce supersonic cruise missiles - report
Iran is secretly developing advanced supersonic cruise missiles with the help of Russia, according to a report.
The C430L programme, which has spanned several years since 2023, has seen Moscow's top missile specialists helping produce the weapons that would significantly strengthen Tehran's military, with the prospect of total peace with the US currently looking bleak.
It was reported by the Financial Times, which dubbed the programme as one of the most "significant known transfers of strategic military technology from Moscow to Tehran".
Moscow is assisting Tehran in developing a ramjet propulsion system, which would enable cruise missiles to fly much faster than the speed of sound, according to military patents and leaked Russian correspondence, the FT reported.
Iranian missile expert Fabian Hinz, an analyst at Conflict Armament Research, told the outlet: "This is the transfer of a highly strategically sensitive military technology from Russia that the Iranians have wanted to acquire for decades.
"A programme like this would require political clearance from the very top in Russia."
