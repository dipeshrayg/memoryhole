The International Cinematographers Guild (ICG), IATSE Local 600, will officially commemorate its 100th anniversary on Nov. 15, celebrating a century of standing up for workers and championing the artistry, innovation and storytelling of its members who have helped shape film and television around the world.
The ICG's lineage traces back to Nov. 15, 1926, with the chartering of IATSE Local 644 in New York, originally organized by silent-era camera pioneers determined to standardize wages, working hours and on-set safety. In 1996, camera locals 644 in New York, 659 in Los Angeles and 666 in Chicago merged to create a national organization. In 2002, the ICG combined forces with the Publicists Guild (IATSE Local 818), bringing camera and publicity professionals together under one union.
John Lindley, ASC, national president of the International Cinematographers Guild, says, "For 100 years, the strength of this Guild has come from its members. This centennial anniversary is an opportunity to honor those who came before us, celebrate the people carrying that legacy forward today, and reaffirm our commitment to protecting the crafts, livelihoods and creative voices of generations of future members to come."
The guild has led the fight for critical labor standards across the entertainment industry, spearheading essential reforms in set safety, fatigue mitigation, multi-employer health and pension protections, and strict contractual guardrails around automation and emerging technologies.
Local 600 has highlighted the moments that defined the century with its "100 Days to 100 Years" campaign, which can be viewed on its Instagram page. The guild will continue to celebrate and commemorate its centennial at its regional holiday events in Chicago on Dec. 5, Los Angeles on Dec. 6, Atlanta and New Orleans on Dec. 12, Santa Fe on Dec. 13 and New York on Dec. 14.
Alex Tonisson, national executive director of the International Cinematographers Guild, says, "Over the past century, Local 600 members have shown what can be accomplished by standing together. Solidarity has been the foundation of our strength, and it is that same unity that will help us rise to challenges facing our industry today and into the next century."
