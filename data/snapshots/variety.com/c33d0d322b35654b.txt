Florence Pugh is shutting down speculation over whether or not she's had plastic surgery done to her face. The Oscar nominee left a comment on an Instagram video posted by Evie magazine in which she called it "disturbing" how people can no longer tell the difference between natural aging and plastic surgery. The video called into question whether or not Pugh changed her face shape by going under the knife.
"Guys. I turned 30. I'm a woman. Body's and faces change. It's not that deep," Pugh wrote. "The fact that people can't even tell between what's aging and what's surgery is disturbing. Sadly puppy fluff doesn't last forever."
In the video posted by Evie magazine, a female commenter is superimposed in front of two pictures of Pugh that are used to show how plastic surgery might've changed the actor's face shape.
"Is this a safe space? People are saying Florence Pugh has fallen victim to Hollywood's war on round faces," the Evie commentator says. "Don't get me wrong, Florence Pugh still looks beautiful, but I think we can all collectively agree that Hollywood seems to have this thing against softer features. The desirable thing in the entertainment industry and the modeling industry right now is this sculpted, snatched looked. A lot of people are pointing out how Florence used to have full, beautiful cheeks that made her face super recognizable. Now that her jaw is more defined, her chin is pointier and her jaw is more sharp, it makes sense why people are wondering if she had work done."
The commentator added later in the video, "What hurts me the most about this is young women seeing [Pugh's face] and thinking, 'Oh, my naturally soft features are something that need to be fixed and its undesirable.'"
Pugh is not one to stand down against social media speculation and/or bullying over her appearance. In August 2023, for instance, she notably railed against the backlash she was getting for exposing skin either in movies or on the red carpet. One year prior, Pugh wore a see-through dress at a Valentino couture show in Rome and had to use Instagram to condemn "vulgar" trolls who sought out to "totally destroy a woman's body."
"I think the scariest thing for me are the instances where people have been upset that I've shown 'too much' of myself," she said in 2013. "When everything went down with the Valentino pink dress a year ago, my nipples were on display through a piece of fabric, and it wound people up. It's the freedom that people are scared of; the fact I'm comfortable and happy."
Pugh continued, "Keeping women down by commenting on their bodies has worked for a very long time. I think we're in this swing now where lots of people are saying, 'I don't give a shit.' Unfortunately, we've become so terrified of the human body that we can't even look at my two little cute nipples behind fabric in a way that isn't sexual. We need to keep reminding everybody that there is more than one reason for women's bodies [to exist]."
In a December 2024 interview with The Sunday Times, Pugh called it "exhausting" to be a young woman in Hollywood and added at the time: "I remember godawful headlines about how Keira Knightley isn't thin anymore, or watching women getting torn apart despite being talented and beautiful. The only thing people want to talk about is some useless crap about how they look. And so I didn't care to abide by those rules."
Pugh is gearing up for a busy rest of the year with the same-day releases of "Avengers: Doomsday" and "Dune: Part Three," both of which hit theaters on Dec. 18.
