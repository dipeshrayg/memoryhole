Mark Tildesley, the acclaimed British production designer who worked on major titles including the most recent James Bond movie "No Time to Die," has died. He was 63.
Tildesley is understood to have passed away on Nov. 24 following a long illness.
Across a career spanning more than 30 years, Tildesley collaborated with an array of leading directors both in the U.K. and beyond, on titles ranging from smaller indie films to big-budget Hollywood productions. Among those he worked with were Danny Boyle, Richard Curtis, Michael Winterbottom, Martin McDonagh, Mike Leigh, Paul Thomas Anderson, Cary Joji Fukunaga, Ron Howard, Ben Wheatley, Oliver Stone and Sam Mendes.
Winterbottom would become a seven-time collaborator, with Tildesley working with the director almost back-to-back in the late 1990s and early 2000s on films such as "I Want You," "Wonderland," "The Claim," "24 Hour Party People" and "Code 46."
Tildesley first joined forces with Boyle on his 2002 groundbreaking zombie hit "28 Days Later," but the two would team up again on films including "Millions," "Sunshine," "Trance" and "T2 Trainspotting." Boyle also tapped Tildesley as his designer alongside Suttirat Anne Larlarb for the 2012 London Olympics opening ceremony, a four-hour, wildly acclaimed extravaganza. It would win Boyle, Larlarb and Tildesley the Emmy for outstanding art direction for variety or nonfiction programming.
With Mendes, he served as production designer on both "Empire of Light" and the upcoming "The Beatles - A Four-Film Cinematic Event" alongside Stefania Cella and Neal Callow.
"Mark was a one-off: gentle, funny, warm, mischievous, entirely original and extraordinarily gifted," said Mendes. "My abiding memory of him is from 'Empire of Light,' flying down the Margate seafront on a motorised scooter, his technicolored scarf flapping in the wind, twinkling like a schoolboy playing hooky. A unique spirit and a wonderful artist. I am one of hundreds of people who are desperately sad that he is gone."
Other credits include Leigh's "Happy-Go-Lucky," Curtis' "The Boat that Rocked," Lone Scherfig's "One Day," Bill Condon's "The Fifth Estate," Wheatley's "High-Rise" and McDonagh's "The Banshees of Inisherin." With Fernando Meirelles he teamed on both "The Constant Gardener" and "The Two Popes." Tildesley's most recent credits were Noah Baumbach's "Jay Kelly" and Joseph Kosinski's "F1: The Movie."
"We are very sad to hear the news of Mark's death," said the British Film Designers Guild (BFDG), which awarded Tildesley three times (for "High-Rise," "Empire of Light" and "F1: The Movie"). "Besides being a well-respected and extremely talented production designer, he was a friend and colleague to many of our members and will be very much missed by everyone in the Industry who knew and worked with him."
