Andrea Scrosati, the Italian business exec was behind Fremantle's aggressive expansion plan that saw it move into film with several high-profile investments, is set to leave the company after eight years to launch a new entrepreneurial venture.
Currently Group COO and CEO of continental Europe, Scrosati will remain at Fremantle until the end of January 2027 to support the transition.
After joining in late 2018 from Sky Italia, Scrosati was central to Fremantle's growth, overseeing an M&A drive in which it acquired production companies and labels including Element Pictures, Asacha Media Group and Lux Vide.
In that time, thanks to its new labels, Fremantle became a major film force. Under Scrosati's tenure, Fremantle-backed titles included the likes of "Poor Things," "Priscilla," "The Hand of God," "Maria," "Bugonia" and "Queer." Accolades included four Oscars from 17 nominations and the Golden Lion in Venice.
Scrosati also helped drive Fremantle's expansion through the launch of the Fremantle Documentaries and Sports businesses, and the growth of its FAST channel business, and spearheaded the creation of AI-focused Imaginae Studios.
Fremantle Global CEO Jennifer Mullin said that Scrosati has made an "enormous contribution" to the company.
"He has been instrumental in our growth and transformation, from helping build Fremantle into a true portfolio company and leading our M&A strategy, to establishing our film strategy and constantly pushing us to think differently about innovation and the future of our business," she added.
Scrosati, meanwhile, described his time at Fremantle as "extraordinary privilege."
"I leave with enormous pride in what we have achieved together and great affection for the company and everyone who makes it what it is," he said. "The time feels right for me to take the next step and focus on building something new. I want to thank Jen for her leadership, vision and trust, all my colleagues across Fremantle, and RTL Group and Bertelsmann for their unwavering support over the years."
