Prince Harry has given Robert Pattinson his royal seal of approval as the new 007.
Appearing on daytime show "This Morning" on Friday, the British royal, who recently moved back to the U.K. with his wife Meghan and their two children, was asked by hosts Dermot O'Leary and Alison Hammond to choose between James Bond and the "Mission Impossible" franchise.
"Uh, James Bond, one hundred percent," Harry replied. "I want to make sure that it's in good hands."
Pressed on what he thinks of "Batman" star Robert Pattinson taking over the franchise from Daniel Craig, the royal said: "I think it's a good idea. I can't wait to see it."
Amazon MGM Studios broke the internet last week when they unveiled Pattinson as their new super spy after months of searching. Pattinson reportedly beat out names including "Slow Horses" star Jack Lowden, "Masters of the Air's" Callum Turner and "The Crown's" Josh O'Connor for the role.
The new film, which is not yet titled, is tentatively expected to bow in 2028 with "Dune" helmer Denis Villeneuve directing. "Peaky Blinders" creator Steven Knight has written the screenplay.
Prince Harry wasn't the only royal to give Pattinson his blessing. Shortly after Amazon revealed the casting the British Royal Family's official X account also weighed in, posting: "Welcome to His Majesty's Secret Service, Mr Bond."
Incidentally, Pattinson will be the first-ever Bond to serve under His Majesty's Secret Service. Creator Ian Fleming published his first 007 novel in 1953, a year after Queen Elizabeth II acceded to the throne. She died in 2022, a year after Craig's final Bond outing in "No Time to Die," and was succeeded by King Charles.
On "This Morning," Harry, Charles's son, also touched on his return to the U.K. from California, where he has been living since taking a "step back" from royal duties in 2020. "It's great being back on home soil, and I'm throwing myself into the new winter weather, which is also nice. I missed that a bit," he said.
"It's always interesting when you're living overseas and looking back in, you can see all sorts of stuff happening. But then I think now that I'm back, everything feels quite familiar and similar. Obviously there's lots of change, but I think as far as London, as far as the U.K., it's home."
