India's "How Love Moves" won the Al Jazeera Documentary Co-Production Award Wednesday at the prizegiving ceremony of Forum, the co-production and co-financing market of leading documentary festival IDFA.
Pallavi Paul's film centers on a century-old graveyard in New Delhi, where the "slow seeping poison of communal hatred and the everyday beauty and brutality of the city come to a head, while an unassuming gravekeeper wages a lone struggle to preserve the sanctity of the dead."
It is produced by Aman Mann and Shaunak Sen for Kiterabbit Films.
The award, which recognizes "projects addressing an urgent issue of our time" presented in the Pitch or Roundtable categories, comes with a $25,000 prize.
Jury members Mohamed Elmongy and Brigid O'Shea said the project, which they described as "politically and creatively sophisticated," explored "the vulnerability of communities deemed dangerous and wrong through state-inflicted violence."
They added, "In doing so, we believe audiences around the world will have access to a place and the people living there who otherwise we may never encounter, and correct or add a new layer of complexity beyond the mainstream narrative.
"And more importantly, it is an exciting opportunity to support a woman-director's voice, we hope at a critical junction in the life of her project."
Alina Baitokova's "Stitch by Stitch" from Kyrgyzstan received the ARTE European Talent Development Award, which comes with a €10,000 prize. The award recognizes a project in development or early production presented in the Pitch or Roundtable categories of IDFA Forum.
The film follows Elmira, a single mother and seamstress in an informal sewing workshop in Kyrgyzstan, who struggles to make ends meet while trying to rebuild the fragile bonds with her children after years of painful separation.
The producer is Adilet Karzhoev for Karzhoev Films, and the co-producers are Marianna Kaat for Baltic Film Production and Christian Popp for Tanjo Films.
Jury members Olga Chernykh, Anne-Laure Négrin and Hanne Phlypo said, "We decided to award a project from a country whose stories are rarely told, with high need of support right now, and to give a director tools to move the project ahead and tell the story that shows people struggling for their life in a rarely seen part of the globalization. It's a film brought by a promising team, a delicate emerging voice."
Parsa Ansari's "Shooting Stars" from Iran received the IDFA Forum Award for best pitch project, which is accompanied by a €1,500 prize.
In the film, a boy begins to question who the real aliens are while investigating mysterious UFO sightings from his childhood. His search gradually shifts from extraterrestrial fantasies toward the invisible outsiders living among us on Earth.
It is produced by Milad Khosravibaledi for Seven Springs Pictures, in co-production with Thibaut Amri for Avant la Nuit, Steven Rubinstein Malamud for Grom Productions, Maria Robstad Nielsen for Emjay Productions and Anita Norfolk for Folk Film.
Jury members Nicolas van Hemelryck and Zane Krūmiņa said, it is "a personal and emotional story where imagination and artistic originality intersect. It is an inspiring narrative about the artist's creative journey that can serve as an encouragement for the young generation."
The IDFA Forum Award for Best Roundtable Project was awarded to Daniela Muñoz Barroso's "Silence Diaries" from Spain and Cuba. The award is accompanied by a €1,500 prize.
The autobiographical documentary traces "a journey in search of a new language" and explores the director's "experience of uprootedness and the gradual loss of sound until the birth of my daughter."
The producers are Muñoz Barroso and Leila Montero for Estudio ST, in co-production with Efthymia Zymvragaki for Gris Medio.
Jury members Renko Douze and Mariia Ponomarova said, "Taking full command of its medium, this project struck us with its artistic strength, evocative approach and sharp conceptual thinking, all within a highly intimate film. Through gesture, rhythm and mesmerizing imagery, the filmmaker convincingly weaves three separate threads, and in doing so tells a deeply personal story with remarkable agency and sensitivity."
Nikos Pilos and Arsinoi Pilou's "Resisting Residents" from Greece received the award for Best Rough Cut Project, which is accompanied by a €1,500 prize.
The logline reads: "In the heart of Athens, a community resists, survives and redefines solidarity."
The producer is Dafni Kalafati for Filmiki.
Jury members Chalida Uabumrungjit and Cleo Veger said, " 'Resisting Residents' is a powerful reminder of how cinema can champion what we believe in. This brave documentary captures a community standing firm for their homes, their dignity and their right to live together, proving that film can be an essential voice for justice and human solidarity.
"We were moved by the courage of the residents, who take in newcomers, face their own conflicts and fight eviction together, and by a film team honest enough to show all of it. Shot by a two-person crew living inside the community, it's close, trusting and alive, and we can't wait to see it finished."
The IDFA DocLab Forum Award for Best Project went to "This Pleasant Lady" by Victoria Mapplebeck. The award is accompanied by a €1,500 prize.
The U.K. project is a coming-of-age story, said the director, "in which, at 61, I write my 'fuck you' list, learn to love again and stop being pleasant. An immersive journey through my brain, breasts and heart becomes a defiant celebration of survival, desire and late-middle-aged rebellion."
It is produced by Mapplebeck for First Person Films.
Jury members Min-Wei Kuo and David Massey said, "The jury unanimously agrees that this project is inspirational and addresses serious subjects in an irreverent but impactful way that will engage with audiences no matter of their cultural background or ages. The spirit of the project and the artist fits the vibe of IDFA DocLab. We're also now encouraged to create our own fuck you list!"
