Russell Brand was "ruthless and calculated" with an "indiscriminate and voracious" desire for sexual contact, the prosecution alleged in its opening statement at the actor and comedian's rape trial in London.
Brand has been charged with three counts of rape, three counts of sexual assault and one count of indecent assault. He has pleaded not guilty and denied all charges.
The jury was selected on Tuesday, consisting of eight men and four women. The case involves six female plaintiffs, all of whom are given automatic lifelong anonymity according to U.K. law, and relates to alleged offenses that occurred between 1999 and 2009.
In the mid-2000s, Brand was best known as a comedian and actor, presenting shows such as "Big Brother's Big Mouth" and starring in films such as "Get Him to the Greek" and "Forgetting Sarah Marshall." He has since pivoted to podcasting and influencing online.

Arriving to Southwark Crown Court on Wednesday morning, Brand sported a black suit, pinstriped shirt, loose white tie and sunglasses. He was accompanied by his wife, Laura Gallacher, whose hand he clasped. According to BBC News, Brand told reporters on his way inside: "I feel blessed. I feel of course concerned, but hopeful that this process will lead to good outcomes for everybody - that is my prayer," he said. Inside the courtroom, Brand appeared to be rubbing what looked like a rock or pebble in his hand.
Riel Karmy-Jones KC is leading the prosecution and set out the case against Brand. As the BBC reports, the six alleged victims "did not know each other, have never met each other" and "came forward independently of each other," Karmy-Jones noted.
Brand was "ruthless and calculated" and "sometimes might tell women what they wanted to hear" to satisfy his sexual appetite, Karmy-Jones alleged. "The circumstances of the offending were as wide and as varied as the defendant's career," she said. "His strong desire for some form of sexual contact was indiscriminate and voracious."
Karmy-Jones alleged that Brand is a "sexual predator" and called him "a flirt with a huge sexual appetite - childlike in his expectation that his every desire should be met." He would "appear indifferent" after an alleged assault, she claimed. "Sometimes he would leave promptly after the incident, sometimes making an excuse to get away or sometimes simply continuing as though nothing wrong had happened," she said, according to BBC News.
The prosecution brought in testimony from a former friend of Brand's, who claimed that the comedian bragged to him that he wanted to sleep with "5,000 women" in his lifetime and had laid out "rules" to achieve this, including telling women "what they want to hear."
Turning to the first alleged victim, who was in her 20s at the time of the incident in 1999, the prosecution said she first met Brand at the Labour Party conference in Bournemouth and assumed he was gay. Later in her hotel room, she invited him to make himself a cup of tea while she used the bathroom. When she emerged, she was shocked to find him "partially undressed."
"Perhaps some of her confusion can be explained by how the defendant was behaving, because as she came out of the bathroom he was prancing around the room - a bit like an ostrich, doing silly walks like Basil Fawlty," Karmy-Jones said, as reported by BBC News. "She was taken aback, she was shocked and didn't really know what to do - and in her words she froze a bit."
The prosecution then alleged that Brand raped her. She "described drifting out of her head, knowing there was nothing she could do, as he pulled her trousers and underwear down, penetrated her vagina with his penis - raping her," Karmy-Jones said.

The alleged incident with the fifth complainant occurred in 2009. The alleged victim met Brand when he was performing at London's Hammersmith Apollo, and their sexual encounter began consensually. They had vaginal sex and she asked him to wear a condom, which he did, the prosecution said. Brand then allegedly raped her anally. She asked him to stop and he did not, the prosecution added, and then she realized he had allegedly taken off the condom. According to BBC News, Karmy-Jones said the woman "spent months and years blaming herself for what had happened, and became extremely anxious and continued to be unwell as a result."
The summer after the incident, the prosecution said the alleged victim saw Brand at a work event and ran out. She then received a text from him, on a phone she no longer owns. When the woman asked Brand why he was messaging her, he allegedly replied: "Because you're dirty, and I wanna push you further." She says she responded, "I think you pushed me far enough - you treated me like an animal," to which Brand allegedly said, "I'm sorry."
The prosecution added that when interviewed about the incident, Brand provided a prepared statement to the police. "He set out that he has never engaged in non-consensual sexual activity, and that any accounts had been reinvented or imagined after a defamatory documentary and subsequent press reporting," Karmy-Jones said.
The sixth woman alleges that Brand demanded a massage from her while she was on a work assignment in 2009. "She said no, and they said she had to," Karmy-Jones said, according to BBC News, referring to Brand and others with him. She touched his shoulders and said he was "really bony," which made the others with him laugh and caused Brand to become "petulant and obstructive." She did complain and "it went higher up the ladder," the prosecution said, but was ultimately "not taken seriously." She then formally reported the incident again at work in 2023, and then to the police. Brand responded to questioning with the same prepared statement as with the fifth incident.
As reported by BBC News, Karmy-Jones concluded her opening statement by saying: "Six women, separate women, different allegations. When you hear them I have no doubt that you will find their evidence compelling."
The defense will present its argument later in the trial, which is expected to last for six weeks.
