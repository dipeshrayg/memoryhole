Ed Sheeran addressed the controversy surrounding Macklemore's removal from his Loop Tour on Saturday night, speaking from the stage at Philadelphia's Lincoln Financial Field during his first concert since the rapper was dropped from the trek.
"Before I play some songs tonight, I hope it's okay if I say a few words about this last week," Sheeran said at the top of Saturday's show. "I'm used to my music being criticized, and I'm always happy to take that because I'm lucky. A lot of you like my stuff, and to be honest, I'm surprised. I'm not surprised that I've even done it. You know, it's normal. But this week, the criticism was about something much more important, and I've had to take and make difficult decisions, and I am making mistakes, and I'm so, so sorry."
Sheeran continued: "I don't want to disappoint fans, and I've never wanted to be an activist musician, but this has put me in the middle of an important and passionate argument about freedom of speech and the most complex political issue on the planet. My concerts have always been a safe space for everyone, and that matters hugely to me. Honoring my commitment to my fans is also completely essential to who I am, and that is why I'm here tonight on my own and continuing my current tour."

Later in the set, Sheeran introduced the musicians (including violinist Alicia Enstrom) who replaced Beoga, the traditional Irish folk group that pulled out of his remaining U.S. tour dates. "There are certain songs that just don't work on the loop pedal, so when my friends dropped out of the show, I was thinking, 'How am I gonna keep the show the same, and how am I gonna get musicians to stand on stage with me in a week when no one wants to stand on stage with me?'"
"I just wanna say before they come on stage [that] they said yes to being on stage with the knowledge that the world thought I was a certain type of person," he continued. "They said yes with the knowledge that the world may hate them too for standing on stage with me and I am so grateful for them being on stage tonight and standing on stage with me in front of you guys."
The controversy also prompted Sheeran to scrap the B stage - a smaller secondary platform closer to the crowd where he typically performs several songs - because he "was scared of what might happen if [he] stood in the middle."
"I'm gonna be totally honest with you, I didn't know what this audience would be like tonight," he said on stage. "I didn't know if I would have things thrown at me. I didn't know if people would be angry at me."
Near the end of the show, Sheeran thanked his fans in another emotional speech, this time without reading off a teleprompter, for their support during "one of the worst weeks of my life."
"I really didn't know if this tour would be carrying on," he continued, the crowd responding in an uproar of boos. "I really didn't know whether I'd be even getting on stage again, whether I even wanted to be an artist at all. In these situations, you really find out who your friends are"
He went on to say that people in his life urged him to cancel the tour. "So many people freaking out in my life who I've known for years and years and years, people just backing away," he said. "And in the back of my mind, I saw the ticket sales and I didn't see the cancellations. I saw people buying tickets for this gig. And everyone's telling me to cancel and I'm feeling all of my friends and family and people I know abandon me and I look at the end of the week and I see 50,000 people who haven't abandoned me me."
Saturday's concert marked Sheeran's first performance since a tumultuous week for the U.S. leg of his Loop Tour, which saw Macklemore removed from the remaining dates following the rapper's onstage remarks in support of Palestinians during Sheeran's Sept. 4 and 5 shows at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey.
During his MetLife performances, Macklemore spoke about the war in Gaza and the West Bank and told the stadium crowd, "Free Palestine." His set also included "Hind's Hall," his 2024 protest song supporting Palestinians. The remarks drew criticism from some Jewish and pro-Israel organizations, including the Israeli-American Council, which called for Macklemore to be removed from the tour.
Messina Touring Group, the promoter behind the U.S. leg of the Loop Tour, subsequently announced that Macklemore would no longer appear on the remaining dates. The company said venues had informed the promoter that they would not allow concerts featuring Macklemore to proceed, a situation it said could have resulted in cancellations affecting hundreds of thousands of ticket holders.
While Sheeran addressed the decision in a statement earlier this week, stressing that Macklemore's removal was made by the promoter and venues rather than by him, he again acknowledged that he is "deeply worried about the idea that venues pre-approve content for performance." "This happens in so many places that I perform around the world, but it should never happen in the free world," he said.
At the time of his initial statement, Sheeran said he was "appalled by the conflict between Israel and Palestine," describing the suffering caused by the war as a "human tragedy," while explaining that he has generally sought to keep his concerts focused on "unity, joy, escapism, love" rather than his own political views. On Saturday, he restated these feelings, though also speaking more explicitly about where he stands on the Israel-Palestine conflict.
"I have tried in my career not to be a political commentator of any kind, because I want my music and shows to be about unity, not division," he said. "But this is a humanitarian issue, and I cannot hide how I feel about it anymore. What happened in Israel and at the Nova Music Festival on Oct. 7 was horrific and compounded centuries of Jewish pain. What is happening in Gaza is catastrophic and unjustifiable, and disproportionate. Our hearts can be broken by the scale of devastation and loss of civilians' lives, children's lives and the systemic injustice we're seeing unfold in the West Bank cannot be overlooked."
He continued, "I am already speaking to people across the spectrum to work out how to make a contribution that helps victims of these terrible times, and listening and learning because I do not know enough."
After Macklemore was dropped, he responded to his removal in a statement, saying that he and Sheeran had held "difficult conversations" in the aftermath of the MetLife shows. While expressing respect for Sheeran personally, the rapper challenged the singer's preference for remaining publicly neutral and said he did not regret using the tour stage to speak in support of Palestinians. Macklemore has since said he will donate $1 million to Palestinian relief organizations.
The fallout quickly spread beyond Macklemore. Singer-songwriter Aaron Rowe and Danish band Lukas Graham, who had been expected to support Sheeran on upcoming North American dates, both withdrew from the tour. Finneas, who was scheduled to join Sheeran for a run of South American dates later this year, also pulled out, while Beoga - longtime collaborators who had been performing as part of Sheeran's live show - exited as well. All four cited Macklemore's removal in announcing their decisions.
The Philadelphia date nevertheless proceeded as scheduled Saturday at Lincoln Financial Field, kicking off the next stretch of Sheeran's U.S. stadium run.
