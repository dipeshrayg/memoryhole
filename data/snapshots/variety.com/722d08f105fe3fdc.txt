If you're looking to get through a few more books before the end of the year, now is a particularly good time to sign up for Audible. As part of its Black Friday sale, the Amazon-owned audiobook platform is offering eligible subscribers three months of its Standard membership for just 99 cents a month, plus a $20 bonus to spend on audiobooks. The limited-time deal runs through Dec. 28.
Whether you're looking for something to listen to on a long-haul flight home for the holidays or hoping to get a head start on your 2027 reading goals, Audible's sprawling library runs the gamut from buzzy celebrity memoirs and bestselling romantasy novels to immersive, full-cast productions of literary classics. In recent years, the audiobook format has become an entertainment medium in its own right, with ambitious original productions featuring A-list voice casts, cinematic sound design and original music.
From the latest installments in some of BookTok's most beloved fantasy franchises to a reimagining of Jane Austen's "Pride and Prejudice," we've rounded up the best audiobooks to listen to on Audible right now.
A Court of Splintered Harmony
BookTok's favorite romantasy author returns with the sixth installment in her bestselling "A Court of Thorns and Roses" series. Narrated by Elizabeth Evans, the long-awaited release kicks off a new story arc dubbed "The Valkyrie Cycle," with the next installment slated to arrive in January 2027.
Exoplanet by Andy Weir
From the author of "Project Hail Mary" and "The Martian" comes a cinematic Audible Original, co-written with Benjamin Percy and featuring a starry voice cast led by Anthony Mackie, Ariana DeBose and John Leguizamo. The sci-fi thriller follows the survivors of a spacecraft disaster who find themselves stranded on the same hostile planet across different points in time.
Sunrise on the Reaping (The Hunger Games): A Hunger Games Novel
Fresh off its big-screen adaptation, the fifth novel in Collins' "Hunger Games" franchise transports readers back to Panem 24 years before Katniss Everdeen enters the arena, chronicling a teenage Haymitch Abernathy's fight for survival in the Second Quarter Quell. "Yellowstone" star Jefferson White narrates the audiobook.
Famesick by Lena Dunham
The "Girls" creator didn't hold back in her long-awaited second memoir. In "Famesick," Dunham recounts her tumultuous relationships with Adam Driver and her former best friend Jenni Konner, her fallout between her and Jack Antonoff and her struggles with addiction following her hysterectomy. Check out the biggest revelations from the book here.
Roll the Calls by Ari Emanuel
Hollywood superagent Ari Emanuel opens up about his rise through the entertainment business in "Roll the Calls," a candid account of building Endeavor and navigating decades of high-stakes deals, rivalries and relationships. The memoir also gets personal, covering Emanuel's childhood struggles with ADHD and dyslexia, his marriage and family life and plenty of behind-the-scenes encounters with some of Hollywood's biggest names.
Daddy Issues by Sofia Franklyn
The former "Call Her Daddy" co-host tells her side of the story in a candid memoir that goes well beyond her highly publicized split from the podcast. Narrated by Franklyn herself, the book delves into the complicated intersections of fame, friendship, ambition and self-worth.
Klara and the Sun: A Novel
Few novels feel more timely than Nobel Prize-winning author Kazuo Ishiguro's 2021 bestseller, which explores the nature of human connection through the eyes of an AI companion purchased to care for an ailing teenage girl, played by Jenna Ortega in Taika Waikiki's new adaptation. The audiobook is narrated by Sura Siu.
Pride & Prejudice by Jane Austen
Harris Dickinson as Mr. Darcy? Say no more. Audible's full-cast adaptation of Austen's literary classic stars the "Babygirl" actor opposite "Industry" breakout Marisa Abela as Elizabeth Bennet, with supporting performances from Glenn Close, Jessie Buckley, Bill Nighy and Will Poulter, alongside an original score by Morgan Kibby.
This Is Me: A Reckoning by Hayden Panettiere
In "This Is Me: A Reckoning," the now 36 year-old actor recounts the traumatizing Hollywood experiences and tabloid scrutiny she experienced through the early-aughts, in addition to candid stories about postpartum depression, addiction and domestic abuse.
Never Say Die by Ke Huy Quan
Quan tells the unlikely story of his Hollywood comeback in "Never Say Die," beginning with his childhood as a Vietnamese refugee and early success in "Indiana Jones and the Temple of Doom" and "The Goonies." Quan also writes about the decades of rejection that led him to step away from acting, his work behind the camera and the eventual return that culminated in his Oscar-winning performance in "Everything Everywhere All at Once."
Peculiar Stars by Rebecca Yarros
Described as a "slow-burn romance novel about love, family, and what it means to become the truest version of yourself," bestselling author Rebecca Yarros's newest book follows the main character Callista as she develops a relationship with her fiancé's cousin in the aftermath of a tragedy.
Remarkably Bright Creatures by Shelby Van Pelt
Now a Netflix film, "Remarkably Bright Creatures" follows grieving widow named Tova who forms an unlikely friendship with a remarkably intelligent giant Pacific octopus named Marcellus. The audio version is narrated by Marin Ireland with Michael Urie as the voice of Marcellus.
Project Hail Mary
Stranded aboard a spaceship with no memory, Ryland Grace, who Ryan Gosling portrayed in the blockbuster adaptation earlier this year, must piece together who he is, why he's there, and how to save Earth from an extinction-level threat. Andy Weir's signature mix of suspense, science, humor, and high stakes shines through in this interstellar odyssey. Narrated by Ray Porter, a standout voice in speculative fiction audiobooks.
Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone, Book 1
A chorus of more than 200 voices, ranging from young newcomers to Hollywood stars, bring new life to all seven of J.K. Rowling's "Harry Potter" book series in a new production for Audible. The first audiobook in the bunch, "Harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone," dropped in November.
Game of Nines by James Patterson
This high-stakes crime thriller drops FBI agent Sarah Cole into a chilling game: somebody has issued her an invitation to the "Game of Nines," a secret murder puzzle that's been baffling law enforcement for decades. Given one week to catch the killer - who promises to vanish and strike again in nine years if not caught - Cole must outthink a cat-and-mouse built for the most twisted of minds. Narrated in full audio-drama style by Shailene Woodley, Morena Baccarin, Sasha Roiz, and Ben Shenkman.
Semi-Well-Adjusted Despite Literally Everything: A Memoir
In this 11-hour self-narrated memoir, actress, dancer, and YouTube personality Alyson Stoner takes listeners through the twists and turns of growing up in the spotlight - the expectations, the struggles with identity and mental health, the pressure to perform, and the moments of self-discovery.
Robert Redford: The Biography
For those looking to revisit Robert Redford following the iconic filmmaker's death this week, this biography traces his rise from youthful ambition to iconic Hollywood status while also unfolding his off-screen passions - from preserving the wild to championing new voices in film. Long before Sundance and screen legends, Redford's story starts in small-town Colorado and arcs through acting breakthroughs, directorial risks and environmental activism.
Heartburn by Nora Ephron
Meryl Streep narrates the audiobook version of Nora Ephron's beloved novel "Heartburn," which tells the story of Rachel Samstat who, seven months into her pregnancy, discovers that her husband Mark is in love with another woman. The "Sleepless in Seattle" and "Harry Met Sally" creator reminds us all why she's the queen of romance (and anguish) with this sidesplitting novel.
Me: Elton John Official Autobiography
If you find yourself fascinated by Elton John after watching his upcoming documentary, you'll want to read his debut autobiography in which the music icon himself narrates his remarkable life - from a young shy boy (born Reginald Kenneth Dwight) in a small London suburb, to the legendary artist who ended up changing the music world forever with his songs. Aside from his rise to success and extravagant lifestyle, all things shown in the bio-pic "Rocketman," John also recounts his experience with the AIDs crisis and writes candidly about getting clean from addiction.
Heads Will Roll
Less of a book and more of an audio comedy, Audible Original's "Heads Will Roll" is a 10-episode series created by "Saturday Night Live" star Kate McKinnon and co-creator/star (and sister!) Emily Lynne. The story follows Queen Mortuana of the Night Realm (McKinnon) and her ditsy sister JoJo (Lynne) who work together to hilariously put down an impending peasant rebellion that threatens their throne. The star-studded satire features performances by Meryl Streep, Tim Gunn, Peter Dinklage and "Queer Eye"'s Fab Five.
Just Kids
Patti Smith's unmistakable throaty drawl adds dimension to the pioneering punk poet and singer's exquisitely-observed and heartbreaking story of how she fled New Jersey in her early 20s, lived on almost nothing in New York, and moved in with photographer Robert Mapplethorpe while starting to perform at CBGB and Max's Kansas City.
Crying in H Mart: A Memoir
The musician Michelle Zauner, known as Japanese Breakfast, proves she's as adept at writing a sensitive memoir about family ties as she is at creating dreamy electro-pop. Zauner writes of growing up as a Korean American in Eugene, Ore., moving to the East Coast and beginning a band, and returning to Oregon to take care of her mother when she's diagnosed with cancer. Memories of special meals she shared with her mother and grandmother and her efforts to come to terms with her family are woven throughout the book, which Zauner narrates herself.
