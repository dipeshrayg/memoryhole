Denis Villeneuve is not exactly thrilled that "Dune: Part Three" will be going head to head with "Avengers: Doomsday" at the box office.
Both blockbusters will hit theaters on Dec. 18, paving the way for a huge box office event similar to "Barbenheimer," with people already coining it "Dunesday." It will likely be a great weekend for cinema with many fans opting to see both movies, but Villeneuve admitted in a new interview with Time magazine that he does have his concerns.
"Listen, I will be honest. The first 'Dune' came out at the end of the pandemic. We made a great box office in a very rough environment. But we had the winds against us," the director said. "Then the second movie, same thing. We were meant to release in the fall, and we had to release at the end of winter because of the two Hollywood strikes. We had success, but it was not easy."
For the third and final "Dune" film, Villeneuve was looking forward to breathing a sigh of relief: "Finally, there is no pandemic, no strike in sight. We will have a normal release," he remembers thinking. "Then Disney put the biggest franchise of all time against us. I'm no fool. Those movies are a mammoth."
The Marvel tentpole was originally set to release on May 1, 2026, but was delayed last year to Dec. 18, the same date "Dune 3" already held. The last "Avengers" movie, 2019's "Endgame," still ranks as the highest-grossing movie of all time with $2.94 billion worldwide.
"I was deeply disappointed," Villeneuve said of the shift. "But we had committed to this date, to Imax. It was impossible to move. So I accepted my fate, praying to the gods of cinema. 'Dune' is not a mammoth like the 'Avengers,' but it's a sexy little animal that has a lot of firepower, and I hope we will survive."
Disney did not immediately respond to Variety's request for comment.
However, Villeneuve is trying to stay optimistic. "It could be one of the biggest box office weekends of all time because of both movies," he concluded. "Am I in competition with the 'Avengers'? Absolutely not. It's a T. rex. But I stand proud because I think the film delivers."
Besides, the director is sure to have another box office juggernaut (or three) on his hands with the new James Bond movie, starring Robert Pattinson as 007. Though the Time interview took place before Pattinson's casting was announced, Villeneuve teased: "I don't sleep a lot right now because it's like I have electricity in my veins thinking about this new project. It is so different."
He added that he doesn't see himself changing the world of Bond too much. "I love traditions. I love the unapologetic way the character behaves, the panache. I'm not here to change the DNA of James Bond. I'm here to embrace it, to bring it to the world of today," he said. "I'm a hardcore James Bond fan, and I want other fans to have fun watching my film. Of course, it will be tainted by my sensibility. But I wake up every morning with a stupid smile because it's a world of pure cinematic pleasure."
