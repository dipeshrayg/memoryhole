Apple, which helped spawn the popularity of podcasts (which are named after its now-obsolete iPod), is finally going to bring video versions of podcasts to its Apple TV app.
The move by the tech giant comes after YouTube has become a major distribution platform for podcasts, while Spotify has been trying to capitalize on video podcasts for the past five years.
"With the Apple TV app available across Apple and supported third-party devices, this creates more opportunities for creators to reach new audiences while retaining control of their content and monetization," the company said in announcing the new feature.
Video podcasts from Apple Podcasts are coming "soon" to the Apple TV app, to be available across multiple platforms including the Apple TV set-top box, Roku, Amazon's Fire TV devices, smart TVs and more, the company announced Monday. Video podcasts from Apple Podcasts will be available for testing in the latest 27.3 developer beta release of the Apple TV app as soon as Monday.
Apple Podcasts has supported video formats since 2005. However, until earlier this year, the Apple Podcasts app has distributed video podcasts using RSS - so if you wanted to have both audio-only and video versions of a podcast, that has required two separate show entries in Apple Podcasts. Now, Apple has shifted to using the HTTP Live Streaming (HLS) protocol, which the company developed, for video podcast delivery.
"It's still early for its HLS video experience, and this expansion marks the next step in helping audiences discover shows and creators grow their reach - whether people choose to watch or listen," Apple said.
According to Apple, users will be able to take advantage of synced playback between Apple Podcasts and the Apple TV app, so you can pick up where you left off and move between screens.
In the last three months, the number of shows publishing video on Apple Podcasts has more than doubled, while the video episode catalog has nearly tripled, the company said. Top podcasts that introduced video saw their total show audience grow by more than 20% on average, according to Apple.
Alongside video podcast availability on macOS and tvOS devices this year, in Apple's CarPlay, updates in iOS 27.1 include a redesigned home screen featuring Apple Podcasts with "new episode" cards, a "continue playing" option and recommendations, plus a "new" tab that mirrors the experience on iOS.
