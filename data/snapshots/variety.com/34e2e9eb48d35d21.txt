Gordon Ramsay is the new host of "Top Gear."
Longtime TV chef Ramsay is swapping hobs for hubcaps as he takes the wheel of the BBC's best-known motoring show.
Ramsay, an avowed car fan who holds a racing license, has previously appeared as a guest on the show three times.
"Top Gear," which is produced by BBC Studios, will return in 2028 with filming to take place "across the U.K. and around the word" next year according to the studio, who promise more "epic adventures, daring tests and spectacular stunts."
The Stig, the show's racing driver, will also return to the show.
"My love affair with cars began as a child, growing up a field away from Silverstone, where I would climb over the fences and sneak in for a glimpse of the racing," said Ramsay. "That early excitement sparked a lifelong passion for cars, motor sport, beautiful design and extraordinary engineering."
"At its very best 'Top Gear' has always been about the unexpected - surprising, entertaining and just the right amount of chaos. That's the spirit we want to build on, bringing a fresh energy and ambition to create something truly thrilling for a whole new generation of audiences. It's an incredibly exciting opportunity, and I genuinely can't wait to get started!"
The series ran from 1977 to 2022 when it was taken off air after co-host Andrew Flintoff, a former cricketer, was badly injured in a crash at the show's test track. He suffered life-changing injuries after his open-topped three-wheeled car overturned.
Earlier this year the BBC's director of entertainment, Ed Havard, confirmed the broadcaster was planning to resurrect the show.
Former "Top Gear" hosts have included Jeremy Clarkson and "Friends" star Matt LeBlanc.
