Tyson Fury does not have schizophrenia, despite revelations made in the promo for his new Netflix feature doc. These revelations have now been removed from the film.
In the trailer for "Tyson Fury," the British boxing champion is seen discussing his mental health struggles, noting that when quizzed by a psychiatrist he "ticked all the boxes ... depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder..." Fury's wife then adds that he's a "mild schizophrenic."
The comments sparked numerous headlines and also prompted British boxing regulators to look into the matter to ensure that Tyson would be medically cleared to fight Anthony Joshua in their long-awaited bout due to take place Dec. 11. But now Paris Fury has walked back those claims, putting out a statement denying that her husband has schizophrenia. Netflix has subsequently edited the scene in question out of the documentary.
"I want to clarify a statement I made in the original version of the Tyson Fury documentary," Paris Fury said. "I recalled a conversation from many years ago when discussing Tyson's condition and I used the word 'schizophrenic'. While Tyson did show some symptoms associated with schizophrenia, he doesn't have this condition and has never received a diagnosis."
Added a Netflix spokesperson: "Following new information shared by the family relating to Tyson's official mental health diagnosis, we have worked alongside them to update the documentary accordingly ahead of launch."
Now showing on Netflix and directed by Lucy Wilcox, "Tyson Fury" charts the triumphs and setbacks of the heavyweight champion's career, his struggles with mental health and addiction, the loss of a child, complex family relationships and the question of what it means to be a man.
The film was produced by Optomen, part of Banijay Entertainment, which also produced Netflix's "At Home With the Furys" reality show featuring the boxer and his family, now onto its third season.
