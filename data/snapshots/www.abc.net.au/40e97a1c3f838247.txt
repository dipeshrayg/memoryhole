Australia slumps to 176-run defeat against South Africa on day five of third Test after dismal batting display at Newlands in Cape Town
David Warner has slammed Australia's "deplorable" batting after the tourists limped to a 176-run defeat following a monumental top-order capitulation in the third Test at Newlands.
South Africa wrapped up a 2-1 series victory just before tea, although the result was not in doubt from the early morning as Australia completely imploded on a turning track in Cape Town.
Kagiso Rabada took 3-39, including two wickets in the first over, while player of the match Simon Harmer befuddled Australia with 6-16 in 21.1 overs.
Australia were 8-62 at lunch, and all out for 111 only thanks to the efforts of Nathan Lyon (33 not out) and Pat Cummins (11) in a chastening defeat.
"Within the first hour, the game was away from us," Pat Cummins said.
"We didn't give ourselves a chance."
Australia started the final day of a fiercely contested series with legitimate aspirations of victory.
Six balls later it became clear that if Australia were going to get anything out of the final day's play it would have to be via a recovery job of epic proportions.
Rabada, off colour in the first bowling innings due to illness, was a man reborn, bowling with pace and accuracy to end Australia's hopes before they even got going.
Matt Renshaw (0) feathered a leg side delivery to Ryan Rickelton may be considered unfortunate.
Marnus Labuschagne's decision to leave a ball that angled in from outside off stump to be given out LBW without playing a shot for just 4 runs, cannot.
The Queenslander's torrid tour ended on the sourest note possible, his immediate future surely to be spent away from the Test team as patience finally runs out with his lack of form.
Rabada was not done yet, finding the edge of Travis Head (14) off the last ball of his fourth over to leave Australia 3-22.
But it got no better, as Australia's batters gave up their wickets in increasingly fragile fashion to be 7-60 at lunch.
Josh Inglis was incomprehensibly run out for 4 attempting an insane single, precipitating a collapse of 3-2 as Alex Carey (2) and Cooper Connolly (0) were both bamboozled by Harmer.
Mitch Starc (0) also fell to the veteran off spinner following a baffling 25-ball stay in which he turned down runs.
"It's deplorable. It's un-Australian. There is no fight whatsoever, it's disgraceful," David Warner said on Fox Cricket.
"The batters have let the bowlers down all series ... I think the batters have been disgraceful, utterly disgraceful."
He was far from the only one to be disappointed.
"Words can't describe how disappointing it is for Test cricket today," Phil Jaques said on commentary for ABC Sport.
"I bounced out of bed this morning hoping for a really good day of cricket ... and then Australia just didn't show up.
"They've been found wanting on numerous occasions ... it's just been pretty poor from Australia, to be honest, and really deflating as a cricket fan."
After lunch, if possible, things got even worse, Australia scoring just three runs in an hour, turning down runs regularly in a spell that saw a 78-ball period without a single run scored as right-hander Green was sheltered from Harmer's off breaks.
In that time though, remarkably, Cameron Green still found a way to get out after Harmer switched ends, with Green changing the 37-year-old Essex legend who bowled him expertly through the gate for 27.
Nathan Lyon and Pat Cummins endured for 17 more frustrating overs in a partnership worth 45 runs, showing a resilience that their top order counterparts could do well to learn from.
However, with players crowding the bat, they could not hold out for ever as Harmer trapped Cummins LBW for a 90-ball 11 in the shadows of tea, bowling Josh Hazlewood soon after to claim his first ever Test 10 wicket haul.

Lyon got through a monstrous 44 overs in South Africa's second innings, bowling 10 maidens, but did not take many wickets, ending with innings figures of 2-101.
However, Ryan Rickelton thinks that Australia will find it very tough, especially their right handers.
"It was a lot easier for me as a left-hander because there wasn't much turn from the straight areas of the pitch," Rickelton said.
"When the off-spinner went wider and bowled into the rough, trying to lure me into playing outside off stump, there was a much greater chance of an edge or a dismissal.
"I'm actually surprised there isn't more turn from the straight, considering the [hot] weather we've had in Cape Town over the last few days.

What is the highest score chasing to win at Newlands?
Just so we know what Australia is up against here today, these are the highest successful chases at Newlands in Tests.
- 6-334 Australia vs South Africa 2002
- 2-236 South Africa vs Australia 2011
- 3-212 South Africa vs India 2022
- 5-211 South Africa vs India 2007
- 3-186 South Africa vs West Indies 2008
- 6-182 South Africa vs Pakistan 2013
So Australia, if they are to win today, will have to record the second highest fourth innings run chase in the ground's Test history by knocking off 288.
Of course, that 2002 Test was a cracker, an unbeaten 100 from Ricky Ponting getting the Aussies over the line for a four-wicket win as they successfully chased 331.
Australia still needed 202 runs to win heading into the final day, with Pointing anchoring the victory after a 101-run opening partnership between Matthew Hayden and Justin Langer.
There have been higher fourth innings scores than that at Newlands that have not resulted in a win:
- 5-354 West Indies vs South Africa 2004 (draw)
- 335 South Africa vs New Zealand 1962 (loss)
- 306 Sri Lanka vs South Africa 1998 (loss)
- 304 England vs South Africa 2005 (loss)
- 9-296 England vs South Africa 2010 (draw)
So it is more than possible to score big on this ground on a wearing pitch.
If Australia scores 288 it will be the seventh highest fourth innings score in Newlands' history as a Test venue.
