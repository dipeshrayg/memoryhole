In short:
A Kimberley man has been sentenced to 10 years' jail for the fatal assault of a woman in the Mowanjum Aboriginal Community.
Craig Charlton Rastus was sentenced in the Derby Supreme Court over the April 2025 incident.
Family members of the victim shouted, "He should have got more" as the sentence was given.
A West Australian man has been jailed for 10 years for killing a woman in a sustained and violent assault in the state's remote Kimberley.

Craig Charlton Rastus faced the Supreme Court in Derby for sentencing today over his fatal assault of the 39-year-old woman last year.
The woman, who cannot be identified for cultural reasons, was found unresponsive in Mowanjum Community, 2,240km north of Perth, on April 20.
She was taken to Derby Hospital, but died as a result of her injuries.
Rastus was initially charged with her murder, but the charge was subsequently downgraded to manslaughter.
He pleaded guilty in July.
When the sentence was announced, members of the woman's family in court yelled out, "He should have got more."
Argument over alcohol sparked violence
Prosecutor Tracy Tan told the court Rastus started assaulting the woman in the early hours of the morning after "an argument over alcohol".
She said it was a sustained physical assault lasting about 30 minutes, with the woman receiving blunt force injuries to the head and face.
Ms Tan said Rastus also used a power cord to repeatedly whip the woman.
The attack happened outside a Mowanjum property, with Rastus later moving the woman's unresponsive body inside.
He called authorities for help at 7am the following day, with the woman pronounced dead at Derby Hospital at 9am.
Not an isolated incident
In his sentencing remarks, Justice Matthew Howard said the killing "could not be regarded as an isolated incident".
Justice Howard said Rastus was issued with a 72-hour order prohibiting him from contact with the woman about 11:20pm on April 19 last year, "some three hours before the incident occurred".
"At the time of your assault there were in effect two orders binding on you to protect the deceased; you ignored both of them," he said.
"Importantly you were subject to a protective bail order and were serving a suspended imprisonment order."
Justice Howard applied a 25 per cent sentence discount for Rastus entering a plea at "the earliest possible opportunity".
Family says sentence not enough
The small public gallery of Derby Courthouse was packed by the 39-year-old victim's family and friends.
Speaking outside court, her sister, Yelonda Donation, said Rastus's 10-year sentence was not enough.
"We're still grieving, we're still processing," she said.
"Today was a hard one to swallow ... 10 years is not enough for us; it feels very unfair."
Ms Donation said her sister's absence was felt deeply across Derby and surrounding communities.
"She was a beautiful, loving, caring ... strong family woman," she said.
"She loved her kids and her family; she loved outings and family gatherings.
"Wherever she'd been, she made friends and family as well."
