Donald Trump praises Anthony Albanese as Quad leaders unveil new defence and critical minerals deals
In short:
Donald Trump has announced new military and critical minerals cooperation with Australia, India and Japan at the first Quad leaders' summit in two years.
The plans include joint military exercises and agreements to secure critical minerals supplies.
What's next?
Anthony Albanese now travels to Shenzhen ahead of the APEC summit where he is expected to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping.
Donald Trump has unveiled new defence and technology deals with his "friends" Australia, India and Japan to strengthen regional security and loosen China's grip on critical minerals supply chains.
Speaking alongside Prime Minister Anthony Albanese at the Quad leaders' summit in Manila, Mr Trump praised the growing importance of the four-nation partnership and foreshadowed major developments in the year ahead.
It was the first time the talks, which also included Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, had been held at a leader-level in two years and the first since Mr Trump's return to the White House.
The meeting comes at a delicate moment for the region, with Mr Trump preparing to travel to China for talks with President Xi Jinping, as countries across the Indo-Pacific navigate growing strategic competition between the world's two largest economies.
The Quad has long been seen as a counterweight to China's growing military and economic influence, although its members have avoided explicitly framing the grouping as an alliance against Beijing.
Mr Trump announced the Quad would conduct a joint military exercise involving maritime patrol aircraft, building on recent naval drills near Japan.
He also highlighted the growing importance of submarines and singled out Australia's cooperation with the United States in a clear reference to the AUKUS agreement.
"We make the greatest submarines anywhere in the world, and we're working with Australia and others on that," he said.
'We're all friends': Trump praises Albanese
Mr Trump used his opening remarks to emphasise the importance of the partnership, taking credit for reviving the Quad in 2017 during his first term as president.
"Our countries are the four corners of a free, open, stable and prosperous Indo-Pacific," he said.
He also went out of his way to praise his fellow leaders, including Mr Albanese, stressing their personal relationships and declaring his door was always open to them.
"We're all friends. It's a lot easier that way," Mr Trump said.
"My phone is always open, and the Oval Office is always open to you."
Trump says 'lot of things happening' on critical minerals
The US president announced several new critical minerals agreements aimed at reducing the countries' vulnerability to disruptions in global supply chains.
"We've also made several important, new critical mineral deals to secure our supply chains and ensure that none of our nations is vulnerable to coercion by any single foreign supplier," he said.
While Mr Trump did not name China, Beijing dominates the processing of many critical minerals and rare earths, giving it considerable leverage over other countries reliant on those supplies.
He also suggested further significant agreements were on the horizon.
"We have a lot of things happening over the next 12 months that will very much surprise people,"
he said.
The leaders also discussed cooperation on artificial intelligence, which Mr Trump referred to as super intelligence, including the recently announced Quad accelerator and a pledge to spend more than US$85 million ($126 million) to expand access to the technology among trusted partners in the Indo-Pacific.
"We want to ensure that the future of this critical technology is dominated by nations that share our values and our common interests," he said.
Mr Trump described AI as potentially more significant than the Industrial Revolution, claiming advances in science and medicine were already accelerating at extraordinary speed.
Albanese says 'sum' of Quad greater than parts
In his opening remarks, Mr Albanese welcomed the renewed focus on practical cooperation, particularly in critical minerals, AI and maritime security.
"What these four countries represent is greater than the sum of just the individual parts,"
he said.
Mr Albanese also backed plans for the first Quad Business Leaders Forum in 2027, saying private companies would play a crucial role in translating government agreements into economic growth.
Mr Modi described the Quad as a trusted partnership providing practical solutions for the Indo Pacific, while Ms Takaichi said the group would help shape the future of the Indo-Pacific.
"We would like to realise a free and open Indo-Pacific by strengthening resilience of countries in the region and promote their freedom to decide for themselves across a wide range of areas including maritime security, economic security, energy and critical and emerging technologies," she said.
Trump brings top officials to Quad meeting
Mr Trump had arrived in Manila about 45 minutes prior to the scheduled start of the meeting, with the Quad the first of several meetings he is due to have in the region over the next two days.
The four leaders sat around a square table, with Mr Trump flanked by Secretary of State, and national security adviser, Marco Rubio on his right and Secretary of Treasury Scott Bessent on his left.
Mr Trump's side of the table also included his new chief of staff Stephen Miller, US Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick, US trade representative Jamieson Greer and Sergio Gor, who is the US Ambassador to India and Special Envoy for South and Central Asia.

Mr Albanese is expected to travel to China on Wednesday ahead of the APEC summit in Shenzhen.
The annual gathering has been somewhat overshadowed by a looming trilateral meeting between Chinese President XI Jinping, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Mr Trump due Thursday.
