In short:
Britain has insisted its airbases remain safe after the US military moved several aircraft from RAF Mildenhall, in eastern England.
It comes as US media report the evacuations were prompted by a threat from Iran.
Iran has denied any links to specific attacks in Britain, but its Revolutionary Guards have said airbases used by US forces there and in other countries are legitimate targets.
Britain has insisted its military bases remain safe after the US moved large refuelling aircraft from a UK airbase amid US media reports of a threat from Iran.
The removal of aircraft from RAF Mildenhall, first reported by the Wall Street Journal, marks the second such apparent evacuation in three months.
Commercially available flight tracking data showed at least 10 Boeing KC-135R Stratotanker refuelling planes left the base, in eastern England, on Monday, local time.
One flew to a base in Germany, one to Scotland and two to Spain, while the destinations of the other six were not reported.
At least three Osprey multipurpose aircraft also departed from Mildenhall and landed at Lakenheath, another RAF base four miles away.
Video filmed by a member of the public from outside the base and verified by Reuters showed two Ospreys taxiing on the runway after landing at Lakenheath.
Asked about the moves, the US European Command said the Air Force moves aircraft between locations for a variety of reasons, and it would not comment on force protection measures for operational security reasons.
A spokesman for Prime Minister Andy Burnham said the protection of Britain's military bases was a top priority, and that the government worked closely with the police and allies to protect them.
Security measures were kept under constant review and the government was investing more than £2 billion ($3.8 billion) in air defence and counter-drone capabilities, he said.
Asked if he was confident that Britain's bases were safe, he said: "Yes."
The White House and the Pentagon did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
US removed bombers in September
According to the Wall Street Journal, the evacuations were prompted by a threat from Iran. The precise nature of the threat was under discussion among US officials, it said.
Iran has denied any links to specific attacks in Britain, but its Revolutionary Guards have said airbases used by US forces there and in other countries are legitimate targets.
Britain has been warning for months that Iran has been planning attacks, recruiting people online to carry them out.
In September, the United States removed bombers that had been used to strike Iran from another British base, RAF Fairford, on security grounds after a group of men approached the base in white vans.
Two men were charged last month in relation to that incident, with prosecutors alleging one of them had tried to assist Iranian intelligence in Britain.
Britain's domestic spy chief Ken McCallum said in a speech last month that Iran-linked operations had led to more than 50 arrests over the past year. Iran was ramping up attempts to hit harder targets such as military sites in the UK, even though its previous activity had been limited by the lack of sophistication of the criminals it used as proxies, he said.
Security posture upgraded
The US upgraded the security posture at RAF Mildenhall and another UK base, RAF Lakenheath, to FPCON Charlie, the second-highest defensive threat level, the WSJ report said, citing a US official familiar with the matter.
The Fairford incident, which remains largely unexplained, attracted huge international attention at a time of heightened concern about the security implications for Western states of the war between the United States and Iran.
Mr Burnham has said the activity at RAF Fairford was "highly likely" to have been coordinated in Iran.
