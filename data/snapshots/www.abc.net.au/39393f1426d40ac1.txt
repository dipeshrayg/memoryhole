Trump, Xi and Putin release joint statement after historic trilateral talks
In short:
US President Donald Trump, China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin have held historic talks in Shenzhen.
Before the three leaders met, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese directly called on Mr Putin to end his illegal invasion of Ukraine.
But official accounts of the meeting do not mention the war in Ukraine.
The official account of a landmark trilateral meeting between Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin does not mention the war in Ukraine, despite the US president saying the conflict would be on the agenda.
Instead, the three leaders emerged from their historic talks in Shenzhen with a broad statement on nuclear security, global energy supplies and potential reform of the United Nations, but few concrete details about how those ambitions would be achieved.
Hours before the meeting, Prime Minister Anthony Albanese used his speech at the Asia Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in China to directly call on Mr Putin to end his illegal invasion of Ukraine "now".
The meeting between Mr Putin, Mr Xi and Mr Trump on the sidelines of APEC lasted about 90 minutes on Thursday evening.
A readout published by Chinese state broadcaster CCTV after the talks wrapped up made no reference to Russia's invasion of Ukraine, but included an agreement that the International Criminal Court (ICC) and similar bodies should not abuse their jurisdiction.
The declaration was particularly significant for Mr Putin, who remained subject to an ICC arrest warrant over the alleged unlawful deportation of Ukrainian children during Russia's invasion.
The three leaders also recognised the immunity of state officials, a potentially contentious position given the ICC's role in prosecuting alleged war crimes.
The leaders pledged to work together to reduce nuclear risks and prevent nuclear proliferation, reiterating that a nuclear war "cannot be won or fought".
They also agreed to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum products, emphasising the importance of stable global energy flows, although no specific targets or timelines were announced.
At the start of the trilateral, the leaders gathered in front of flags from all three nations at the sprawling Kylin Villa in China's tech hub of Shenzhen and stood for a photo, without shaking hands, before entering the meeting room.
Trump confirms Ukraine talks before trilateral
"This is the first time the heads of nations from China, Russia, the US meet; it has groundbreaking significance," Xi said, according to Chinese news agency Xinhua.
Mr Trump was effusive in his praise of Mr Xi, congratulating him on hosting a successful APEC, while he also described it as an "honour" to be meeting with Mr Putin.
Ahead of the meeting, the US president confirmed Ukraine was on the agenda for the meeting, and said the conflict had "already" been discussed with Mr Putin earlier in the day.
"I think he [Mr Putin], and I hope the other side, would like to see an end to that war," he said.
Mr Trump also used his opening remarks to attack the International Criminal Court, which he described as "out of control".
"I believe we all agree [it] has really strayed far from beyond its jurisdiction," he said.
Mr Trump also said that, as the three largest nuclear powers, the US, China and Russia were "uniquely positioned" to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth.
"It's quite frankly a very hard thing to do because once you have the nuclear weapons, it's very hard not to have them," he said.
"You have thousands of weapons, and you're supposed to give them up."
Mr Putin said Russia, China and the United States "hold joint responsibility for maintaining international peace and security" during his opening remarks.
"Our countries have numerous shared interests in the settlement of regional conflicts, including obviously the conflict in Ukraine," Mr Putin said.
"I am convinced that Russia, the US and China, our people are capable of making a significant contribution to strengthening peace and stability over the world, if we cooperate constructively on the basis of mutual respect, equality, and a balance of interests."
Ahead of the meeting, Mr Trump referred to Mr Putin as his "friend".
Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk immediately took to social media.
"God, protect us from such friends," he wrote on X.
Albanese directly calls on Putin to end Ukraine war
Hours before the high-stakes meeting was due to begin, Mr Albanese used his allotted speaking time at APEC to single out Mr Putin over Russia's illegal invasion of Ukraine.
"I say very directly to President Putin: end your war, end it now," Mr Albanese said.
The prime minister was looking directly at Mr Putin as he delivered the words, but the Russian leader did not look up when addressed.
Mr Xi and Mr Trump were also in the room during the prime minister's APEC speech.
Mr Albanese warned global conflicts, including in Ukraine and the Middle East, were driving up inflation and putting pressure on household budgets.
"Military conflict, disrupted global supply chains, and increasing challenges to the international rules that have underpinned decades of growth are impacting our national economies," he said.
"I welcome the meeting being held this afternoon between President Xi, President Trump, and President Putin, and we hope for positive outcomes in the interests of the world."
Meeting sparks 'Yalta 2.0' fears
The gathering has drawn comparisons with the 1945 Yalta Conference, when US president Franklin D Roosevelt, British prime minister Winston Churchill and Soviet leader Joseph Stalin met to discuss the future of Europe as World War II ended.
That summit helped shape the post-war international order and became synonymous with powerful countries making decisions about the future of smaller nations without their involvement.
In the lead-up to the Shenzhen meeting, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned against Mr Trump, Mr Xi and Mr Putin using the meeting to carve up spheres of influence, amid concerns Russia could seek concessions over its ongoing invasion of Ukraine.
"We really don't want this to turn into 'Yalta 2, 3, 4,' with a redistribution of influence," Mr Zelenskyy said last month.
