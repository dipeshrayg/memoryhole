In short:
The Sydney office of independent MP Alex Greenwich have been graffitied with homophobic slurs.
Mr Greenwich said he has previously received "written threats, phone abuse, social media, but never anything like this".
What's next?
NSW Police are investigating the incident and urge anyone with information to come forward.
Homophobic graffiti sprayed on the outside walls and windows of Sydney MP Alex Greenwich's office is under investigation by NSW Police.
Mr Greenwich told the ABC that the vandalism was unprecedented.
"I've got lots of written threats, phone abuse, social media, but never anything like this," he said.
Reports of the attack at the Oxford Street premises in the inner-east Sydney suburb of Surry Hills were received by police on Sunday about 12pm.
"A crime scene has been established, and investigators are reviewing CCTV and conducting inquiries to identify the people involved," police said in a statement.
In an interview with 702 ABC Sydney on Monday morning, Mr Greenwich drew a link between the colour of the graffiti, "the same as sort of One Nation orange", and called on the party to join condemnation of the attack.
A spokesperson for the office of NSW One Nation told the ABC that the party "strongly condemns all forms of graffiti, vandalism and acts of political intimidation".
"We support police treating targeted graffiti and political intimidation with the seriousness they deserve," the spokesperson said.
Mr Greenwich said he was feeling OK following the incident but said "this sort of stuff is just exhausting".
"We try to stand up for what we believe in, we advocate for the communities we represent and then people try to intimidate and bully you with homophobic slurs."
Mr Greenwich said he was receiving "more abuse than ever" as he claimed "people from the far right" were becoming "more and more emboldened".
"I think this is a real time for people to stand up against bigotry, homophobia, racism, to seek to bring us together, not to pander to those who seek to tear us apart," he said.
Allegra Spender, the federal member for Wentworth, condemned the incident as "vile", saying she was "disgusted and appalled".
"There is no place for homophobia or hate in our community, and certainly not in Australian politics,"
she said.
"Words matter, and this kind of attack is designed to intimidate and harm."
She added that her electorate was "sending you all our love and support".
Focus on violence against politicians
The vandalism comes after growing awareness and discussion on violence against politicians, after the shocking car attack on Victorian Independent MP Helen Haines, which led to both her legs being amputated.
NSW MP Jodie Harrison told the ABC last month that the attack on Ms Haines had resurfaced memories of nine years ago when two envelopes containing death threats were slipped under the door.
"It made me feel physically sick," she said.
"One of the downsides of public life is that people who might not like us know that we can actually easily be found, because we're public people."
Recently, AFP Commissioner Krissy Barrett told the ABC's Insiders that there was a direct link between inflammatory political discourse and threats against parliamentarians.
"It does have a direct correlation to what we see in terms of threats and abuse, and sometimes it's very immediate," she said.
"I would challenge any Australian to read some of these threats and some of the abuse that is reported to us from federal parliamentarians and for anyone to argue that that is free speech or political discourse."
Police are urging anyone with information to contact Crime Stoppers on 1800 333 000 or Surry Hills Police Area Command.
