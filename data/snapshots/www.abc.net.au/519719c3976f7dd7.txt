Mexico's military says it has captured the leader of a drug trafficking group among the 10 most wanted fugitives sought by US law enforcement.
Fausto Isidro Meza (whose alias is "Chapo Isidro") and two other leaders of his gang were captured during a special forces raid on Friday night, local time, in Sinaloa state, the Mexican National Defense Secretariat announced on X.
Giving his name as Fausto Isidro Meza-Flores, the FBI listed him as one of their 10 most wanted fugitives and the alleged leader of the Beltran Leyva gang, which is allied with one of two rival factions in the powerful Sinaloa cartel.

The FBI had offered a $US5 million ($7.55 million) reward for information leading to his arrest.
The US intelligence organisation's bulletin says his group is responsible for importing "large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl, and marijuana into the United States".
He was captured alongside his number two in the group and another man identified as a financial operator.
The Mexican government said in an early Saturday report that intelligence from the Trump administration helped authorities capture him, but provided few details about how the two countries coordinated.
He was also a key ally to "Los Mayos," a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel, which was designated as a foreign terrorist organisation by the Trump administration last year.
His capture represents a blow to the cartel as it fights with another faction for control of the northern Mexican state.
Videos and photos from the city of Guasave, Sinaloa - where Meza Flores was captured - show screaming civilians fleeing gunfire at a baseball game, as well as burnt cars.
Mexican authorities said they used "stealth and surprise" to capture him.
They said after being attacked with guns and explosives from likely cartel operatives, the officials entered a home and arrested him.
They later evacuated Meza Flores and others detained in the clash in a Mexican Air Force plane to Mexico City.
He faces criminal charges in both Mexico and the United States.
Mexican forces' move against cartel fuels violence
The capture of Meza Flores and his No. 2 in the city of Guasave, Sinaloa, set off a wave of violence when a firefight broke out, according to residents of the 290,000-person municipality.
Soon, military helicopters and drones were flying overhead, and helicopters opened fire on key locations across the city.
Videos show screaming civilians fleeing gunfire at a baseball game, burning cars and businesses, and circling helicopters shooting at people below. Such shows of force by cartels are common following the capture or killing of leaders.
"The fighting was happening all over Guasave," said Alma Camacho, a Guasave resident and local journalist.
"It spread throughout the city."
Mexican security analyst David Saucedo said the capture of Meza Flores marked another case of the governments of Mexico and the US increasingly falling back on a longstanding security strategy that critics warn could fuel more violence in the long run.
The so-called kingpin strategy relies on zeroing in on capos such as Meza Flores as a way to combat cartels.
But experts say that it often triggers violent power disputes among groups, like the one underway in Sinaloa.
"What is evident is this is a product of pressure from the United States,"
Mr Saucedo said.
"What they do is capture high-profile kingpins - the leaders - but they don't dismantle the entire organisation. They arrested the No. 1 and No. 2, but the entire network of hit men, street-level drug dealers, businesses and political and police connections remains intact."
