In short:
A judge has released footage showing the lead-up to a fatal electric motorbike crash in Edgewater that claimed the life of Shelley Spinks.
The video was shown in the Perth Children's Court during the manslaughter trial of a teenager who was allegedly riding the motorbike.
What's next:
The trial is due to continue until the end of the week.
Shocking aerial footage has been released to the public for the first time, showing the lead-up to the death of a woman who was fatally struck by an electric motorbike in a suburban Perth park.
WARNING: The following story contains details and vision that may distress some readers.
The video was shown in the Perth Children's Court during the trial of a teenager who was allegedly riding the motorbike in an Edgewater park in July last year when he collided with 59-year-old nurse Shelley Spinks.
Ms Spinks was taken to Joondalup Hospital, but died from her injuries.
The teenager, who cannot be identified as he was 17 at the time of the incident, has pleaded not guilty to manslaughter.

The 25-minute clip shows the teen riding at speed through suburban streets, veering onto the wrong side of the road and narrowly avoiding other vehicles, before cutting across a park.
At several points, he mounts footpaths while continuing to ride at speed and rides close to a group of pedestrians.
At 2:40pm the vision shows the teen veering across the road and into Picnic Cove Park, and two minutes later his motorbike can be seen lying on its side, as Ms Spinks lies on the grass near a footpath.
The moment of impact was obscured from the police camera vehicle by trees in the park.
A police officer is then seen approaching the teen, while several bystanders tend to Ms Spinks and call for help.
Police officer Alessandro Caratozzolo gave evidence in the trial and said he was riding his police motorcycle when he first came across the teenager.
Officer Caratozzolo said the accused flipped him "the bird" in a car park, which he told the court was a "clear gesture."
The court heard that the police officer later arrested the motorbike rider, and his body-worn video was played to the court.
The 17-year-old accused can be heard repeatedly asking in the video, "Why'd she jump in front of me?" and "Will she be okay?"
As the footage was played in court, the accused and his family were visibly emotional.
Earlier in the day, the court heard from witnesses who saw the boy riding his bike before the collision.
Linda Hughes told the court she was walking two dogs with her partner when she saw a bike in the park travelling with "astonishing speed".
Ms Hughes said her partner Mario Geisner ran to the scene to give first aid to a woman.
Her partner also gave evidence and said he heard the motorbike first, describing it as "so quick".
Another witness, Julian Harris, told the court he had been jogging in Picnic Cove Park before the fatality.
After hearing a police helicopter, Mr Harris said he then heard the sound of a motorbike.
Mr Harris said the motorbike was travelling "quite fast".
"I was quite worried that it was going to hit someone," he said.
In his opening address, defence lawyer Simon Watters said the footage showed the teenager was "in control of his bike throughout the pursuit" and that he was "seeking to avoid the deceased" at the time of impact.
The trial continues.
