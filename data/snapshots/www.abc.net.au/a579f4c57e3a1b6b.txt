Addressing a coronial inquest for the first time, James Hollands's mother says her son is dearly missed and describes him as a vulnerable teen "poisoned" by online extremist propaganda.

The inquest concluded today.
The final day of a coronial inquest into the death of a radicalised Perth teenager shot dead by police has heard from his mother, who said he was "poisoned by online extremist propaganda" and is dearly missed.
WARNING: This story contains details and footage that may distress some readers.
On the night he died in May 2024, James Leslie Hollands stabbed a stranger in the back and chased others with a knife before police arrived.
The inquest heard evidence two police officers tasered the 16-year-old, but he continued to advance towards police with a knife before a third officer shot him.
James had been diagnosed with autism and ADHD, and had been participating in a police-led anti-extremism and support program, the court heard.
Vulnerable son 'poisoned'
Today, the inquest heard a heartfelt statement from James's mother, Tracey Hollands, read out in court by a lawyer.
Ms Hollands said while the inquest heard about what James suffered, and what he did, not a lot had been said about who James was.
"No doubt to some people, James was just a terrorist, or a terrorist who got what he deserved, but he was not this to us," the statement read.
She said James was a teenager who loved his family, loved their family cats, and made his friends and family laugh.
He enjoyed drawing cartoons, making and painting miniature models, and playing chess.
He also loved the outdoors, climbing the tallest trees and helping with the bees.
"Those of us who knew James best and who loved James believe that he was so vulnerable, and finally poisoned by online extremist propaganda," the statement read.
"Though so many people helped him so much, we could not - even all of us together - rescue or save him.
"I know what the officers who were first on the scene that night were confronted with was a situation that was physically and emotionally difficult, and I am grateful that no further harm was done by James.
"James was my son, and he was loved, and is missed."
Police defend use of force
The inquest was shown CCTV on Thursday of James approaching a car with a knife in the Willetton Bunnings car park before police exited their vehicle, and James advanced toward police.
The inquest previously heard from all three of the officers who attended, including Officer B - who discharged the firearm on the night - all of whom testified they feared for their lives.

The court was shown Officer B's body-worn camera footage on Thursday, which showed James take a step toward the officer before the firearm was discharged.
Earlier, the inquest heard James sent a message to 33 people not long before the attack, which stated he would be going "in the path of jihad tonight".
In closing submissions on Friday, James's family's lawyer, senior counsel Grant Donaldson, said the family could not accept the only means for police to deal with the situation was by drawing tasers or a firearm.
The details of police training and policy have been suppressed by the court.
The inquest previously heard a homicide squad investigation found the discharge of the firearm was justified, and a separate internal affairs investigation concluded the force used was reasonable and consistent with training and policies.
Senior counsel Tim Russell, who appeared on behalf of WA Police, said in order to protect members of the community, it was necessary for the officers to intervene.
He submitted police had repeatedly told James to drop the weapon and tasered him.
"None of the police officers involved wanted to be in that position," Mr Russell said.
"The threat was real. The officers had tried other options ... the use of force was appropriate."
CVE program supported
The inquest also investigated the merit of police-led or health-led models for countering violent extremism (CVE) programs and heard evidence in favour of both approaches.
It has been widely accepted throughout the inquest James had comprehensive supports around him, including through public psychologists, a private psychiatrist, disability supports through the NDIS, a spiritual mentor and supports in his school.
Mr Donaldson told the court James's family did not think what happened on May 4, 2024 was caused by a failure in CVE.
"It is undoubtedly an excellent program," he said.
The corner is expected to hand down his findings next year.
