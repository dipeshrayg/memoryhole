In short:
Eight people have been killed by Russian strikes in Ukraine.
Russian President Putin vowed a "painful" response for Kyiv's strikes on the Crimean bridge this week.
Poland reported Russia violated its airspace three times overnight.
Russia pummelled Ukraine with dozens of missiles and hundreds of drones overnight, killing eight people, hours after President Vladimir Putin had vowed a "painful" response for Kyiv's strikes on the Crimean bridge this week.
Residents took shelter in the metro as explosions echoed around the capital Kyiv, where Russia hit three bridges.
The strikes on the bridges that cross the Dnipro River and link the two sides of Kyiv were the biggest since Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022.
Kyiv authorities said an attack on a water utility facility killed three employees and some buildings could temporarily lose their water supply.
Russia also targeted electricity infrastructure across Ukraine, causing widespread blackouts, officials said.
In Lviv, two people were killed in an attack on the Sknyliv railway station.
Ukraine's Air Force said Russia had launched 84 cruise missiles in the attack, almost all of which were destroyed, as well as an unspecified number of ballistic missiles and more than 400 attack drones.
Russian President Vladimir Putin said on Thursday that Moscow would hit back hard after Ukraine struck the bridge between Crimea and Russia's Krasnodar region as part of a months-long campaign against Russian logistics.
Mr Putin vowed to retaliate, after a historic meeting with US President Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit.
Russian and Ukrainian officials met for US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi last week, but there were no major breakthroughs on key issues.
Poland condemns Russian airspace violations
Russia violated Poland's airspace overnight with three objects that were likely two missiles and a drone, the Polish defence minister said on Friday.
"There has been a violation of Polish airspace and an incursion by objects from the Russian Federation," Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz told a news conference.
The incursions happened as Poland was set to hold military exercises of the 'Coalition of the Willing' of Ukraine's allies on Friday.
"These exercises, combined with the presence of defence ministers from France, the United Kingdom, and the Baltic states - along with eight chiefs of staff, including those from our key allies - suggest that the convergence of these events is no accident," Kosiniak-Kamysz said.
He said that the exercises would go ahead and also said Poland would ask NATO for more support securing the alliance's eastern flank.
Estonia closes Russian border for six months
Estonia will close its border crossings with Russia for at least six months from December 1 on security grounds, the Baltic country's government said on Friday.
Estonia this week said it would close all border crossing points with Russia from next month following a suspected arson attack on an Estonian defence company that Tallinn blamed on Moscow, but did not specify how long the closure would last.
"The decision is a response to Russia's ongoing hostile actions against Estonia and other European countries," the government said in a statement.
Moscow has regularly denied accusations by European authorities of involvement in sabotage operations.
Estonia, a NATO and European Union member state and a strong supporter of Ukraine, currently has five crossing points where travellers can enter or leave Russia, according to the government.
