In short:
Zach Bates has qualified on pole for Sunday's Bathurst 1000, topping the time sheets during the Top 10 Shootout at Mount Panorama.
Qualifying king Brodie Kostecki failed to become the first driver to snare four straight pole positions, finishing second.

Supercars rookie Zach Bates - not qualifying king Brodie Kostecki - will start the Bathurst 1000 in pole position.
Kostecki was on track to become the first driver to snare four straight pole positions after laying a mighty lap of two minutes and 4.4912 seconds as the fifth driver in Saturday's top-10 shootout.
Proven race winners Will Brown, David Reynolds and Anton De Pasquale tried, but failed to beat Kostecki's time.
But provisional pole-sitter Bates refused to waver, proving his mettle as the last driver with a searing lap around the famous Mount Panorama track.
A day after becoming the fastest Gen3 racer, the 22-year-old clocked 2:04.2062 to deny Kostecki by 0.285 seconds.
Bates had earlier become the first to go below the 2:04 benchmark in the Gen3 era after taking provisional pole with a time of 2:03.9376.
In just his second pursuit of the holy grail of Australian motorsport, Bates is the third rookie to claim pole position after Klaus Ludwig (1987) and Marcos Ambrose (2001).
The Canberra local is the son of former rally and Supercars driver Rick Bates, and the nephew of four-time Australian Rally champion Neal Bates.
His surprise success comes after his mother Alison Drower died from cancer weeks before his Bathurst 1000 debut last year, in which he finished 10th as part of the Triple Eight Race Engineering wildcard with the great Craig Lowndes.
Team 18 driver De Pasquale will start in the second row with fourth-fastest Cooper Murray, while series leader Matt Payne was 10th.
But Grove Racing star Payne will be far from disheartened, given he had started from 18th en route to last year's epic wet-weather triumph with former co-driver Garth Tander.
Reynolds will start on the grid in eighth after a family of ducks threatened to derail his bid for a second Bathurst 1000 title.
Tickford co-driver Mark Winterbottom was forced to a sudden halt when ducks were spotted crossing the track at Conrod Straight during practice on Friday.
Winterbottom's sudden braking caught out Reynolds' co-driver James Courtney, who locked up and slammed into the rear of the Tickford Ford Mustang.
But both cars were able to return to complete the 60-minute practice session before the shootout.
Defending Supercars champion Chaz Mostert was among the big names to miss out on the top-10 shootout and will start from 16th for the best qualifying result in the Toyota Supra.
Payne's teammate Kai Allen fell agonisingly short in 11th, while title contender Broc Feeney was 12th ahead of Cam Waters in 13th.
Kostecki's rookie teammate Rylan Gray will start from the back row with Ryan Wood after the duo crashed out in separate incidents in Friday's first practice session.
