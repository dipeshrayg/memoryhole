In short:
Russian President Vladimir Putin has freed a former US teacher accused of serving as a mercenary against Russia in 2022
The gesture of goodwill kicked off talks between the Kremlin and Washington after US special envoy Steve Witkoff visited on Wednesday, local time.
What's next?
Russia says it is ready to resume trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia peace talks next month.
Russian President Vladimir Putin says Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old US citizen held in Russian custody since 2022, has been freed and is at Pulkovo Airport in St. Petersburg.
Mr Putin did not give further details in comments published by Russian state media on Wednesday, local time.
He made the announcement during a meeting with US envoy Steve Witkoff in St Petersburg.
Mr Hubbard, a retired schoolteacher from Michigan, had been serving a nearly seven-year prison sentence in Russia after being convicted of mercenary activity at a closed-door trial.
He was arrested in April 2022 in the eastern Ukrainian city of Izyum, where Russian prosecutors alleged he had been manning a Ukrainian military checkpoint.
Russian state media reported he pleaded guilty to the charges.
Although his US-based lawyer, Martin De Luca, previously told Reuters Mr Hubbard had said the charges were false and that he was not part of any combat or military unit.
At the time of his release, Mr Hubbard was the only US prisoner held by Russia who was designated by Washington as "wrongfully detained".
Mr Witkoff, who is in St Petersburg for talks, thanked the Russian president for the gesture of goodwill.
The visit marks the ninth time that Mr Witkoff has met Mr Putin in person and comes more than two years after US President Donald Trump promised in his successful election campaign to end the Ukraine war within 24 hours.
After the meeting, Russia said it was ready to resume trilateral US-Ukraine-Russia talks next month.
"Our president informed Steve Witkoff that we are, in principle, ready to resume the mentioned talks", Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said, saying these could take place in mid-November "with the main topic of building security ... after the end of the conflict".
Mr Witkoff had urged Mr Putin to consider reviving peace talks and to review new ideas on ending the war amid Washington's latest push to stop Europe's bloodiest conflict since World War II.
"One thing that I hope to accomplish today is to have some good conversation between us about the resumption potentially of trilateral talks between Ukraine and Russia and the United States," Mr Witkoff told Mr Putin.
"Even if we don't have a resolution today, if we just talk about and sort of consider what that next step might be I think that would be important and the world is waiting to hear that."
The meeting comes amid concerns in Kyiv and among its allies that the United States is backing away from Ukraine, at times holding back real-time intelligence on Russian attacks and hobbling Ukraine's military communications.
Mr Witkoff flew into Russia's second city on Wednesday morning local time, a day after conducting what a US official called "constructive" talks in Oslo with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, the leaders of Finland and Norway and European and NATO representatives, as Mr Trump said it was time for Ukraine to end the war or face "a very bad thing".
US-Ukraine ties have deteriorated sharply since Mr Trump announced a diesel export deal with Russia earlier this month to help lower prices in the United States.
High fuel costs since he launched strikes on Iran have hurt his Republicans ahead of November legislative elections.
Since the diesel deal, Ukraine has complained of disruption to some intelligence sharing and to its use of the Starshield military satellite system, though Mr Zelenskyy said that the Oslo meeting had reached an agreement to resolve those issues.
White House denies interference with Starlink satellite capabilities
Citing US officials and people familiar with the matter, the Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday local time that billionaire Elon Musk had last week ordered the slowdown of a military-grade version of SpaceX's Starlink satellite service that Ukraine relies on to track Russian ballistic missile strikes.
Reuters could not immediately verify the report.
Mr Musk has said any such decision is taken by the US military or the president. Mr Trump has said he was not responsible.
US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said the problem lay with the terms of service Kyiv needed to comply with.
US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Wednesday that Mr Witkoff's meeting with Mr Zelenskyy was positive and that Mr Witkoff would share the results with Russia.
He also reiterated that Washington had not pulled back Kyiv's ability to access Starlink.
"He will now, armed with those ideas, be able to go to St Petersburg and share them with the Russian side, and let's see if we can make some progress," he said.
A trilateral meeting between the Russian President Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping is also "being prepared", according to the Kremlin.
Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said on Wednesday that the aim was to include the meeting in the programme of the Asia-Pacific summit in Shenzhen next month.
