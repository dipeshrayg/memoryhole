Thu, 3 Sept, 8:10 pm, Final score, Dockers 40, Hawks 72
Geelong star Georgie Prespakis has held back tears as she joined older sister Maddy in becoming an AFLW league best and fairest winner.
Having admittedly struggled in her sister's shadow, Prespakis took centre stage on Monday night after polling 30 votes to claim the league's highest individual honour.
The count went down to the final round, and last year's runner-up Prespakis pipped Gold Coast's Charlie Rowbottom by two votes.
Jasmine Garner was the hot favourite to break through for an elusive best and fairest award, but the North Melbourne captain finished third on 24 votes.
Prespakis went to another level this year to help transform Geelong from a 13th-place finish last season to second under new coach Mick Stinear.
She averaged 30.8 disposals, eight clearances and eight score involvements a game, before Geelong's run ended in a surprise semi-final loss to Sydney.
Finishing second to Ash Riddell last season, Prespakis is the first to win the top AFLW gong for Geelong and also earned her third All-Australian blazer.
She accepted the honour with Essendon star Maddy in the crowd, six years after Georgie presented the 2020 award at home to her sister - who was playing for Carlton at the time - due to the COVID-19 lockdown.
The sisters join Albert and Harry Collier (1929 and 1930 respectively) as the only siblings across the AFL and AFLW to become league best and fairest winners.
"I feel very grateful, obviously, looking at my sister, to think that she's been up here and been able to achieve this," Georgie Prespakis said.
"I'm full of emotion and trying to hold back some tears for sure.
"I've always wanted to follow in Maddy's footsteps, and at times, I probably felt a little bit in her shadow.
"And to be in this journey now, where we're both able to play - I know she's looking at me, like, 'Shut up', but yeah, I'd always felt like that.
"And I suppose now in my career to be able to share these amazing moments together ... to be able to lean (on) and support one another is something that's meant a lot to me."
Garner once again missed out, but underlined her status as an AFLW great with a record ninth All-Australian blazer.
She surpassed teammate Emma Kearney for the most AFLW All-Australian selections and was named captain of this year's representative side.
It is the third consecutive year Garner has captained the All-Australian team and the fourth overall.
Melbourne midfielder Jordyn Allen claimed the Rising Star award after receiving 37 of a possible 40 votes, ahead of Richmond's Olivia Wolmarans (33) and Collingwood's Ash Centra (24).
Wolmarans had momentarily stolen the show when she jokingly threatened to repeat Tigers assistant coach and ex-Carlton forward Brendan Fevola's drunken antics at the 2009 Brownlow Medal count.
Last year's No.1 draft pick, Wolmarans moved in with the Fevola family when she relocated from Western Australia.
"I actually messaged him and said that I should recreate that night," Wolmarans said.
"Yeah, give me another glass."
Gold Coast co-captain Lucy Single won goal of the year and her Suns teammate Dekota Baron took out mark of the year.
