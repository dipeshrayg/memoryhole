In short:
Jensen Huang has been reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive just days after the AI company board ruled out his return.
US President Donald Trump has congratulated Nvidia on the "great" decision and shares in the company have soared.
What's next?
Investigations into the company's conduct are continuing.
Nvidia says it is reinstating Jensen Huang as its chief executive, effective immediately.
Mr Huang resigned from the global artificial intelligence giant earlier this month after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman US authorities suspected of spying for China.
Beijing had denied the spying allegations and Nvidia says the board unanimously voted to reinstate Mr Huang after it was satisfied with his "candour and ability to lead" the company.
The move comes just days after the board ruled out Mr Huang's return.
Mr Huang, who co-founded the business, thanked the board for its "confidence and deliberation" over the past few weeks.
"I am honoured to return to Nvidia and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution," Mr Huang said in a statement.
"I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.
"I can't wait to get back to work."
Nvidia board 'satisfied' Mr Huang poses no national security risk
The board now says it is satisfied with Mr Huang's leadership ability and, after receiving input from senior US government officials, saw no national security risk in his return.
"The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," board chair Harvey C Jones said in a statement.
"We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the company."
The board's reversal saw the company's shares soar more than 30 per cent in pre-market trading on Monday.
Mr Huang's departure on November 12 reverberated from Silicon Valley to Taipei and Washington.
A petition calling for his reinstatement attracted more than 180,000 signatures, including one from Elon Musk.
Trump says US government found no evidence of unlawful conduct
US President Donald Trump, who last week urged Nvidia to reinstate Mr Huang, said the board had made the "correct and proper decision".
"Congratulations to NVIDIA on having made, what I consider to be, an absolutely GREAT decision," he wrote on Truth Social.
"The country needs Jensen's leadership at Nvidia in order to ensure the success and prosperity of not only the company, but the American economy.
"The Attorney-General of the United States has confirmed that the US government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.
"On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen and he is a great patriot and national treasure."
Mr Trump later told CNBC he spoke to Mr Huang on Sunday night and then "convinced" Nvidia's board to reinstate him as chief executive after providing "assurance".
Tech trillionaire Elon Musk also said "the mistake has been corrected".
Mr Trump bought as much as $US265,000 ($400,000) in Nvidia stock and sold as much as $US15,000 worth of the stock in September, weeks before reports surfaced of Mr Huang's ties to the alleged Chinese spy.
US politicians raise security concerns
US politicians have also been asking questions about Mr Trump's knowledge of Mr Huang's personal life.
Democratic representative Raja Krishnamoorthi, who is also a member of the House Intelligence Committee, said the move was "outrageous".

"We still don't know what information Chinese intelligence may have obtained from him, and neither the government nor Nvidia's own board has completed its investigation."
Democratic senator Elizabeth Warren, who is set to chair the Banking Committee starting in January, called the decision "reckless".
She went on to say the board still owed Congress answers about the company's compliance with US export controls.
"Jensen Huang's return only increases the urgency of passing bipartisan export control bills into law and making sure Nvidia does not lobby to kill that legislation and put our national security at risk," she said.
Illinois Governor JB Pritzker, a potential 2028 presidential contender, accused the company and administration of showing "greed and lack of accountability".
Even Republicans questioned the move.
"Jensen Huang was compromised and exploited by a Chinese spy," outgoing Republican congressman Don Bacon alleged on X, saying Nvidia should have ignored the administration's calls for his reinstatement.
Texas Republican senator John Cornyn also questioned the board's move: "No national security risk? Based on what?" he posted on X.
Investigations continue
Multiple investigations of the company are underway, including by the US Securities and Exchange Commission and the Department of Justice, related to everything from potential accounting issues to alleged chip smuggling.
Mr Trump said Nvidia should continue its internal investigation, including the company's compliance with US export controls.
The Nvidia board said the committee's investigation into whether Mr Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort would proceed to conclusion and it would cooperate with ongoing government inquiries.
It also said it would recruit for a new position of chief compliance officer to oversee compliance across Nvidia.
The company's chief financial officer, Colette Kress, had been serving as interim chief executive officer.
