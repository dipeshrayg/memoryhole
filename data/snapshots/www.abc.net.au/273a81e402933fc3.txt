Jensen Huang, the co-founder of global artificial intelligence giant Nvidia, has resigned as chief executive effective immediately.
The news comes days after the California-based company launched an independent investigation following Mr Huang's acknowledgement that he had an affair with a Chinese business associate.
According to The New York Times, US intelligence determined that the woman - former Megaspeed managing director Alice Huang - was gathering information for China.
Nvidia's board of directors held an emergency meeting that reportedly continued into the early hours of Thursday morning, local time.
Following the meeting, the company released a statement saying Mr Huang had resigned as president, chief executive officer and as a member of the board.
The company's Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress, who has worked for NVIDIA since 2013, will serve as Interim Chief Executive Officer until a permanent replacement is found.
"NVIDIA's strength has always come from its people, and together with our leadership team, I am committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years," Ms Kress said in a statement.
The company thanked Mr Huang for his "immeasurable contributions" to not only NVIDIA but the wider industry.
NVIDIA's new board chair Harvey Jones said he expected "the company's operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption".
Shares of Nvidia, one of the largest companies in the world, plunged 13 per cent following the announcement.
The company and Mr Huang are also facing a proposed class action accusing them of defrauding shareholders by concealing Mr Huang's ties with Ms Huang - a Chinese citizen and former executive at the Singapore-based data centre company.
US President Donald Trump earlier this week confirmed the FBI was looking into the affair, and described Mr Huang as a "very honourable man".
The company has since become the top supplier of AI chips at the centre of the global AI boom and US-China technology rivalry.
But US House Speaker Mike Johnson said he was "in favour" of limiting exports of semiconductor chips to China.
"And I think there's a developing consensus in Congress that is an important position to take. Especially in the light of recent developments, it makes sense," he said.
Mr Huang's departure could leave a leadership vacuum at the roughly $US4 trillion ($5.8 trillion) chipmaker he co-founded and built over more than three decades.
Mr Huang, 63, has no obvious successor and owns about 3.6 per cent of Nvidia's stock.
Asian shares also fell on Thursday as investors kept a nervous eye on what action Nvidia's board would take.
Nvidia stock has shed 24 per cent of its value over the past month, wiping around $US1.4 trillion ($2 trillion) off its market capitalisation.
Nvidia has said it had "no reason to believe" that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but that its inquiry would examine all available information.
Singapore bans Alice Huang over 'possible offences'
Alice Huang was banned from re-entering Singapore after she left the country in February, Singapore's home affairs ministry has confirmed.
The ministry said Ms Huang, who is a former executive at Nvidia customer Megaspeed, was investigated for possible offences including buying servers that were exported via Singapore to Malaysia without entering the Singapore market.
She was investigated "for suspected involvement in the possible offences in Singapore, given her position as then-CEO, chairperson and one of the key controllers of Megaspeed," the ministry said in a statement. It did not investigate espionage.
But the ministry said there was insufficient evidence to find Ms Huang had committed any offences and she was issued a warning.
In its first statement since the scandal broke, Singapore-headquartered Megaspeed International said on Thursday that it took its obligation to comply with US export control laws "extremely seriously" and was "committed to working constructively and transparently" with the US government.
