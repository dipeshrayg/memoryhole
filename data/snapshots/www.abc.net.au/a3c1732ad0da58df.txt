In another incident in the community, officers and a teenage girl allegedly had arrows fired at them.
In nearby Peppimenarti, NT Police have arrested a man who allegedly fired a rifle in the air during a fight.
Northern Territory Police have reported a group of up to 100 people armed with axes, spears and crossbows have been involved in fresh unrest in the remote Northern Territory community of Wadeye.
Police were notified of the fighting at the intersection of Bunduck and Mollinjin streets just before 3pm on Monday.
They said a victim in their 20s was allegedly struck in the abdomen with a metal bar, while others reported being threatened and having arrows fired in their direction.
Police said they had identified five males aged between 18 and 37 as the main culprits allegedly involved, and investigations were continuing.
The unrest comes after an incident last week where two people allegedly fired arrows at a teenage girl and police officers in the community.
Police said they were called to a home about 10pm on Friday after receiving reports a 21-year-old man had threatened his 18-year-old partner with a bow.
When police arrived and were speaking with the 18-year-old, two arrows were allegedly fired in the direction of her and the officers.
Police took shelter, and when the man fled the scene, they took the victim to a safe location before calling in other officers to help the search for the alleged offender.
Officers then arrested a 21-year-old man and a 14-year-old boy and seized a bow and several arrows.
Wadeye, about 420km south-west of Darwin and the NT's second-largest Indigenous community, has been at the centre of violent unrest for some months now.
Over the space of several weeks in September, groups of up to 150 people armed with crossbows, machetes and spears were fighting in community, attacking homes and police, burning cars and seriously injuring one man.
That violence was part of a decades-old feud between some of Wadeye's 24 clans, and elders told the ABC they feared it was only a matter of time before someone was killed.
Man allegedly fires rifle, crashes stolen car
Separately, in another West Daly community, a 24-year-old alleged fugitive armed with a rifle has been arrested in an extensive operation involving tactical police officers.
In a statement on Tuesday afternoon, police reported an alleged fugitive had been involved in a dangerous driving incident earlier this month in the remote community of Peppimenarti, about 320km south-west of Darwin.
"Police will allege the offender attended a residence, challenged an occupant to a fight and produced a rifle before firing three shots into the air," police said in a statement.
"He then drove away at speed in a dangerous manner while three adult passengers were travelling unsecured in the tray of the vehicle.
"A short time later, the three passengers were thrown from the vehicle, suffering injuries.
"One woman suffered fractured ribs and a collapsed lung, requiring medical treatment."
NT Police said the man had been on the run since his bail was revoked earlier this year.
"As part of an extensive investigation, an arrest plan was developed and led by detectives ... supported by local and specialist police resources," police said.
Before dawn on November 13, members of the Territory Response Group arrested the man in Peppimenarti.
He's since been charged with three counts of recklessly endangering life, and multiple stolen vehicle and dangerous driving charges, and been remanded in custody.
