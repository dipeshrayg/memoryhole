Rugby League World Cup: New Zealand Kiwis defeat Tonga - blog and scores
Kalyn Ponga has returned to the field 23 days after breaking his arm to lead New Zealand to its first Rugby League World Cup final since 2013.
Ponga, who suffered the broken arm in the tournament's opening match against Australia, had three try assists and a try-saving tackle to inspire the Kiwis to a 34-10 win.
But the highlight of the contest came before the opening whistle, when both sets of players engaged in one of the great moments in rugby league.
After New Zealand performed its Haka, in reply to the Sipi Tau from Tonga, the two sides stared each other down as time stood still.
A packed house at Hunter Stadium in Newcastle got louder as the stare-down lengthened, a precursor to the historic semifinal that was to come.
Kiwi centre Casey McLean, who scored a crucial second-half try for his country, told ABC Sport the pre-match stare-down was a special moment that had him ready to give everything for his teammates on Saturday.
"We have such mana boys in our team that are leaders," he said.
"We've just got to follow them, especially myself.
"I can learn so much off them, not just about being a player, but [also] my culture. They're front runners for Māori boys and Kiwi boys."
Ponga's impact on the match was immediate, setting up back-rower Briton Nikora for the opening try in the third minute.
New Zealand dominated the opening quarter, before Tonga winger Jojo Fifita made a line break from his own goal-line, setting up a break down the left wing by Tolutau Koula, who then threw it back to Fifita for the length-of-the-field try.
The belief in the Tongan camp grew, and they looked destined to score again when forward Demitric Vaimauga was clear and only had the much smaller Ponga to beat.
But Ponga produced a stunning try-saving tackle, one-on-one, to stop the much larger man.
"It just shows how much this Kiwi jersey means to him," McLean said.
"To be able to put on a performance like that, it just shows how much of an amazing person he is."
Just one minute later, Fifita was in for a second to give Tonga the lead off a perfectly executed scrum play.
But a mistake from fullback Lehi Hopoate allowed Kiwi Dane Gagai to score and give the Kiwis a 12-10 half-time lead.
Down by just two points, Tonga had every reason to believe they could advance to the World Cup final for the first time.
But a significant change in weather at half-time meant they played into a howling headwind for the second 40 minutes.
Hopoate was peppered with high kicks he could not reach as the wind made predicting the landing near impossible, while halfback Isaiya Katoa struggled to kick the ball much further than 30 metres at the end of sets.
It meant the Kiwis were able to camp themselves in the Tongan half of the field for the entire second half, wearing them down and scoring four tries to seal a 24-point win.
"No excuses, you've got to take your hats off to the Kiwis," Katoa said.
"The way they played footy tonight is the way you play in these conditions."
New Zealand will face either Australia or Samoa in next Sunday's final at Lang Park in Brisbane.
