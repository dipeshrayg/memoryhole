Benjamin Netanyahu's main rival is demanding an investigation into the Israeli Prime Minister's Office, following explosive allegations it may have fabricated claims of electoral fraud.
Gadi Eisenkot, who is currently steering negotiations to form a new coalition government, said Mr Netanyahu posed "a tangible, direct and immediate threat to Israeli democracy".
For weeks since Israel's inconclusive election on October 27, Mr Netanyahu and his Likud party have been claiming the election was "stolen", describing the surge in support for Arab-Israeli parties at the ballot box as illegitimate and the result of voters being paid off.
On Sunday night, Israel's Channel 12 reported that the prime minister's chief of staff, Ido Norden had contacted the Israeli police commissioner's office with a complaint about members of the United Arab List (UAL), the party often referred to as Ra'am in Hebrew.
The network said Mr Norden's source was actually a former employee of the Prime Minister's Office.
The intervention led to the homes of three UAL members being raided on the morning of the election without search warrants and the men arrested on allegations of electoral fraud, after envelopes of cash were discovered.
Mr Netanyahu used the arrests to ramp up his rhetoric on election day, accusing Arab-Israeli or Palestinian-Israelis of trying to steal the election.
But the men were released days later.
It has also been reported that the head of Israeli intelligence agency Shin Bet, David Zini, tried to wire-tap three members of the other Arab-Israeli party, the Joint List ahead of the election amid suspicion of foreign interference in the poll.
His request was denied by Israel's Attorney-General, a public servant and the country's legal advisor, and his agency failed to provide more evidence to back up the demand.
Reports have also suggested Mr Zini, who was hand-picked to be Shin Bet chief by Mr Netanyahu, wanted some Arab-Israeli politicians arrested on election day.
Eisenkot calls for Netanyahu to stand down as PM
The Netanyahu government has been highly critical of the Attorney-General, Gali Baharav-Miara, relentless accusing her of blocking its agenda.
Gadi Eisenkot said the affair was further reason why Mr Netanyahu should not "remain at the helm of government for even one more minute".
"Under no circumstances must Israel be dragged into another election with him as prime minister.
"It is clear that as long as he remains in office, Israel can no longer expect elections that are fair and lawful."
Another member of Mr Eisenkot's Yashar party, former Shin Bet director Yoram Cohen, wrote to the Attorney-General on Monday, local time, demanding a criminal investigation into whether the police and intelligence agencies were being used by Mr Netanyahu to undermine the election.
"The fear that the state's enforcement powers were used to advance political interests in the midst of election day requires a thorough, independent and impartial investigation," Mr Cohen wrote.
Former prime minister Yair Lapid described the situation as an "orchestrated" attempt by Mr Netanyahu's office and his Likud party to steal the election.
"As more and more details come to light, it becomes clear," he posted on X.
"This wasn't an attempt to disrupt the elections. It was an attempted coup."
Likud described Mr Eisenkot's comments as "outrageous incitement" and "appalling".
"While the prime minister extends a hand for unity, Eisenkot and his colleagues issue inflammatory, incendiary statements every day in venomous language," it said.
"Eisenkot is inciting against the chief of staff, a law-abiding citizen who passed information to the police about suspicions of a serious criminal offence, as he was obligated to do."
The United Arab List and the Joint List secured 19 out of the 120 seats in the Israeli parliament, the Knesset.
Israel Police launches internal probe
Israel Police has confirmed an internal investigation is underway, with the force's deputy commissioner looking at "all systemic, organisational and command-related aspects concerning the allegations made against Israel Police".
"It should be emphasised that all police actions, including the activity carried out on the morning of election day, were conducted with a commitment to transparency and accountability to the public, and with the aim of safeguarding the integrity of the elections," a spokesperson said in a statement.
Ido Norden, Mr Netanyahu's chief of staff, has threatened Channel 12 with a defamation suit worth 2.5 million shekels (1.14 million AUD).
Mr Norden's lawyers claimed threats had been made against his life since the report went to air.
"[The report] constitutes an act of fraud on a national scale, masquerading as "journalistic work," and represents a new and dangerous low in the contamination of public democratic discourse in Israel.
"Your decision to disseminate these baseless, false claims-along with malicious lies-in the midst of an investigation into the conduct of the elections, is intended solely to exert an improper and unlawful influence on the outcome of the elections in Israel."
