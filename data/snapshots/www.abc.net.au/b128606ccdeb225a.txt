In short:
Ukrainian drones and missiles have struck a vital bridge linking Russia to the occupied Crimean Peninsula.
Volodymyr Zelenskyy has described the operation as "unprecedented".
Moscow's forces, meanwhile, fired ballistic missiles and drones at Kyiv, hitting two of the Ukrainian capital's main bridges.
Ukraine has struck a critical bridge linking southern Russia and occupied Crimea for a second straight day, causing what appears to be serious damage.
President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack was retaliation for Russian strikes on Ukrainian infrastructure.
Videos and images, widely shared by Ukrainian officials, appeared to show a section of the Crimean Bridge, also known as the Kerch Bridge, collapsed into the water.
Mr Zelenskyy said the strike followed an "unprecedented" operation that methodically destroyed Russian air defences, radar systems and electronic warfare capabilities.
Mykola Lukashuk, head of the Dnipropetrovsk Regional Council, wrote on X: "The road arch of the crossing lies in the water."
Sergei Aksyonov, Crimea's Russian-installed governor, said two people were killed in the strike and traffic across the 19-kilometre road and rail bridge had been suspended.
"Russia strikes our infrastructure every day, including bridges," Mr Zelenskyy said in a post on X.
"We are ... reminding Russia that war will always return to where it began."
Ukraine's General Staff said the arch and a span of the bridge's road section had collapsed after being dealt powerful blows, but that the rail part of the bridge was still standing albeit with a damaged arch.
The Crimean bridge is a flagship project closely associated with President Vladimir Putin after Russia annexed the peninsula from Ukraine in 2014.
Dmitry Peskov, Mr Putin's spokesman, said the president had been informed about the attack.
Russian investigators said the bridge's structure had been damaged, while Mr Peskov said a repair plan would be prepared and that the bridge would emerge stronger than before.
Largest Ukrainian drone attack
The overnight attack was part of what state news agencies called the largest Ukrainian drone attack on Russia since the start of the conflict in 2022.
Russia's Defence Ministry said it shot down 1,441 drones over 22 regions, surpassing the previous record of 1,336 set on Monday.
Air defences repelled a major Ukrainian drone attack on the industrial cities of Ufa and Salavat in Russia's Bashkortostan region, home to several oil refineries, state media reported, citing local officials.
Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin said that around 800 Ukrainian drones had flown towards the surrounding Moscow region, 321 of which had been brought down en route to the capital.
The scale and frequency of Ukrainian drone attacks have increased as Russia has intensified its own drone and missile campaign in a grinding war of attrition, with both sides targeting energy infrastructure and other strategic assets.
Attacks have continued despite US-brokered peace talks in Abu Dhabi last week, the first in nine months, and separate meetings between US envoys and Mr Putin and Mr Zelenskyy.
Kyiv's bridges struck
In Kyiv, witnesses reported a series of loud explosions in the capital overnight into Tuesday local time.
Vitali Klitschko, Kyiv's mayor, said Russia struck the capital's North and South bridges, an assertion later confirmed by Moscow, and partially damaged the metro system.
The attacks caused some power and water outages and prompted transport restrictions, he said.
Russia has targeted Kyiv's bridges before, causing widespread disruption.
Overnight Russian strikes on energy infrastructure left consumers without power in eight Ukrainian regions, Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said.
Outages were reported in Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia, Mykolaiv, Odesa, Sumy, Chernihiv and Chernivtsi regions.
"The situation is extremely difficult in the Dnipropetrovsk and Zaporizhzhia regions. Since last week, Russia has been continuously attacking energy facilities in the Dnipro region," Mr Nekrasov said.
In other attacks, local officials on Telegram said three people were injured in the Cherkasy region. They said Kharkiv, Ukraine's second-biggest city, Dnipropetrovsk and Zaporizhzhia, and other regions were also targeted.
Russia's defence ministry confirmed the strikes, saying on Telegram its forces had hit transport, military and energy facilities in Kyiv, Zaporizhzhia, Cherkasy and Dnipropetrovsk, along with port infrastructure and a cargo ship.
Both sides deny deliberately targeting civilians.
