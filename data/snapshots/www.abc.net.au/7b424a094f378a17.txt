Rain ruins day one of South Africa-Australia second Test despite Scott Boland double-strike
Rain has almost entirely washed out the first day of Australia's second Test against South Africa in Gqeberha, but not before some Scott Boland magic gave the tourists something to celebrate.
Boland, included in this Test ahead of spinner Nathan Lyon, struck twice in two balls during a brief window of play to leave South Africa 2-20 after 12.2 overs.
Boland's inclusion meant Australia went with four seamers for the match under heavy skies and on a green pitch which looked likely to provide encouragement for the quick bowlers.
After Pat Cummins won the toss and chose to bowl, a first batch of rain delayed the start by two hours before another took the players off after only a handful of overs.
Play resumed for another short while after a belated lunch break, during which time Boland dismissed Aiden Markram (11) and David Bedingham (0) in successive deliveries.
Boland's mastery was on full display as he bowled Markram with a ball which seamed back sharply into the batter, before having Bedingham caught behind from a ball which moved in the opposite direction.
Test debutant Marques Arkerman will resume on 5* alongside South Africa's captain Temba Bavuma (4*) on day two, though the forecast suggests more weather frustration could be ahead.

Let's learn about St George's Park
There's nothing I like better than a classic cricket ground, and St George's Park definitely fits that bill.
St George's Park is the oldest Test venue in South Africa, hosting the 31st Test in history back in March 1889.
It was the first Test to ever be held outside of Australia or England and just the seventh Test venue ever used after the MCG, The Oval, Lord's, SCG, Adelaide Oval and Old Trafford.
England won that first Test by 8 wickets, the match finishing inside two days.
England were the first four opponents to play at the ground, and won all of those matches, which were played in 1896, 1914 and 1949.
Australia played there for the first time in 1950 and won by an innings and 259 runs, Arthur Morris (157), Neil Harvey (116) and Lindsay Hassett (167) all scoring big in their first innings total of 7-549 declared.
Keith Miller than took 4-42 to help bowl the South Africans out for 158, before they were bowled out again following on for 132.
South Africa won their first match at the ground in 1954, beating New Zealand by 5 wickets and, in 1957, beat England there for the first time by 58 runs.
The lowest team total ever scored at the ground was 30 all out by South Africa against England in 1896, with the next lowest coming much more recently, Zimbabwe bowled out for just 68 in 30.1 overs in 2017, and Bangladesh falling for 80 in 2022.
Herschelle Gibbs' 196 against India in 2001 is the highest ever score at the venue, while Keshav Maharaj possesses the best innings figures of any bowler in recent years with a 7-40 against Bangladesh in 2022.

Renners's revival
It was hard not to be heartened by the superb return to the Test team of Matt Renshaw last week.
A first innings score of 190, backed up by 82 in the second innings helped him earn player of the match honours and should earn the 30-year-old an extended spell in the line up.
Australia had been in a precarious position at 6-124 on the first day, but Renshaw's stunning, gritty score rescued the Aussies with a proper display of batting over a dig that lasted close to eight hours.
The Yorkshire-born Queenslander has been in and out the Test team since making his debut a decade ago against the Proteas in Adelaide, playing just 16 Tests.
He averages 32.26 as an opener in Test matches, compared to his first class career average of 38.49.
His overall Test average is 37.00.
Given he also took 2 wickets with his bowling, Renshaw achieved a never-before seen statistical quirk in Durban.
According to Ric Finlay, Renshaw is the first Australian to score 250 runs and take two wickets in a Test match.
The closest anyone else has come was Steve Waugh against Sri Lanka in Adelaide when he scored 231 runs and took 4 wickets over 30 years ago.
'Not a happy change room' after first Test disaster for Proteas
So what went wrong for South Africa last time out?
According to their skipper, they simply did not show enough fight and were let down by their mental aptitude.
"It's disappointing. It's not the way you want to start a series, right? Any kind of series, let alone one against a top outfit like Australia," Proteas captain Temba Bavuma said after the first Test
"There are obvious things that we could definitely improve on, whether it's from the batting point of view or the bowling but yeah, it's not a happy change room."
South Africa negated losing the toss, often crucial at the Durban venue, by having the tourists floundering on the first day, only for Matt Renshaw's 190 to take the game away.
"You have to praise the Australians the way they hung in when we had them 6-124," Bavuma said.
"As a bowling attack, you're betting on yourself to restrict them to less than 250 and for them to go and get that big score, I think mentally took a little bit out of us."
When South Africa went out to bat, they were faced with the same favourable batting conditions.
"We weren't able to make the most of it," Bavuma added.
"Australia's lower order spent time at the crease and really showed what was possible once the ball got soft.
"In the second innings they played like a team that was in front. They put us under pressure, took command of the game, and in our second innings, there was just not enough fight and mental aptitude from us."
Aiden Markram was the only South African batsman to score more than 50 in the match, batting for most of Tuesday as he tried to keep a rearguard fight intact before being dismissed near the end for 75.
It was South Africa's first Test since their 2-0 series win in India last November. So were the Proteas just "rusty".

Welcome to St George's Park
We're in the Eastern Cape today, in the city of Gqeberha.
Prior to 2021, Gqeberha was known by its colonial name, Port Elizabeth, or PE.
Now though, it is known by its Xhosa name.
This is, in fact, the first time Australia will play in the city under its new name.
All seven Tests Australia has played in the city have been at St George's Park, where today's match is set to take place.
South Africa has won four of the seven matches they've played against Australia here, including the last two.
Overall, the Proteas have won five of their last seven Tests at the ground, winning 15 of the 33 they've played here since 1889.

Hi stumcin.
So, the name was changed from Port Elizabeth on the advice of the Department of Sport, Arts and Culture.
It was actually part of a sweeping raft of changes to locations across the Republic.
The main reason was to remove colonial and apartheid-era legacies and recognise the indigenous heritage of the area: Gqeberha is the Xhosa name for the Baakens River that flows through the city.
Port Elizabeth was founded in 1820 by British settlers and named in memory of the late wife of the Cape Colony's then governor.
It wasn't a universally popular decision, the BBC reported that 32,000 people signed a petition to keep Port Elizabeth.
The pronunciation is a tough one for non-Xhosa speakers though.
Xhosa uses a click sound in its lexicon, which is the sound that the "Gqe" represents.
So the pronunciation is <click> bear-gha, with the g sound at the end a guttural g.

Stop changing the batting order
Australia has chopped and changed the batting order a few times of late, with a rotating cast of middle order batters tried and discarded up top.
Former Australia captain Steve Waugh has led the calls for the team's selectors to stop changing it up and to let the players settle in fixed roles, despite some questionable returns in Durban.
"They've got to pick and stick now," Waugh said.
"Don't mess around with that now. [Renshaw] has been successful, and he's comfortable, so I'd keep him there, for sure."
Makeshift opener Travis Head scored just 9 and 11 at Kingsmead, with the returning Nic Maddinson managing a 50-minute, 33-ball duck before a far improved 48 in the second innings.
Meanwhile, Matt Renshaw, who has opened for Australia and Queensland in the past, totalled match-winning scores of 190 and 82 at number five.
"It's got to be pretty compelling to move him [Renshaw] from there," Pat Cummins said after the first Test.
"We're always working out the six batters, or seven plus [Alex Carey] and how is that going to function as a whole?
"I still think he [Renshaw] can be a great opener ... we're pretty certain that we like Marn [Labuschagne] at number 3, Smithy at number 4, and then, the six and seven felt pretty locked up.
"I think bringing in Maddo, we really like the idea of bringing that flexibility, that unlocks a lot [such as] Trav back to number 5, Renners down to number 5, even Maddo himself at five.
"We haven't had that option [in] the last few years where we feel like we've got plenty of options at the top.
"We're very big on that flexibility. I think having the structure that we do now with a few guys that can open, that's really unlocked that."
Matthew Wade said the players were happy to be flexible.
"If the conditions tell us it's time to change the batting order, we'll make that change," he said.
"We've seen over the last little bit that we've been able to adapt really quickly and more importantly the players take it on so easily - and credit to them."

South Africa make changes
After a pretty comprehensive 214-run defeat last time out, South Africa made a couple of tweaks to their squad, calling up Matthew Breetzke and dropping Tristan Stubbs.
That has meant a batting order reshuffle for today's second Test.
Proteas coach Shukri Conrad said on Wednesday that there was no need for knee-jerk reactions to the defeat and that his team just "need to get bloody better" this week.
However, on Thursday he announced that Ryan Rickelton will be the wicketkeeper, which is likely to mean Kyle Verreynne, who has not scored a Test 50 in his last 13 innings, will be dropped from the team, despite still being in the squad.
Breetzke, who has played two previous Tests for South Africa, in Bangladesh in late 2024 and Zimbabwe last year and has a first-class average of 37, last played a first-class match in February.
He did score a century in the first One Day International against the Aussies last month though.
"Matt has been part of the squad before and the fact that his ODI form is as hot as it is gave him an inside lane and also, more importantly, the attitude and the character Matt brings, and has, will go a long way," Conrad said.
"With Ricks [Rickelton] taking the gloves, there are obviously decisions to be made over whether he does move down the order or whether he can continue to open the batting and take the gloves, which he has done before.
"We'll have a look at what the conditions are like, but there are a few things to consider before play starts."
That puts Breetzke in contention to open with Aiden Markram.
