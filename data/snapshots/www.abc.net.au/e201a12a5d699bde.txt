In short:
The latest Lowy Institute report found the US has lost ground in power and influence across Asia.
South-East Asia faces mounting geopolitical pressure, with diplomatic influence declining across the region.
India has strengthened its overall influence while Australia's comprehensive power has dipped slowly.
The United States has lost a significant amount of power and influence in Asia this year, research has shown, with China gaining ground.
The Lowy Institute's 2026 Asia Power Index found that the Trump administration's war with Iran and other unpopular policy decisions had contributed to a record decline in US diplomatic influence in Asia.
China, by contrast, held steady across most measures, while strengthening its military capability, cultural influence and resilience, adding to its overall power in the region.
China ranked first in the Index's measures of Economic Relationship and Diplomatic Influence, ahead of the US in both.
While Washington remains the dominant power in the region according to the index, the gap between the US and China's military capabilities is now at its narrowest point on record.
"This narrowing divide owes more to Washington's missteps than to any advances on Beijing's part," authors Hunter Marston and Jack Sato wrote.
"Respondents to the Asia Power Index survey saw the United States as distracted and disengaged in Asia."
Australia's comprehensive power score dipped slightly from last year, and it fell one place to sixth, behind Russia.
The report credited Prime Minister Anthony Albanese with advancing Australia's economic interests but rated him less effective at furthering diplomatic interests in Asia.
US loses influence in region as China closes gap
The index found the Trump administration's diversion of resources away from the Pacific to the Middle East coincided with deeper defence ties among US treaty allies in the region.
"The United States' unresolved war with Iran has eroded US military capabilities by depleting its stockpile of long-range missiles, and profoundly undermined perceptions of US diplomatic influence both regionally and globally," the report said.
That, along with the US pressing countries to spend more on their own defence and calling for fewer joint military exercises with South Korea, left negative impressions of US leadership, the report said.
With the US recording declines in economic and military capabilities, the two superpowers - China and the US - are now almost tied in economic capability in the region.
"The gap between the two is now 5.3 points, the narrowest since the inception of the Index, but it's still a clear lead for the US," said Mr Sato, a data analyst and researcher on the Asia Power Index at the Lowy Institute.
He told the ABC that the narrowing comprehensive power scores reflected three factors.
"A series of missteps by a more transactional US", conflicts elsewhere "drawing US attention and its defence networks away from the Indo-Pacific", and China's fast military expansion decreasing the US lead in Military Capability "from 27 points in 2018 to just over 15".
"The narrowing gap reflects a decline in US power, rather than a rise in China's,"
Mr Sato said.
South-East Asia under pressure
It was a tough year for countries in the Asia-Pacific region, with the disruption of global energy supplies from the Middle East conflict and Strait of Hormuz closure hitting the region particularly hard.
Higher fuel and fertiliser prices constrained food and agricultural production, putting more political pressure on leaders.
As a result, government effectiveness declined across nearly all South-East Asian nations, with Singapore, Myanmar and Laos the exceptions.
Diplomatic influence also fell in nearly every country covered by the index.
Countries also struggled to navigate US tariffs, rising tensions in the South China Sea and ongoing competition between the United States and China.
Indonesia saw the greatest drop in diplomatic influence, a decline the report said largely reflected the country's "haphazard approach to foreign policy" under President Prabowo Subianto.
India now a major power in Asia
In a year when most countries failed to gain diplomatic influence, India became more influential overall in Asia.
The country's Prime Minister Narendra Modi is now considered to be the second-most effective regional leader, behind Chinese President Xi Jinping.
"For the assessment of Modi's regional leadership, it's largely stability at a time of upheaval," Mr Sato said.
"A key factor we've found is continuity. Both Prime Minister Modi and External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar have established themselves in their roles."
India has more political influence in the region now than when the index began eight years ago, although China's power remains 80 per cent higher than India's.
"India did struggle in 2026 with many internal challenges, which saw its scores for Economic Capability and Resilience decrease," Mr Sato said.
He also said the country's Diplomatic Influence score dipped slightly, "but by much less than others in the region".
"India has a very strong demographic and economic outlook, with, comfortably, the third-highest Future Resources score in the region."
