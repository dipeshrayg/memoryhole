Ipswich City Council voted to direct a councillor to make a public apology and undertake training and counselling after he made comments including that a female contractor was part of the "school short skirt brigade".
That was one of three allegations against Cr Andrew Antoniolli outlined in an investigation summary tabled at the council meeting today.
Cr Antoniolli excused himself from the debate, which was held in closed session, and the vote due to his conflict of interest.
The ABC has contacted Cr Antoniolli for comment.
Investigation summary outlines three allegations
The complaint was made by Ipswich City Council CEO Sonia Cooper to the Office of the Independent Assessor in February and then referred to the council for investigation.
The investigation summary stated the first allegation was that, on or around January 28, 2026, at a meeting with a council employee and representatives of a contractor, Cr Antoniolli referred to a female contractor as "being part of the school short-skirt brigade".
The second allegation was that, at the same meeting, when introduced to a female contractor, Cr Antoniolli "looked her up and down and said words to the effect that 'women do not normally work in facilities management'".
The third allegation was that, on or around January 29, at a councillor workshop at the Nicholas Street Precinct, in the presence of the council CEO and fellow councillors, Cr Antoniolli queried "whether they could find someone with Queensland Rail experience that had 'slept with the boss' when working at Queensland Rail to assist in dealing with property tenure issues".
The council spent multiple hours debating the item in a closed session before coming back to vote unanimously that "on the balance of probabilities" the first two allegations had been proven, and that Cr Antoniolli had admitted to the third.
It also required him to make a public apology and undertake training and counselling.
Mayor Teresa Harding did not speak in the public meeting on the matter.
Deciding on fellow councillor 'difficult'
Division 2 councillor Paul Tully said the council had "a very thorough discussion of the matters".
"It's probably been one of the most difficult decisions or recommendations for this matter to be raised," he said.
"I will say that the legislation has changed from the 1st of July. If these matters had arisen after the 1st of July, they would not have been referred back to council.
"[This is] probably the last matter in Queensland that will go to any of the 77 councils in Queensland for a decision.
"We've evaluated the evidence, evaluated matters which are in detail over a period of hours. It's not just a rushed decision.
"And in relation to the recommendation about the training and an apology, I think that is appropriate in all of the circumstances."
Concern some will 'misuse' decision
Division 1 councillor Jacob Madsen said the state legislation should not "ever include forcing councillors to make determinations about their fellow councillors".
"I gravely hope that people don't sort of misuse this to treat the subject councillor poorly," he said
"I do genuinely worry with how this may be potentially misused online and in media to do him harm."
Deputy Mayor Nicole Jonic, Division 4 councillor David Martin and Division 1 councillor Pye Augustine all echoed the comments that it had been unpleasant to make the determination, but praised the council's robust debate and unanimous decision.
The full investigation report, redacted to protect the identities of those involved in the investigation, will be published soon.
