Inexperienced Australia have pulled off a record MCG run chase to beat world number one England in the first of five T20 Internationals on home soil.
In front of 39,289 fans on Saturday night, Australia snapped England's seven-match T20 winning run by chasing down 188.
Young all-rounder Nikhil Chaudhary announced himself on the international stage, blasting a brilliant 66 off 38 balls and taking a handy 2-21 with the ball.
He set up Australia's chase before Jack Edwards (25 off 11 deliveries) landed the plane, hitting a four on the second-last ball to give his team a three-wicket win.
Debutant Sam Harper (32 off 15) and captain Mitch Marsh (37 off 15) were other key contributors with the bat.
Missing a raft of their T20 regulars, Australia were uncharacteristically poor in the field early as England posted 9-187.
The hosts dropped five catches in the first seven overs.
While scooping up easy chances from openers Phil Salt (0) and Aneurin Donald (10 off 7), England captain Harry Brook was afforded lives on two by Jake-Fraser McGurk and three by Adam Zampa.
Left-arm quick Ben Dwarshuis (1-23 off two overs) put down a tough caught-and-bowled chance from Tom Banton on three, before Tim David dropped him again on 11 at long on.
Brook and Banton punished the Australians for their mistakes, pushing the run rate past 12 an over.
In his Australia debut, hometown hero Harper also put down a flying edge from Donald.
But the 29-year-old Victorian made up for it by pouching a fine edge to remove Brook for 65 off 28 while keeping to the stumps to Chaudhary.
Soon after, fellow debutant Lachlan Shaw put on his Superman cape with a diving catch to send Banton back to the sheds for 39 off 22.
Zampa then trapped Will Jacks in front cheaply for three and the runs started to dry up for England.
Shaw showed more poise in the outfield to claim the wicket of all-rounder Sam Curran (21 off 20), as did Joel Davies running around in the deep to remove Jordan Cox (27 off 20).
Jamie Overton and Liam Dawson (17 off 14) both fell in the last over as Australia fought back to restrict England well below 200.
