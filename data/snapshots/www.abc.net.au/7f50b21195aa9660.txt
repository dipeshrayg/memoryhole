Students in Victoria are still sitting exams, even as a message on the compromised VCAA website claims that exam papers were downloaded and distributed.
What's next?
The VCAA says it is conducting a forensic investigation to determine the extent of the attack.
The Victorian Curriculum and Assessment Authority (VCAA) says it's confident no exam papers were accessed or released after its website was hacked during the exam period.
The website remained down this morning after being hacked on Wednesday evening.
The hacker claimed to have downloaded 2026 exam papers and distributed them, but VCAA CEO Andrew Smith said there was no sign of this.
"We've worked overnight; there is no evidence that these exams have been leaked," he told 774 ABC Melbourne.
"The arrangements that are in place to secure them are well established arrangements, they're very stringent."
On Wednesday evening, the VCAA homepage displayed an error message that has since been replaced with a message saying "We'll be right back".
"Our site is currently undergoing maintenance," the website stated.
"We apologise for any inconvenience and appreciate your patience."
The message remained on the website on Thursday morning and a VCAA spokesperson confirmed the breach was under investigation.
"The VCAA is conducting a forensic investigation to determine the extent of the attack," they said in a statement.
"There is no indication that exam papers from today or previous weeks have been accessed."
Exams for students in Victoria are scheduled through to November 18.
Hacker claims to have distributed exam papers
The compromised version of the VCAA website included an expletive-riddled message critical of the VCAA's digital security standards and those of others, including providers of printing and legal services.
"The success of this campaign could not have been possible without all their help together," the hacker, who signed off as DaggerElite, wrote.
"I'm here to say a big THANK YOU to a much cooler group of people --- those who purchased an Official VCA 2026 Exam from me ahead of the Exam day this year.
"Thanks to the support of these awesome hundreds of students statewide, we will hopefully be able to prepare the exam pages to be sent out even earlier than we have been able to this year, so we can study the paper you will sit in 2027 well in advance!!"
The hacker claims they downloaded a copy of the 2026 exam papers from the printing provider's internal file servers and distributed copies using email details obtained from the legal service provider.
"And before anyone seeing this decides to go ask people on Reddit to share the papers for the next few exams, I would like to remind you that those holding a paper are very well aware that each sent copy is individualised with an identifiable signature embedded in it, and it's in their best interests to keep their papers to themselves," the text on the website said.
"This is DaggerElite, and it has been an absolute pleasure working with all of you this year."
In 2024 an exam blunder led to some questions being leaked in online practice material.
In April this year, the Victorian government announced more than $222 million to rebuild the VCAA with the aim of "safeguarding exams for the long-term".
