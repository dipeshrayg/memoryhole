A fire has swept through a confectionary factory in Indonesia's Banten province, leaving 34 people unaccounted-for, officials say.
The blaze broke out at the PT Fonusa Agung Mulia factory in the Serang district of Banten province, which borders the capital, Jakarta, at 5pm local time on Saturday.
Flames engulfed the three-storey factory, sending thick black smoke into the sky, television reports showed.
Debris littered the floor of the burnt-out structure, including twisted metal and blackened, corrugated iron roofing.
A company representative said on Sunday that 300 workers had been inside the factory compound.
The company's chief executive, Steven Wijaya, said as of Sunday afternoon, local time, 24 workers had returned home.
In a statement released late on Sunday, authorities said 63 people were believed to have been in the affected area when the fire broke out.
Of those, 29 were confirmed alive, including five hospitalised with injuries, while 34 workers remained missing.
Human remains were recovered by rescue teams and transferred to a hospital for identification.
Authorities said on Sunday it was too early to determine the death toll.
Authorities said the intensity of the fire had rendered the recovered victims unidentifiable via visual inspection, prompting efforts to collect DNA samples from relatives and personal belongings of the missing workers.
The cause of the fire was not immediately known. Survivors said the fire originated from a cooling unit on the third floor, local media reported.
Tubagus Mahfud, operations chief of the Serang fire department, said the blaze had been largely contained, but firefighters were still carrying out intensive cooling operations before they could access all parts of the factory.
He added that the remaining hotspots were concentrated in the central and rear sections of the building.
"We are conducting sweeps in the areas that have already been brought under control," Mr Mahfud said.
"Parts of the structure had collapsed, while poor lighting conditions required firefighters to proceed cautiously."
He also said the factory's production materials could cause the fire to reignite, making it critical to complete cooling operations.
Rizky Dwianto, an operations official at the Banten Search and Rescue Agency, said 15 fire trucks with water cannons were deployed to battle the flames in several parts of the facility more than 12 hours after the fire began.
A joint rescue team - including police, the military, the Indonesian Red Cross and volunteers - was at the scene.
"Authorities continue to coordinate with families and the company to determine the whereabouts of employees believed to have been inside the factory when the fire broke out," he said.
Anxious relatives waited for news outside the burnt-out factory.
The factory produces Milkita, an Indonesian brand of milk-based candies and snack products.
It is part of Indonesia's United Family Food company, founded in 1981, according to its website.
It produces consumer goods across Indonesia, employs about 5,000 people and operates from its national headquarters and four international offices, serving a market of more than 250 million consumers.
Indonesia has experienced a series of deadly industrial fires in recent years, raising concerns about workplace safety standards and emergency preparedness at manufacturing facilities.
