George Russell wins Formula 1 Azerbaijan Grand Prix, Oscar Piastri spins late to finish 14th
George Russell has claimed a nail-biting victory in Baku, holding off Max Verstappen by just 0.196 seconds to secure his third victory of the season.
Russell, who had never led a race from start to finish before in his career, narrowed the gap to his teammate Kimi Antonelli, who recovered from a 16th place start to finish fifth.
The Englishman was successfully holding Verstappen at arms length right up until the final moments of the last lap, when waved yellow flags for a Valtteri Bottas crash through the old town section of the track saw Russell lose power.
That allowed Verstappen to rapidly close the gap, but the Dutch four-times world champion ran out of time and had to settle for second.
"The first half of the race felt pretty plain sailing," Russell said.
"After the safety car I saw Max behind me, I knew he wasn't going to make it easy.
"He drove incredibly at the end of the race.
"When the yellow flag came out, I lifted off the power and, when I went back, I had no turbo, so then I had no power.
"Max nearly got me on the line, which would have been a real shame.
"It is amazing to be back on the top step of the podium."
Earlier, Oscar Piastri let a promising position slip, locking up and spinning onto the run off area during his 40th lap to drop from third to 15th.
The Australian eventually finished in 13th and cut a dejected figure on what was a disappointing day for McLaren.
"I need to go and look because it certainly took me by surprise," Piastri said of his lock up.
"I didn't think I braked super late or super hard, just instantly locked both fronts.
"Just a disappointing end to the day, the first half of the race was very strong, so a shame to not have anything to show from it."
Piastri's teammate Lando Norris crashed out of the race on lap 36 when he was knocked out by the pair of Alpine cars, who collided at Turn One on a restart following a second safety car period of the race.
A turgid opening half of the race gave little indication of the drama to come after the safety car was first deployed.
Piastri, starting third, darted up the inside of Charles Leclerc on the opening corner and then led a procession of the two Red Bulls as Russell disappeared into the distance.
Verstappen appeared to be struggling with his medium tyres but did not allow his teammate Isack Hadjar to overtake, despite plenty of discussion taking place via the pit wall.
The action seemed set to continue in a static holding pattern until Alex Albon sent his car into the wall, precipitating the first safety car.
The restart caused chaos, Franco Colapinto locking up as he darted up the inside of Turn One, collecting teammate Pierre Gasly, who in turn knocked Norris out of the race.
Verstappen, who started eighth on the grid, had already overtaken Piastri at that point to move into second, but it was at the second restart that disaster befell the Australian, Piastri locking up his wheels while defending Hadjar and spinning into the run off area.

Closest Grand Prix finish since 2002
The final gap between first and second was 0.196 seconds.
The next closest Grand Prix finish was all the way back in 2002, when Rubens Barrichello beat Michael Schumacher at the US Grand Prix in Indianapolis by 0.011 in something of a staged finish.
That wasn't the tightest finish in F1 history.
In 1971 at the Italian Grand Prix, Peter Gethin beat Ronnie Peterson by 0.01 seconds.
