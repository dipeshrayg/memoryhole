In short:
Five people have died in separate road crashes on WA roads in less than 24 hours.
So far this year, there have been 126 road fatalities, higher than the 121 deaths recorded in the same 2025 period and above the 101 deaths of 2024.
A government minister has urged the public to play its part in improving road safety.
Five people have died in separate crashes in Western Australia in less than 24 hours, as the state's road toll continues to soar.
The first fatality happened on Friday afternoon when a male driver died after his car crashed into a tree on George Road in Wuraming, near Boddington in the Peel region, at about 1.10pm.
Just five minutes later in WA's Mid West, a 46-year-old man suffered the same fate in Cape Burney, near Geraldton, after the vehicle he was driving left Brand Highway and hit a tree.
On Friday night in Martin in Perth's south-east, a female passenger was killed after two vehicles collided at the intersection of Mills Road West on Tonkin Highway at about 10:45pm.
The 30-year-old woman was a passenger in a Toyota Hilux and was taken to Royal Perth Hospital where she later died.
The vehicle's 33-year-old male driver was taken to Armadale Hospital with serious injuries, while the 38-year-old male driver of the other vehicle was taken to Royal Perth Hospital.
In the state's north west this morning, Major Crash investigators travelled to Mount Anketell in Karratha, where police said two people are believed to have died in a crash on the North West Coastal Highway.
Police said at about 5:30am, two vehicles collided head-on near the intersection of De Witt Road, with one vehicle catching fire.
The driver of the second vehicle was critically injured and taken to hospital.
Police urge any witnesses or people with video footage of the four crashes, to contact Crime Stoppers.
Surging road toll
So far this year, there have been 126 road fatalities, higher than the 121 deaths recorded in the same 2025 period and above the 101 deaths of 2024.
WA minister Simone McGurk said the government was committed to improving road safety across the state but urged the public to also play its part.
"As a state government we've spent hundreds of millions of dollars trying to improve our roads ... but we also need the public to get on board," she said.
"I don't know the exact circumstances of these fatalities. I just urge people to think about fatigue, seatbelt, speed, distraction and also alcohol and other drugs.
"Please take care on our roads because the price of not being careful can be just too much."
Insurer RAC recently predicted the state's yearly road toll would exceed 200 deaths and said the fatalities had been at crisis levels for several years.
WA has the second highest road death toll of any state or territory in the nation, even though road deaths per capita have decreased over the last 10 years
