Russell Brand has been jailed for more than 10 years for rape and three counts of sexual assault.
The disgraced actor and comedian targeted four women between 2004 and 2009.
During sentencing, the judge referred to Brand as a "sexual predator".
British actor and comedian Russell Brand has been jailed for 10 years and six months for rape and sexual assault.

Judge Joel Bennathan said that as Brand was becoming famous he was "clever and, in many people's eyes, funny and charismatic" but he had also "become a sexual predator".
He added that Brand's celebrity status "protected" him until 2023, when journalists collected and published allegations about him that then "empowered a number of women to come forward and tell their stories".
After a four-week trial at London's Southwark Crown Court, Brand, who targeted four women between 2004 and 2009, was last week found guilty of the four charges, and acquitted of a further two counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault.
The 51-year-old had pleaded not guilty to all charges and claimed he never engaged in any non-consensual sexual activity.
When the sentence was read out Brand stood still and showed no emotion.
The comedian's wife Laura, who was sitting in the public gallery with a security guard and small group of friends, closed her eyes and put her head down.
As Brand was led away by court officers, he mouthed to his wife "I'll be OK".
Brand was one of the UK's highest-profile comedians, presenters and actors during the 2000s.
The rape conviction relates to a fan who was 21 years old when Brand raped her at his North London home in 2009.
They met after he picked her out of the crowd in the foyer following one of his live comedy shows at the Hammersmith Apollo.
During the trial, the woman told the court that what started as consensual sex, then changed suddenly into an activity she did not consent to.
She said she begged him to stop but he did not.
"She was a huge fan of yours, which you were happy to exploit," the judge said.

Judge labels Brand a sexual predator
The first of the sexual assault convictions relates to a woman in a bar in 2004.
"In your trial you said you had never touched a woman sexually without her consent, and that included this woman," the judge said to Brand.
"In the light of the jury's verdicts, that was one of a series of lies you told."
Brand was also convicted for sexually assaulting a woman at a radio station in front of his entourage.
"No doubt your companions thought it was outrageous laddish behaviour that only you could get away with: It was outrageous and possibly laddish, but also a criminal sexual offence, and as it turns out you haven't got away with it," the judge said.
The final conviction related to a woman he was working on a project with for Channel 4 in 2009.
Brand demanded a massage from her and put his head in her crotch while two of his colleagues watched on.
"The whole episode reeks of casual disrespect and your belief that fame meant you could act how you liked, when you liked, unhindered by the rules by which decent people live," the judge said.
By law in the UK, the women who made complaints about the comedian are all granted lifelong anonymity by the court.

Victim-survivors share ongoing impact
Before Judge Bennathan handed down his sentence, he considered victim impact statements from some of the women.
Ms C, who was groped by Brand at a London bar, attended court in person but read out her statement from behind a screen so she could only be seen by the judge.
"That undeserved feeling of shame and what if I'd done this or done that has been carried around inside me for over 20 years," she told the court.
"For many years, as his star was rising, it was impossible to escape seeing him all over the media, being celebrated for his disgusting behaviour against women like it was all a joke, and there were many times I felt visibly sick at the sight of him parading around thinking he can get away with anything."
She said during the trial she felt her character was "being torn apart on social media by people believing all of us women were making it up".
"There's been accusations and death threats from manic supporters of him on social media," she said.
Crown Prosecutor Riel Karmy-Jones read out statements sent in by three of the other women, including the rape victim, Ms E.
"The consequences of what happened have affected almost every aspect of my life," Ms E wrote in her statement.
"Even now, I continue to experience anxiety, difficulties with trust, reduced confidence and the lasting effects of trauma.
"What happened is not something that I have simply been able to move on from."
Brand's lawyer claims rape was 'momentary' failure to respect consent
Earlier, Brand's barrister Ian Winter KC told the court his client had abused the influence and power that came with fame, but was a "fundamentally different person today and has been for a very long time".
He asked the judge to consider that the rape was a "short" and "momentary" failure to respect her consent.
He said the case was having a devastating impact on Brand's family.
"Words are totally inadequate to convey the total devastation that [Brand's wife Laura] is experiencing," he said.
"They are wrestling with how to explain to a child, who's just turned 10, what has just happened here."
He also told the court Brand was previously a person of "positive, good character" who did a lot of charity work and had helped a number of alcoholics recover from their addictions.
The judge said he would reduce the sentence by a small amount because of Brand's good character in recent years.
"I am also very conscious that your imprisonment will deprive your children of the presence of their father. That sad loss to them, however, is caused by your serious offending," the judge said.
Brand is likely to only spend about seven years in prison as sentencing laws in the UK allow sexual offenders to be released after two-thirds of the sentence has been served.
Authorities praise 'powerful' women who came forward
Outside court after the sentencing, Metropolitan Police Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy said he hoped the sentence would provide "some closure" for the women who had shown "immense bravery, dignity and resilience".
"Their courage in coming forward has not only led to this outcome but may also give confidence to others who have suffered sexual violence to seek help and report what happened to them," he said.
"I hope they recognise just how powerful their actions have been, and the difference they may make to others."
Senior prosecutor for the Crown Prosecution Service David Roberts said Brand was "a manipulative sexual predator, using his growing fame and power to create situations in which he could pursue women sexually".
"Today's result shows that fame, status and the passage of time do not change the fundamental issue of consent," he said.
"No matter who the abuser is, or what the situation or circumstances are, we will always take allegations of rape and sexual assault seriously."
