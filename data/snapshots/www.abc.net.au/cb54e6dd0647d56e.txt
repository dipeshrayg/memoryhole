In short:
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy has said Ukraine was behind an attack on two Russian vessels near Benghazi.
One Russian warship was sunk and a cargo ship damaged, he said.
What's next?
The United Nations has expressed concern about Libya becoming a battleground for foreign conflicts.
Ukraine has claimed responsibility for a series of drone strikes on two Russian vessels near the Libyan port of Benghazi.
In a post on X, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said drones on Sunday sunk a Russian corvette Merkury and struck a cargo ship he said Moscow used to transport weapons.
A combat military helicopter was also destroyed in the attack, he said.
"It all took place off the coast of Libya. Our warriors deployed seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Despite all the enemy's efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk," he wrote on X.
Russian officials made no immediate comment.
Mr Zelenskyy said the Merkury was one of Russia's newest ships commissioned during the 2023 invasion.
"Our second target was the Russian dry cargo vessel MV ANGARA, which the enemy used to transport weapons. Result: target hit," he wrote.
"We continue our operations in designated waters. We are responding to Russia's aggression. We are developing Ukrainian weapons."
Mr Zelenskyy described the assault as "an exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia's war".
He said he received detailed briefings on Sunday and Monday, adding that "the targets we set were achieved".
The attacks drew condemnation from the United Nations who warned against the prospect of a Libya still recovering from civil war becoming "a battleground for foreign conflicts".
It comes as Ukraine steps up its strikes against Russia away from the frontline.
At the same time, Russia's strategic bombing of Ukraine has devastated the power grid ahead of winter and damaged civilian buildings.
A Russian strike on a five-storey apartment block in a village near Kyiv killed 11 people on Sunday morning, police said. A seven-year-old child and a 79-year-old woman were pulled from the rubble after the attack, rescue services said.
Libyan officials say 10 people wounded
The strikes severely damaged the Russian navy corvette at the Benghazi Naval Base in eastern Libya, according to two Libyan officials.
Residents in Benghazi heard loud explosions and saw fire at the base early on Sunday.
Civil defence rushed to the site and extinguished the fire, authorities in eastern Libya said.
The vessel had docked to refuel and take on supplies as part of a cooperation agreement between Russia and Libya, the statement said without providing details about the vessel or its cargo.
At least 10 people, including Libyan and Russian nationals, were wounded in the attack, one of the officials said.
United Nations condemns attacks
The UN mission in Libya expressed "serious concern" over the attacks, and called for "an immediate, independent and transparent investigation".
"Libya cannot afford, and must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts," the UN mission wrote on X.
The government in eastern Libya has written to UN Secretary-General António Guterres and the UN Security Council, calling for an investigation into the potential use of western Libya to prepare and launch the attack.
Sunday's strikes came after a drone hit a Russian cargo ship in the eastern Baltic Sea on Saturday, close to Finnish waters.
Kyiv has not commented on that attack but the Finnish military said it was likely to have been carried out by Ukraine. All 16 crew were evacuated onto a nearby Russian ship, one of them slightly injured.
On Monday, Russia's Foreign Ministry accused Estonia of allowing Ukraine to use its airspace "on the quiet" to launch attacks on Russian targets.
It labelled such moves provocative and warned it risked triggering an open war with Russia.
