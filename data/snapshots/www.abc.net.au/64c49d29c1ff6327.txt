Britain's King Charles has joked that he and Sir Elton John were "still standing" in a clip recorded for the singer's radio show which was aired on the monarch's 78th birthday.
King Charles had joined Sir John for a special edition of the singer's Elton John's Rocket Hour on Apple Music to celebrate 50 years of The King's Trust and musicians who have supported his youth charity.
During the episode, which was first broadcast on Saturday to coincide with the king's birthday, the pair discussed benefit concerts held in support of the Trust.
This included a Rock Gala held at Wembley Arena in 1986 where Sir Elton performed his 1980s hit, I'm Still Standing.
"I know people in all kinds of circumstances find this song inspiring, not only for its marvellous spirit, but also its message of resilience," King Charles said.
"And certainly 40 years on from that remarkable concert, we and the Trust are definitely still standing."
Sir John, who turns 80 in March, replied, "I'm still sitting now, though", prompting laughs from the king.
"With occasional bits dropping off here and there," King Charles said.
"I know this is a terrible problem as we get older."
Both men have suffered well-documented health problems in recent years, with the king still undergoing treatment for cancer and Sir Elton suffering problems with his eyesight.
When he was the heir, King Charles founded the Prince's Trust in 1976 using his Royal Navy severance pay to help disadvantaged young people prepare for work, find jobs or start businesses.
It was renamed the King's Trust after Charles became king in 2022.
The charity says it has supported more than one million young people since its launch.
"Music has always been at the heart of The Prince's, and now The King's Trust," King Charles said, adding that the program offered a chance to showcase performers who had supported the charity and newer artists it had helped launch.
The pair recalled benefit concerts stretching from Wembley Arena's rock galas in the 1980s to shows in Hyde Park and at the Royal Albert Hall.
Among them is British rock band Muse, which received a grant to buy sound equipment when it performed under its former name Rocket Baby Dolls.
The group later held a sold-out Royal Albert Hall concert in aid of the Trust in 2018, Buckingham Palace said in a statement.
"If you champion a young artist and show you believe in them, it makes all the difference," Sir Elton said.
"Young people are our future."
Sir Elton received the Order of the Companions of Honour from Charles, then Prince of Wales, at Windsor Castle in 2021 for services to music and charity.
One of the best-selling artists of all time, with hits including "Goodbye Yellow Brick Road", "Rocket Man" and "Your Song", Sir Elton was knighted by Queen Elizabeth in 1998.
Rocket Hour is a weekly show that has aired globally on Apple Music 1 since 2015.
