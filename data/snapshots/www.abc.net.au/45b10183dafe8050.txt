Medical staff are rushing to help a rare set of conjoined twins born in remote Papua New Guinea, with hopes of bringing the girls to Australia for treatment.
It is the country's second case of conjoined twins in just over a year after the birth of twin boys Tom and Sawong in October 2025 captured global attention.
Conjoined twins are very rare, estimated to occur only once in every 50,000 births and once in every 200,000 live births.
Many are stillborn or die soon after they are born.
The girls, who have not yet been named, were delivered two weeks ago at Nonga Base Hospital on the island of New Britain, about 800 kilometres from the capital, Port Moresby.
It is unclear at this stage whether they share any major organs.
Enquiries are being made about transferring the sisters to Australia because Papua New Guinea's heath system does not have the resources to treat conjoined twins.
Jurgen Ruh, a pilot who founded an NGO that provides emergency care to women in PNG, said he was reaching out to contacts in four different countries asking for help.
"Our favourite is Australia. It's closest," he told the ABC.
"But I'm going to put four hands in the fire and whichever comes first, we will action."
He said the hospital had very limited facilities but medical reports he had seen indicated the girls were doing OK.
"They're breathing a bit faster than normal, but apart from the vital signs we have seen, they look pretty normal," he said.
"We are hopeful that we've got all organs separate and they're just joined basically at the chest."
Mr Ruh helped seek overseas care for twins Tom and Sawong.
He said he could not believe there was another case of conjoined twins born in Papua New Guinea so soon after.
"I thought I wasn't going to see it in my lifetime again," Mr Ruh said.
His NGO, Mountain Area Medical Airlift (MAMA) Foundation, has sent staff to Nonga to help fly the twins to Port Moresby this week.
A local private hospital and commercial airline have also agreed to assist.
The CEO of East New Britain's health service, Ako Yap, said he was grateful for the support.
"I give my full authority for partners to escalate further treatment for the twins and we hope and pray for a better outcome all around," Dr Yap said.
