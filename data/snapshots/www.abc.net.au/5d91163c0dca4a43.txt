Commonwealth Games: Australian swimmers add nine golds with dead-heat, Kai Taylor win and Shayna Jack tears
Australia's swimmers clinched a record medal tally in the pool, finishing the Commonwealth Games with an unprecedented 37 gold medals after an epic finale at Glasgow's swimming centre on day six.
Kyle Chalmers earned the 10th gold medal of his Commonwealth Games career, propelling him past Ian Thorpe's Australian men's record tally of 10 golds and one silver.
Chalmers, resigned to the end of his domestic 100m freestyle reign after his defeat by Flynn Southam 24 hours earlier, shared his glorious moment with compatriot Ben Armbruster as they both struck gold together in a Games record 22.73 seconds.
Chalmers responded as only the great champions can by also downing his fellow big gun Cam McEvoy, who took bronze in yet another Australian 1-2-3 in the pool.
The win gave Chalmers his 12th Games medal overall to rise above Thorpe.
"Maybe I should have swam that heat of the medley relay this morning," said Chalmers, who could have had an 11th gold.
"I said I wouldn't do that because I wanted to really prioritise tonight. It's amazing to go past a legend of the sport."
The milestone came as Australia's all-conquering team stormed to a new record tally of golds at a single Games on the final night of the program, roaring past the previous mark of 28 won at Gold Coast 2018.
Queensland speedster Meg Harris became the most successful of all, joining the elite band who've scooped six golds at a single Commonwealth Games as she led another green-and-gold sweep in the 50m freestyle, ahead of Alex Perkins and Shayna Jack, in her farewell race.
Then the 24-year-old, who had powered to victory in 24.23, went on to anchor the women's 4x100 medley relay team, featuring Iona Anderson, Sienna Toohey and Perkins - who ended up with five golds and a silver - to the penultimate win of the week.
Kai Taylor, son of former teen phenomenon Hayley Lewis, was cheered on by his mum while earning a shock 200m freestyle gold.
Brittany Castelluzzo won the 200m fly ahead of bronze medallist Lizzy Dekkers, while Sam Short took the 1500m freestyle for a distance treble as compatriot Ben Goedemans claimed bronze.
Lakeisha Patterson continued the Dolphins' para domination too as she struck 100m free S9 gold ahead of Emily Beecroft.
Fittingly, the last title of the week went to the Dolphins as the men's 4x100 relay quartet of Henry Allan, Sam Williamson, Matt Temple and Flynn Southam landed Australia's eighth gold of the night.
Meanwhile, the Australian women's 3x3 basketball team claimed gold with a 21-11 win over Uganda in the final, as the men's team took silver after a 19-17 loss to England in the final.
The Australian men's wheelchair 3x3 successfully defended their Commonwealth Games title, pulling clear of England 19-13, while the wheelchair women's team won bronze with their 19-17 win over Canada.
Australian weightlifter Eileen Cikamatana was a class above in the women's 86kg event, nailing a 108kg snatch and a 140kg clean and jerk for a Games record total of 248kg.
She is the third female weightlifter in history to achieve the Commonwealth Games feat of three titles in a row, joining India's Mirabai Chanu (women's 48kg) and Canadian weightlifter Maude Charron (63kg) after they also won gold in Glasgow.
Cikamatana won her first gold medal with Fiji, before changing allegiance to Australia for the 2022 Games in Birmingham.
Ollie Saxton won bronze in the men's 94kg event earlier in the day.
After six days of competition, Australia stands well clear on the medal tally, with 47 gold, 21 silver and 35 bronze for a total of 103 medals.

Chad le Clos surpasses Emma McKeon's Games record
South African Chad le Clos has officially become the most decorated athlete in history at the Commonwealth Games.
By winning bronze alongside Pieter Coetzé, Michael Houlie and Ruard van Henen in the men's 4x100m medley relay, he claimed his 21st Commonwealth medal.
That saw him surpass the 20 medal total put up by our Aussie swimming legend Emma McKeon.
His medal tally now includes seven gold, five silver and nine bronze medals, across five editions of the Games: Delhi 2010, Glasgow 2014, Gold Coast 2018, Birmingham 2022 and Glasgow 2026.
