The road outside the electorate office of independent MP Helen Haines has become an active police scene after the 65-year-old was struck by a vehicle yesterday.
Ms Haines was critically injured in the incident, with Prime Minister Anthony Albanese describing it as a "deliberate act".
Here's what we know.
What happened to Helen Haines?
Ms Haines was struck by a Toyota LandCruiser on Murphy Street, outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, Victoria, at about 3:30pm yesterday.
She was retrieving items from the boot of her vehicle at the time.
Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said the driver of the LandCruiser allegedly "rammed her" twice.
"What witnesses have described is the driver doing a U-turn to get into the car park and to ram Helen against her vehicle, then to reverse and do it a second time," Mr Burke said this morning.
The driver was stopped by a member of the public and soon after taken into custody, police said in a statement.
What condition is Haines in?
Ms Haines suffered critical injuries and has lost one of her legs.
She was taken to a local hospital yesterday afternoon then flown to Melbourne to undergo emergency surgery for severe injuries to her lower body.
Ms Haines had surgery overnight and remained in a critical condition early this morning.
The ABC confirmed later in the morning that Ms Haines had lost a leg.
Her family released a statement yesterday thanking first responders for their swift response and asking for privacy.
"This afternoon, our beloved Helen was involved in an incident outside her Wangaratta Electorate Office, and is receiving medical care," the statement reads.
"Our family, and Helen's staff, will be making no further comment at this time, and ask that their privacy be respected by the public and media.
"Please keep Helen in your thoughts."
Who was the driver?
The driver was this morning identified as Brendan Nugent, a 43-year-old from Tarrawingee.
He has been in custody since his arrest yesterday afternoon and was formally questioned by detectives this morning.
A source with close knowledge of the investigation told the ABC that Mr Nugent was known to police.
It is understood he has undergone a mental health assessment.
Acting Superintendent Ashley Mason said the scene was "quite dynamic" and that detectives from the Major Collision Investigation Unit would investigate.
"Our investigators will endeavour to interview the driver of the motor vehicle this morning," he told ABC News Breakfast earlier today.
"They are ascertaining exactly what occurred, how and why."
Was it an accident?
Acting Superintendent Mason said police would investigate whether the incident was a targeted attack.
Mr Albanese described the incident as a "deliberate act".
He said a joint counterterrorism unit had been activated but had no updates about what could have been behind the attack.
"The motivation is not clear at this time," Mr Albanese said this morning.
"It's important we don't speculate on that but allow facts to be found."
Acting Superintendent Mason said it was "an isolated incident".
"There is no intelligence that we are aware of to indicate that there is any further or ongoing threat to any individual or the community," he said.
How have politicians responded?
Mr Albanese called Ms Haines a "beacon of light" for the regional community in Northern Victoria.

Who is Helen Haines?
Helen Haines is an independent federal member of parliament, representing the electorate of Indi.
As an independent, Ms Haines is not a member of any political party.
She has been described as respected across party divides and a neutral, non-polarising figure.
Local state MP Tim McCurdy, a member of the National Party, said Ms Haines was "the most inoffensive person you come across".
"She's a decent person," he said.
"She's a good human being and she didn't deserve this. No-one deserves this."
Wangaratta local Robert Holloway described her as "just a normal person and everyone loves her for it".
"I don't think you would find anyone in town with a negative word to say about her," Mr Holloway said.
Ms Haines has long served regional communities in Victoria, both as a nurse and midwife, before becoming a federal MP in 2019.
She made history with her election, becoming the first independent elected to succeed another independent in the federal parliament.
What is Haines's background?
On her website, Ms Haines introduces herself as a healthcare professional from a small farming community.
She grew up with four brothers on a dairy farm in the south-west of the state, where she learned "the value of pitching in and looking out for others".
As a 26-year-old, Ms Haines took on the role of matron at Chiltern Bush Nursing Hospital.

She also worked at Wangaratta Hospital, leading midwifery services and helping to build one of Australia's longest-running rural midwifery programs.
"Over three decades, I've worked across rural health as a nurse, midwife, researcher and academic," she wrote.
Years later, Ms Haines said, her career in nursing remained a guiding force in her political work.
"As a nurse and a midwife, you're with people in their most joyous and difficult moments," she wrote.
"It's taught me that life is precious, that everyone deserves dignity and respect, that good policy starts with listening, and - as a health researcher - that facts matter."
