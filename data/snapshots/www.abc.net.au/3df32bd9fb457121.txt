In short:
Federal independent MP Helen Haines has been critically injured after being struck by a Toyota LandCruiser outside her regional office in Wangaratta, Victoria.
Police located a knife inside the vehicle and have since taken a 43-year-old Tarrawingee man into custody.
What's next?
Police are working to establish a motivation for the incident.
The road outside the electorate office of independent MP Helen Haines has become an active police scene after the 65-year-old was allegedly struck by a vehicle this afternoon.
Ms Haines was critically injured in the incident, which has been described by the prime minister as an "attack".
Here's what we know.
What happened?
According to police, initial investigations have determined Ms Haines was struck by a Toyota LandCruiser about 3:30pm on Murphy Street, outside her electorate office in Wangaratta, Victoria.
One witness told the ABC she heard a bang and saw a woman pinned between two cars.

A 43-year-old Tarrawingee man was stopped by a member of the public and has since been taken into custody, police said in a statement.
Police said a knife was found in the vehicle and they were investigating whether the incident was "intentional".
But Prime Minister Anthony Albanese has described the incident as a deliberate attack.
The prime minister told 7.30: "We know that this was a serious incident."
"The first I was told was that there'd been a car accident, so I did not think of anything other than an accident,"
he said.
"This wasn't an accident and the suspect is in custody."
It is understood the driver is known to police and will undergo a mental health assessment.
Acting Superintendent Ashley Mason said the scene was "quite dynamic" and that detectives from the Major Collision Investigation Unit would be investigating.
Mr Albanese has said a joint counter terrorism unit had been activated but it was too early to say what was behind the incident.
MP suffers critical injuries
Victoria Police said Ms Haines had suffered critical injuries as a result of the alleged attack.
She was rushed to hospital and flown to Melbourne to undergo emergency surgery for severe injuries to her lower body.
Ms Haines's family has released a statement on the incident, thanking first responders for their swift response and asking for privacy amid the ongoing investigation.
"This afternoon, our beloved Helen was involved in an incident outside her Wangaratta Electorate Office, and is receiving medical care," the statement reads.
"Our family, and Helen's staff, will be making no further comment at this time, and ask that their privacy be respected by the public and media.
"Please keep Helen in your thoughts."
How have politicians responded?
Ms Haines has been described as a respected political figure across party divides.
Mr Albanese has called Ms Haines a "beacon of light" for the regional community in Northern Victoria.
He said Ms Haines was someone "loved and admired because she's someone of integrity".
The prime minister also called for the "political temperature" to be lowered amid increasing rhetoric of violence against representatives.
Opposition Leader Angus Taylor said he was deeply shocked by the incident.
"Our nation cannot become a place where violent attacks are normalised,"
he said.
Victorian Premier Ben Carroll said he was "appalled" by the "violent attack" on Helen Haines.
"My thoughts, and the thoughts of all Victorians, are with Helen, her family, her friends, her colleagues and her community," he said.
"I know how devastating this will be for the Wangaratta community and all those who Helen represents.
"Thank you to police, paramedics, firefighters and witnesses for their immediate response.
"An individual has been apprehended, and I thank police for their swift action."
Who is Helen Haines?
Helen Haines has long served regional communities in Victoria both as a nurse and midwife, most recently as a member for parliament, representing Indi as an independent leader since 2019.
She became the first independent elected to succeed another independent in the federal parliament.
On her website, Ms Haines introduces herself as a health care professional from a small farming community.
She says she grew up alongside her four brothers on a dairy farm in the south-west of the state, where she learned "the value of pitching in and looking out for others".
As a 26-year-old, Ms Haines took on the role of matron at Chiltern Bush Nursing Hospital.
She also worked at Wangaratta Hospital, leading midwifery services and helping build one of Australia's longest-running rural midwifery programs.
"Over three decades, I've worked across rural health as a nurse, midwife, researcher and academic," she wrote.
Years later, Ms Haines, says her formative career in nursing remains a guiding force in her political work.
"As a nurse and a midwife, you're with people in their most joyous and difficult moments," Ms Haines wrote.
"It's taught me that life is precious, that everyone deserves dignity and respect, that good policy starts with listening, and - as a health researcher - that facts matter," she wrote.
