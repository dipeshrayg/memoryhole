Kevin Parker, the West Australian polymath behind Tame Impala, is going to need a bigger trophy cabinet.
Scoring seven pointy trophy awards, Parker was the biggest winner of tonight's 40th ARIA Awards.
He won Best Solo Artist, both Best Pop Release and the audience-voted Song of the Year for global synth-funk hit Dracula, as well Best Engineered Release and Best Produced Release - categories Parker has swept each time he's nominated.
Tame Impala's fifth album Deadbeat fended off strong competition to win Album of the Year. That brings Parker to four total wins over the years, surpassing Powderfinger in holding the record for most ARIA Album of the Year wins ever.
Not only that, tonight catapults Tame Impala to 22 total wins, overtaking Silverchair and John Farnham's respective 21 victories and making Parker the most decorated artist in ARIA Awards history.
Not bad for a self-confessed loner who used to lurk in Fremantle venue basements and inked his first record deal in 2008 by presenting his bedroom solo recordings as the product of a trio of friends.
Tame Impala was also presented the Global Impact Award, by Darren Hayes, recognising Parker's enduring popularity and acclaim overseas.
The accolades cap a blockbuster year. That includes a 2026 world tour, charting Gold in 16 countries with Dracula (and its remix featuring Jennie of Blackpink), and Loser featuring in the latest Spider-Man movie.
Headlining next year's Coachella festival, Tame Impala recently landed four Grammy Award nominations, adding to his two previous Grammy wins.
"This is the closest to my heart," he said on the red carpet. "Don't tell America that..."
'Our music is not AI's to take'
That was the recurring, co-ordinated message many rallied behind throughout the ceremony, based on the newly minted campaign from ARIA urging the Australian government to stop AI companies using Australian creative work to train their models without permission.
"There has never been a more important time to make noise about this," said Laneway Festival boss Danny Rogers, the first at the winner's podium to draw attention to the issue.
He dedicated his Best Festival win to "original music" and "incredibly talented and dedicated human beings ... At a time when their livelihoods are increasingly under threat."

Tim Minchin, presenting the award for Best Original Soundtrack or Musical Theatre Cast Album, spoke about the importance of artistry and "heartfelt, f**king human-ness" - flaws and all - over AI to rousing applause.
Later, Sir Elton John appeared with a video message addressing the Australian government.
"You have a duty to protect Australian artists and their work. Don't let AI use any Australian artist's work without full permission."
In a segment shortly after, joined by fellow musicians Josh Pyke, Dobby and KLP, Jack River protested against the "handful of trillion-dollar AI companies [trying] to bully our government into changing Australia's copyright laws for their benefit".
Pyke added, "If [we] legalise theft in our backyard, it's not just our work the government will be handing over, it's the whole f***ing world's".
Dobby emphasised it wouldn't just be lyrics and songs from "Yothu Yindi, Missy Higgins, Gurrumul and Jimmy Barnes you'll be handing over. But Elton John, Beyonce and The Beatles' work too.
"Do not let Australia be the weak link that lets the world down."
(Re)Introducing Ecca Vandal
Born in South Africa with Sri Lankan Tamil heritage and based in Melbourne, Ecca Vandal is a first-time nominee who flipped seven nominations into four wins.
"It's been a long journey ... a lot of closed doors," she said, accepting the Michael Gudinski Breakthrough Artist award.
She called out those in the room who "have said 'no' to me along the way. And there's been a few of you. Genuinely.... Thank you because I realised I didn't need a green light to go after what was inside of me.
"The biggest breakthrough for me ... I didn't have to be anyone else other than myself."
If you hadn't heard of her before tonight, it may be because it's as much a comeback as a breakthrough.
After releasing her 2017 self-titled debut album, Vandal shut down her social accounts in 2020. She holed up in the childhood home of co-producer (and partner) Richie Buxton and emerged with Looking for People to Unfollow.
An explosive collision of alt-rock, neo-soul, punk-pop, hip-hop and beyond showcasing her versatility, the album's unpredictable fusion extended to Vandal's highly stylised fashion and self-directed music videos.
Looking for People to Unfollow won Best Independent Release, Best Alternative Release and Best Cover Art. The victory comes after Vandal scoring the $50,000 Australian Music Prize earlier this month.
"To any artists out there told they're too much, don't fit the mould, has been overlooked: keep going," she said.
"You've got to be unapologetically yourself and the world doesn't need any more of the same."
A surprise Genesis Owusu upset
Between Tame Impala and Ecca Vandal taking home almost half of the 29 categories, plus rock trio DMA'S and drumming pop star G Flip taking two awards each, there wasn't much room for anyone else.
Just ask Genesis Owusu.
Going in, the Ghanaian Australian seemed like the one to beat, not only matching Tame Impala in leading the nominations pool, but he had form.
The genre-masher's seven previous ARIA victories included his first two albums claiming Album of the Year. But despite eight nods, and a recent J Award win for his third album, the 28-year-old walked away empty-handed.
If there was to be a consolation prize, it would be for Owusu's high-octane performance of Stampede, exhibiting the commanding charisma that's powered him through triple J's One Night Stand and a recent US tour.
It's a shame, Owusu certainly makes it count when he's at the podium. In 2023, he proclaimed: "Ceasefire now, ceasefire now, free Palestine."
It would have been interesting to hear his take on AI.
