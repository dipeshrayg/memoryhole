In short:
Dissatisfied mobile and internet customers are increasingly turning to the industry ombudsman for compensation amid rising network outages.
What's next?
Consumer advocates say telcos should provide automatic compensation to those affected.
A woman unable to receive updates about her critically ill mother was among more than 13,000 Australians who lodged official complaints with the Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman (TIO) in the September quarter.
The 12-hour Telstra network outage on July 8 fuelled a 72 per cent surge in complaints that mentioned network outages, including domestic violence victim-survivors, people in ill-health and customers in financial hardship.
In total, telcos were forced to pay out $1.9 million in compensation to 2,267 complainants in the quarter, via a combination of direct compensation, bill adjustments, refunds and account credits.
Network outages are an increasingly common and expensive headache for phone and internet customers, according to the Australian Communications Consumer Action Network (ACCAN).
Its chief executive, Carol Bennett, said the ombudsman complaint figures were "only the tip of the iceberg" when it came to customer harm caused by outages.
"Since April 1 this year, Optus has had 110 outages, and Telstra has had 334," Ms Bennet said.
"Outages are not only inconvenient, they are a huge impost on consumers and a drain on our productivity."
Outage cost half a billion
Modelling by FTI Consulting estimates the July 8 Testra outage cost the Australian economy $552 million and the 2023 Optus outage in the order of $2 billion.
Telecommunications Industry Ombudsman (TIO) Cynthia Gebert said telcos should act swiftly and fairly to address the harm caused by phone or internet outages.
She added that consumers always had the option to lodge a complaint with the TIO seeking compensation if they were unsatisfied with their provider's response.
"Losing service can be much more than an inconvenience," Ms Gerbert said.
"It can affect someone's ability to earn a living, manage their health, access support or contact emergency services. For a small business, losing a phone or internet service can mean losing customers, sales and income."
Disputed charges top complaints
Overall, complaints about telcos rose 8.4 per cent on the previous quarter, with the top three complaints being disputed charges (4,151 complaints, down 7.3 per cent on the previous quarter), compensation for poor experience or inconvenience (2,032 complaints, up 58 per cent) and complaints about 'no service' (1,311, down 9 per cent).
The number of complaints to the ombudsman seeking compensation for a poor customer experience or for being inconvenienced by their telco provider has doubled over the past year.
A spokesperson for Telstra said 99 per cent of consumer and small business customers who had complained directly to the telco about the July 8 outage had had their complaint "resolved and closed".
"We have processed credits of almost $1 million for consumers and businesses who contacted us or lodged a complaint on the July 8 outage,"
the spokesperson said.
"We encourage anyone that believes they still require assistance to reach out to us."
Telstra typically dominates the list of most complained about telcos, in part due to its large customer base and large regional and remote coverage.
Onus 'should not be on customers'
ACCAN's Ms Bennett said the onus should not be on customers to go cap-in-hand to retailers to prove their damages to get redress.
"Right now, if your service goes down, it's largely up to you to chase your telco for compensation, and any goodwill gesture is entirely at their discretion. That isn't good enough for an essential service."
Consumer groups are pushing for telco customers who suffer outages to be awarded compensation automatically, similar to what happens in the energy sector when service disruptions occur.
ACCAN is also calling for enforceable standards to ensure companies invest sufficiently in networks to provide reliable services, along with the implementation of 'domestic mobile roaming' so that customers can connect to another provider in the case their network fails in an outage.
This echoes the findings of a Senate inquiry in September that recommended the federal government mandate domestic mobile roaming for both voice calls and text messages.
It also recommended the introduction of "substantial" civil penalties for telcos that fail to meet reliability and performance standards, and to introduce "a comprehensive automatic compensation scheme for those directly or indirectly affected by service outages."
