At least 30 people have died in a crash between two trains in central Spain, officials say.
The high-speed trains collided near the city of Toledo in the La Castilla Mancha region on Wednesday morning, shutting down the line connecting Madrid to the southern Andalusia region, rail operator Adif said.
"Traffic on the Madrid-Andalusia high-speed rail line has been suspended following a rear-end collision between two trains," it said on X.
Regional spokesperson Esther Padilla said the death toll was "very likely" to increase.
She said 147 people were on board the Iryo train and 397 were on the Renfe train, and that several carriages had plunged down an embankment.
An official briefed on the accident shared that an Iryo express train was stopped at a signal when it was struck by a Renfe high speed train.
Ms Padilla told state broadcaster TVE that one train ran into the tail of the other, which was stationary, knocking two of its carriages off the track.
She said it was not known what had caused the crash, which occurred on a flat stretch between the towns of Urda and Los Yébenes.
Spanish Health Minister Mónica García told TVE that emergency workers were focusing on rescuing survivors and bodies from the two most damaged cars, and that injured passengers were being sent to different hospitals in the area.
"It was a high-speed impact, so we are expecting this to be a tragedy," she said.
Urda Mayor Manuel Galán told TVE that civil protection authorities had asked that the town prepare a sports pavilion to attend to injured passengers.
A military emergency brigade was deployed to help emergency services, firefighters and police to the crash site.
Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez cancelled his agenda for Wednesday to focus on the crash. In a post on social media he said he was "dismayed by the serious train accident in Toledo."
In January this year 45 people were killed and 120 others injured when an Iryo train derailed at high speed into the path of an oncoming train run by Renfe AVE, near Adamuz in southern Spain.
The accident is still under investigation. A preliminary report by Spain's Civil Guard in February pointed to defects in the track, rail or weld between two sections of track as the main hypothesis for the accident, but kept seven other theories open.
