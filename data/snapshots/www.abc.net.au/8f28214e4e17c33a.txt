A Singapore Airlines flight has been forced to make an emergency landing at Rockhampton Airport on Wednesday after smoke was seen in the cabin.
The Singapore-bound plane had just departed Brisbane when the incident occurred.
It touched down safely just after 4pm. No injuries were reported.
Paramedics and fire crews were called to the scene.
Passengers reported seeing smoke fill the cabin and plane screens go black.
A Singapore Airlines spokesperson said the company was investigating the cause of the smoke.
"We are providing our customers with all necessary assistance, including on ground refreshments, arranging for a relief flight and rebooking onward travel where necessary," the spokesperson said in a statement.
"The safety of our customers and staff is our top priority."
This morning, Rockhampton Mayor Tony Williams said it was an "eventful night" for staff at the airport.
"It was a good experience; everyone is safe," he told the ABC.
Cr Williams said diversions to the city's airport were not uncommon, as it was equipped with a large tarmac used by the defence force.
"It's one of the largest regional airports in the country, we see military exercises on a regular basis," he said.
The mayor confirmed the plane remained on the tarmac.
Passengers who were on board the flight were contained to an internal lounge in the airport.
