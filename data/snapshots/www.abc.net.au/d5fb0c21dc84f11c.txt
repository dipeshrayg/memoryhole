The ABC has won seven gongs at this year's Walkley Awards, including the scoop of the year prize.
Nine newspaper journalist Nick McKenzie took out the highest accolade in Australian journalism, the Gold Walkley.
The 71st Walkley Awards for Excellence in Journalism were hosted in Sydney last night, with 30 categories.
McKenzie was awarded the Gold Walkley for his investigation into Victorian underworld figure Mick Gatto, and how the Victorian Labor government allowed wrongdoing to occur on its $100 billion infrastructure program, Big Build.
McKenzie also won the investigative journalism category for that story, bringing his tally of Walkley awards throughout his career to 22.
The judging board described it as "truth-to-power journalism in the face of attempts to shut him down with actual violence".
"It's not just a Victorian story, it speaks to our nation and the way our democracy works. Tenacious reporting presented in accessible ways, instrumental in bringing down a government," it said.
The ABC took out the coveted scoop of the year for coverage of ASIO's failures ahead of the Bondi terrorist attack.
Sean Rubinsztein-Dunlop, Olivia Caisley and Michael Workman were given the award for their story Bondi: What ASIO Knew, produced for ABC Investigations, ABC News Online and 7.30.
The Walkley Foundation chief executive Shona Martyn said this year's award showed the "excellence and breadth of Australian journalism".
"Public interest journalism is in good hands with these Australian journalists who are working against a backdrop of challenges to journalism, press freedom and news organisations in a shifting world," Ms Martyn.
