In short:
The ABC has won seven Walkley awards this year, including scoop of the year.
ABC radio, television and digital journalism has been recognised.
The Walkley Foundation's chief executive said public interest journalism was in good hands.
The ABC has won seven gongs at this year's Walkley Awards, including the coveted Scoop of the Year prize for coverage of ASIO's failures ahead of the Bondi terrorist attack.
The 71st Walkley Awards for Excellence in Journalism was hosted in Sydney, Thursday night, with 30 categories.
The Walkley Foundation's chief executive, Shona Martyn said: "Once again the Walkley Awards have proved the excellence and breadth of Australian journalism."
"Public interest journalism is in good hands with these Australian journalists who are working against a backdrop of challenges to journalism, press freedom and news organisations in a shifting world."

Investigative Journalism
For the second time, The Age's Nick McKenzie took home the Gold Walkley.
Nick McKenzie's investigation into Mick Gatto and the CFMEU found that Victoria's Labor government had been repeatedly warned about union misconduct and alleged criminal infiltration of the state's construction sector.
The piece helped Mr McKenzie walk out with the award for the Investigative Journalism category, bringing his tally of Walkley awards throughout his career to 22.
Press Photographer of the Year
Jason Edwards was named the Press Photographer of the Year for his portfolio of work for the Herald Sun.
"It is a testament to the fact that, despite challenges, Australian journalists are producing work of great public interest, originality and courage." said Gay Alcorn, chair of the Walkley judging board.
