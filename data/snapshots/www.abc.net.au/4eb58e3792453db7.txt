Kyiv is urging the US to do more to achieve a peace outcome with Russia.
Ukraine President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Donald Trump's relationship with Russia's Vladimir Putin is comparable to being friends with Adolf Hitler as Kyiv grows increasingly frustrated at how the US is handling peace negotiations.
In an interview with the BBC in Kyiv, Mr Zelenskyy said it was "strange" for the US president to call Mr Putin a friend and would be similar to making the same comment about Hitler during World War II.
"He's an aggressor ... and a killer. He can't be a friend," he said.
"It's the same that somebody say about Hitler during the Second World War that he's a good friend. No, he's not a friend."
Mr Zelenskyy was speaking a week after Mr Trump had embraced the Russian president as a "friend" at a summit in China.
Days later Mr Trump posted a photo on his Truth Social platform of the pair shaking hands there and titled it "My Friend".
Mr Trump's relationship with Mr Zelenskyy has long been uneasy, most notably breaking down publicly in February 2025 when the US president confronted him in the Oval Office.
The pair have continued to clash more recently with the US president blaming Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries for high energy costs and threatening to cut off intelligence support.
Mr Zelenskyy has said Mr Trump's position is encouraging Russia to continue attacking Ukraine.
Speaking with the BBC, Mr Zelenskyy complained the US was not doing enough to negotiate peace with Russia and called on "real helpful allies, who can push Russia" to achieve an outcome.
"They have to pressure on the situation. The situation is when Russia is aggressor," he said.
"It's not about comfortable talks. It's about strong country who can pressure because this country (US) has power and respect."
Kushner praises Putin's 'restraint'
It comes as US envoy Jared Kushner praised the Russian president "grandmaster of politics" and a "historic figure" who had "shown restraint" by agreeing to not use nuclear weapons against Ukraine.

Mr Kushner, Trump's son-in-law who is mediating Russia-Ukraine talks with envoy Steve Witkoff, said that after talking at length with Putin, he believed Putin wanted peace almost five years after Moscow's invasion.
Mr Putin "has agreed not to use nuclear weapons, which obviously is a big thing, and he's reassured us that that's not his intention", Kushner said on a podcast by German media group Axel Springer Creators.
Mr Kushner added: "What (Putin) is saying is peace, but I think it's always peace on what terms" as the talks have hit "a little bit of a deadlock" over the fate of Ukrainian territory.
Mr Zelenskyy and European leaders have criticised Mr Putin for what they describe as Moscow's refusal to compromise and persistence to press on with an invasion which has killed hundreds of thousands of troops and thousands of Ukrainian civilians.
According to Mr Kushner, the Kremlin wants a new relationship with Washington as Russia seeks to keep NATO away from its borders.
Mr Trump has said on social media he would like to see Putin "pull back all Military aggression against territories throughout Europe, in the hopes that a Deal can be made between Russia and Ukraine, and made quickly".
Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov said the US was approaching peace talks with an understanding of the need to eliminate what Russia calls the "root causes" of the conflict, including "the creation of threats to Russia's security right on our borders".
Ukraine races to bolster air defences
Mr Zelenskyy has warned Ukraine's intelligence indicates that Moscow is "completing preparations for a new massive strike" which Kyiv says aims to deprive civilians of power and running water as the winter approaches.
Russian missile and drone attacks have battered Ukraine's power grid, killed civilians and choked the economy since the invasion.
"The Russians continue to target substations, overhead powerlines, and the entire power grid," Mr Zelenskyy said on social media.
Ukraine is pushing its allies to provide more air defence supplies as crews work to restore electricity in six Ukrainian regions after a Russian assault on Wednesday night involving ballistic missiles and 113 drones.
Ukraine has used its own long-range weapons to hit back at targets in Russia, and Russian vessels docked at a Libyan port in an attack which has caused alarm amongst some European leaders.
