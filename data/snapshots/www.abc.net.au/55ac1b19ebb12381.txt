Swimming: Three Aussies into the 400IM final
The 400 IM is the great test of a swimmer's overall skill - and it's a brutal test.
The greatest swimmer in the world, Summer McIntosh, is unfortunately not here at these Games, opting to keep her powder dry for the Pan Pacs in California next month.
Jenna Forrester is a joy of a swimmer to watch, so languid and easy in the way she moves through the water in the first two legs, particularly the backstroke.
Teammate Ella Ramsay closed her down a bit over the breaststroke, but it was Forrester who claimed the win in heat two.
Tara Kinder came second in her heat, swimming 4:41.81 behind heat winner Amalie Smith of England, who is just 16 years old, and flew through in a 4:39.13 to claim the fastest qualifying time.

Meet the Aussie sprinters up first today
Rohan Browning and Edward Nketia will compete in the men's 100m sprint soon - there's 11 heats happening at Scotstoun Stadium tonight, we're in the 7th and 11th races.
Our boys be eyeing the 23-year-old record time of 9.93s, which was set by Aussie sprint star Patrick Johnson.
No Australian has won this event, or its predecessor (the 100y, as it was known), since the Auckland Commonwealth Games in 1950.
The last time we secured a medal in the men's 100m was at the 1962 Commonwealth Games in Perth, when Michael Cleary claimed bronze.
Browning is just setting up on the starting line now, c'mon green and gold!
