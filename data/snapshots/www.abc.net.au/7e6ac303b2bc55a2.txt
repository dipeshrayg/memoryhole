In short:
Two Libyan army personnel have died from injuries sustained in a Ukrainian attack on two Russian vessels in Benghazi on Sunday.
Ukrainian allies have urged caution about using international waters to carry out attacks.
What's next?
Libya's eastern rulers have threatened to respond "at the appropriate time and place, and by the appropriate means".
Ukraine has defended its right to self-defence after launching a series of lethal air strikes on Russian ships in Libya as the deadly conflict threatens to escalate beyond eastern Europe.
Two Libyan army personnel have since died from wounds sustained in Sunday's drone attacks that sunk a Russian ship and damaged another at the Benghazi naval base in the country's east.
The Libyan National Army (LNA), which controls the country's eastern portion and has ties to Russia, has threatened to retaliate over the strikes.
Meanwhile, leaked data has shed light on the extent of Russia's losses in Ukraine, with Russian government statistics revealing nearly 500,000 Russian soldiers have died fighting on the frontline since 2022, the BBC and The Guardian have reported.
It comes as both countries traded a fresh wave of drone strikes on Tuesday night that targeted energy infrastructure and caused widespread power outages.
Libya threatens to respond
Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said both Russian vessels, a navy corvette and a cargo ship transporting military equipment, were valid targets and Sunday's operation embodied a legitimate act of self-defence.

"Ukraine is not 'dragging' anyone into the war or 'transferring' the war anywhere," Mr Sybiha said on the Telegram messaging app.
"Ukraine respects the sovereignty and territorial integrity of Libya. We emphasise that Ukraine's actions are aimed at countering Russian armed aggression and are not intended to harm Libya or its people."
He said international law did not limit the right to self-defence to the territory where the aggression takes place, but could also be exercised on the territory of the aggressor or in international waters.
The naval attacks caused concern at the United Nations mission in Libya, which warned against turning the country, still recovering from civil war, into a "battleground for foreign conflicts".
Italy's Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani also expressed concern about exporting the conflict to "just a few kilometres from Italy's coastline", while French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot backed Ukraine in its "absolute right to self-defence".
The LNA command confirmed on Tuesday that two of its military personnel had succumbed to wounds sustained in the attack. In a statement, the LNA said its response to the strike would come "at the appropriate time and place, and by the appropriate means".
Norway has also urged caution after Ukrainian drones attacked a Russian cargo ship in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday.
"All attacks would stop immediately if Russia stopped this war," Norwegian Defence Minister Tore Sandvik said.
"That said, we are focused on the fact that Ukraine must do this [these actions] within the rules of international law. So we take this matter up with them."
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the operation had struck a legitimate military target.
"Every vessel with a Russian flag, everything that carries weapons or any sanctioned cargo that can carry God knows what, it is a legitimate target for us in Russian territorial or neutral waters," he told Bloomberg.
Close to 500,000 Russians killed in war, data shows
Leaked data from Russia's statistics agency has revealed nearly 500,000 Russian soldiers have died since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
The numbers, leaked by hackers in September and analysed by the BBC and The Guardian, vastly surpass previous estimates and do not account for soldiers declared missing.
It paints a picture of a war which has grown increasingly deadly.
According to The Guardian, Russia recorded nearly 20,000 military deaths in the war's first year, and 50,000 in 2023. The toll hit more than 134,000 in 2024, and rose further to almost 200,000 in 2025 in what was the deadliest year of the war so far.
Ukraine's military losses are estimated at around 115,000 since 2022 with 105,000 missing, according to the online UA losses project.
Fresh wave of strikes cause sweeping power outages
Ukraine and Russia carried out a series of drone strikes on Tuesday night targeting energy and industrial infrastructure in both countries.
Ukraine's Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said Russian strikes caused overnight power outages in five regions, including Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk, Zaporizhzhia, Odesa and Kharkiv.
The Russian-occupied Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant lost external power for the 17th time this year and switched to diesel generators, Mr Nekrasov said.
In Russia's Samara region, industrial facilities also came under attack with a Ukrainian drone strike killing one and triggering power cuts.
Regional governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev said air defence forces downed 145 Ukrainian drones overnight. Unverified reports suggested two oil refineries were the subjects of the attack.
