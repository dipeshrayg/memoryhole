In short:
Two Italian nationals working for the arms company Rheinmetall are dead, after an Iranian drone attack on Kuwait.
Kuwait's defence ministry said that the country's army detected a number of drones within its airspace.
The attack was one of several reported across the region over 24 hours, including two targeting ships transiting through the Strait of Hormuz.
Two Italian nationals have been killed by an apparent Iranian drone strike in Kuwait, as disruption across the region stemming from the US-Israeli war with Iran continues.
Kuwait's defence ministry said earlier that the country's army detected a number of drones within its airspace, in what it described as an Iranian attack.
Italian Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani later said that the two Rheinmetall Italia technicians were working at an air-defence surveillance station in Kuwait when it was targeted by Iranian drones.
"Following a powerful explosion, both of our compatriots, according to information coming from Kuwait, have died," Mr Tajani said.
"Two other people were injured: a Pakistani national and a captain in the Kuwaiti army."
Kuwait's Defence Ministry confirmed that the two Italians had died of their injuries after initially reporting that four people had been wounded by falling debris when a 'vital facility' was targeted in what it said was an Iranian attack.
It confirmed that the other two people injured were a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national.
An Iranian military spokesperson denied it was responsible for the attack on the Kuwaiti site.
Ongoing threat in Strait of Hormuz
The attack on Kuwait came as two vessels reported being struck in the Strait of Hormuz.
A British military monitor, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations, said two tankers were hit by unknown projectiles in the region, one on Wednesday afternoon and one early Thursday, local time.
The first ship struck was east of Oman, and the second one was located 93 kilometres west of Dubai.
The UKMTO said in both cases all crew were safe and accounted for.
There has been an increase recently of incidents involving commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz and nearby waters.
The strategic waterway at the mouth of the Persian Gulf, through which about a fifth of the world's traded oil and natural gas passed before the Iran war, has been effectively shut by Iran's attacks on shipping since the start of the war in February.
October saw the highest number of reported attacks on commercial shipping since March, during the war's opening weeks, according to the UKMTO.
The organisation has also noted that some ships have been reluctant to report incidents.
Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps, or IRGC, said that two tankers were trying to transit without its authorisation and caught fire after hitting naval mines.
Footage posted by Iranian state television showed thick smoke rising from the two vessels. The AP could not independently verify these claims.
Houthi strikes on Saudi facility
The Saudi-led military coalition said on Thursday that Yemen's Houthis had attacked the Jazan refinery in Saudi Arabia, amid escalating hostilities between the kingdom and the Iran-aligned group.
The coalition added that targeting civilians and civilian infrastructure would be met with a firm military response.
That attack came a day after Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis announced they had attacked Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery, Ras Tanura, which sits alongside one of the world's largest crude export terminals.
Satellite images showed thick black smoke over the installation.
