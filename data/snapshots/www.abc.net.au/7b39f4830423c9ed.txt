A passenger plane from London has made an unscheduled landing at Warsaw-Modlin airport in Poland after a bomb threat alert.
Another plane from Copenhagen is being checked at Warsaw-Chopin airport.
Security services were verifying the reported threat in Modlin, the airport said in a statement.
"At 9:54 a.m., a bomb threat alert was announced on board an aircraft operating a flight on the route London Heathrow (LHR) - Warsaw Chopin Airport (WAW)," it said.
"At 10:01 a.m., a Boeing 737 MAX aircraft landed at Warsaw/Modlin Airport.
"The services are underway to verify the reported threat and ensure the safety of all people present at the airport."
Warsaw-Modlin airport temporarily closed but later said it had resumed operations.
The aircraft was still being searched after the 181 people aboard the plane were evacuated, it said.
"The aircraft which was the subject of a report of a possible bomb threat, is currently being checked by the relevant services," Warsaw-Modlin airport said.
"Passengers and crew members remain in the arrivals hall to wait for further decisions," it added.
A spokesperson for Polish airline LOT confirmed it was a LOT flight.
A bomb threat was also reported on an SAS flight from Copenhagen to Warsaw, which landed at Chopin Airport and was being checked, a Warsaw-Chopin Airport spokesperson said, adding that the airport was continuing operations.
Warsaw has two airports. Chopin is within the city limits and Modlin, mostly used by budget airlines, is about 36 kilometres from the capital.
