An Adelaide man has given evidence in his own murder trial, telling a jury he wanted to "get back" at a man who had swung a knife at him during an attempted drug deal.
Liam Slann and his father, Brian Slann, are standing trial for the murder of 21-year-old William Holdback, after a disagreement over drugs in April 2024.
Both men have pleaded not guilty to murder, but Liam Slann has entered a guilty plea to manslaughter, which the prosecution did not accept.
On Tuesday, Liam Slann took the stand telling the court he was worried and angry after he thought he was being robbed during the deal.
Liam Slann told the court he asked his father to drive him to the deal in Welland in Adelaide's inner north-west.
"It wasn't really a thing that Dad liked doing, but I asked him to do it anyway," he told the jury.
Liam Slann said the alleged victim approached the car wearing a COVID mask, asked to see the drugs, grabbed the bag of cocaine and swung a knife at him before the knife dropped into the car.
"His hand came straight through the window, came straight for my face," Liam Slann said.
"I reckon I screamed."
He told the court the alleged victim smashed his dad's back window and taunted him.
"He kept laughing, jumped side to side," Liam Slann told the jury.
The court heard that Liam Slann jumped out of the car and said to the alleged victim, "You're f****** c**** ... I'm going to stab you."
Liam Slann told the court he chased Mr Holdback, "trying to get the coke back".
"I wanted to get back at him for what he did to me," he said.
Liam Slann told the court that when he caught up with the victim, Mr Holdback swung his skateboard at him before he [Liam Slann] lunged at him with the knife.
When his lawyer, Elizabeth Mansfield, asked about what he wanted to do when he eventually caught up with the alleged victim, Liam Slann said he wanted to "hurt him".
"[In] reality, obviously hurt him ... only to the extent of what happened ... very unlucky where he got stabbed," he said.
The accused told the jury he remembered stabbing the alleged victim's shoulder but that his memory became hazy after that.
"I honestly can't remember ... after the first lunge,"
he said.
The court previously heard Mr Holdback was stabbed 10 times and was left crying out for help on the street.
When asked about his recollections after the stabbing, Liam Slann said he had been "in shock".
"I remember seeing him face down; it was a feeling that I'd never like to feel again," he said.
Under cross-examination, prosecutor Nicola Moore asked Liam Slann about CCTV that captured his father asking him where he stabbed Mr Holdback, to which the son replied "everywhere".
"Can't remember saying that," Liam Slann told the court today.
The prosecutor put to Liam Slann that he left the house with the knife before the alleged murder.
"You left the house, in the early hours of the morning, with a knife," Ms Moore said to the accused.
"That was one of the things you would take with you if you were meeting somebody in the early hours ..."
"No, that's not correct. That's not right at all," Liam Slann responded.
The trial is continuing.
